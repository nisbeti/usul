It is incumbent upon the Muslim to remain steadfast in their faith, to distance themselves from falsehood and its people, and not to be deceived by hollow political slogans. The revealed texts of Islam are comprehensive, sufficient, and provide full guidance—free of the need for such illegitimate forms of political participation.

Response to the Claim that Prophet Yusuf (Joseph) Ruled by Non-Divine Law

Some advocates for participating in non-Islamic political systems cite the verse in which Yusuf عليه السلام says: “Appoint me over the storehouses of the land; indeed, I am trustworthy and knowledgeable” (Yusuf 12:55). They interpret this as evidence that Yusuf held a governmental position within a non-Islamic (pagan) system—therefore, justifying participation in secular systems. They also point to the verse: “He could not have taken his brother under the king’s law unless Allah willed it” (Yusuf 12:76), interpreting it to mean that Yusuf operated under the king’s law, not divine law.

They argue further: there is no scriptural restriction on applying this example to others, and since prophetic stories are for emulation, Yusuf's case supports the permissibility of such political involvement. They add: ruling by other than what Allah revealed is a fundamental principle that applies across all revelations—and Yusuf, while affirming “Judgment belongs only to Allah” (Yusuf 12:40), still took office!

Detailed Refutation:

1. Yusuf was a Prophet Sent with Divine Law Whose Mission Was to Rule by It

Qur’an: “Mankind was one community; then Allah sent the prophets as bringers of good tidings and warners and sent with them the Scripture in truth to judge between the people concerning that in which they differed.” (Al-Baqarah 2:213)

“We sent Our messengers with clear proofs and sent down with them the Scripture and the balance that the people may maintain justice.” (Al-Ḥadīd 57:25) The phrases “to judge” and “that people may maintain justice” indicate that one of the principal purposes of prophethood is the implementation of divine justice through revealed law.

It is inconceivable that Allah would appoint a prophet and then allow him to legislate or enforce man-made law—contrary to divine legislation—without rebuke or correction.

Yusuf was a divinely guided, infallible prophet. Any interpretation that contradicts this infallibility must be rejected.

Even if his law differed slightly from Islamic law, it would have been rooted in revelation. The general principle that prophets must govern by divine guidance rules out the possibility that Yusuf governed by non-divine law.
