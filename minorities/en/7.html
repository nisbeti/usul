True renewal in fiqh does not require demolishing its principles, but rather reactivating them with awareness—reading contemporary realities through the lens of the revealed texts, not reshaping the texts to conform to reality.

Practical Examples That Demonstrate the Danger of Building Upon Minority Jurisprudence

First Case Study: The Fatwa Permitting Interest-Based Loans to Purchase Homes or Cars

In the 1990s, several prominent fatwa-issuing bodies—such as the European Council for Fatwa and Research (ECFR), Dr. Yusuf al-Qaradawi, and the Fiqh Council of North America (FCNA) in 1999, among others—issued rulings permitting Muslims living in the West to take out interest-bearing loans to purchase homes. The justification was framed around necessity, civil integration, and public interest (maslahah), especially in light of high rental costs and the desire to avoid long-term financial harm. They invoked the concept of “removing severe hardship.”

Official Position (Resolution 15/5, 2001):

"It is permissible for a Muslim residing in the West to take out an interest-based loan to purchase a home if the following conditions are met:

(a) No Shar‘i-compliant alternative is available. (b)

The individual fears moral or religious harm to themselves or their family due to lack of homeownership. (c) The housing need is deemed a necessity, not a luxury." (Source: ECFR Journal, Issue 8, p. 183)

Contradiction with Sharia Evidences:

Qur'an: “Allah has permitted trade and forbidden usury (riba).”

(Al-Baqarah 2:275)

Hadith: “Allah has cursed the one who consumes riba, the one who pays it...” (Sahih Muslim)

Dr. Yusuf al-Qaradawi argued in his book Fiqh al-Aqalliyyat al-Muslima:

“Necessity permits the forbidden.

The inability to own a home in the West can lead to family disintegration and deviation of children, which constitutes a necessity permitting the use of riba under strict conditions.”

ISNA's 2009 Conference: “It is permissible for Muslims in America to take out riba-based loans for housing or cars when no alternative exists, as need is treated as necessity.”
