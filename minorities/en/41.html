Educate Muslims on core Islamic beliefs, law, and political theory, especially the invalidity of secularism, the concept of divine sovereignty (ḥākimiyyah), and the obligation of implementing Islam comprehensively.

Usul al-fiqh (Islamic legal theory)

Islamic worldview (tasawwur islami)

Islamic systems of governance, economy, and social life

Refutations of liberalism, nationalism, and modernist distortions

3. Preservation and Assertion of Islamic Identity

Resist forced assimilation by strengthening Islamic identity markers:

Arabic language

Islamic dress

Distinct Islamic moral codes (ḥayā’, gender interaction, family structure)

Teach Muslim youth to critically challenge secular-liberal norms, including:

Individualism

Gender ideology

Moral relativism

Promote pride in Muslim civilization and history—not as nostalgia, but as an intellectual and practical alternative to Western models.

4. Constructive Social Engagement Without Compromise

Engage positively in society: contribute to public welfare (health, education, economy) in a manner that showcases Islamic ethics.

Avoid political entanglement in secular legislative systems that legitimize kufr laws.

Uphold Islamic ethics in interactions, volunteerism, and professional life without diluting religious principles.

Advocate for Muslims’ civil rights through lawful and public means—media, legal advocacy, petitions—without aligning with secular ideologies.
