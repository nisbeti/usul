The fatwa: If the swimsuit is modest according to Islamic law, it is permissible, because preserving activity (health interest) is greater than the harm.
The methodology: Linking "need" and "interest" to the legal ruling.
The flaw: Is there a legal text that permits the swimsuit? Its ruling is related to fulfilling a condition of the reason (like protecting modesty), but the methodology here assumes "interest" without a detailed text to rely on.
Eighth: The permissibility of a Muslim woman marrying a non-Muslim (a disbeliever from the People of the Book) in the West:
The established legal ruling: The definitive texts prohibit it categorically. Allah, Exalted is He, said: "And do not marry polytheists until they believe" (al-Baqarah: 221), and His saying: "And if you know that they are believing women, do not return them to the disbelievers; they are not lawful for the disbelievers, nor are the disbelievers lawful for them" (al-Mumtaḥanah: 10). Consensus is established on the prohibition.
The contradictory fatwa of "minority jurisprudence": The "European Council for Fatwa and Research" (ECFR) issued a fatwa (Decision No. 4/19 in Session 19, 2003) permitting a Muslim woman in the West to marry a non-Muslim (specifically a Christian) under the title of "civil marriage" only, provided that the marriage is civil and not religious, and ensuring her right to practice her religion and raise her children as Muslims.
Their justifications based on interest:
The necessity of integration into Western society and avoiding the isolation of Muslims.
The difficulty of finding a suitable Muslim partner in the Western environment, leading to spinsterhood, fornication, or apostasy from the religion.
Protecting the Muslim woman from falling into unlawful relationships if she is not able to marry.
Considering that civil marriage is not a legitimate marriage in the eyes of Islam, and thus does not entail the complete rulings of legitimate marriage! (A dangerous methodological justification).
Critique of the methodological concession:
Contradiction of the definitive text and consensus: Rational interest cannot abrogate a fixed ruling established by a definitive text and consensus, no matter the pressures. This undermines a fundamental principle of the religion.
Pure rational interest: The alleged necessity (difficulty in finding a Muslim husband) does not rise to the level of the legal necessity that permits prohibitions (like dying of hunger to permit consuming carrion). Spinsterhood or difficulty is not a justification for permitting a definitive prohibition. The interest here is "purely rational," considering comfort and social integration at the expense of a fixed legal ruling.
