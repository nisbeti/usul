A monarchical system based on sovereign administration relies on "ma'at" as a comprehensive principle for achieving balance and peace in the universe and society, rather than on fixed legislative texts or a written constitution, nor on comprehensive laws to regulate life, but rather on scattered legislations such as the legislation on the punishment for theft, for example. The king was the highest source of legislation, issuing orders according to his perceptions of "truth" and "justice," which were executed through a bureaucracy of ministers, priests, and deputies, without any original legislative support. This means that the laws were based on an authoritarian system rather than a legislative system, that is, through an executive order in specific matters that produced organizational effects, but did not rely on a religious foundation or a constitution, rather on the king's absolute authority. Thus, the delegation and empowerment that the king granted to Joseph (peace be upon him) when he made him the chief overseer of grain storage and crisis management allowed him to manage it as he wished. He could violate the situational royal sovereign legislation as he pleased without review from the king. Therefore, this situation cannot be applied to modern legislative systems that enact laws applicable in life and society across different life systems.

Summary:

The story of Joseph (peace be upon him) proves that he was empowered to govern, that he applied the law of Allah in judgment and politics, and that he was not part of a tyrannical system, nor of man-made laws that regulate the systems of life and society. Rather, it was merely a sovereign administration with a circumstantial nature, and he did not submit to the orders of an unjust king, but acted as he wished according to what his Lord commanded him. For this reason, Allah thanked him and praised him. Indeed, his story was a source of comfort for His Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him) when he faced great harm, especially during the Year of Sorrow. The lesson at the end of the surah is that his patience on the truth and on the path based on insight from Allah, which he did not deviate from, will lead him to governance just as our master Joseph reached before him. This precise analysis shows that the inference against the context of the surah and the reason for its revelation is completely misguided! It is not permissible in any way to use his story as evidence for the permissibility of entering into man-made parliaments or for the permissibility of acting contrary to the law of Allah.
