And indeed, it is a severe trial and a great sin for the bearer of the call to resort to taking the interest (maslaha) that his mind perceives, which has not been considered by the Sharia, as evidence to justify for himself the violation of the text that is definitively established and definitively indicative. Or to resort to what does not even reach the level of a dubious proof to take it as a justification for participating in a ruling of disbelief that rules by other than what Allah has revealed, even though this participation in the ruling of disbelief contradicts the definitive evidence that is definitively established and definitively indicative, which mandates ruling by what Allah has revealed and prohibits ruling by other than what Allah has revealed.

This is similar to taking the story of the Negus, whom the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) announced to the Companions on the day of his death and prayed the funeral prayer for him, as a justifying evidence for participating in a ruling of disbelief that rules by other than what Allah has revealed, going so far as to say that the Negus had embraced Islam during the time of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) and continued to rule by the system he had ruled by before he embraced Islam, even though it was a non-Islamic system. They cited six hadiths reported by al-Bukhari, which pertain to his death and the prayer for him, three of which were narrated by Jabir ibn Abdullah al-Ansari, and three by Abu Huraira. However, these six hadiths do not suffice to serve as a justifying evidence for participating in a ruling of disbelief that rules by the laws and systems of disbelief. The clarification of this is as follows.

1 – When al-Bukhari narrated these hadiths, he titled five of them as (The Chapter on the Death of the Negus), and the sixth he included in (The Chapter on Funerals). The six hadiths relate to the death of the Negus, the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) informing the Companions of his death, that he was a righteous man, that he was their brother, and the request for them to seek forgiveness for him and to pray the funeral prayer for him. This indicates that he was a Muslim.

2 – Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani commented in his book Fath al-Bari, the explanation of Sahih al-Bukhari, on al-Bukhari's title (The Death of the Negus) and his failure to title it with his Islam by saying: "It is perplexing that he – meaning al-Bukhari – did not title it with his Islam – meaning the Negus – and this is its place, and he titled it with his death because when the story of his Islam was not established with him, which is explicit in his death, he titled it so that it could be inferred from the prayer for him that he had embraced Islam."

3 – The wording of the hadiths that al-Bukhari reported indicates that the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) learned of the death of the Negus and his Islam on the day of his death through revelation, and it also indicates that the Companions did not know of his Islam and death until the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) informed them of that. In the hadith of Jabir, it is stated: "The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said when the Negus died: 'Today a righteous man has died, so stand and pray for your brother As'hama.'" In the hadith of Abu Huraira, it is mentioned: "The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) announced to them the Negus, the king of Abyssinia, on the day he died," which indicates that the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) learned of the death of the Negus and his Islam on the day of his death through revelation. And the statement of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) to the Companions as narrated by Jabir ibn Abdullah: "Today a righteous man has died" and his saying: "So stand and pray for your brother As'hama" indicates that they did not know of his Islam, because if they had known of his Islam, there would have been no need for the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) to use such expressions as "a righteous man" and "your brother," for he would not have used such expressions when calling them to pray for those who die among them from the Companions.
