Second: Scholars’ Explanations of the Condition
Meaning of ‘Necessity’:
Ibn Abidin in his Hashiyah (5/45): "Necessity means fear of perishing or losing a body part if the prohibition is not violated."
Condition of ‘No Exit’: Al-Qarafi in Anwar al-Buruq (4/200): "Committing the lesser of two harms is not permissible unless avoiding both is impossible. If both can be avoided, neither should be committed."
Verification by Ibn Taymiyyah: In Majmu‘ al-Fatawa (20/58): "When compelled to commit prohibited acts, the concession applies only to what is absolutely necessary. If one can avoid both, he must do so."

Conclusion: The foundational condition—as affirmed by the majority of jurists—is:
"That the legally responsible person (mukallaf) is inevitably compelled to commit one of the prohibited actions, with
no ability to avoid both."
This rests on key legal maxims:
Necessities permit the prohibited (to the extent of the necessity).
Harm is not removed by equal or greater harm.
Greater harm is removed by lesser harm (only under necessity).
