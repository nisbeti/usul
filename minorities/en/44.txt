Appointing Sharīʿah Judges Within Secular Systems
Response: A judge in this context is bound by secular law, and thereby enforces disbelief. Muslims must not be deceived by claims of “harm reduction.”
Exposing the Structural Illusion:
The judge in secular systems is a prisoner of procedural codes, not religious edicts.
He is obliged to prioritize the state constitution over Sharīʿah law. Example: Article 2 of the Egyptian Constitution states: “The principles of Sharīʿah are a main source of legislation”—i.e., not binding.
The judge is prohibited from enforcing ḥudūd (Islamic penal codes) such as apostasy or adultery, under the guise of “individual freedoms.”
Court Structure Evidence:
