Therefore, if a speculative interpretation conflicts with the certainty of their infallibility, that interpretation is nullified and not acted upon, because the evidence of infallibility is definitive.

Joseph was established in power, both by law and by authority.

Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And thus We established Joseph in the land, that he might settle therein wherever he willed﴿ [Yūsuf: 56].

This Qur'anic text indicates complete empowerment and absolute authority in action, such that one cannot claim that he was compelled to enforce the law of the king or that he was under the authority of a system that prevents him from applying the law of Allah.

Joseph used the law of Jacob in the incident of detention.

Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾It was not for him to take his brother in the religion of the king except that Allah willed﴿ [Yūsuf: 76].

The royal law prohibited the enslavement of the thief, while Joseph (peace be upon him) sought to issue a ruling from his brothers according to the law of Jacob ﴾Whoever is found in his saddlebag, his punishment is due﴿. This is the only detailed case that mentions a ruling given by our master Joseph (peace be upon him). He acted according to the law of Allah, not the law of the king, and he actually opposed the royal law. Had it not been for the authority he had, he would not have been able to oppose the royal law; thus, this is evidence that he was ruling by the Sharia.

Secondly: The indication of the sequence of attributes (al-‘Azīz → the King)

At the beginning of the story and during Joseph's (peace be upon him) interaction with his brothers, the Qur'an describes him as al-‘Azīz (the Mighty) (Yūsuf: 63, 88).

After his family arrived and his parents were raised to the throne, the description changed to that he was granted kingship: ﴾My Lord, indeed You have given me of the kingdom﴿ [Yūsuf: 101].

This progression indicates that Joseph became a person of supreme authority, possibly at the level of a king, or fully delegated, which makes him free to implement whatever rulings he wishes.
