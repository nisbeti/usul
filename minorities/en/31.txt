A Comprehensive Study on the Juristic Principle of "The Lesser of Two Evils" or "The Lesser of Two Harms" Foundational Introduction: The principle of "the lesser of two evils" or "the lesser of two harms" is among the well-known legal maxims frequently cited by jurists. However, it must be precisely understood within the confines of its strict Sharia-based regulations and not misused as a blanket justification for deviations or political participation in systems of disbelief, as some modern Islamic movements have done. This study explores the origin of the principle, the conditions for its application, the supporting evidence, and examples from foundational juristic texts such as those of al-Ghazali and Izz al-Din ibn Abd al-Salam. It also critiques the contemporary misapplication of the principle and refutes its wrongful use.
First: Definition of the Principle and Its Juristic Scope
The Principle: If a morally responsible person (mukallaf) is compelled to commit one of two prohibitions (i.e., cannot avoid both), then he must choose the one that is less harmful.
