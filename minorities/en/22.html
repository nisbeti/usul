It is not permissible to hold a position of authority in a system that rules by other than what Allah has revealed:

Al-Bukhari narrated that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Whoever performs an action that is not in accordance with our command, it is rejected."

And Muslim narrated: "There is no obedience to a creature in disobedience to the Creator." The point of evidence: political action in Western systems contradicts the command of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), because its origin is not legitimate, but rather conventional, based on opposing the Sharia and legislating what contradicts it; thus, it cannot be considered among the actions of Muslims, but is rejected.

Fifth: Fatwas prohibiting participation from scholars:

Sheikh Bakr Abu Zaid in his message "The Ruling on Belonging": "It is not permissible for a Muslim to participate in the democratic system because it is an acknowledgment of ruling by other than what Allah has revealed."

Sheikh Abdul Aziz bin Baz and Ibn Uthaymeen: They rejected participation in elections that involve conventional legislation or empowering those who do not follow the law of Allah.

Sheikh Abu Ishaq al-Hawaini (Egypt): He said in one of his recorded lessons: "Democracy is based on the idea that what is lawful is what the parliament permits, and what is unlawful is what the parliament prohibits, and this is explicit disbelief in Allah, because Allah alone is the legislator… So how can I be a Muslim and acknowledge this disbelief?" He emphasizes that elections contribute to the entrenchment of the rule of tyranny.

Sheikh Abdul Salam Yasin (Morocco) – Founder of the Justice and Spirituality Movement: He said in the Prophetic Methodology: "Participation in conventional legislative institutions is an implicit acknowledgment of the legitimacy of their laws, which are based on principles that grant sovereignty to humans and not to Allah." Despite his educational and political methodology, his stance on parliamentary elections under a conventional rule was explicitly rejecting.

Sheikh Omar Flatah (Tunisia) – From the scholars of Zaytuna: He clarified in one of his fatwas: "Running for office or voting in democratic systems that refer to humans and not to revelation is a form of referring to tyranny, and Allah has forbidden us from it in His saying: ﴾They wish to refer to tyranny﴿."

Sheikh Muhammad Ahmad Hussein (Jerusalem – Mufti of Palestine): He issued several fatwas through the Palestinian Fatwa House confirming that: "Any system that makes the people a source of legislation in place of divine revelation is a tyrannical system, and it is not permissible for a Muslim to participate in it," with the exception of elections that do not involve legislation (such as those related to purely service institutions).

Sheikh Abdul Qadir Auda (Egypt): In his book Islamic Criminal Legislation: "Legislation is a right exclusive to Allah, Exalted is He, and any human intervention in legislation is considered a serious transgression that the Sharia does not accept, and participation in it is an acknowledgment of it."
