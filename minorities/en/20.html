And this is precisely what the verse of condemnation indicates: ﴾They took their rabbis and their monks as lords besides Allah﴿ [al-Tawbah: 31], and the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) explained it in the hadith of Adi ibn Hatim – when he found the verse perplexing and said: "We did not worship them, O Messenger of Allah" – he (peace and blessings be upon him) said to him: "Did they not make lawful for you what is unlawful, and make unlawful for you what is lawful, so you follow them?" He said: Yes. He said: "Then that is your worship of them." (Narrated by al-Tirmidhi and classified as good).

The meaning is that granting humans legislative authority – that is, allowing them to permit and prohibit – is a form of deification and worship apart from Allah, even if it is not accompanied by prostration or rituals.

From here, the participation of a Muslim in Western elections, even with the intention of reform, is considered an implicit authorization and a practical agreement to a system that makes humans legislators alongside Allah, or without Allah. This is a form of associating partners with Allah in governance (shirk in al-hakamiyya), and it is – in its constitutional form – more severe and explicit than merely following an unjust ruler, because it makes legislation itself one of the rights acquired by citizens and representatives through general delegation. This is not befitting of the religion of monotheism (tawhid), nor does it leave any authority for the Sharia over the people.

Therefore, a Muslim who understands this meaning is not permitted to participate in this system, neither by running for office nor by voting, because in doing so, he would have participated in the deification of others besides Allah, acknowledged the rule of the tyrant (taghut), and assisted in legislating by other than what Allah has revealed.

The prohibition of ruling by other than what Allah has revealed, and the prohibition of enabling it through voting or participation:

The ruling by other than what Allah has revealed is categorically forbidden in Islam. Allah, Exalted is He, has placed it among the traits of disbelievers, oppressors, and sinners in three consecutive verses from Surah al-Ma'idah, depending on the state of the doer. He, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And whoever does not judge by what Allah has revealed - then it is those who are the disbelievers﴿, ﴾... then it is those who are the oppressors﴿, ﴾... then it is those who are the sinners﴿. These are decisive texts condemning those who rule by other than the law of Allah, or who are pleased with it, or who change it; and more serious than that is what has been mentioned in His statement:

﴾Have you not seen those who claim that they have believed in what has been revealed to you and what was revealed before you? They wish to refer to taghut (the tyrant) while they were commanded to disbelieve in it. And Satan wishes to lead them far astray.﴿ [al-Nisā’: 60]. The point of evidence: This verse decisively indicates that the mere desire to refer to taghut – that is, to anything other than the Sharia of Allah – nullifies the claim of faith. Thus, Allah has made their faith merely a "claim," because the desire to refer to anything other than what Allah has revealed undermines the very essence of submission to the Sharia. So how about one who wishes to make taghut a legislator and then elects it and appoints it to legislate man-made laws on its behalf in parliament?! This act is further removed from faith!
