How is it conceivable that Allah would send a prophet to a people, then empower him on earth, and grant him authority, yet he does not implement the law of Allah, but rather rules the people by the laws of tyrants?

This invalidates the claim that Joseph participated in the rule of disbelief or operated within its legislation.

❖ Fourth: Refuting the argument using the story of Joseph regarding the permissibility of entering secular parliaments

Joseph did not act according to the king's law; rather, he explicitly opposed it in a judicial context.

The incident of enslaving the thief can only be explained by the fact that he violated Egyptian law and acted according to the law of Jacob (peace be upon him).

Whoever claims that he worked under a disbelieving king and did not change anything has failed to reflect on the context of the texts.

Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And the king said, "Bring him to me; I will employ him for myself." And when he spoke to him, he said, "Indeed, you are today among those established and trusted."﴿.

Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And thus We established Joseph in the land.﴿.

﴾He may settle therein wherever he wills.﴿.

﴾My Lord, indeed You have given me [something] of sovereignty.﴿.

All of this indicates that he was not a subordinate being ruled, but rather a ruler who was empowered and held the decision-making authority.

❖ Fifth: Misapplying the analogy to an inappropriate context

Any attempt to apply the story of Joseph to the reality of contemporary disbelieving parliaments, or to the reality of political participation under tyrannical regimes that rule by other than what Allah has revealed, is a flawed application of analogy in an inappropriate context, because:

Joseph was neither coerced nor ruled; rather, he was a ruler.

Joseph did not submit to a secular constitution; rather, he submitted to the revelation of Allah.

Joseph ruled by the law of Allah, while secular parliaments legislate by other than His law.

Ancient societies, including Egypt during the Hyksos era or before the complete pharaonic period, did not know comprehensive constitutions, parliaments, or systems of legislative delegation as in modern systems.

Legislation was not an independent profession, and the king was not bound by a specific law. Rather, authority was absolute and individual, and governance was based on political decision-making.
