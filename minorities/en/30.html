Quraysh, after they requested him to do so against the wishes of his nobles, prevented them from both, and protected them, saying to them: "You are safe in my land; whoever insults you will be liable." And regarding his comment on Ja'far's response to him when he asked what the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) had brought to them, he said: "Indeed, this and what Jesus brought comes from the same source." And regarding Ja'far's response the next day when he asked him what they say about Jesus, he took a stick from the ground and said: "By Allah, I do not say anything about Jesus, son of Mary, except this stick" [Sirah Ibn Hisham]. They mistakenly thought from all of this that he had converted to Islam, even though the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) did not declare that he had converted, just as Umm Salamah, the wife of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), who was among the emigrants to Abyssinia, did not mention that he had converted when she spoke about him and what happened to them in the land of Abyssinia, where she said: "When we arrived in the land of Abyssinia, we were under the protection of the best neighbor: the Negus. We were safe in our religion, and we worshiped Allah, the Exalted, without being harmed, nor did we hear anything we disliked... And she said: By Allah, we were upon that when a man from Abyssinia came to dispute with his kingdom. She said: By Allah, we did not know a sadness that was greater upon us than the sadness we felt at that time, fearing that this man would prevail over the Negus and that a man who did not know our rights as the Negus knew them would come. And she said: After Allah aided the Negus against his enemy, and established him in his land, by Allah, we did not know a joy that was greater than that joy. She said: The Negus returned after Allah had destroyed his enemy, and he was established in his land, and the affairs of Abyssinia were secured for him, so we were in a good situation with him until we came to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) while he was in Mecca" [Sirah Ibn Hisham]. Umm Salamah's narration does not indicate that the Negus had converted to Islam.

On one hand, it seems that those who said that the Negus, whom the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) prayed for, is the same Negus who sent the emigrants, and the Negus who sent him the letter inviting him to Islam, did not take into account the narration of Anas ibn Malik, which was reported by Muslim in his Sahih, in which it is stated: "The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) wrote to Khosrow and to Caesar and to the Negus and to every tyrant inviting them to Allah, and it is not the Negus whom the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) prayed for."

As for the two letters mentioned by Muhammad Hamidullah in his book (Political Documents of the Prophetic Era), one is that the Negus wrote to the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) a letter in which he declared his Islam and his readiness to come to the Messenger if he commanded him to do so, and that he sent to him his son Arha ibn al-Asham ibn Abhar, and this letter was sent while the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) was in Mecca. As for the second letter, it is reported that the Negus sent it to the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) with those returning from Abyssinia from the companions of the Messenger while he was in Medina.

These two letters are not mentioned in any of the authentic hadith books. The author of (Political Documents of the Prophetic Era) stated that he took these documents from the historical books of al-Tabari, al-Qalqashandi, Ibn Kathir, and others, and he did not mention that he took either of them from any book of hadith. Historical books are not reliable, as they do not concern themselves with the verification of hadith like the books of hadith do; they gather their news like one who gathers firewood at night and does not know whether he is picking up a stick or a snake. Therefore, these two letters have no value, especially since they contradict the narration of Anas reported by Muslim, and the narration of Umm Salamah regarding the Negus and the emigrants in Abyssinia, and the lack of mention of the emigrants to Abyssinia, the last of whom to return was Ja'far, and any news indicating that the Negus had converted to Islam, even though Ja'far returned to the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) in the seventh year after the conquest of Khaybar, and after the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) had sent letters to the kings and princes. Therefore, these two letters are not valid, nor can they be used as evidence.
