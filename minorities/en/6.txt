Minority Jurisprudence has expanded the concept of ḍarūrah (necessity) beyond recognition. Even a doubtful necessity is now used to justify the permissibility of usury, the uncovering of private parts, or abandoning walāʾ and barāʾ (allegiance and disavowal). This represents a dangerous departure from the stringent criteria established in usul al-fiqh: that necessity must be real, certain, temporary, irremediable by other means, and strictly limited in scope.
Conclusion
The practice of “Minority Jurisprudence”—as undertaken by certain actors—reveals a deep methodological crisis. It entails dismantling the usulī architecture that governs ijtihād and replacing it with circumstantial rationalism that ultimately undermines the immutable foundations of the faith.
