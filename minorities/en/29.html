4 – These hadiths indicate that the Negus (al-Najashi) had embraced Islam shortly before his death, but they do not specify when his conversion occurred. Their wording suggests that the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) learned of his death and his Islam on the day he died through revelation, as we mentioned earlier. There is no authentic report indicating that the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) informed others of the Negus's Islam at any other time.

5 – These six hadiths do not indicate that the Negus whom the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) mourned to the Companions and for whom he performed the funeral prayer was the same Negus who was the ruler of Abyssinia at the time of the Muslims' migration there. Furthermore, there is nothing in them to suggest that he is the same Negus to whom the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) sent the letter inviting him to Islam, because the term "Negus" is not a proper name for a specific individual, but rather a title given to any ruler who governs Abyssinia, as mentioned by al-Nawawi in the twelfth volume of his commentary on Sahih Muslim, and as noted by Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani in the third volume of his book Al-Isabah.

6 – It is stated in the twelfth volume of Sahih Muslim in al-Nawawi's commentary that the Negus to whom the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) sent the letter inviting him to Islam at the end of the sixth year of the Hijra after returning from the Battle of Hudaybiyyah is not the same Negus for whom the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) performed the funeral prayer. The text of the hadith states: "… from Anas that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) wrote to Khosrow, to Caesar, to the Negus, and to every tyrant inviting them to Allah, the Exalted. And he is not the Negus for whom the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) prayed." From this hadith, it becomes clear that the Negus for whom the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) prayed is different from the Negus to whom the Muslims migrated to Abyssinia to live in his vicinity, and he is not the Negus to whom the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) sent a letter at the end of the sixth year inviting him to Islam. Rather, he is the Negus who came to the throne after the death of the Negus to whom the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) sent a letter with Amr ibn Umayyah al-Damri inviting him to Islam, who did not respond to the Messenger's invitation and did not embrace Islam. If he had responded to the Messenger and embraced Islam, the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) would have informed the Companions of that, and he would have prayed for him. Ja'far ibn Abi Talib and the emigrants with him would have known of his Islam, as they returned to the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) in the seventh year after the conquest of Khaybar, that is, after the Messenger sent the letter to the Negus. If he had embraced Islam, it would have been a cause for joy among the Muslims, especially after Allah granted them victory at Khaybar. The Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) would have given them glad tidings of his Islam, and it would not have been limited to his statement upon Ja'far's arrival: "I do not know which of them I should be more pleased with: the conquest of Khaybar, or the arrival of Ja'far" [Sirah Ibn Hisham]. He would have added to that: "Or the Islam of the Negus." However, he did not mention the Negus in this hadith, even though the situation would have warranted it had he responded to his invitation and embraced Islam.

7 – Those who claim that the Negus for whom the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) performed the funeral prayer is the same Negus to whom the Muslims migrated and sought refuge, and the same Negus to whom the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) sent the letter inviting him to Islam at the end of the sixth year of the Hijra have erred in their assertion. This misconception arose from the fact that the Negus to whom the Muslims migrated was praised and commended by the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) when he described him to those who were asked to migrate to him: "He is a king under whom no one is wronged, and his land is a land of truth" [Sirah Ibn Hisham]. This is also due to the fact that he provided good protection to those Muslims who migrated to him, ensuring their safety so that they could worship Allah without fear from anyone, and because he refused to hand them over to the Messenger's emissaries.
