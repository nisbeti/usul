Sheikh Sa‘īd Ḥawwā (Syria): In Jund Allah Thaqāfah wa Akhlāqan, denounced democracy as incompatible with Islam’s premise that sovereignty belongs to Allah alone. Seventh Argument: Necessity Cannot Justify Foundational Prohibitions
Qur’an: “Whoever is forced [by necessity], not intending transgression, incurs no sin.” (Al-Baqarah 2:173) This applies to physical necessity (food, drink)—not ideological participation in systems of ṭāghūt.
Imam al-Shāṭibī: “Necessities permit the forbidden only to the extent required.”
“The exception must not become the rule.”
Jurisprudential Conclusion: Participation in parliamentary elections within systems that assign legislative authority to human beings is a form of engaging in ḥukm al-ṭāghūt. It involves affirming and empowering a legal order that claims to replace divine revelation with popular will.
This contradicts the essence of faith (īmān), and depending on belief and intention, may constitute shirk akbar (major idolatry). The believer is commanded to disbelieve in ṭāghūt: “Whoever disbelieves in ṭāghūt and believes in Allah has grasped the firmest handhold...” (Al-Baqarah 2:256).
Seventh Argument: Muslims Have No Real Political Influence in the West
Muslims in Western countries lack meaningful political weight—not only Muslims, but all identity groups. The liberal system is designed to fragment society into minorities (whites, women, LGBTQ+, racial minorities, Muslims, Christians, etc.), none of whom wield real legislative influence. Actual power remains with political parties and elite capitalist circles that control media and authority. The idea that Muslims can achieve political leverage through the ballot box is an illusion inconsistent with how Western societies are structured.
Muslim participation is often used to legitimize the system and promote cultural assimilation, not to support Islam.
Political parties prevent Muslims from voting according to their religious principles and require adherence to party platforms that often contradict Sharī‘ah.
Conclusion
Participation in Western elections—whether by running for office or casting a vote—is not consistent with Shar‘ī guidelines. It:
Clearly violates the Qur’an and Sunnah.
Contradicts the positions of trusted early and contemporary scholars.
Enables evil and facilitates the alteration of religious principles.
Is based on speculative rational interests that do not justify what Allah has forbidden.
