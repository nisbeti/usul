He says: Umm Mubashshir told me that she heard the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) say in Hafsa's presence, "None of the people of the Tree who pledged allegiance beneath it will enter the Fire, if Allah wills." She said, "Indeed, O Messenger of Allah!" So he rebuked her. Hafsa then recited: {There is none of you who will not come to it} [Maryam: 71]. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said, "Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has said: {Then We will save those who were mindful of Allah, and leave the wrongdoers there, fallen on their knees}" [Maryam: 72].

Here, Hafsa denied one of Allah's attributes of action, namely, His saving the people of Badr and al-Hudaybiyya after their coming to the Fire, and affirmed for Him an attribute of action, namely, His punishing them first. Yet he (peace and blessings be upon him) did not declare her an unbeliever, if the hadith is authentically established from him. Both matters belong to the tenets of belief.

It is related in Musnad Ahmad (44/590, al-Risala edition):

" Ibn Idris narrated to us, saying: al-A'mash narrated to us, from Abu Sufyan, from Jabir, from Umm Mubashshir, the wife of Zayd ibn Haritha, who said: The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) was in Hafsa's house and said, 'No one who witnessed Badr and al-Hudaybiyya will enter the Fire.' Hafsa said, 'Does Allah, Mighty and Majestic, not say: {There is none of you who will not come to it} [Maryam: 71]?' She said: The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said, 'Enough! {Then We will save those who were mindful of Allah} (2) [Maryam: 72].'"

Shu'ayb said: "Authentic."

This narration, too, explicitly shows that Hafsa was unaware of these attributes of action belonging to Allah, yet he (peace and blessings be upon him) did not declare her an unbeliever on that account. This matter, too, is one of the matters of belief.
