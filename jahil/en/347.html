Among the clearest indications of the error in the claim that Allah punishes no one for a sin he commits or a misguidance he believes in unless he commits it after knowing that it is correct, and does so in defiance of his Lord, is that, were this so, there would be no difference between the party of misguidance, who went astray while thinking that they were rightly guided, and the party of guidance. Yet Allah distinguished between them in their names and rulings in this verse." End quote.

And Aba Butayn quoted Ibn Jarir in his commentary on the words of the Exalted: {A group He guided, while another group deserved misguidance. They took the devils as allies instead of Allah, yet they think that they are rightly guided.}

) Ibn Jarir said: "This indicates that the ignorant person is not excused." End quote. Al-Durar, 10/392. Also see Ibn Jarir's discussion of verse 104 of Surat al-Kahf.

Al-Tabari's statement concerning the Kharijites, which Ibn Hajar quoted from him, has already been mentioned: "Among those who inclined toward some of this discussion was al-Tabari in his Tahdhib. After citing the hadiths in this chapter, he said: In them is a refutation of the view that no one among the people of the qibla leaves Islam, after becoming subject to its ruling, unless he knowingly intends to leave it. This view is invalidated by the hadith's words: 'They speak the truth and recite the Quran, yet they pass out of Islam and do not cling to anything of it.' It is well known that they deemed the blood and property of Muslims lawful only because of an error in their interpretation of Quranic verses, taking them to mean something other than what was intended. He then related, with an authentic chain of transmission, from Ibn Abbas, who was told about the Kharijites and what they encounter when reciting the Quran. He said: 'They believe in its clear verses, but perish over its ambiguous ones.' The view mentioned is further supported by the command to kill them, alongside the previously cited hadith of Ibn Masud: 'It is not lawful to kill a Muslim except for one of three reasons,' including 'one who abandons his religion and separates from the community.'"
