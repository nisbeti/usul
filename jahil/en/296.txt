All of this, despite the falsehood and sins it may involve, has nothing to do with the idolatrous, unbelieving legislation whose author, supporter, administrator, and anyone who willingly seeks its judgment are deemed unbelievers ... The fact that such things are written down and codified does not change their nature at all. Commanding someone to commit a sin in writing is like commanding it verbally; merely writing it down does not make it idolatrous legislation.

The point is that administrative regulations which are permissible and involve neither sin nor violation are matters on which the Companions unanimously agreed and acted accordingly ... This includes what some scholars call in works of jurisprudence and legal theory "unrestricted interests" (al-masalih al-mursala). If such regulations involve violations that do not amount to deeming something forbidden lawful, then although they are regulations that contravene the Sharia, they are not of the idolatrous legislative kind.
For not every contravention of the Sharia is unbelief; some are sins, while others are unbelief ...

Failure to distinguish between these two kinds has led some astray, prompting them to declare an unbeliever anyone who engages in lease contracts and similar sales, or who abides by transport fares
