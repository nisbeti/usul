No one should say here that Imran only intended to ask whether the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) had taught them or not, since it is apparent that the Prophet did not teach them. How, then, could he answer "Yes"? This is supported by the hadith about those who are driven away from his Pool: he says, "My Companions," and it is said to him, "You do not know what they introduced after you." The apparent meaning of this is that he does not know who among them are the people of Paradise and who are the people of the Fire.

As for the Companions' saying to Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, "We can only think that you have become an unbeliever," Imam al-Nasa'i, may Allah have mercy on him, said (vol. 7, p. 7): Abu Dawud informed us; he said: al-Hasan ibn Muhammad narrated to us; he said: Zuhayr narrated to us; he said: Abu Ishaq narrated to us, from Mus'ab ibn Sa'd, from his father, who said: "We were discussing something, and I had only recently left the Age of Ignorance, so I swore by al-Lat and al-Uzza. The Companions of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him and his family) said to me, 'What a bad thing you have said! Go to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him and his family) and tell him, for we can only think that you have become an unbeliever.' So I went to him and told him. He said to me, 'Say, "There is no god but Allah alone, with no partner," three times; seek refuge in Allah from Satan three times; spit lightly to your left three times; and do not do it again.'"

Here, then, the Companions interpreted Sa'd as being an unbeliever destined to remain in the Fire because of the words he had said. All of this is interpretation concerning creed: first, that swearing by other than Allah is unbelief; and likewise, belief in what follows from this unbelief, namely, that its perpetrator remains in the Fire.
