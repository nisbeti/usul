The righteous are exempted from the prohibition of wine. Those whom Umar required to repent made this same mistake, and there are other examples of this kind. They are to be called upon to repent and the proof is to be established against them. If they persist, they then become unbelievers; they are not judged to be unbelievers before that. The Companions likewise did not judge Qudama ibn Maz'un and his companions to be unbelievers when they made the mistake they made in their interpretation.(1)
This incident shows that, by the consensus of the Companions, error in interpretation prevents a person from being declared an unbeliever. It also falls under the general meaning of the words of Allah, Exalted is He: {There is no blame upon you for what you do by mistake} (al-Ahzab: 5).
Nevertheless, not every error in interpretation is considered an acceptable excuse that prevents a person from being declared an unbeliever:
An error in interpretation that is excusable is one that arises from examining a legal proof, but misunderstanding it.
An error in interpretation that is not excusable is one that arises from mere opinion and caprice, without relying on a legal proof. An example is Iblis's refusal to prostrate to Adam, arguing that {I am better than him: You created me from fire and created him from clay} (al-A'raf: 12). This was mere opinion. Another example is the esoteric interpretations by which the Batinites set aside religious obligations; these arose from mere caprice.
This error in interpretation is what prevented him from being declared an unbeliever, just as error in interpretation prevented Qudama ibn Maz'un (may Allah be pleased with him), who was also one of the people of Badr, from being declared an unbeliever when he drank wine, believing it to be permissible and interpreting the words of Allah in this way: {There is no blame upon those who believe and do righteous deeds for what they have consumed, so long as they are mindful of Allah and believe and do}
