These caliphs were opposed by people of their own time: Zayd, Ali, and others opposed Umar; Umar opposed Uthman; and Umar opposed Abu Bakr in many cases. Yet none of them said to anyone who opposed him, "Why have you opposed me, when I am the imam?" Had following them by imitation been obligatory, they would not have allowed anyone to act otherwise than as required. As for the sophistry of those who cite the words of Allah, Exalted is He: {O you who believe, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. If you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger, if you believe in Allah and the Last Day. That is better and fairest in outcome}, this verse invalidates imitation beyond any doubt. It is the greatest proof against them, because He, Exalted is He, commanded obedience to those in authority only in what they transmitted to us from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), and not in anything else. If they say, "Rather, it is in what they say by their own reasoning," we have already shown that this assumption is invalid. And even if we conceded that, it would only make obedience obligatory to all of them, not to some of them, because Allah, Mighty and Majestic, did not say, "some of those in authority among you." Rather, He commanded us to follow those in authority among us, meaning all the people of knowledge. When they reach consensus on a matter, there is no disagreement that following them is obligatory. Allah, Exalted is He, made this clear in the very same verse and left us in no ambiguity, saying: {O you who believe, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. If you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger, if you believe in Allah and the Last Day. That is better and fairest in outcome}. Thus, when disagreement arises, Allah, Exalted is He, omitted referral to those in authority and required referral only to the Quran and the Sunnah. He commanded obedience to those in authority among us only so long as there is no disagreement. This is our position, and praise be to Allah. As for the report, "Muadh established this practice for you," we have said that it is a hadith whose chain of transmission is unsound. Even if it were sound, it would not constitute proof for them, because joining the imam in prayer is an established practice not because Muadh did it, but because the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) approved it and commanded it. His words were: "Whatever you catch, pray; and whatever you miss, make up." Otherwise, Muadh did something in prolonging the prayer that angered the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), who forbade him from doing it again. If what Muadh did were a Sunnah, then prolonging the prayer when leading people would be a Sunnah, and that is wrong. It is therefore established that neither Muadh's actions nor anyone else's are a Sunnah unless the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) commands and approves them. This is our position, not theirs. As for the report, "Follow the two who come after me," it is an unsound hadith, because it is narrated from an unknown client of Rib'i and from al-Mufaddal al-Dabbi, who is not a reliable authority.
