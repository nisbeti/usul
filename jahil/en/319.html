Deeming it lawful to shed the blood of worshippers who affirm the oneness of Allah is an error, and the error of leaving a thousand unbelievers alive is less grave than the error of shedding the blood of a single Muslim. Among the proofs cited by those who did not declare them unbelievers is his statement in the third hadith of the chapter, after describing them as passing out of the religion as an arrow passes through its target: "The archer looks at his arrow," until he said, "Then he examines the notch to see whether anything has caught on it." Ibn Battal said: The majority held that

The scholars held that the Kharijites were not outside the body of the Muslims, based on the words, "he doubts about the arrowhead," since doubt is a form of uncertainty. And since uncertainty arose in this matter, they could not be definitively judged to have left Islam, for whoever has entered Islam with certainty does not leave it except with certainty. He said: Ali was asked about the people of al-Nahrawan, "Have they disbelieved?" He replied, "They fled from disbelief." I say: If this report from Ali is authentic, it may be understood to mean that he had not learned of the belief they held which led those who declared them unbelievers to do so. There is room to question his use of the words, "he doubts about the arrowhead," for in some versions of the hadith mentioned—as previously indicated and as will be discussed later—nothing of it clung to the arrow, while in others it passed through the dung and blood. The way to reconcile the two is that he was uncertain whether anything was on the arrowhead, then became certain that nothing had clung to the arrow, nor to any part of it, from what had been shot. It is also possible to attribute the difference to differences among individuals within their ranks, and to understand the words, "he doubts," as indicating that some of them may have retained something of Islam. Al-Qurtubi said in al-Mufhim: "The view that they are unbelievers is more apparent from the hadith." He said: "According to the view that they are unbelievers, they are fought and killed, and their property is taken as spoils. This is the view of a group of hadith scholars regarding the property of the Kharijites.
