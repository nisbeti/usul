Consider how he said that the reason Ali did not fight them and kill them was that it had not become clear to him that they were the people intended by those hadiths. It is evident from the words of these scholars that these hadiths refer to specific people against whom proof had been established, as it must be. Whoever agrees with them in any of their doctrines, and whose ignorance could be excused and whose interpretation could be considered, has proof established against him. But if his ignorance cannot be excused, because the matter is necessarily known to be part of the religion, he is judged to be an unbeliever. Al-Tabari judged them to be unbelievers even though, in his view, they were interpreting the texts, because his school of thought does not excuse ignorance in matters of creed, as will come, Allah willing. However, as stated earlier, it has not been established that these people were interpreting the texts, so there is a basis for al-Tabari's statement.

Second: Those who held that they were not unbelievers did so on the basis of the Prophet's statement, "And he doubts about the arrowhead"—that is, he is uncertain ("he doubts about the arrowhead"): the archer wonders whether there is any trace of the prey in the notch of the arrow or not. This means that one is uncertain whether these Kharijites retain any remnant of religion or not.

However, as stated earlier, this uncertainty on the part of the observer is countered by the Prophet's explicit statement that they are the worst of creation and the dogs of the people of Hell, and other such statements. This statement (that is, the one who wonders whether any trace of the prey remains in the arrowhead or not) concerns the observer of their condition, not the reality of their case. What the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said did indeed come to pass: some people doubted whether they were unbelievers and did not judge them to be so, as is clear from the words of Ibn Hajar cited earlier.

Third: They cited Ali's statement, "They fled from unbelief." It is found in Abd al-Razzaq's work, and its chain includes someone who is unnamed. As stated earlier, it is possible that this was Ali's personal reasoning before he came to know their true nature, as indicated by the discussion cited earlier from Ibn Hajar.
