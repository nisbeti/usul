Perhaps this ruling by other than what Allah has revealed would have continued. Likewise, it is established in al-Bukhari that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said regarding the captives of Badr: "If al-Mut'im ibn Adi were alive and then spoke to me about these foul men, I would release them to him." Here, the Prophet would have released some of the leading men of Quraysh, and there is no doubt that this could have led to their disbelief continuing. Likewise, giving to those whose hearts are to be reconciled, according to the view that polytheists who are hoped to enter Islam may be given from the zakat; and likewise, the Prophet's supplication to Allah that He lift the drought from Quraysh, when they were polytheists at war with him, as related in al-Bukhari. The drought was lifted, and he had sought Allah's help in this matter.

There is no doubt that they continued in their disbelief thereafter, and in their rule by disbelief and their barring people from the way of Allah, until Allah granted the conquest of Mecca afterward. Thus, retaining these people meant retaining their disbelief. But this was certainly not what the Messenger of Allah intended by his supplication for them. Likewise, his statement, peace be upon him, that we would make a truce with the Romans, and his approval of that; there is no doubt that this truce meant the Romans remained in power and their disbelief continued. Likewise, the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah undoubtedly entailed the disbelievers' rule in Mecca being retained and their not being confronted in battle. But since there was a greater benefit, the truce was given precedence. Likewise, the Muslims rejoiced at the Romans' victory over the Persians. There is no doubt that this strengthened the Romans' rule based on disbelief. But since this rule was less harmful to Islam than that of others, they rejoiced in it. No one would say that they rejoiced in disbelief; rather, they rejoiced that the Persians' disbelief would be diminished as a result of their defeat.
