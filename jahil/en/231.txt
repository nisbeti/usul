Here, establishing the proof came from another angle: the faith of those whose knowledge and testimony they trusted in the soundness of the proof. Sometimes you present someone with proof from an authentic report or from the Sunnah of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), and he says to you, for example, "I follow such-and-such scholar, whom I trust." So you present him with that scholar's statement affirming the soundness of the evidence you cited and acting in accordance with its implications. If he still does not submit, then the proof has been established against him. This indicates his arrogance and wrongdoing, and that he is not excused on the grounds of his interpretation. For Allah cited as proof against them the faith of their leading scholar, Abdullah ibn Salam. This was therefore a sound argument and a valid counterargument that exposed the matter in establishing the proof.
