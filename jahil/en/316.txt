Second: what shows that they are unbelievers is his saying "the worst of creation"
and his saying "the dogs of the people of the Fire", which shows that their standing is lower than that of the people of the Fire. This is also the evident view of Abu Umama, in his saying "These were Muslims and then became unbelievers". As for what Ibn Battal reported in his words: ((The majority of scholars hold that, in this rebellion of theirs, they did not leave the body of the believers, because of his saying (peace and blessings be upon him): "and he has doubts about the notch of the arrow", for having doubts is uncertainty, and when there is uncertainty about this, it cannot be ruled with certainty that they have left Islam entirely, since one whose bond of Islam is established with certainty is not ruled to have left it except with certainty. And it is narrated from Ali ibn Abi Talib by several routes that he was asked about the people of Nahrawan: "Are they unbelievers?" He said: "It is from unbelief that they fled." It was said: "Then are they hypocrites?" He said: "The hypocrites remember Allah only a little." It was said: "Then what are they?" He said: "They are a people whose effort went astray and who were blind to the truth; they rebelled against us, so we fought them."))
The reply to this is found in Fath al-Bari by Ibn Hajar (12/299):
"It has been cited as evidence by those who hold that the Kharijites are unbelievers, and this is what al-Bukhari's arrangement implies, since he placed them alongside the heretics and gave those who interpret a separate chapter heading. Judge Abu Bakr ibn al-Arabi stated this plainly in his commentary on al-Tirmidhi, saying: The correct view is that they are unbelievers, because of his saying (peace and blessings be upon him) 'they pass out of Islam', and his saying 'I will kill them as 'Ad were killed', and in another wording 'Thamud', and each of these perished only through unbelief; and because of his saying 'they are the worst of creation', and only unbelievers are described in this way; and because of his saying 'they are the most hateful of creation to Allah, Exalted is He'; and because they judge everyone who differs from their creed to be an unbeliever who will abide forever in the Fire, so they are more deserving of the name than those they judge. Among the later imams who inclined to this view is Shaykh Taqi al-Din al-Subki."
