3. It is related in Sahih Muslim (1/96):

On the authority of Usama ibn Zayd—and this is the hadith of Ibn Abi Shayba—who said: "The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) sent us on an expedition. We attacked the Hurqat of Juhayna at dawn, and I caught up with a man who said, 'There is no god but Allah.' I stabbed him, but I felt troubled by what I had done. I mentioned it to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), and the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said, 'Did he say, "There is no god but Allah," and you killed him?' I said, 'Messenger of Allah, he only said it out of fear of the weapon.' He said, 'Did you split open his heart so that you could know whether he said it or not?' He kept repeating this to me until I wished that I had only become Muslim that day." Here, Usama thought the man was an unbeliever, and the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) excused him and did not punish him.

Sahih al-Bukhari (5/160)

From Salim, from his father, who said: The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) sent Khalid ibn al-Walid to Banu Jadhimah and called them to Islam. They could not properly say, "We have embraced Islam," so they kept saying, "We have become Sabi'ah, we have become Sabi'ah." Khalid began killing some of them and taking others captive, and he gave each of us a captive. Then, one day, Khalid ordered each of us to kill his captive [p. 161]. I said, "By Allah, I will not kill my captive, and none of my companions will kill his captive," until we came to the Prophet.
