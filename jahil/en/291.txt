Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyya said in Majmu al-Fatawa (29/25–26):
"A person's words have two kinds of implications:
The first is the implication of his words that is true. He must adhere to this, for what truth entails is itself true, and it may be attributed to him if it is known that he would not refuse to adhere to it once it became clear to him.
The second is the implication of his words that is not (1) true. He is not required to adhere to this, since at most it means that he has contradicted himself. It is established that contradiction occurs in every scholar other than the Prophets. Then, if it is known that he would adhere to it once it became clear to him, it may be attributed to him; otherwise, it is not permissible to attribute to him a statement that he would not adhere to if its error became clear to him, since he has said what entails it without realizing that the statement is erroneous, and it does not (2) bind him.
This distinction regarding people's disagreement over the implications of a school of thought—whether they are part of that school or not—is better than stating either position without qualification. Whatever implications the speaker accepts once they have been made clear to him are his words; whatever he does not accept is not his words, even if he is inconsistent." End quote.
