He did not declare this person an unbeliever, judging him by the presumption that his status of unbelief remained. This is evidence that one is not declared an unbeliever on account of an erroneous interpretation.

Moses' words to al-Khidr, "You have certainly done a terrible thing" and "a grave thing," show that Moses interpreted it as impermissible to kill the boy, since he had killed no one. He judged al-Khidr's act to be a grave wrong, whereas in reality it was obedience to Allah. Thus Moses, on the basis of his interpretation, prohibited something recommended or obligatory, because the established principle for him was that it was impermissible to take a life except in retaliation for another life. Yet Allah did not declare him an unbeliever, nor did al-Khidr; rather, they excused him, even though he was a prophet. How much more so should those who are lesser than them in knowledge and understanding be excused?

It is related in Sahih al-Bukhari: Yahya ibn Yahya narrated to us; Hammad ibn Zayd informed us, from Yazid al-Duba'i; Mutarrif narrated to us, from Imran ibn Husayn, who said: It was asked, "Messenger of Allah, are the people of Paradise known from among the people of the Fire?" He replied, "Yes." It was asked, "Then what is the point of the deeds performed by those who act?" He said, "Everyone is facilitated toward that for which he was created."

This hadith clearly indicates that Imran was unaware of Allah's knowledge of what would come to pass, since his question was phrased in the passive voice, thus encompassing the possibility that the knowledge belonged to anyone whatsoever. The basic purpose of a question is to seek knowledge. Yet here the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) did not declare him an unbeliever for not knowing these two grave matters: first, Allah's knowledge of what deeds His servants would perform and of their ultimate fate; and second, the matter of predestination, which is one of the foundations of faith.
