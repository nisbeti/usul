Al-Shawkani's statement in al-Sayl al-Jarrar (4/373), commenting on the author of Hada'iq al-Azhar's statement, "Execution is the prescribed punishment for a belligerent disbeliever and an apostate, whatever the form of disbelief": He, may Allah have mercy on him, said: "As for his words, 'whatever the form of disbelief,' the author intended to include, by convention, those who disbelieve on the basis of interpretation under the category of apostasy. This is a grievous misstep, one that makes the hands and mouth cry out; a stumble that cannot be excused, and a slip that cannot be forgiven. If this were sound, most of the Muslims on the face of the earth would be apostates, because the followers of the four schools are Asharis and Maturidis, and they declare the Mu'tazilites and their followers to be disbelievers, while the Mu'tazilites declare them disbelievers. All of this is one of the promptings of Satan the accursed, and a pulse of extreme partisanship and great injustice." End quote.

Al-Shawkani also said, commenting on the statement, "One who interprets [the texts] is like an apostate": "I say: Here tears are shed, and Islam and its people are mourned for what religious partisanship has brought upon most Muslims: accusations of disbelief hurled at one another, not on the basis of the Sunnah, nor the Quran, nor a clarification from Allah, nor proof, but because the fires of religious partisanship burned fiercely, and Satan the accursed gained the power to divide the Muslims. He prompted them to make accusations against one another based on things as insubstantial as dust in the air and a mirage in a barren plain. Alas for the Muslims, in the face of this ..."
