Belief concerning wine is divided into that which corresponds to reality and that which does not. The one who says of something, "It is lawful," and the one who says, "It is forbidden," with one of them being right and the other wrong, are like the one who says that He, Glorified is He, will be seen and the one who says that He will not be seen, with one of them being right and the other wrong. Lying about Allah, Exalted is He, whether by mistake or deliberately in this case, is like lying about Him deliberately or by mistake in the other case. For one person reports of Allah that He commanded such-and-such and permitted it, while the other reports that He forbade it and declared it unlawful. One of them is certainly mistaken.
If it is said: "The difference between the two is that it is possible that, in reality, it is neither lawful nor forbidden; rather, it is lawful for the one who believes it to be lawful and forbidden for the one who believes it to be forbidden."
It is said: "This is false for many reasons. We have mentioned in the book al-Miftah and elsewhere (among them) that it contradicts the explicit text of the Quran and the Sunnah, and the consensus of the Companions."
This preceding discussion is directed at those who declare others unbelievers on the basis of interpretation, because they say that creed is established by solitary reports; thus, the argument applies to them.
As for those who say that creed is not established by a solitary report, the following is the response to them:
First: His saying, Exalted is He, "When Allah and His Messenger have decreed a matter": "matter" is indefinite in the context of a conditional clause, so it encompasses knowledge and practice.
Second: "Whatever the Messenger gives you, take it": the word "whatever" is general.
Third: "So that they may gain understanding of the religion": religion encompasses knowledge and practice.
