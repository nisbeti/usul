No one is declared an unbeliever when there is a possibility [of another interpretation]. The general view is that a traveler who has not resolved to settle somewhere continues to shorten his prayers indefinitely and is therefore not considered a resident. Thus, many cases are not covered by the prohibition in the hadith.
The third argument: The hadith concerns someone who chooses to reside among the polytheists; it does not apply to someone who goes to them out of fear of oppression or the like, or to someone unable to leave because of citizenship issues or similar circumstances. Someone excused for residing there is not covered by the prohibition on fighting alongside the polytheists in such a situation; that is, he is permitted to reside among them for the reasons we have mentioned. If, after that, he assists them, joins their army, and fights alongside them for the sake of money, the hadith prohibiting this does not apply to him!
The fourth argument: The early generations—of whom no innovation is known—did not disagree that choosing to reside among the polytheists is not disbelief. This is demonstrated by their consensus that inheritance passed between the Emigrant and the non-Emigrant. It is well known that an unbeliever does not inherit from a Muslim. Al-Harith al-Muhasibi(56) said: "They agreed that the first verse was abrogated, and that Allah—glorious is His praise—established inheritance among the believers on the basis of kinship, not emigration."(57) He then said: "They used to inherit from one another on the basis of Islam and emigration: a man would embrace Islam but not emigrate, and so he would not inherit from his brother. This was abrogated by: 'And those related by blood are closer to one another in the Book of Allah than the believers and the emigrants.'"(58) Allah made it obligatory to support those who had not emigrated if they sought our help in religion. He, Exalted is He, said: "And those who have believed but have not emigrated—you have no duty of protection toward them until they emigrate. But if they seek your help in religion, then you must help them, except against a people with whom you have a treaty. And Allah sees all that you do."
.
The fifth argument: Scholars have explained this hadith in several ways that do not entail that someone who resides among the polytheists is an unbeliever. These are as follows:
The first interpretation: It means that he is absolved of liability for his blood-money and of retaliation against the person who killed him. Al-Shafi'i said: "If this is established, I think the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) gave what he gave to them voluntarily, and informed them that he was absolved of responsibility for every Muslim among the polytheists—Allah knows best—in the land of polytheism, so that they would know that no blood-money or retaliation was due to them."(59) Ibn Hazm said: "By doing so, he has wronged himself. If someone who does not know that he is a Muslim kills him, there is no retaliation and no blood-money; only expiation is due, according to the explicit text of the Quran."(60)
The second interpretation: It is possible that the hadith means disavowal of their guardianship, which concerns inheritance. This is of the same kind as the meaning of His saying, Exalted is He: "And those who have believed but have not emigrated—you have no duty of protection toward them until they emigrate." The scholars unanimously agreed that this refers to inheritance, but the ruling was abrogated.(61)
