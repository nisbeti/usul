But if this interpretation concerns a matter of belief, such as the attributes of Allah, then he is declared an unbeliever. Their distinction is supported by no evidence from the Book, the Sunnah, or the words of a Companion. If they are asked, "Why did you excuse the one who interprets matters of practice?" they begin citing the verse, "There is no blame upon you for what you do by mistake," and other verses and hadiths that excuse one who errs in interpretation. It is then said to them: What is the difference between matters of knowledge and matters of practice in their being covered by these general texts? And on what basis have you restricted this general wording to matters of belief alone? Second, every matter of practice entails a matter of belief. For example, a man holds that the witr prayer is obligatory. This act entails several matters of belief, including the belief that Allah recorded it in the Preserved Tablet—and this is a matter of belief—and the belief that Allah said to this matter, "Be," and it came to be; this is an attribute of action belonging to Allah, Glorified and Exalted is He. Likewise, he believes that He, Exalted is He, commanded Gabriel to convey this to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him); this too is an attribute of action belonging to Allah, Exalted is He. By implication, he also denies the attribute that Allah, Exalted is He, permitted its omission; likewise, he denies that He recorded permission to omit it in the Preserved Tablet, and denies that Allah commanded Gabriel to convey permission to omit it. All of these, as you can see, are matters of belief, inseparable from any of the matters they have called practical. And I wonder: what is the difference between someone who denies an attribute of action belonging to Allah, Glorified and Exalted is He, that is connected to a practice—such as His making something obligatory—while interpreting the matter mistakenly, like someone who denies that Allah made rinsing the mouth and sniffing water into the nose obligatory in ablution, for example, and someone who denies an attribute of action belonging to Allah that is not connected to it…
