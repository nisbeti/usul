(If a non-Arab utters a word of disbelief, faith, divorce, emancipation, sale, purchase, settlement, or release from a claim, he is not held accountable for any of that, because he has not undertaken its implications and did not intend it. Likewise, if an Arab utters a foreign word that indicates one of these meanings, but does not know what it means, he is not held accountable for any of that, because he did not intend it; for intention can only be directed toward something known or supposed. If an Arab intends, by uttering any of these words, to convey their meanings, knowing what they mean, then that takes effect from him. But if he does not know their meanings—for example, if an Arab says to his wife, "You are divorced according to the Sunnah" or "according to innovation," while she is pregnant (1), without knowing what the two expressions mean, or if he utters the word for khulʿ (wife-initiated divorce), or another word, or the word for taking back a divorced wife, marriage, or emancipation, without knowing what it means, despite being an Arab, he is not held accountable for any of that, since he has no awareness of what it denotes and therefore cannot intend the expression that signifies it.) I say: In our time, this applies to someone who does not know the meaning of the word "democracy" and what it denotes, and so praises it, supposing that it means only—as many ordinary people believe—what stands in opposition to oppression, enslavement, and the confiscation of freedoms and rights, and the like ... Such a person is not declared an unbeliever until he is made aware that it fundamentally means the people legislating for the people, or the sovereignty of the masses rather than the sovereignty of Allah alone ... Until he knows this, he has no awareness of its meaning of disbelief and cannot intend it.
For the Sharia does not recognize any distinction between two things that are alike and equivalent ... "Are your disbelievers better than those, or have you been granted immunity in the scriptures?"
