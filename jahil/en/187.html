Third: that one person reports something and claims to have heard it from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), and he does not deny it. What indicates that it is true is that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) does not tacitly approve of a lie.

Fourth: that one person reports something and claims that a large number of people heard it from him, and none of them denies it. This indicates that it is true, because if it were a lie, they would not all have agreed to remain silent rather than declare him a liar. The knowledge arising from all of this is acquired, because it results from reflection and inference.

Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyya said: I say, his restriction of the solitary reports that yield knowledge is not comprehensive, because among the things that yield knowledge are reports that the Messenger himself also received with acceptance, such as his reports concerning Tamim al-Dari and what he reported, affirming him in it. Another example is two people reporting an incident when one knows that they did not collude over it, and it is ordinarily unlikely that they would agree to lie about it or make a mistake, and so on.

I say: The solitary reports that yield knowledge are not confined to these cases. Rather, a person receiving a report may attain knowledge of which he has no doubt through many such reports. For example, if someone who has never been found to lie tells him that he witnessed it, he then becomes certain of it with certainty that is necessary or nearly so. Likewise, if reporting it would bring harm upon the person, yet he reports it out of religious devotion and fear of Allah, Exalted is He; for example, if he comes forward voluntarily and reports against himself a crime for which he has incurred a prescribed punishment, seeking to be purified of it through that punishment; or if he admits against himself a right claimed against him when there is no evidence and no oath being demanded of him, and he faces no fear of harm if he denies it; or if he informs the mufti of something he did so that he may obtain for himself
