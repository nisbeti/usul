He may do so based on an exercise of independent reasoning like his own: by following the example of shaykhs and scholars who did it, and who did so because they thought it beneficial; or because they had heard a fabricated hadith. If proof establishing the prohibition has not been presented to such people, they will not be punished. As for reward, they may be rewarded in the sense that they are superior to others of their kind; but they will not be rewarded for drawing near to Allah through such acts.

The excusable error in independent reasoning occurs in both types of questions: those concerning transmitted reports and those concerning knowledge, as has been explained at length elsewhere. An example is someone who believes something to be established on the basis of a verse or hadith, while there is evidence that contradicts it and clarifies what is meant, but he does not know that evidence. This includes someone who believes that the one to be sacrificed was Isaac, on the basis of a hadith he believes to be authentic; or someone who believes that Allah cannot be seen, citing His words: {Eyes cannot perceive Him} and His words: {It is not for any human being that Allah should speak to him except by revelation or from behind a veil}. Aisha used these two verses as evidence that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) did not see Allah. They indicate this only in a general sense.
