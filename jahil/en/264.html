Hearing may be mistaken, as a group among the early and later scholars believed. Or someone may believe that the dead do not hear the speech of the living, because he thinks that His saying, {For you cannot make the dead hear}, indicates this. Or he may believe that Allah does not marvel, as Shurayh believed, because he thinks that marveling occurs only through ignorance of the cause, whereas Allah is exalted above ignorance. Or he may believe that Ali is superior to the Companions, because he thinks the hadith of the bird is authentic: that {the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said, "O Allah, bring to me the one dearest to You among Your creation, to eat this bird with me."} Or he may believe that whoever spies for the enemy and informs them of the Prophet's campaign

is a hypocrite, as Umar believed of Hatib, saying, "Let me strike off the head of this hypocrite." Or he may believe that whoever becomes angry on behalf of some hypocrites is a hypocrite, as Usayd ibn Hudayr believed of Sa'd ibn Ubadah, saying, "You are a hypocrite, arguing on behalf of the hypocrites." Or he may believe that certain words or verses are not part of the Quran because their transmission has not been established for him by a reliable report. It is related that more than one of the early scholars rejected certain Quranic expressions, such as some of them rejecting {And your Lord decreed}, saying, "Rather, it is 'And your Lord enjoined.'" Some rejected His saying, {And when Allah took the covenant of the prophets}, saying, "Rather, it is the covenant of the Children of Israel," and so it is in the reading of Abdullah. Some rejected {Have not those who believed despaired}, saying, "Rather, it is 'Have not ...'"
