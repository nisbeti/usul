We know that this is one of the matters of Islam in which it is permissible to investigate. The other person holds that some of those opinions do not entail declaring anyone an innovator or an unbeliever. Differences of this kind appeared among the Companions and the Successors, while affection and love remained between them. Whenever a new matter arose over which they differed, and their disagreement led to allegiance and aversion, turning away from one another and severing ties—and perhaps even reached the point of declaring one another unbelievers—you know that this has nothing to do with religion. Rather, everyone of sound reason must avoid such matters and refrain from delving into them.

Allah Most High has made it a condition of our adherence to Islam that we become brothers in this regard. He, Exalted is He, says: {And remember Allah's blessing upon you: when you were enemies, He brought your hearts together, and by His blessing you became brothers} [Al Imran: 103].

He said: "If someone says: Delving into matters of divine decree, the divine attributes, and faith leads to severing ties and turning away from one another, so they must be set aside and avoided, as you have established."

"The answer is: We said this only about newly introduced matters. As for these matters, they must be accepted according to what has been established through transmission from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) and his Companions. It is not permissible for us to turn away from transmitting, narrating, and explaining them, just as with the foundations of Islam, calling people to affirm the oneness of Allah, and proclaiming the two testimonies of faith. We have made clear that the straight path is with the people of hadith, and that the truth lies in what they narrated and transmitted."))
