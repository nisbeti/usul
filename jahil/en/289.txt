This is what Allah, Exalted is He, says: {On the day We seize with the greatest seizure, We will indeed take vengeance} [al-Dukhan: 16]—the day of Badr. Abu Abd Allah said: Asbat added, from Mansur: The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) prayed, and they were given rain. It poured over them for seven days, and the people complained of the excessive rain. He said, "O Allah, let it rain around us, not upon us." The cloud then moved away from above him, and the people around them received rain." There is no doubt that the lifting of drought from the unbelievers strengthened their position, but this was not what the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) intended. Thus, not every act that results in strengthening the position of an unbeliever is itself disbelief, especially when its purpose is to lessen another form of disbelief and remove harm from the Muslims.

(Another proof)
Imam al-Tirmidhi, may Allah have mercy on him, said (vol. 9, p. 51): Al-Husayn ibn Hurayth narrated to us; Muawiya ibn Amr informed us, from Abu Ishaq al-Fazari, from Sufyan, from Habib ibn Abi Amra, from Said ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas, concerning the words of Allah, Exalted is He: {Alif Lam Mim. The Romans have been defeated in the nearest land} (1). He said: They were defeated, and then they would be victorious. The polytheists wanted the Persians to prevail over the Romans, because both they and the Persians worshipped idols, while the Muslims wanted the Romans to prevail over the Persians, because the Romans were People of the Book. They mentioned this to Abu Bakr, and Abu Bakr mentioned it to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him and his family). He said, "Indeed, they will be victorious." Abu Bakr then told them, and they said, "Set a term between us and you: if we prevail, we will have such-and-such, and if you prevail, you will have such-and-such."
