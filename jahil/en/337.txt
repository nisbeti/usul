As for the second group, they held that the basis for judging these people to be Kharijites is their declaring Muslims unbelievers and consequently deeming it lawful to kill them.
In response to them, we say that declaring someone an unbeliever takes several forms and types.
First: declaring someone an unbeliever for something that, by the consensus of Ahl al-Sunna, does not in itself constitute unbelief: namely, committing major sins. This is what the scholars have said about the Kharijites, but it is not mentioned in the hadiths, unless it can be inferred by implication from the Prophet's words (peace and blessings be upon him): "They kill the people of Islam." If so, one may say that this is among the grounds for the ruling. But, as noted earlier, since this occurred among a group other than the one mentioned, proof must be established before ruling that they are unbelievers, because the grounds for the ruling in the hadith are mixed together: their leaving the religion by rejecting the Sunnah, or denying something necessarily known to be part of the religion, such as solitary reports, and their deeming it unlawful to kill the people of idolatry, among other things mentioned earlier. Therefore, proof must be established.
But do you not see that the Mu'tazilites held that a person who commits a major sin will abide forever in the Fire, even though they did not declare him an unbeliever? Nevertheless, the imams deemed many of their leading figures upright, and some of them were caliphs, such as al-Mu'tasim. Likewise, the Mu'tazilites do not excuse people on the grounds of ignorance. They say that reason is sufficient to determine what is good and bad, even if a person has not received the scriptural text.
