Disagreement over declaring one who abandons prayer to be a disbeliever is well known.

In all these issues, scholars differed over the individuals involved. According to some, they are disbelievers, abiding forever in the Fire; according to others, they are believers. Second: They said that the cause of declaring someone a disbeliever is ignorance of the true nature of Islam. The answer is that this same cause is present in issues over which the early generations differed. For example, one who declares a person who abandons prayer to be a disbeliever considers those who do not declare him a disbeliever to be in a state of compounded ignorance about the true nature of Islam, since, in his view, prayer is a pillar whose absence nullifies Islam altogether. The other side, by contrast, regards its being an obligation as having no bearing on the validity of Islam. Thus, this person is ignorant of the true nature of Islam, yet the early generations did not declare one another disbelievers on this basis. Abu Ubayda al-Jazairi himself said, relying on this very rationale—that the early generations did not declare one another disbelievers in certain issues—as evidence that we do not declare such a person a disbeliever: "One does not declare a person a disbeliever if he doubts or refrains from declaring one who abandons prayer a disbeliever, or refrains from declaring the Kharijites disbelievers; likewise, one who refrains or doubts regarding the disbelief of someone who has apostatized, but the grounds for it have not become clear to him.
