Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: {Wherever you find them, kill them.} And he said: {If I live to see them, I will kill them as the people of Ad were killed.} Umar said to Sabigh ibn Asal, "If I found you with your head shaved, I would strike the part of you that contains your eyes." Ali ibn Abi Talib sought to have Abdullah ibn Saba, the first of the Rafidah, killed, until he fled from him. These people are among the greatest corrupters on earth. If their corruption can be stopped only by killing them, then they are to be killed. But it is not obligatory to kill every one of them if this view has not become manifest, or if killing him would cause a greater harm. For this reason, the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) initially refrained from killing that Kharijite, lest people say, "Muhammad kills his companions." At that time, he was not causing widespread corruption. For this reason, Ali refrained from killing them when they first appeared, because they were numerous, and outwardly they remained within obedience and the community; they had not fought the people of the community, and it was not clear to him that they were the ones in question.
As for declaring them unbelievers and judging them to remain in Hell forever, the scholars likewise have two well-known positions on this, which are two reports from Ahmad. These two positions concern the Kharijites and those who have seceded, including the Haruriyya, the Rafidah, and others like them. The correct view is that the statements they make, which are known to contradict what the Messenger brought, constitute unbelief; likewise, their actions that are of the same kind as the actions of unbelievers against Muslims also constitute unbelief. I have set out the evidence for this elsewhere. However, declaring any particular individual among them an unbeliever and judging that he will remain in Hell forever depends on the conditions for declaring someone an unbeliever being established and the impediments to doing so being absent. We state the general rulings found in the texts concerning promise, threat, declaring someone an unbeliever, and declaring someone a sinner, but we do not judge that a particular individual falls under that general ruling until the cause for it is established in his case, with no countervailing factor. I have explained this principle at length in "The Principle of Declaring Someone an Unbeliever." End of quotation.
