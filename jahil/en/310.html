Muslims infer the ruling of Allah and His Messenger from the relevant proofs. These people have ruled on general rulings, and their ruling in this matter is invalid by consensus."

It is stated in Majmu al-Fatawa (35/378):

"Allah has made it obligatory upon the Muslim rulers to follow the Sharia, which is the Book and the Sunnah. If some Muslims disagree over any matter of religion, even if the disputant is one of the ordinary students of knowledge, the rulers may not compel him to follow a judge's ruling. Rather, they must make the truth clear to him, just as a learner makes the truth clear to an ignorant person. If the truth with which Allah sent His Messenger becomes clear to him and is manifest, yet he then opposes it out of obstinacy, he deserves punishment. As for someone who says, 'What I have said is my own view, or the view of a group of Muslim scholars, and I have said it by way of independent reasoning or following another,' then, by the agreement of the Muslims, he may not be punished, even if he has made an error that contradicts the Book and the Sunnah. Were such a person punished, all Muslims would be punished, for there is not one among them who does not hold views based on his own reasoning or on following another, in which he is mistaken. Were Allah to punish those who err, He would punish all creation."

Imam al-Shafii said: "People are agreed that if the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) has become clear to someone, he may not abandon it for anyone else's opinion." I'lam al-Muwaqqiin
