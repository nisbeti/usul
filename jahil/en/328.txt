Whoever follows another in some of his affairs is of him in that matter. For when someone says, "I am of this, and this is of me," he means, "I am of his kind, and he is of my kind," since two people can be one only in kind, as in the words of the Exalted: {Some of you are from others} [Al Imran: 195] (2) (3), and his words (peace and blessings be upon him) to Ali: "You are of me, and I am of you" (4). Thus, when someone says, "I have nothing to do with this," he means, "I share nothing with him; rather, I disavow all his affairs."))
It is stated in al-Muhalla bi al-Athar (12/125):
We know that whoever leaves the Abode of Islam for the Abode of War has deserted Allah, Exalted is He, and the imam of the Muslims and their community. This is made clear by his hadith (peace and blessings be upon him): "He is free of responsibility for every Muslim who resides among the polytheists." He (peace be upon him) only disavows himself of an unbeliever. Allah, Exalted is He, says: {The believing men and believing women are allies of one another} [Al-Tawbah: 71].))
Fourth: His words (peace be upon him), "Their faith does not pass beyond their throats." It is stated in Fath al-Bari by Ibn Hajar (12/293):
This is like his other statement about them: "Their faith does not pass beyond their throats," meaning that they utter the two testimonies of faith but do not know them in their hearts.)) It is also stated in Fath al-Bari by Ibn Hajar (12/288):
What is meant is that they believe through speech, not through the heart.)) This too is a basis for the obligation to kill, and a basis for declaring someone an unbeliever. It is the same basis as before: the Prophet informed us that these people's faith does not pass beyond their throats. One who is an unbeliever in his heart is an unbeliever, but no one can know this; therefore, it is clear that he knew it only through revelation.
