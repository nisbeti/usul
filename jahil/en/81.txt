He says: "As for those who are not of the people of Islam, whether Christian, Jew, Magian, or adherent of any other religion, or the esotericists who claim that a human being is divine, or that someone was a prophet after the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him), they are not excused on the basis of interpretation at all. Rather, they are unbelievers and polytheists in every case." End quote from Al-Durra fi ma yajibu i'tiqaduhu, p. 441.
"Al-Fasl fi al-milal wa al-ahwa wa al-nihal" (3/141):
"Some of them argued (that is, against excusing people on the basis of interpretation in any case), saying: Allah, Exalted is He, says: {Say, 'Shall We inform you of those who are greatest losers in their deeds? Those whose striving in the life of this world has gone astray, while they think that they are doing good.'}
Abu Muhammad said: The end of this verse refutes their interpretation, for Allah, Mighty and Majestic, connected His words 'they are doing good' to His words: {Those are the ones who disbelieved in the signs of their Lord and in meeting Him, so their deeds came to nothing, and on the Day of Resurrection We will assign them no weight. That is their recompense: Hell, because they disbelieved and took My signs and My messengers in mockery.} This makes clear that the beginning of the verse concerns the unbelievers who oppose the religion of Islam as a whole. Then we say to them: If this verse had been revealed concerning those who interpret matters among the people of Islam, as you claim, then every person who errs in an interpretation in a legal opinion would be included in its scope. This would entail declaring all the Companions unbelievers, may Allah be pleased with them, because they differed, and we know with certainty that each of them may be right or wrong. Indeed, it would entail declaring the entire community unbelievers, because every one of them must be right in some matters and wrong in others. It would even entail declaring oneself an unbeliever, because anyone who speaks about any matter of religion must at some point retract a statement he has made in favor of another statement that becomes clear to him to be more correct, except that..."
