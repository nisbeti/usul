Abu Dharr reported: I asked the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), "Have you seen your Lord?" He replied, "There is light; how could I see Him?" Narrated by Muslim (Faith/261).

Ibn Abbas reported: "The heart did not deny what it saw. And he certainly saw him another time." He said, "He saw him with his heart twice." Narrated by Muslim (Faith/258).

Furthermore, one of the Companions asked the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), "How can knowledge be taken away when the Book of Allah is among us?" Yet he did not declare him an unbeliever. Aisha, interpreting the matter, thought that the believer who is saved from the Fire would be called to account on the Day of Resurrection; this is a matter of belief, yet he did not declare her an unbeliever. He explained to her that his deeds would merely be presented to him. The hadith is in al-Bukhari. Hafsa thought that those who had pledged allegiance beneath the tree would enter the Fire, interpreting the words of the Exalted, "There is none of you who will not pass over it." This is a matter of belief, yet he did not declare her an unbeliever. The hadith is in Musnad Ahmad, and it is authentic. A woman interpreted the matter as permitting a woman with irregular bleeding to leave the prayer; leaving the prayer is unbelief according to the majority of the Companions, because she thought that irregular bleeding was menstruation. In a hadith, Abu Dawud (may Allah have mercy on him) said (vol. 1, p. 488): Wahb ibn Baqiyya narrated to us; Khalid narrated to us, from Suhayl—that is, Ibn Abi Salih—from al-Zuhri, from Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, from Asma bint Umays, who said: I said, "Messenger of Allah, Fatima bint Abi Hubays has had irregular bleeding for such-and-such a time and has not prayed." The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him and his family) said, "Glory be to Allah! This is indeed from…"
