What is remarkable here is that, after citing weak statements from some scholars concerning the excommunication of anyone who denies some of the attributes—categorically, without discussing the case of a particular individual—when they came across a report from al-Shafi'i excusing someone ignorant of an attribute, they rejected it and said that al-Hakkari's report from al-Shafi'i is weak. So sometimes a weak report is proof when it agrees with their views, and sometimes it is not proof when it contradicts them. Can there be any religion left after that? Let the reader of this book know that we rely only on the Book or an authentic Sunnah. Nothing else constitutes proof for us. But we will meet the proponent of the view on his own terms in order to demonstrate the corruption of his doctrine.

(The ruling on the one who interprets) There is a difference between this issue and that of the heedless ignorant person. The view that seems strongest to the researcher is that someone who turns away from learning is not excused for affirming or denying of Allah what he does not know. But if he holds the general belief and is ignorant of something that he is not required to know in detail, then he is excused so long as he remains ignorant, does not affirm or deny anything in religion without knowledge, entrusts knowledge of it to Allah, and refrains from judgment. The present issue, however, is a different one: the discussion here concerns the one who interprets, by whom I mean a man who has exerted his utmost effort to understand the words of Allah
