They differed over whether one who is ignorant of one of the attributes of Allah, Exalted is He, is to be declared an unbeliever. Qadi Iyad said: Among those who declared him an unbeliever was Ibn Jarir al-Tabari. Abu al-Hasan al-Ashari initially held this view as well. Others said that he is not declared an unbeliever for this, unlike one who denies the attribute. Abu al-Hasan later returned to this position, and it became the settled view of his school. He said: "This is because he holds that belief with certainty that he is correct, and regards it as a religious doctrine prescribed by the Sharia. Only one who believes that his own doctrine is true is declared an unbeliever."
It is stated in
"Al-Irf al-Shadhi: Commentary on Sunan al-Tirmidhi (1/81):
They differed over whether the Rafidites are to be declared unbelievers. The Hanafis have two opinions: one says that they are unbelievers, and the other says they are not. The preferred view is that they are unbelievers, for whoever declares the majority of the Companions unbelievers is an unbeliever, and the Rafidites restrict Islam to nine or seven Companions."
It is stated in
"Sharh al-Tahawiyya by Yusuf al-Ghufays (19/18, according to the automatic numbering of al-Maktaba al-Shamila):
Disagreement over declaring one who abandons fasting and pilgrimage an unbeliever
As for fasting and pilgrimage, a group of the early generations held that abandoning the fast is unbelief, and a group of the early generations held that abandoning pilgrimage is unbelief. The vast majority of the early and later generations hold that abandoning fasting alone, or abandoning pilgrimage alone, is not unbelief."
