Fifth: He said in al-Nihaya: "Conjecture is doubt that occurs to you, then you ascertain it."

Sixth: "Al-Ihkam fi Usul al-Ahkam — Ibn Hazm" (1/120):

"If we find proof that a solitary report, with an unbroken chain of transmission to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), concerning the rulings of the Sharia, cannot be subject to falsehood or error, then our position and theirs—that a report from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) concerning the Sharia cannot be subject to falsehood or error—is established. If we find no proof of this, then that is their position. Proof of it has indeed been established, and praise be to Allah, as we shall mention, if Allah Most High wills.

We know that the Quran contains verses that have undoubtedly been abrogated, because Allah Most High says: {Whenever We abrogate a verse or cause it to be forgotten, We bring one better than it or the like of it. Do you not know that Allah has power over all things?} Scholars have differed concerning them: one group has said that a particular verse is abrogated, while another has said that it is not abrogated but remains in force. Yet no Muslim has ever said—not Ibn Kaysan nor anyone else—that one must refrain from acting upon any part of the Quran for this reason, out of fear of acting upon something abrogated, which it is impermissible to act upon. Rather, one must act upon every verse in it until its abrogation is established, at which point one ceases to act upon it.
