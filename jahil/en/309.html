If the caliph and ruler do not claim this for themselves, nor impose their ruling on the subjects in this matter in favor of one opinion over another, except by the Book of Allah and the Sunnah of His Messenger, then anyone whose authority is less than that of the ruler has even greater reason not to exceed his bounds or set himself up in a position he is not entitled to occupy. Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, and Ali—the Rightly Guided Caliphs—were all the more entitled to do so than anyone beneath them. For they, may Allah be pleased with them, only required people to follow the Book of their Lord and the Sunnah of their Prophet. Umar, may Allah be pleased with him, used to say, "I have only sent my officials—that is, my deputies—to you to teach you the Book of your Lord and the Sunnah of your Prophet, and to distribute your spoils among you." Rather, this is a matter on which Muslim scholars who know what the legal proofs—the Book and the Sunnah—indicate may speak. Whoever has greater knowledge of the Book and the Sunnah is more entitled than another to speak on it, even if he is not a ruler. A ruler has no say in it merely by virtue of being a ruler; rather, if he has knowledge, he may speak on it like any other scholar. These people have ruled on a matter over which, by consensus, they have no authority to rule. This is a form of ruling that is invalid by consensus.

Thirteenth: The general rulings that Muslims share—whether agreed upon or disputed—are not for judges to rule on. Rather, a learned ruler, like any other scholar, states what he knows; a judge only rules on specific cases.
