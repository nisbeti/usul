However, if there is much obfuscation surrounding a matter that is not known or clear to everyone—I mean the true nature of the work of representatives and the acts of disbelief they engage in—and a person is among those to whom this may be unclear and who do not know about it, but elects a representative and appoints him to act on his behalf, intending that he provide some services for him, his clan, or his town or village, then he did not intend to appoint him to engage in acts of disbelief, nor did he choose him for that purpose. He is mistaken, but neither deliberate nor intending to support the acts of disbelief committed by those legislators when he chose them.

Therefore, it is not permissible to hasten to declare someone like him an unbeliever until the proof has been established against him and he has been informed of the true nature of the actions of the legislative representatives and of the acts of unbelief they commit, which contradict the religion of Islam and the oneness of the Lord of all worlds. If he nevertheless persists in electing them, he is an unbeliever. (I say:

It is therefore necessary to distinguish among those who vote: between those who intend to choose the legislator and those who do not intend that, but instead intend to choose something other than the legislator. The latter is not declared an unbeliever until proof has been established against him. For although, outwardly, he may have committed an act of unbelief in the eyes of someone who does not know his intention, the confusion and intermingling of circumstances, and the fact that democracy and parliament are innovations and foreign names and terms whose true nature is unknown to many people, have led some to undertake an act without knowing its real meaning. Such a person is like one who utters a word or says something whose meaning is unknown. The scholars hold that he is not held accountable for it until its meaning has been explained to him and proof has been established against him.
