Ibn Hajar said regarding "one ignorant of the attribute": "Al-Hafiz said: Al-Khattabi said: This may seem problematic, and one might ask: How can he be forgiven when he denies the resurrection and the power to bring the dead back to life? The answer is:
He did not deny the resurrection; rather, he was ignorant and thought that if this were done to him, he would not be brought back and so would not be punished. His faith was evident in his admission that he had done this only out of fear of Allah.
Ibn Qutayba said: "Some Muslims may err regarding certain attributes, but they are not thereby declared unbelievers."
Al-Tabari said in his book Al-Tabṣir fi Ma'alim al-Din, after mentioning some of the texts concerning the divine attributes: "These meanings that have been described, and their like among those with which Allah has described Himself and His Messenger, are such that their true nature cannot be established through thought and reflection; nor is anyone deemed an unbeliever for being ignorant of them until they have reached him." It is stated in Tabaqat al-Hanabila (1/283):
Al-Maimuni said: I heard Ahmad ibn Hanbal say, "There are six for whom I pray at dawn; one of them is al-Shafi'i." Let us now mention his creed.
I read to al-Mubarak and said: "Did he tell you?" Muhammad ibn Ali ibn al-Fath al-Ashari said: "Ali ibn Mardak informed us." He said: "Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Hatim informed us." He said: "Yunus ibn Abd al-Ala al-Misri narrated to us: I heard Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i say, when he was asked about the attributes of Allah and what one ought to believe: 'Allah, Blessed and Exalted is He, has names and attributes that His Book has brought and His Prophet has reported—'"
