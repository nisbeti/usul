Ahmad ibn Mani (16) (may Allah have mercy on him) said: Whoever claims that the Quran is created is a Jahmi. Whoever suspends judgment concerning it: if he is someone who does not understand, such as grocers, women, and children, leave him alone and teach him; but if he is someone who understands, then his reward is in the valley of the Jahmiyya. Abu Hatim al-Razi (may Allah have mercy on him) said: Whoever claims that it is created and made is an unbeliever with an unbelief that takes him out of the religion. Whoever doubts the unbelief of such a person, while understanding and not being ignorant, is an unbeliever. Whoever is ignorant should be taught; if he accepts the truth regarding declaring him an unbeliever, then well and good; otherwise, he is deemed an unbeliever.
/ He said in the narration of Abu Talib(17): Whoever says, "The Quran is created," is an unbeliever. As for whoever does not declare an unbeliever the one who says, "The Quran is created," we do not declare him an unbeliever. [The Two Narrations and Two Positions, Issues of Creed (p. 111), and al-Furu (6/568)]
The author of al-Furu said: The author of al-Muharrar said: "The correct view is that a follower is not deemed sinful for any innovation that does not entail unbelief, because of its slightness—for example, someone who gives Ali precedence over all the other Companions, or refrains from declaring unbelievers those innovators whom we have declared unbelievers."
Then he mentioned the report of Ibn Hani' (18) cited above, and al-Marwadhi's (19) statement to Abu Abd Allah: "There are people who declare to be unbelievers those who do not declare others to be unbelievers." (16) Ahmad ibn Mani ibn Abd al-Rahman, Abu Ja'far al-Baghawi, who settled in Baghdad, the Deaf, trustworthy, a hadith memorizer. Al-Taqrib, 1/27
(17) Abu Talib Ahmad ibn Humayd al-Mishkani, a companion of Abu Abd Allah Ahmad ibn Hanbal and an authority in this field. He transmitted many questions from him, and Ahmad honored and esteemed him.
Tarikh Baghdad (4/122) and Tabaqat al-Hanabila (1/39–40)
(18) Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Hani, al-Iskafi al-Athram al-Ta'i, one of the leading scholars, compiler of the Sunan, and a student of Imam Ahmad. Abu Bakr al-Khallal said: "Al-Athram was held in high esteem, a hadith memorizer.
Tadhkirat al-Huffaz (2/570–572); Tahdhib al-Kamal (1/476–480)
(19) Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn al-Hajjaj al-Marwadhi, a companion of Imam Ahmad, was an imam in the Sunnah and a staunch follower. He held an extraordinary position of esteem in Baghdad and narrated many legal questions from Imam Ahmad.
Siyar A'lam al-Nubala' (13/175).
