The Mothers of the Believers, Ibn Umar, Abu Hurayra, Abu Musa, Zayd ibn Thabit, Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib, and the rest of the eminent jurists of Medina and others held that the ruling requiring ablution after eating food touched by fire had been abrogated.
All of this is a matter of widespread affliction and general occurrence. All of this, and what follows, refutes the claim made by those among the Hanafis and Malikis who give no weight to their words: that when a matter is one of widespread affliction, a solitary report is not accepted.
It is astonishing that both groups accepted reports that others contradicted, even though the matter was one of widespread concern: the Hanafis accepted that laughter invalidates ablution, while others were unaware of it; the Malikis accepted a ruling allowing an oath to be accompanied by one witness, while others were unaware of it. There are very many examples of this kind. Muhammad ibn Sa'id narrated to us: Ahmad ibn Abd al-Nasir narrated to us: Qasim ibn Asbagh narrated to us: Muhammad ibn Abd al-Salam al-Khushani narrated to us: Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna narrated to us: Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi narrated to us: Sakhr ibn Juwayriya narrated to us: Amir ibn Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr narrated to us, that Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Aswad informed him, saying: I was sitting with my father at Arafat, while Ibn al-Zubayr was addressing the people. Ibn al-Zubayr said, "This is a day for magnifying Allah, praising Him, and proclaiming His oneness, so magnify Allah, praise Him, and proclaim His oneness." My father got up and made his way through the crowd until he reached him, then leaned close and said, "I testify that I heard Umar ibn al-Khattab on this very pulpit reciting the talbiya." Ibn al-Zubayr then recited, "Here I am, O Allah, here I am," in a loud voice. Abu Muhammad said: As you can see, this report was unknown to Ibn al-Zubayr and others, although it is well known from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him). Umar had forbidden people to be named after the prophets, yet he saw Muhammad ibn Maslama coming and going to visit him, though he was one of the eminent Companions. He also saw Abu Ayyub al-Ansari and Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, who were known among the Companions only by their kunyas. He saw Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr al-Siddiq, who was born in the presence of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), during the Farewell Pilgrimage. His mother asked the Prophet for a ruling when she gave birth to him, asking what she should do about her state of consecration while in postpartum bleeding. Umar knew with certainty that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) knew the names and kunyas of those we have mentioned, approved them, and called them by them, without changing any of them. When Talha and Suhayb informed him that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) had permitted this, he refrained from forbidding it. He had intended to abandon jogging during the pilgrimage, but then recalled that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) had done it, and said, "We have no right to abandon it." Here is Uthman (may Allah be pleased with him): they have reported that he sent to al-Furay'a, the sister of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, to ask her what the Messenger of Allah had ruled concerning her waiting period, and that he acted on that ruling. He also ordered a woman who had given birth after six months of pregnancy to be stoned, but Ali cited the Quran to him, pointing out that a pregnancy could last only six months, whereupon he withdrew the order to stone her. And here is Ali (may Allah be pleased with him): he acknowledged that many of the Companions had related to him things from the Prophet that he did not know, and that he would make them swear to it, except for Abu Bakr, whom he would not make swear. Allah, Exalted is He, would thereby benefit him with whatever He wished of what he heard, which he had not known before. Here is Talha, who permitted gold to be exchanged for silver on deferred terms until Umar reminded him. And here are Ibn Umar and Ibn Abbas, who sold one dirham for two until they were reminded, whereupon they stopped. Ibn Umar then related it from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him); Muslim recorded it. Ibn Umar then returned to that ruling and abandoned his own view. Ibn Umar later related it and said, "This is the instruction our Prophet gave us." Malik recorded it from Humayd, from Mujahid, from Ibn Umar. Ibn Umar spoke truly, and we say of a hadith from the Prophet, when it reaches us, "This is the instruction our Prophet gave us." In this way we understand all cases in which a Companion narrated a hadith and then acted contrary to it: he had given a ruling contrary to the hadith before it reached him, and once he was informed of it, it is not permissible to think anything else of the Companion. This is precisely what we have explained about Ibn Umar, in a way that admits no obscurity: they interpreted what they had heard of the hadith. Anyone who understands this otherwise necessarily places the Companion under one of two possibilities, from both of which Allah, Exalted is He, has absolved them; both are misguidance and sin. Either they openly opposed the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him)—which is impermissible for anyone, and it is impermissible to think this of them—or they possessed knowledge that required them to act contrary to what they had narrated, in which case they would not be justified in concealing it from us, narrating what had been abrogated and concealing the abrogating ruling from us. Such conduct would be disbelief on the part of whoever did it, and deception in religion. No one attributes this to them except someone whose heart has deviated or whose heart is blind. Thus their false assumption is disproved, and our position is established. Praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds. There is absolutely no third possibility, except that they may have forgotten at the time some of what they had previously narrated. That too is possible. If they interpreted the reports, then their interpretation, may Allah be pleased with them, was conjecture, whereas their narration from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) was certain. It is not permissible for a Muslim to abandon certainty for conjecture. Thus the difficulty in this matter is entirely resolved. Praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds. As for them, may Allah be pleased with them, they are excused, since this was their own exercise of judgment, and such cases on their part are also very few. The same does not apply to someone who follows them after being alerted to what we have explained. Here are Aisha and Abu Hurayra (may Allah be pleased with them): the wiping over leather socks was unknown to them, and to Ibn Umar as well, while Jarir knew of it, though he had only embraced Islam a few months before the Prophet's death. Aisha acknowledged
She had no knowledge of it and was instructed to ask someone who was expected to know about it, namely Ali (may Allah be pleased with him). This is Hafsa, Mother of the Believers, who was asked about intercourse that leaves the man in a state requiring ritual bathing: is bathing required or not? She said, "I have no knowledge of that." And this is Ibn Umar, who thought that a prohibition from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) against leasing land might have been issued, more than forty years after the Prophet's death. So he refrained from it, while acknowledging that they had leased it during the time of Abu Bakr, Umar, and Uthman. He did not say that it was impossible for something known to Rafi, Jabir, and Abu Hurayra to have escaped the notice of these men. Yet these are our brothers, who say whatever they wish: "If this were true, it would not have escaped Umar." But the permissibility granted by the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) for a menstruating woman to depart without performing the farewell circumambulation escaped Zayd ibn Thabit, Ibn Umar, and most of the people of Medina, until Ibn Abbas and Umm Sulaym informed them of it, whereupon they retracted their position. And Ibn Umar did not know that one should remain until the deceased had been buried, until Abu Hurayra and Aisha informed him of it. He then said, "We have missed out on many qirats."
