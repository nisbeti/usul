And it is inconceivable that you should suppose that all of this would reach us only through complete and mass-transmitted reports. Indeed, if they had said that, they would not have been Muslims. Likewise, no two people disagree that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) sent his messengers to distant lands only to convey to their people the Quran, the Sunnah, and the laws of religion, and that he did not send them to establish for them a religion that he himself had not brought from Allah, Exalted is He. All of this makes it clear that everything reliably transmitted from one trustworthy person to another, reaching back to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), whether Quran or Sunnah, must be accepted, acknowledged, affirmed, believed in, and followed as religion.
Second: Their claim that an error may occur on the part of someone transmitting the Sunnah. We say: By the same token, an error may occur on the part of someone transmitting the Quran. A reciting teacher may make a mistake, but that does not prevent the Quran from conveying knowledge.
It is likewise established that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) made certain judgments through personal reasoning, such as taking ransom from the captives of Badr, and that he was not left to that judgment; rather, the revelation came down: "Had it not been for a prior decree from Allah, you would have been afflicted by a great punishment for what you took ..." [end of verse]. Likewise, Allah's saying, "And you fear the people," and His saying, "He frowned and turned away." He (peace and blessings be upon him) was not left to such matters; rather, clarification came down to guide him. It is also established in the Sunnah that he (peace and blessings be upon him) forgot, but he was reminded, even though he was a model to follow. He said, "I am only a human being; I forget just as you forget. So when I forget, remind me."
None of this prevented his words (peace and blessings be upon him) from being authoritative in matters of belief and practice.
