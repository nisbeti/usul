They replied, saying: "If someone engages in disbelief and associating partners with Allah out of ignorance, or because there is no one to alert him, we do not rule that he is an unbeliever until proof has been established against him. But we do not rule that he is a Muslim either. Rather, we say: This deed of his is disbelief that makes his property and blood lawful. Although we do not rule on this particular person because proof has not been established against him, it should not be said, 'If he is not an unbeliever, then he is a Muslim.' Rather, we say: His deed is the deed of unbelievers, and applying the ruling to this particular person depends on the prophetic proof reaching him. The scholars have mentioned that the people who lived during intervals without prophets will be tested on the Day of Resurrection in the gathering place, and they did not assign them the ruling of unbelievers or the ruling of the righteous." Al-Durar, 10/137.

Shaykh Abd al-Aziz, the judge of Diriyah, said in Al-Rasa'il wa al-Masa'il al-Najdiyya, 5/576, in response to a question about a believer in Allah and His Messenger who says or does something that constitutes disbelief, being unaware of that: "Do you not declare him an unbeliever until proof has been established against him?"

He replied: "If someone engages in disbelief and associating partners with Allah out of ignorance, and because there is no one to alert him, we do not rule that he is an unbeliever until proof has been established against him. Rather, we say: His deed is the deed of unbelievers, and applying the ruling to this particular person depends on the prophetic proof reaching him.
