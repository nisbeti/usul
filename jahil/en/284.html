2754 - Then he mentioned the hadith of al-Zubayr (may Allah be pleased with him), when he was with the Negus and an enemy attacked him. On that day, al-Zubayr fought well alongside the Negus, and thereby attained a good standing with him. On the apparent meaning of this hadith, those who permit Muslims to fight alongside polytheists under their banner rely as evidence. But, in our view, this can be interpreted in two ways. First, the Negus was Muslim at that time, as has been reported, and so al-Zubayr considered it permissible to fight alongside him.

Second, the Muslims had no refuge at that time other than him, according to what has been reported from Umm Salama (may Allah be pleased with her). She said: "When we had settled in the land of Abyssinia, we were in the best of homes, with the best of neighbors, worshipping our Lord, until an enemy of the Negus marched against him. No great misfortune from him had ever befallen us. We said, 'If he prevails over the Negus, he will not recognize our rights as the Negus did.' So we devoted ourselves to supplication, asking Allah to grant the Negus victory. Then we said, 'Who will go and find out what is happening with the people?' Al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam said, 'I will.' He inflated a waterskin, mounted it, and crossed the river. He met the people, and al-Zubayr joined them. We continued to devote ourselves to supplication until al-Zubayr appeared on the Nile, waving his garment and calling, 'Rejoice! Allah, Exalted is He, has granted the Negus victory, established him in the land, and destroyed his enemy.'" She said, "So we remained with the best of neighbors." This hadith makes clear the soundness of the interpretation we have given. And Allah knows best.
