Every Companion and Successor who was asked by someone seeking a legal ruling about a religious matter never once told him, "You may not act on what I have told you from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) until the soothsayers tell you the same," as they said to them regarding what they had reported seeing from them, without requiring them to accept it. If it is said, "Then make this very argument proof for accepting a mursal report," we say: That is not so, for there was never any consensus, either in early times or later, on accepting a mursal report. Among the Successors were those who did not accept it, such as al-Zuhri and others. They would ask those who informed them who had informed them, until they traced it back to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him).
That requirement was waived only for those who lacked the ability to understand and know the chain of transmission. Al-Zuhri said to the people of Syria, "Why do I see that your hadiths have neither halters nor reins?" They then followed his instruction. There were many others besides al-Zuhri. Thus it is established that the entire Muslim community agreed to accept the report of a trustworthy individual from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him). Every group followed this in its own learning, including the People of the Sunnah, the Kharijites, the Shi'a, and the Qadariyya, until the Mu'tazilite theologians arose after the first century and opposed the consensus on this matter."
