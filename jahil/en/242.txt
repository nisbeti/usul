The praiseworthy disputation is obligatory. It is the disputation in which the person engaging in it seeks to make the truth manifest. The blameworthy kind has two forms, as stated in the verses we have cited: one is disputing without knowledge, and the other is disputing in support of falsehood, through contentiousness and deception, after the truth has become clear to him. This shows that the truth is one, and that nothing is established except by a proof grounded in reason. These blameworthy people are those of whom Allah, Exalted is He, says: {Have you not seen those who dispute concerning the signs of Allah? How are they turned away?} And His saying: {Among the people is one who disputes concerning Allah without knowledge and follows every rebellious devil.} And His saying: {Among the people is one who disputes concerning Allah without knowledge, guidance, or an illuminating Book, turning his neck aside to lead others astray from the way of Allah. For him there is disgrace in this world, and on the Day of Resurrection We shall make him taste the punishment of the burning Fire.} And by His saying:
Exalted is He: {None dispute the signs of Allah except those who disbelieve, so do not let their movement through the lands deceive you. The people of Noah denied the truth before them, as did the confederates after them.
And every nation plotted against their messenger, to seize him, and argued falsely in order to refute the truth by means of it. Then I seized them; so how was My punishment?} Thus, as you see, Exalted is He makes clear that the forbidden disputation is that by which one argues to support falsehood and invalidate the truth without knowledge. Abu Muhammad said: It may be said to one who refuses to engage in disputation and undertake the pursuit of proof: Pharaoh said, {O my people, today you hold dominion, prevailing in the land. But who will protect us from Allah's might if it comes upon us?} Pharaoh said, {I show you only what I see, and I guide you only to the path of right guidance.} {And the one who believed said, "O my people, follow me; I will guide you to the path of right guidance."} By what means can one distinguish which of them is in the right and which is in the wrong? Is it permissible to know this by anything other than evidence apart from their words? This is the speech of the Almighty, the Irresistible, the Creator, the Originator. We have cited it as proof for following evidence and rejecting the claim of one who has no proof in hand. This is what no Muslim may oppose—not the saying of one who says, "Go to someone who doubts as you do, and debate him." It may be said to him: "Do you think ..."
