This is Ibn Hajar's account of his concise presentation and division of the generations, may Allah have mercy on him.

The generations: The first: the Companions, with their varying ranks, distinguishing those among them who had nothing more than merely seeing the Prophet from others.
The second: the generation of the senior Successors, such as Ibn al-Musayyib. If someone was a mukhadram, I stated that explicitly.
The third: the middle generation of the Successors, such as al-Hasan and Ibn Sirin.
The fourth: the generation that follows them, most of whose narrations are from senior Successors, such as al-Zuhri and Qatada.
The fifth: the junior generation among them, those who saw one or two Companions, but for some of whom hearing from the Companions is not established, such as al-A'mash.
The sixth: a generation contemporary with the fifth, but for whom meeting any of the Companions is not established, such as Ibn Jurayj.
These people must therefore stop at this generation and not cite those below it as evidence. Yet their books are replete with citations of those below that generation, namely the following, as Ibn Hajar said:
The seventh: the generation of the Successors of the Successors, such as Malik and al-Thawri.
The eighth: the middle generation among them, such as Ibn Uyayna and Ibn Ulayya.
