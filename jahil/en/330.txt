As for one who calls people to an interpretation that does not invalidate any Sunnah, but rather to something like Muawiya's interpretation that he should exact retribution from Uthman's killers before pledging allegiance to Ali, he is excused, because this involves no rejection of any part of the religion; it is merely a particular error concerning a specific incident, which does not extend beyond it.
As for one who rose up solely for worldly gain, as Yazid ibn Muawiya, Marwan ibn al-Hakam, and Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan did when they rose against Ibn al-Zubayr; as Marwan ibn Muhammad did when he rose against Yazid ibn al-Walid; and as those who also rose in support of Marwan did, such people are not excused, because they have no interpretation at all: it is sheer transgression.
As for one who calls people to enjoin what is right, forbid what is wrong, uphold the Quran and the Sunnah, and rule with justice, he is not a rebel. Rather, the rebel is the one who opposes him. And Allah, Exalted is He, is the source of success.)) Here Ibn Hazm mentioned the matters for which the Kharijites declared others unbelievers and by which they contradicted the established consensus; among them he mentioned the abrogation of stoning.
It is stated in al-Tanbih wa al-Radd ala Ahl al-Ahwa wa al-Bida (p. 185):
Hassan ibn Farukh said: Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz (may Allah be pleased with him) asked me what the Azariqa say. I told him. He said, "What do they say about stoning?" I replied, "They declare it unbelief." He said, "Allah is greatest! They have disbelieved in Allah and His Messenger.")) And among the matters Ibn Hazm mentioned were declaring someone an unbeliever for a sin, deeming the killing of women and children lawful, denying divine decree, denying the vision of Allah, believing that Allah knows nothing until it comes to be, disavowing some or most of the Companions and declaring them unbelievers. The basis for unbelief here is denying the verses that praise the Companions.
