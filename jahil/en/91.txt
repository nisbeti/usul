Ibn Taymiyya said: "An apostate is one who associates partners with Allah, Exalted is He; or hates the Messenger and what he brought; or ceases to reject an evil in his heart; or supposes that any of the Companions, Successors, or their successors fought alongside the unbelievers or permitted that; or denies something established by definitive consensus; or places intermediaries between himself and Allah, relying on them, calling upon them, and asking them. Whoever doubts one of Allah's attributes, when someone like him would not be ignorant of it, is an apostate; but if someone like him could be ignorant of it, he is not an apostate. For this reason, the Prophet did not declare the man who doubted Allah's power and His restoring him to life an unbeliever, since this can only occur after the message has been conveyed. This includes the statement of Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her): 'Whatever people conceal, Allah knows it.' He said: 'Yes.'"
He said:
"Whoever doubts one of the attributes of Allah, Exalted is He, when someone like him would not be ignorant of it, is an apostate. But if someone like him could be ignorant of it, he is not an apostate. For this reason, the Prophet did not declare the man who doubted Allah's power and His ability to resurrect him an unbeliever, since this can only be known after the message has been conveyed. This is also illustrated by Aisha's statement (may Allah be pleased with her): 'Whatever people conceal, Allah knows.'"
And it is stated in The Creed of the People of the Sunnah by al-Lalaka'i (2/193):
Chapter on the comprehensive doctrine of the oneness of Allah, Mighty and Majestic, and His attributes and names,
and that He is Living, Powerful, Knowing, Hearing, Seeing, Speaking, Willing, and Everlasting.
An account of what is indicated by the Book of Allah, Mighty and Majestic, and what has been narrated from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), concerning that...
The obligation to know Allah, Exalted is He, and His attributes through revelation, not reason ... This indicates that knowledge of Allah and the messengers is through revelation, as Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has informed us.
And this is the doctrine of Ahl al-Sunna wa al-Jama'a." This is a clear statement from him that knowledge of Allah's attributes is attained only through transmitted evidence, not reason, and he attributes this view to Ahl al-Sunna.
