Abu Hurayra (may Allah be pleased with him) reported: When the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) died, and Abu Bakr succeeded him, some of the Arabs apostatized. Umar said, "Abu Bakr, how can you fight the people when the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said, 'I have been commanded to fight the people until they say, "There is no god but Allah." Whoever says, "There is no god but Allah" has protected his property and his life from me, except by its due right, and his reckoning is with Allah'?" Abu Bakr replied, "By Allah, I will fight whoever makes a distinction between prayer and zakat, for zakat is the due right of property."(10) Here, Umar opposed the caliph by citing the text, and the caliph argued against him by interpreting the text as well. If Umar's opposition had been impermissible, and he had been giving precedence to his own opinion over the text, Abu Bakr would have rebuked him. We therefore know that Abu Bakr's practice was to give precedence to the text over Umar's statement.

2- A woman's disagreement with Umar on the basis of the text:

In the presence of the Companions, no one rebuked her—not even Umar himself—when he prohibited excessive dowries. She countered him with the verse, "And you have given one of them a great amount" [Surat al-Nisa, part of verse 20].(11) Had Umar believed that his opinion could not be opposed by citing the text, he would have rebuked her. Rather, Umar accepted what she said. We therefore know that Umar's practice (may Allah be pleased with him) was to give precedence to the text over the statement of the caliphs, one of whom was himself.

3- Ali's disagreement with Uthman on the basis of the text:

Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib reported: Ali and Uthman (may Allah be pleased with them both) met at Usfan. Uthman was prohibiting tamattu or combining umrah with the pilgrimage, so Ali said, "Why do you prohibit something that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) did?" Uthman said, "Leave us alone." Ali replied, "I cannot leave you alone." When Ali saw that, he entered ihram for both rites together.(12) Al-Qasim ibn Sallam added: "Uthman said, 'Do you see me prohibiting the people while you do it?' He replied, 'I would not abandon the practice of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) for the statement of anyone.'"(13) We therefore know that Ali's practice was to give precedence to the text over the statement or action of the caliphs. Al-San'ani says: "Sixth: Among the proofs cited by those who follow others blindly is their saying: 'You must follow my Sunnah and the Sunnah of the rightly guided caliphs after me, Abu Bakr and Umar; follow the guidance of Ammar and hold fast to the covenant of Ibn Umm Abd.' The answer is that being guided by them means following the Book and the Sunnah, accepting what they contain, calling people to them, and prohibiting blind imitation, for no such practice is reported from them. It is authentically reported from Ibn Mas'ud, who was Ibn Umm Abd, that he prohibited blind imitation and said, 'A man should not be an unthinking follower with no insight.' Moreover, it is well known that none of them would abandon the Sunnah for the statement of anyone. The practice and way of the rightly guided caliphs was to follow the Book and the Sunnah; thus, following their practice is following the Prophetic Sunnah and the Quran."(14)

Third: They said that the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) witnessed the revelation, so they knew best what they had witnessed, and praise of them has been given.

(Response): Ibn Hazm said: "The obligation to praise them does not entail that they be followed blindly, since the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) stated that Abu Bakr and Umar were the best of their men after the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), yet they made mistakes, as ... Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr reported to them that a delegation from Banu Tamim came to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him). Abu Bakr said, 'Appoint al-Qa'qa ibn Ma'bad ibn Zurarah,' while Umar said, 'Rather, appoint al-Aqra ibn Habis.' Then this was revealed: ﴾O you who believe, do not raise your voices above the voice of the Prophet, nor speak loudly to him as you speak loudly to one another, lest your deeds come to nothing while you are unaware...﴿
