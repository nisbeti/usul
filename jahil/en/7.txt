Among their proofs was that Allah called certain sins sins and wrongdoing before sending the messengers. The Exalted said: (And if any of the polytheists seeks your protection, then grant him protection so that he may hear the words of Allah.) Thus He called him a polytheist before he had heard the words of Allah. Likewise, Allah's saying: (And when your Lord took from the children of Adam, from their loins, their descendants, and made them testify against themselves: "Am I not your Lord?" They said, "Yes indeed; we testify"—lest you should say on the Day of Resurrection, "We were unaware of this.") (172) The fact that He, Exalted is He, said to them what means, "so that you do not say on the Day of Resurrection, 'We were unaware of this,'" is proof that He did this so it would be evidence against them, which they would recall on the Day of Resurrection. This included all the descendants of Adam: Allah brought them forth from his loins and made them testify against themselves. Their testimony that He is their Lord means that they knew He is the Creator, Provider, Giver of life and death, and Disposer of affairs; and that He is also Living; and that there would also be a Day of Resurrection, as indicated by His saying before them, "the Day of Resurrection." These divine attributes, and others understood from the verse, were therefore known to them at the time of this testimony. Hence, no one has an excuse for being ignorant of them, and whoever is ignorant of them is an unbeliever and is not excused on account of ignorance, because there is no ignorance in the first place.
This group included Allah's attributes in this category, so they did not excuse anyone ignorant of an attribute, nor anyone who interpreted an attribute figuratively. In their view, such an interpreter is merely doubly ignorant, so his excuse is of no avail. Thus they declared all the groups that interpret the attributes figuratively, including the Ash'aris and others, to be unbelievers, because they say that Allah's attributes are apprehended by innate disposition. They likewise declared to be unbelievers all those who excuse people who interpret the attributes figuratively. In their view, whoever excuses the Ash'aris is an unbeliever, because he has called an unbeliever a Muslim; and this is unbelief, since whoever does not declare an unbeliever to be an unbeliever is himself an unbeliever.
They argued that reason can discern what is reprehensible even without a messenger being sent, citing several proofs, including: ({And when your Lord called Moses: "Go to the wrongdoing people, the people of Pharaoh. Will they not be mindful of Allah?"}) and His saying: ({Indeed, Pharaoh exalted himself in the land and divided its people into factions, oppressing a group among them, slaughtering their sons and sparing their women. Indeed, he was one of the corrupters.}) He called them wrongdoers, transgressors, and corrupters before sending the messenger. And He, Exalted is He, said: (Go to Pharaoh; indeed, he has transgressed.) His saying that he and his people were wrongdoers, transgressors, and corrupters is a condemnation of their actions, and condemnation applies only to evil, reprehensible actions. This shows that actions can be reprehensible and blameworthy before a messenger comes to them, and thus proves that they are held accountable by innate disposition or by reason.
