And his saying (peace and blessings be upon him): "Allah has pardoned my community for mistakes, forgetfulness, and what they are compelled to do." Narrated by Ibn Majah (2043); al-Albani graded it hasan.
These texts indicate that anyone who contravenes what he was charged with, out of forgetfulness or ignorance, is pardoned. The term "one who errs" includes the ignorant, since one who errs is anyone who departs from the truth unintentionally, provided that he has not been negligent in seeking knowledge or fabricated lies about Allah without proof, as will come, if Allah wills. Second: the texts indicating that Allah's proof against His servants is established only after they have received knowledge:
Such as His saying, Exalted is He: {We would never punish until We had sent a messenger} (al-Isra 17:15).
And His saying: {Messengers bearing good news and warning, so that people would have no argument against Allah after the messengers. And Allah is Almighty, All-Wise} (al-Nisa 4:165).
And His saying: {Allah would not lead a people astray after He had guided them until He had made clear to them what they should avoid} (al-Tawba 9:115), along with other verses indicating that proof is established only after knowledge and clarification. So long as a person has not turned away from the truth, neglected seeking knowledge and exerting his utmost effort, and then interpreted the matter and erred, there is no sin upon him.
The story of Dhat Anwat.
Abu Waqid al-Laythi said: "We went out with the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) toward Hunayn. We passed by a lote tree and said, 'O Prophet of Allah, make this a Dhat Anwat for us, just as the disbelievers have a Dhat Anwat.' The disbelievers used to hang their weapons on a lote tree and gather around it."
