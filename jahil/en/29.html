He also said: [Allah says, "We would never punish until We had sent a messenger" (al-Isra: 15). The proof against people is established only on two conditions: that they are able to know what Allah has revealed, and that they are able to act upon it. As for one who is unable to know, such as an insane person, or unable to act, there is no command or prohibition for him. If knowledge of part of the religion is cut off, or one is unable to observe part of it, then for the person unable to know or act, that is like being cut off from knowledge of the entire religion or being unable to observe all of it, as in the case of an insane person, for example. These are times of interruption.] (4).

Ibn Taymiyya also said: [If this is clear, then whoever abandons some obligatory aspect of faith because he is unable to fulfill it, either because he is unable to acquire knowledge—for example, the message has not reached him—or because he is unable to act, is not commanded to do what he is incapable of, and that is not part of the obligatory faith and religion incumbent upon him, even though it is part of the religion and faith that are obligatory in principle.] (5).

He also said: [If religious obligation is conditional upon the ability to acquire knowledge, whose basis is reason, and upon the ability to act] (6).

(And Ibn Taymiyya also said: [This excuse is valid only when one is unable to remove it; otherwise, whenever a person is able to know the truth but falls short in doing so, he is not excused.] (7).
