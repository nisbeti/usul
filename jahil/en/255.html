The scholars held that the Kharijites are not outside the community of Muslims, based on the words, "he is uncertain about the arrowhead," since uncertainty entails doubt. And when doubt arises in this matter, they cannot be definitively judged to have left Islam, for whoever is certainly established in the covenant of Islam does not leave it except with certainty. He said: Ali was asked about the people of al-Nahrawan, "Have they disbelieved?" He replied, "They fled from disbelief." I say: If this is authentically reported from Ali, it may be understood to mean that he had not learned of the belief they held which led those who declared them disbelievers to do so. There is, however, a question about his use of the words, "he is uncertain about the arrowhead," for in some versions of the hadith mentioned—as previously indicated and as will be discussed later—"nothing of it clung to it," while in others, "it passed through the dung and blood." The way to reconcile these is that he was initially unsure whether there was anything on the arrowhead or not, then he established that nothing from the shot had clung to the arrow or any part of it. It is also possible to attribute the difference to different individuals among them, and that the words "he is uncertain" indicate that some of them may retain something of Islam. Al-Qurtubi says in al-Mufhim: "The view that they are disbelievers is more apparent from the hadith." He said: "According to the view that they are disbelievers, they are fought and killed, and their property is taken as spoils. This is the view of a group of hadith scholars regarding the property of the Kharijites. According to the view that they are not disbelievers, they are treated as rebels when they break allegiance and take up arms. As for one of them who conceals an innovation, if he is discovered, whether he is to be killed after being asked to repent, or not killed but instead efforts are made to refute his innovation, scholars have differed, in accordance with their disagreement over whether they are disbelievers." End quote.

Although I am inclined to regard the Kharijites as unbelievers, I have cited this to show that the early generations differed over whether one who interprets the texts erroneously is to be declared an unbeliever, and whether this interpretation is acceptable grounds for excusing its proponent or not. For this reason, he did not declare him an unbeliever.
