After mentioning what has preceded, the following becomes clear.
First: Only a very small number of such cases have been established as coming from the early generations, and even these may concern the general category rather than particular individuals. Here, a distinction must be made between two issues. The first is the ruling on someone who turns away from learning and falsely attributes to Allah a lie involving polytheism or something else; this is the person who adopts a corrupt interpretation of the kind explained above. The same applies to anyone who has not entered Islam. My view is that such a person is not excused. The second is a person who entered Islam and embraced the general faith, then exerted his utmost effort regarding a matter of creed but erred in it through interpretation. In this case, the stronger view is that he is excused. This applies, for example, to questions concerning the divine attributes and other matters. This is a response to those who declare Asharis and Maturidis to be unbelievers as individuals, and who generalize the ruling of disbelief to anyone ignorant of an attribute or interpreting it incorrectly. It is said to them: There is a difference between two issues. The first is a person who is ignorant of an attribute, remains silent, and says, "Allah knows best; I believe in the general faith."
