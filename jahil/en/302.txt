If it is said: You have listed collecting money for an unbeliever among prohibited acts, yet this was the work of Joseph, peace be upon him. How, then, was his work permissible? The answer is: What is prohibited is collecting money according to the unbeliever's judgment and in accordance with his man-made laws, which are rife with injustice. But if the collection is carried out according to the judgment of the Muslim entrusted with collecting it, then it is justice and reform. This was the situation of Joseph, peace be upon him: he had been given authority to conduct affairs according to his own judgment. This is indicated by the king's words to him: {Indeed, today you are established in position and trusted in our presence} (Yusuf: 54). Allah, Exalted is He, also says: {And thus We established Joseph in the land, to settle wherever he wished} (Yusuf: 56). And Allah, Exalted is He, says, relating Joseph's words: {My Lord, You have indeed granted me authority and taught me the interpretation of events} (Yusuf: 101). What greater authority could there be than this? Al-Shawkani said: "His saying, Exalted is He, {to settle wherever he wished} (Yusuf: 56), means that he could reside wherever he wanted ... This expression indicates the completeness of his power" (1). Another indication of his authority is that he applied his own law in enslaving the thief, as stated in Allah's words: {The one in whose possession we found our property} (Yusuf: 79); that was his law (2). In sum, although Joseph, peace be upon him, took charge of the funds, collecting and disbursing them, he did so on the basis that he would exercise his own judgment in managing them, not according to the unbeliever's choice. This is permissible today: if an unbelieving king appoints a Muslim to oversee finances, judge cases, or hold another office, on the condition that the Muslim exercise his own judgment in it and not the unbeliever's, that is permissible (3).
