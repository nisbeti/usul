(Faulty interpretation is not accepted.) This must be emphasized once again: the interpretation for which its author is excused is one that has some basis in linguistic or religious evidence, which led the interpreter to err in his interpretation. As for an interpretation that has no basis in either religion or language, such as the Shiites' interpretations of Allah's saying, "Indeed, Allah commands you to slaughter a cow," when they claimed that what was meant was Aisha, there is no excuse for this.

Al-Qadi Iyad quoted the scholars in al-Shifa as saying: "Claiming an interpretation of an explicit statement is not accepted." End quote. (2/217)

Shaykh al-Islam stated this explicitly in al-Sarim al-Maslul, p. 527.

If someone is known and notorious for heresy and for manipulating the proofs of religion, or engages in acts of disbelief that are explicit and clear and admit of no interpretation, his claim of interpretation is not accepted. There is no independent reasoning or interpretation that makes engaging in explicit disbelief permissible. For every disbeliever has faulty interpretations with which he tries to patch up his disbelief.

For this reason, Ibn Hazm said: "If a report from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) reaches someone through a reliable chain, and he is a Muslim, but interprets it contrary to its meaning—or perhaps another text reaches him, so that the proof of his error in abandoning what he abandoned and adopting what he adopted has not been established against him—then he is rewarded and excused, because he intended the truth but was ignorant of it. But if the proof has been established against him and he persists defiantly, then there is no interpretation after the proof has been established." End quote. Al-Durra (414)
