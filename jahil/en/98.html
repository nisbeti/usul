These people are among the innovators whose excommunication is disputed; whoever claims otherwise has made a grave error. What is reliably reported from Ahmad and other imams is only the excommunication of the Jahmiyya, the anthropomorphists, and the like. Ahmad did not excommunicate the Kharijites or the Qadariyya when they affirmed divine knowledge but denied that actions are created and that the divine will is all-encompassing. However, two reports are related from him concerning their excommunication. As for the Murji'a, his position that they are not to be excommunicated is undisputed. Yet Ahmad did not excommunicate individual Jahmis, nor did he excommunicate everyone who was called a Jahmi, nor everyone who agreed with the Jahmiyya in some of their innovations. Rather, Ahmad and those like him prayed behind Jahmis who called people to their doctrine, subjected them to an inquisition, and inflicted severe punishments on those who did not agree with them. He did not excommunicate them; rather, he considered them believers and legitimate leaders, prayed for them, and held that one should pray behind them, perform the pilgrimage and fight alongside them, and refrain from rebelling against them, just as he held regarding other leaders like them. He condemned the false doctrine they had introduced, which was grave unbelief, even though they themselves did not know that it was unbelief. He would denounce it and strive, as far as he could, to make them abandon it. Thus he combined obedience to Allah and His Messenger in upholding the Sunnah and religion and condemning the innovations of the heretical Jahmiyya, with upholding the rights of the believers among the leaders and the community, even when they were ignorant innovators and unjust sinners. Those well-known figures, such as Hammad ibn Abi Sulayman, Abu Hanifa, and other jurists of Kufa, counted verbal profession and belief in the heart as part of faith. This was also the position of Abu Muhammad ibn Kullab and those like him. Their position on this did not differ, nor is it reported that they said faith is merely affirmation in the heart. Rather, they attributed this view to "Jahm ibn Safwan," saying that he held that faith is merely knowledge in the heart, even without professing it verbally. They strongly condemned this view.))
