Some of the Arabs apostatized, so Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) said: "How can you fight the people, when the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said, 'I have been commanded to fight the people until they say, "There is no god but Allah." Whoever says it has thereby protected his property and his life from me, except by its due right, and his reckoning is with Allah.'" The question here is this: if Abu Bakr's position was that those who withheld zakat were unbelievers and were not excused on account of their interpretation, then his position also entails excusing the one who excuses those who interpret matters in this way—Umar here—on the basis of the hadith's wording at the outset, since Umar did not immediately declare them unbelievers. And according to your claim that the statements of the early generations are authoritative, Abu Bakr is the foremost of the early generations, a follower of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), and one of the Rightly Guided Caliphs, concerning whom it was said, "You must adhere to my Sunnah and the Sunnah of the Rightly Guided Caliphs." So you are obliged to follow him here and excuse the one who excuses the person who interprets matters in this way. Otherwise, you are obliged to declare Umar an unbeliever, and Abu Bakr too, since Umar did not regard withholding zakat—which, according to them, is an act that nullifies faith—as unbelief, and he objected to fighting those involved in it—merely fighting them, as you claim—but even regarded them as Muslims who affirm the oneness of God.

We ask them: did Umar remain an unbeliever throughout this period? If they say yes, they have made a grave false accusation and have ended up declaring the most noble of the early generations after al-Siddiq an unbeliever. If they say, "No, he did not become an unbeliever," then we say to them: excuse the one who excuses those whom you consider polytheists, but whom he called Muslims because of an interpretation he held. This person who excuses them did nothing beyond what Umar did. All of this is granting them, for the sake of argument, that the Companions unanimously agreed that those who withheld zakat were unbelievers.
