[Surat al-Hujurat, verse 2] Ibn al-Zubayr said: "After that, whenever Umar related a hadith from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), he would relate it to him as one whispering a secret." Abu Muhammad said: "Whoever makes a mistake, it is not permissible to accept his opinion without proof that establishes its validity. As for his (peace and blessings be upon him) saying, 'You must adhere to my Sunnah and the Sunnah of the rightly guided caliphs,' we know that he (peace and blessings be upon him) does not command what is beyond one's capacity. We found that the rightly guided caliphs after him (peace and blessings be upon him) differed greatly. There must therefore be one of three possibilities, with no fourth: either we accept everything over which they differed—which is impossible and beyond our capacity, since it would entail accepting a thing and its opposite—so this possibility is invalid; or it is permissible for us to accept whichever of their views we choose. This would be a departure from Islam, because it would mean that the religion of Allah, Exalted is He, is left to our discretion. Moreover, if this were so, then by accepting the view of one of them, we would necessarily have abandoned the view of another. In that case, we would not be following their Sunnah, and would thus have contradicted the hadith cited. Only the third possibility remains: to accept what they unanimously agreed upon. This applies only to what all the other Companions, may Allah be pleased with them, agreed upon along with them in following the Sunnah of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) and affirming it."(15)

Umar asked Abu Waqid al-Laythi what the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) used to recite in the prayers of Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha, even though the Messenger of Allah had performed them for many years. He did not know what to do about the Magians until Abd al-Rahman reminded him of the Messenger of Allah's instruction concerning them. He had forgotten that the Prophet had accepted the jizya from the Magians of Bahrain, a well-known matter; perhaps he had taken a share of that money, as others had. He forgot the Prophet's instruction that a person in a state of major ritual impurity should perform dry ablution (tayammum), and said that he should never perform dry ablution or pray unless he found water, until Ammar reminded him. He intended to divide the wealth of the Kaaba, but Ubayy ibn Ka'b argued against him that the Prophet had not done so, and he desisted. He used to send back women who had menstruated and departed before bidding farewell to the House, until he was informed that the Messenger of Allah had permitted this, whereupon he stopped sending them back. He used to assign different amounts of blood money for the fingers, until he learned from the Prophet that they were to be treated equally; he abandoned his view and adopted equality. He held that blood money belonged only to the agnatic heirs, until al-Dahhak ibn Sufyan informed him that the Prophet had granted a woman a share of the blood money, whereupon Umar adopted that ruling. He prohibited excessive dowries, citing the dowries of the Prophet, until a woman reminded him, and he withdrew his prohibition. He intended to stone a woman who was insane, until he was informed of the Messenger of Allah's saying, "The pen has been lifted from three"; he then ordered that she not be stoned. He ordered that Hatib's freedwoman be stoned, until Uthman reminded him that an ignorant person is not subject to the prescribed punishment, and he desisted from stoning her. He objected to Hassan reciting poetry in the mosque, but Hassan and Abu Hurayra informed him that Hassan had recited there in the presence of the Messenger of Allah, so Umar fell silent.
