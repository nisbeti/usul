That the faith of the imitator is invalid is the position of al-Ashari and the leading scholars among his followers, and the choice of Muhammad ibn Yusuf al-Sanusi (d. 895), author of Umm al-Barahin and other works on kalam relied upon by later scholars. He said in his book al-Manhaj al-Sadid (p. 48): "This is the position of the majority, and some report consensus on it."
According to these scholars, an imitator is someone who has not established proof for the articles of creed; and, in their view, the proofs are the proofs of kalam.
Al-Bajuri said in his commentary on Jawharat al-Tawhid: "The phrase 'some of the people' means: because of his confusion and hesitation, the scholars differed over whether his faith is valid or invalid. The disagreement amounts to several positions, including:
that the faith of the imitator is invalid, in which case the imitator is an unbeliever; this is the position of al-Sanusi."

If the criterion for accusing someone of Kharijism is simply declaring others unbelievers, then that criterion must apply to these people mentioned above. According to their own claim, these hadiths must be applied to them, since in their view the hadiths are not restricted to particular people. They must therefore declare each of them an unbeliever and kill any one of them whom they are able to reach. But they do not say this, and so they have fallen into contradiction, inevitably.
One of the strange things about these people is that if you ask them, "Why are you fighting these people?" they say, "Because they declare us unbelievers and fight us." They are then asked, "Do they implement Allah's law in their territories, or not?" If they answer, "Yes," they are told: "The Crusaders, whom you allied yourselves with and fought alongside, also declare Muslims unbelievers and fight them, and do not implement Allah's law in their territories. So which of the two groups is more deserving of having the hadiths about the Kharijites applied to them, and which group is more deserving of allegiance? Unless you say that those whom you have called Kharijites have apostatized through this act of theirs and become unbelievers, and that an apostate is more deserving of being fought than an unbeliever." They are then told: "You have fallen into the very thing you sought to escape: declaring others unbelievers and refusing to excuse them on grounds of ignorance or misinterpretation. You have declared a large group unbelievers and pronounced them apostates, without excusing them. The hadiths about the Kharijites therefore apply to you, since the relevant criterion is present in you. Another criterion is that you have left the idolaters among the Christians and the polytheists alone, and made an alliance with them.
