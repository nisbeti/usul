Regardless of the intention of the person who devised and established this way of thinking, was his aim to spread division, discord, excommunication, and killing among Muslims in order to weaken them? Is he malicious and cunning in this, with evil intentions, so that no scholar remains respected by Muslims and scarcely any Muslim remains on Earth, leaving no one whose reports can be trusted and no history that is valued? Or was he well-intentioned, with his independent reasoning leading him to this conclusion? I speak only of the person who established this way of thinking, not its followers, since I have found many of its followers whom I consider well-intentioned, but who misunderstand the matter and are excused on that account. To discuss these people, it is necessary to examine some of the important issues on which this way of thinking is based.

First: their claim that the opinion of a Companion or Successor is authoritative. As for the opinion of a Companion, yes, some scholars held that it could be relied upon, but only when there was no explicit evidence to the contrary in the Book and the Sunnah, and when it was not known that another Companion had disagreed with him. In that case, the matter is akin to tacit consensus. Others rejected it outright. But as for holding it authoritative when another Companion disagrees with it, I know of no one who held that view.

Ibn Taymiyya said: "Those scholars who held that a Companion's opinion is authoritative only said so when no other Companion disagreed with him and no text was known to contradict it." End quote from Majmu al-Fatawa (1/283).

Indeed, al-Hazimi, their shaykh, said in the thirtieth recording of Mufid al-Mustafid: "Scholars have differed over whether a Companion's interpretation is authoritative or not. This is a separate issue from whether a Companion's opinion in matters of legal verdicts is authoritative. Some of them even considered a Companion's interpretation to be a Prophetic report, as did al-Hakim and others, but the correct view is that it is not a Prophetic report." Thus, the issue is disputed. It is astonishing and suspicious of them to present it as one of the definitive matters, especially when they add the Successors to the Companions in claiming authority for their opinions; particularly when they rely on the opinions of some of the early Muslims despite the fact that other early Muslims disagreed with them, and especially when they rely on those opinions even if they are weak, as their own proponent has said, and use them in a grave matter such as disbelief and faith. Does all of this not give cause for suspicion?

This is the issue of whether a Companion's opinion is authoritative or not. Someone has summarized it as follows: "The fourth issue: The correct view is that a Companion's opinion is not authoritative. Some have said that it is authoritative without qualification, while others have made distinctions and mentioned several positions: (First) that it is authoritative if it contradicts analogy. (Second) that only the opinions of Abu Bakr and Umar (may Allah be pleased with them) are authoritative. (Third) that the opinions of the four caliphs are authoritative. Our evidence is the text, consensus, and analogy. As for …"
