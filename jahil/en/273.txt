(A Pause to Consider the Excuse for Choosing the Lesser of Two Evils)
The matter here must be precisely defined so that no one is confused. The discussion concerns a man who will not rule by anything other than what Allah has revealed, enact any legislation other than what Allah has revealed, or submit to the judgment of any law other than Allah's law. The only issue is that there are two candidates, one of whom must assume the office: one is close to Islam, while the other is hostile to it and wages war against it. If this hostile candidate will wreak havoc among the Muslims, killing and capturing them, and will put them in a state of necessity, then the previously discussed question of necessity arises. But if the other candidate will not bring the Muslims to a state of necessity and coercion, yet choosing the lesser of two evils would bring benefits to the Muslims and ease their situation, is it permissible in this case to nominate—only nominate—the lesser of two evils? There is no doubt that the person who wishes to choose the lesser of two evils does not intend for this ruler to rule by anything other than what Allah has revealed. His sole intention is to reduce disbelief and injustice. Whether or not he makes this choice, a ruler who does not rule by what Allah has revealed will most likely be installed. This voter has not ruled by anything other than what Allah has revealed, has not directly carried it out, and has not approved of it.
In some countries, there are two candidates for a certain office. One of them would cause severe harm to the Muslims if he assumed the office, while the other would cause less harm and would be conciliatory toward the Muslims, despite committing disbelief by ruling by other than what Allah has revealed. Is it permissible, in such circumstances, for the Muslims to nominate the one who would cause less harm—for example, someone like al-Najashi at the beginning of his rule, before he embraced Islam? Some view this issue from the perspective that whoever does this would, by doing so, be assisting in ruling by other than what Allah has revealed, which is major disbelief. Thus, the person who voted for him would also be a disbeliever for assisting in disbelief, and they would not excuse him on the grounds of ignorance, since he could have acquired knowledge.
But there is another point of view here: the elected representative did not intend, by his action, to help the disbeliever in his disbelief. Rather, he sought the disbeliever's leniency toward Islam and its people, and the reduction of disbelief. I would like to give an example here to clarify this matter. Suppose there are two pans of a balance: on one is a sheet of paper bearing a verse from the Quran, and on the other is a complete copy of the Quran, with impure water beneath the balance. We find two groups of people: one group is clinging to the pan with the complete Quran, trying to lower it so that it sinks into the muddy, impure water; the other group is clinging to the other pan, trying to lower it into the muddy, contaminated water. There is no doubt that both actions are disbelief. But by helping the people on the side of the verse, we will save the complete Quran from falling into the impure mud, because to the extent that we help lower the pan with the verse, the pan with the Quran rises. So there is no doubt that whoever helps lower the pan with the verse into the impure mud with the intention of saving the Quran has absolutely no intention of defiling that verse. Rather, when two prohibited matters conflict, he chooses the lesser prohibition. The researcher who considers something like this permissible does so for several reasons. Among them is that you may help a disbeliever for some legitimate reason, and your purpose in providing this kind of assistance is a legitimate one. Even if this assistance leads to the disbeliever being kept alive and remaining in existence for a longer period, and consequently to the continuation of his acts of disbelief, you do not here intend to preserve disbelief, bring it into existence, or perpetuate it. Your assistance is intended only for a legitimate purpose, even if it leads to a disbeliever being kept alive and continuing to exist, and consequently to his disbelief continuing. But since that is not what you intend, you are not blameworthy for it. Indeed, this may be something that is obligatory to do. I will give examples of this: giving food to a disbeliever—for it is authentically reported in al-Bukhari that the Companions were instructed to gather food for the polytheist woman who owned the waterskin; and his statement to his disbelieving relatives is established: "You have ties of kinship with me, and I shall maintain them as they should be maintained"; and it is established that the Companions disliked giving money to their relatives among the polytheists, so Allah revealed: {You are not responsible for their guidance, but Allah guides whom He wills}. Likewise, the People of the Book are allowed to remain in exchange for the jizya, and thus their disbelief is allowed to remain; and it is also permissible to release a polytheist as a favor, as Allah, Exalted is He, says: {Then either a favor afterward}; and the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) released Thumama ibn Uthal, who was a leader among Banu Hanifa.
