(Note) It is stated in Mukhtasar al-Sawa'iq al-Mursala ala al-Jahmiyya wa al-Mu'attala (p. 590):

"What, then, is the difference between the domain of commands and the domain of reports, such that these reports may be used as proof in one but not the other? This distinction is invalid by the consensus of the Muslim community, for it has always used these hadiths as proof in matters of transmitted knowledge, just as it uses them as proof in practical matters involving commands. This is especially so since practical rulings entail reporting about Allah that He prescribed such-and-such, made it obligatory, and approved it as religion; His legislation and religion relate back to His names and attributes. The Companions, the Successors, those who followed them, and the people of hadith and Sunnah have always used these reports as proof in questions of the attributes, divine decree, names, and rulings. It has never been transmitted from any of them that he permitted using them as proof in questions of rulings but not in reports about Allah, His names, and His attributes.

Where, then, are the early authorities who distinguished between the two domains? Indeed, their predecessors were some of the later theologians, who had no concern for what came from Allah, His Messenger, and his Companions. Rather, they turn hearts away from seeking guidance in this domain through the Book, the Sunnah, and the statements of the Companions, and refer them instead to the opinions of the theologians and the contrived principles of those who strain after argument. They are the ones known to distinguish between the two matters. They divided religion into matters of knowledge and matters of practice, calling them fundamentals and branches, and said: 'The truth in matters of fundamentals is one, and whoever opposes it is either an unbeliever or a sinner. As for matters of the branches, Allah Most High has no specific ruling concerning them, error in them is inconceivable, and every person exercising independent reasoning arrives at Allah Most High's ruling, which is His ruling.' If this division were merely a matter of terminology, it would not distinguish by it …"
