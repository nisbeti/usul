Shirk, disbelief, and their forms, according to this group, are not known through reason; they are known only through the Sharia. Reason has no role here at all. Thus, reason provides no proof here for knowing what constitutes disbelief and what constitutes shirk. In their view, these people are excused if they have not been negligent in seeking knowledge and have some kind of interpretation concerning an aspect of shirk or the divine attributes. This is due to a special quality of this community: the sin of error has been lifted from it, as in the verse, {There is no blame upon you for what you do by mistake}, and the hadith, "If a judge exercises independent judgment and is correct, he receives two rewards; if he exercises independent judgment and errs, he receives one reward." In the language, a judge is anyone who issues a ruling on a matter and then believes it or acts upon it. Thus, it includes anyone who acts or holds a belief, and so encompasses error in action or belief.
Among their proofs were the hadith of Dhat Anwat, the man who scattered his own ashes, and also the hadith of Aisha: "Whatever people conceal, Allah knows it." He said, "Yes." When they were asked, "Why did you distinguish between an original polytheist and a Muslim from whom shirk has occurred, when they are the same?" they replied, "Allah is the one who distinguished between them in what has already been mentioned, and reason has no role here." This is like what some have said: that committing shirk under coercion was not excused, based on their saying, {If they discover you, they will stone you or force you back into their religion}. They did not present a third option, which indicates that coercion was not considered in the case of shirk. But in our Sharia, committing shirk by word or deed under coercion incurs no sin and is excused. Likewise, error in this matter is also excused for this community in particular. This is also suggested by the hadith: "Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with them both) reported that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: 'Allah has pardoned my community for error…'"
