I asked my father (84) and Abu Zur'a (85) about the doctrines of the People of the Sunnah concerning the fundamentals of religion, what they had found the scholars in all the regions adhering to, and what they themselves believed regarding these matters. They said: "We found the scholars in all the regions—Hijaz, Iraq, Syria, and Yemen—adhering to the following doctrine: … Whoever claims that the Quran is created is an unbeliever in Allah the Almighty, an unbelief that takes him out of the religion. Whoever doubts the unbelief of such a person, if he understands, is an unbeliever."(86) (Among its narrators are two truthful narrators, and the rest of its chain consists of trustworthy narrators.) This does not say that a particular individual is always an unbeliever. Likewise, note that it restricts declaring the one who doubts his unbelief an unbeliever to someone who understands. This indicates that an excuser is excused until the proof is established against him and he understands it.

It is related in al-Sunnah by Abd Allah ibn Ahmad (1/112):
Ghayath ibn Ja'far (87) told me: I heard Sufyan ibn Uyaynah (88) say, "The Quran is the speech of Allah, Mighty and Majestic. Whoever says it is created is an unbeliever, and whoever doubts his unbelief is an unbeliever." // Its chain of transmission is good.

(84) Ubayd Allah ibn Abd al-Karim al-Qurashi, the freedman of al-Razi, the hadith master and one of the leading imams. Abu Hatim said: "No one of his like in knowledge, jurisprudence, integrity, and truthfulness came after him; this is beyond doubt." Ishaq ibn Rahawayh said: "Any hadith that Abu Zur'a does not know by heart has no basis." Tahdhib al-Kamal (19/89)
(85) Muhammad ibn Idris ibn al-Mundhir, the imam Muhammad ibn Idris al-Hanzali, was outstanding in memorization and widely traveled in pursuit of the repositories of knowledge. He died in the year 277, may Allah have mercy on him. Tahdhib al-Kamal (24/381)
(86) Its chain of transmission is sound back to both of them, and this creed is authentically established from Ibn Abi Hatim.
(87) Ghayath ibn Ja'far al-Shami al-Rahbi. Ibn Hibban mentioned him in his book al-Thiqat, and al-Daraqutni said:
Uyaynah narrated many hadiths from Ibn Uyaynah.
(88) His biographical entry has already been given.
