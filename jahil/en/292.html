Except for that, in the absence of authority and power for the law of Allah, it is not, in the first place, a matter of seeking judgment from anything.

We have already presented the Messenger of Allah's praise for the Alliance of al-Fudul, even though it was an alliance formed in the pre-Islamic era, established by unbelievers. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) witnessed it as a boy and said, "I would not like to have red camels in exchange for breaking it." According to another narration, "If I were invited to it in Islam, I would respond."

Its form was that of a tribal gathering in the pre-Islamic era, whose members pledged to aid the distressed, support the wronged, and restore people's rights to them through their collective strength and power. This was at a time when Islam had neither authority nor a state, before the Prophet's mission, and amid the benighted pre-Islamic age. It was therefore an institution of the pre-Islamic era. The Prophet's praise for that alliance thus entails that there is no blame on a vulnerable person who turns to it, or to something like it among the unbelievers who are able to ward off injustice from him, or protect his honor or property, or secure some of his rights through their strength, power, and authority, against other oppressive unbelievers, in the absence of authority for the Sharia. Were there even the slightest blame in this, let alone prohibition or unbelief, the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) would have made it clear when praising the alliance. For he (peace and blessings be upon him) was the most God-fearing and scrupulous of the community, and never left any evil unaddressed without warning them against it.
