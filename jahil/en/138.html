Ibn Taymiyya, may Allah have mercy on him, said (12/189): "As for declaring someone an unbeliever, the correct view is that whoever among the community of Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him) exercises independent judgment, seeks the truth, but errs, is not an unbeliever; rather, his error is forgiven. But whoever learns what the Messenger brought (peace and blessings be upon him), then opposes the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) after guidance has become clear to him, and follows a path other than that of the believers, is an unbeliever."

It says in Questions of Abu Ubayd al-Ajuri to Abu Dawud al-Sijistani on Discrediting and Accrediting Narrators (p. 35):

"Those who categorically barred transmission from them (that is, from innovators and followers of sectarian whims) held that innovators were unbelievers, according to the view of those who declared those who interpret texts figuratively to be unbelievers, or sinners, according to the view of those who did not rule that they were unbelievers. Some of them, however, deliberately refrained from transmitting from them as a form of punishment and humiliation, or because they did not consider these people trustworthy against lying. According to this latter view, the following statement by Abu Dawud, may Allah have mercy on him, is an exception: 'Among the followers of sectarian whims, none has more authentic hadiths than the Kharijites.' He then mentioned Imran ibn Hittan and Abu Hassan al-A'raj."5 (Abu Dawud did not declare the Kharijites unbelievers.)

Ibn al-Arabi said in al-Qabas fi Sharh Muwatta Malik ibn Anas (p. 404):

"People have differed over declaring those who interpret texts figuratively to be unbelievers. These are people who do not intend unbelief, but seek faith and end up in unbelief, and seek knowledge, but are led by their interpretation into ignorance. This is a grave question in which the proofs conflict. I have considered it at times: sometimes I declare them unbelievers, and sometimes I withhold judgment, except in the case of one who says that the Quran is created, or that there is another creator besides Allah. In such a case, I have no doubt, and I leave him no share of faith." (6)
