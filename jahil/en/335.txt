From the scholars' statements, three reasons for the name can be identified. First, they departed from the religion; this is what is mentioned in the hadiths. Second, they were called this because they rebelled against Ali. Third, they were called this because they rebelled against the rightful imams. The latter two definitions have no support in the hadiths. But some people have confused the reason for the name with the criterion for the ruling, making the reason for the name the criterion for the ruling. This is manifest ignorance and clear misguidance. We will give examples to make the distinction clear. Among the names of the Kharijites are: "The Kharijites," because they rebelled against Ali ibn Abi Talib; "the Arbitrators," because their slogan was "There is no judgment except Allah's"; "the Haruriyya," because they settled at Harura at the beginning of their affair; "the Sellers," because they said, "We have sold ourselves in obedience to Allah," meaning that they had sold them in exchange for Paradise; and "the Apostates," because they departed from the religion just as an arrow passes through its target—as the Messenger described them. Another name for them is "the Doubters."
That was because, when they rejected arbitration, they said to Ali, "You doubted your own position and appointed your enemy to judge you." Thus they were called the Shakkakiyya. These are some of the names and some of the reasons for their designation. There is no doubt that not everyone who says, "There is no judgment except Allah's," is necessarily subject to the rulings concerning the Kharijites, even if, linguistically, he is called a Muhakkim for Allah.
