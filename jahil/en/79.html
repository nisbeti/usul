- Al-Hafiz said in Fath al-Bari, in the Book of Prayer (1/523), among the lessons of the hadith in which someone said of Malik ibn al-Dukhshun that he was a hypocrite who defended the hypocrites: "Whoever accuses someone who outwardly professes Islam of hypocrisy or the like, on the basis of an indication that he perceives, does not thereby become an unbeliever or a sinner; rather, he is excused on the basis of interpretation." End quote.

Ibn al-Qayyim said, and he also said there (3/117): "The Lawgiver did not make those rulings depend on what is merely in people's hearts, without an indication in deed or speech; nor did He make them depend on mere words, knowing that the speaker did not intend their meanings and had no full knowledge of them. Rather, He has excused the community for what their hearts tell them, so long as they do not act upon it or speak of it; and He has excused them for what they say in error, forgetfulness, under compulsion, or without knowledge, when they do not intend or mean the sense of what they have said. When intention is joined with an indication in speech or deed, the ruling follows. This is a principle of the Sharia, and it is among the implications of Allah's justice, wisdom, and mercy." End quote.

- 610)

Ibn al-Wazir says: "His saying, Exalted is He, in this noble verse, 'But whoever opens his heart to disbelief,' supports the view that those who interpret [the texts] are not unbelievers, because their hearts have certainly not opened to disbelief, whether with certainty, conjecture, allowance, or possibility." End quote. Ithar al-Haqq ala al-Khalq, p. 437.
