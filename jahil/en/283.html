The third interpretation: He is absolved of protecting the blood of any Muslim who chooses to live among the polytheists, because that person has brought harm upon himself, or because rulings follow the territory, as is the position of the Hanafis. Al-Jassas said: "His blood has no value, like that of the people of war who have no covenant of protection."(62)

This is open to objection: Allah made it obligatory to support them if they seek help in matters of religion. He, Exalted is He, says: {Those who believe but have not emigrated—you have no duty of protection toward them until they emigrate. But if they seek your help in religion, then you must help them, except against a people with whom you have a treaty. And Allah sees all that you do.} Supporting them presupposes that their Islam is outwardly established!

The fourth interpretation: It refers to disavowal of his residence and conduct, not disavowal of the person who resides there himself. This is like the saying, "Whoever bears arms against us is not one of us," and "Whoever cheats us is not one of us," and similar sayings.

The fifth interpretation: This applied when emigration was obligatory, but it was abrogated after the conquest of Mecca. This was the view chosen by al-Kiya al-Harrasi,(63) al-Razi,(64) and others.

The sixth interpretation: It refers to someone who lives in the territory of the polytheists and is unable to manifest any aspect of his religion, or fears that he will be tempted away from it there. He is absolved of remaining among them, and must emigrate. If he is safe, then residing in their territory is disliked. Al-Mawardi alluded to this,(65) and al-Shafi'i said: "The Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) indicates that the obligation to emigrate applies to those able to do so only when they are being persecuted for their religion in the land from which emigration will save them. For the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) permitted some people in Mecca to remain there after accepting Islam, among them al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib and others, provided they did not fear persecution. He would instruct his armies to tell those who accepted Islam: 'If you emigrate, you will have what the emigrants have; but if you remain, you will be like the Bedouin Muslims.' He offered them this choice only in matters that were permissible for them."(66)

The seventh interpretation: He disavowed those who joined the unbelievers to fight against the Muslims. Ibn Hazm said: "Whoever voluntarily joins the territory of unbelief and war, fighting against the Muslims in his vicinity, has by this act become an apostate and is subject to all the rulings applicable to an apostate: he must be killed when he can be apprehended, his property may be taken, his marriage is dissolved, and so on. For the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) did not disavow a Muslim."(67)

Given these various possibilities, it cannot be said that someone who assists a polytheist against another polytheist in fighting is covered by this hadith. Consequently, neither is the voter for the lesser harm covered by it, since such a person is generally unable to emigrate. And if he is able, where is he to emigrate, when rule by other than what Allah has revealed has become widespread throughout the world, except in a few places? And Allah knows best.
