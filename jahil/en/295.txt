And the fact that he had a law and religion of his own, contrary to the religion of Allah, did not prevent him from sending to him to inform him that he had been wronged and cast into prison without committing any sin, in the hope that he might release him, remove the injustice against him, and clear him of the accusation for which he had been imprisoned ... Nor did it prevent him from defending himself and seeking to establish his innocence when the king summoned him later. He said to the messenger: "Return to your master and ask him about the women who cut their hands. Indeed, my Lord knows their scheming." Thus he complains of the injustice done to him—or, rather, mentions it before the unbelieving king in order to establish his innocence,

and all of this was the action of an infallible prophet. Safeguarding the principle of monotheism and its purity was the very message with which all the prophets were sent, and to which all their calls were in agreement, as is well known from the call of the prophets and messengers. It was not for the Prophet of Allah, Joseph, to contravene or contradict that, or to depart even by a hair's breadth from the religion of his forefathers Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob ... How could he, when Allah, Exalted is He, declared him innocent and protected him from what was lesser than that, saying: "Thus We turned away from him evil and indecency"; then He said: "Indeed, he was one of Our sincere servants."
All of this shows that there is no objection whatsoever to a monotheist doing something like this.
