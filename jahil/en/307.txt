Al-Kahf: 36. The other then declared him an unbeliever on that account. They were only two men, as Allah Most High says. There are many examples of this among the early generations, including al-Shafi'i's declaring Hafs al-Fard an unbeliever during a debate (3). Ibn Taymiyya held that al-Shafi'i did not declare this Hafs an unbeliever, but rather applied the term unbelief to his statement. However, the established account of the two of them is contrary to what the Shaykh of Islam said (4).
It is not permissible to require other Muslims to follow the judgment of someone who has declared another an unbeliever, so long as what was established for him has not been established for them, and so long as this person's unbelief has not been established by sound legal proof. .......
Is it permissible for someone who knows that a man is an unbeliever to make this known among the people, even if the unbeliever conceals his unbelief? The answer is: Yes; indeed, it is obligatory when harm from him is feared, especially if this unbeliever is one of the callers to innovations, someone from whom knowledge is taken, or someone seeking to marry a Muslim woman, or the like. For religion is sincere counsel. On this, Qadi Iyad (may Allah have mercy on him) said: "If the person who says this is someone from whom people seek to acquire knowledge or transmit hadith, or whose judgment, testimony, or legal opinion concerning people's rights is relied upon, then whoever hears him must make what he heard known, warn people against him, and testify against him regarding what he said. Any Muslim leaders to whom this reaches must likewise denounce it and explain his unbelief and the corruption of his statement, in order to avert his harm from the Muslims and uphold the right of the Master of the Messengers. The same applies if he is someone who preaches to the general public or teaches children, for a person with such an inner state cannot be trusted not to instill this in their hearts. Thus, the obligation is all the more emphatic in their case, in defense of the Prophet's right and the right of his Sharia [(2).
