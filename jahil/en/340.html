Ibn Hubayra said: "They differed concerning one who learns magic and practices it. Abu Hanifa, Malik, and Ahmad said that he thereby becomes an unbeliever. Some of Abu Hanifa's companions, however, made a distinction: if he learns it in order to guard against it or avoid it, he does not become an unbeliever; but if he learns it believing it to be permissible, or believing that it will benefit him, then he becomes an unbeliever. They did not regard the ruling as absolute. If he believes that the devils do whatever they wish, then he is an unbeliever. Al-Shafi'i said: 'If someone learns magic, we say to him: Describe your magic. If he describes something that entails unbelief, such as the belief of the people of Babylon that one draws near to the seven planets and that they do whatever is asked of them, then he is an unbeliever. But if it does not entail unbelief, then if he believes it to be permissible, he is an unbeliever.'"

The disagreement over declaring one who abandons prayer an unbeliever is well known.

Likewise, at the beginning of this study, we mentioned the disagreement among the early scholars over declaring those who interpret the texts figuratively to be unbelievers, and cited those who transmitted this disagreement, such as Qadi Iyad. We established that this was the position of Ibn Jarir al-Tabari. I know of no one who said that these early scholars were Kharijites, nor did anyone accuse Ibn Jarir al-Tabari of being a Kharijite. The position of this contemporary group, which declares those who interpret the texts figuratively to be unbelievers and does not excuse them on grounds of ignorance, is very close to Ibn Jarir al-Tabari's position on this issue. Whoever says that they are Kharijites must say the same of Ibn Jarir al-Tabari, without distinction; otherwise, he is contradicting himself.

In all these matters, scholars differed over the individuals who fell into them: according to some, they are unbelievers, abiding forever in the Fire, while according to others, they are believers. The group that declared them unbelievers did not say that the other group were Kharijites, because an error in declaring someone an unbeliever is not something for which one is held accountable.
