For all of them are the signs of Allah, Exalted is He. Once the proof has reached him, he is an unbeliever. Whoever claims that there is a prophet after the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), or denies something that he knows the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said, is an unbeliever, because he has not accepted the Prophet's judgment in the matter disputed between him and his opponent."

Likewise, Ibn Hajar says (Fath al-Bari, Ibn Hajar, 8/147):
"His words, 'No one will remain in the house except that I will have him flogged, while I look on—except al-Abbas, for he was not present with you': It has been said that this establishes the legitimacy of retaliation for everything a person is deliberately subjected to. This is open to question, because not all of them had done that; rather, this was done to them as a punishment for failing to comply with his prohibition of it. As for the one who did it, the matter is clear. As for the one who did not do it, it was because they failed to forbid what he had forbidden them. It may be inferred from this that a far-fetched interpretation does not excuse its proponent. This too is open to question, because the one who acted contrary to the prohibition—" Ibn al-Arabi said: "He intended that they should not come on the Day of Resurrection bearing a claim against him, and so fall into a grave predicament." It was objected that he could have pardoned them, since he did not take revenge for himself. What seems apparent is that he intended thereby to discipline them so that they would not do it again; thus it was discipline, not retaliation or revenge."
Shaykh al-Islam said, after mentioning the hadith about the man who instructed his sons, at his death, to burn his body: "The person who interprets the matter, being among the people of independent reasoning and eager to follow the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him), is more deserving of forgiveness than someone like this." End quote. (3/148)
