Imam al-Bazzar, may Allah have mercy on him, said, as recorded in Kashf al-Astar (vol. 3, p. 255): Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna narrated to us, saying: Umar ibn Yunus narrated to us, saying: Ikrima ibn Ammar narrated to us, saying: Abu Zumayl narrated to us, saying: Ibn Abbas narrated to us, saying: I heard Umar ibn al-Khattab say: Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a wrote a letter to Mecca, and Allah informed His Prophet of it. He sent Ali and al-Zubayr after the letter. They caught up with a woman riding a camel and took it out from one of her braids. The Prophet of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him and his family) said nothing to them. He then sent for Hatib and said, "Hatib, did you write this letter?" He replied, "Yes, Messenger of Allah." He said, "What made you do that?" Hatib replied, "By Allah, I am sincere toward Allah and His Messenger. But I was a stranger among the people of Mecca, while my family were living among them, and I feared for them. So I wrote a letter that would not harm Allah or His Messenger in any way, hoping it might benefit my family." Umar said: I drew my sword and said, "Messenger of Allah, let me deal with Hatib, for he has disbelieved; let me strike off his head." The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him and his family) said, "Ibn al-Khattab, how do you know? Perhaps Allah looked upon this company of the people of Badr and said, 'Do as you wish, for I have forgiven you.'"
Al-Bazzar said: The story of Hatib has been reported through more than one chain.
Abu Abd al-Rahman said: This is a hasan hadith. The hadith shows that Umar is excused for his interpretation, since he judged Hatib to have disbelieved based on an interpretation.
