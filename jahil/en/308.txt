This concerns legal establishment, that is, correctly establishing that the cause of disbelief has in fact been committed by the person who committed it.))
Therefore, members of movements are not obliged to obey their leaders in declaring another group unbelievers until this has been correctly established according to the Sharia. It is well known that declaring someone an unbeliever is a legal ruling, like all other legal rulings, and disagreement over declaring people unbelievers on certain grounds is a matter of independent legal reasoning (ijtihad), like other matters. Thus, a caliph in an Islamic state may not compel people to adopt one view on this matter, nor require them to declare an unbeliever anyone whom he himself declares one, if an individual believes that this declaration is unjustified and contrary to the Quran and Sunnah. A state governed by Islam should therefore accommodate differences arising from independent legal reasoning, even on the question of declaring people unbelievers; not all students of knowledge understand matters in the same way. The caliphs likewise did not compel anyone to follow their school of thought when another interpretation was possible.
It is stated in Majmu al-Fatawa (27/296):
"The twelfth point: When scholars disagree over a matter, no judge may settle the dispute by issuing a ruling. No judge may say, 'I have ruled that this opinion is correct and that the other opinion is rejected, along with the person who holds it.' Rather, in matters over which Muslim scholars disagree, or on which they have reached consensus, the ruler's statement is like that of any individual scholar, if he is a scholar; if he is merely an imitator, he is like the ordinary people who imitate others. Holding office and authority do not make someone who is not a learned jurist capable of independent reasoning into one. If authority and office were what qualified someone to speak about knowledge and religion, then the caliph and the sultan would be the most entitled to speak about knowledge and religion, and people would seek their legal opinions and refer to them when matters were unclear.
