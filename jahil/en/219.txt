The verse thus explains that there should have been no hesitation concerning their case. Mujahid related that it was revealed about a group of people from Mecca who professed faith and migrated to Medina, then asked permission to return to Mecca to bring back merchandise in which they could trade. They claimed that they had remained believers, and the Muslims differed over their case: were they polytheists or Muslims? This is clarified by what is related from Ibn Abbas: it was revealed about a group of people from Mecca who concealed polytheism while professing Islam to the Muslims, so that they would be safe from the Muslims waging war against them when they went out on trading journeys or the like. The Muslims had heard that they had left Mecca on a trading journey, and a group of Muslims said:
We should ride out to them and fight them. Another group said, "How can we kill them when they have professed Islam?" The Muslims differed over this, and the Messenger of Allah did not censure either group until the verse was revealed. Al-Dahhak related that it was revealed concerning a people who had outwardly embraced Islam in Mecca but had not emigrated, and who supported the polytheists against the Muslims. They were the ones of whom Allah, Exalted is He, said: {Indeed, those whom the angels take in death while they are wronging themselves will say, "What were you doing?"} [al-Nisa: 97]—to the end of the verse. I think all these groups were known at the time the verse was revealed, and thus served as examples of its general purport: it applies to them and to others among all those known to be hypocrites at that time, whether from the people of Medina or the people of Mecca...... It seems that the verse was revealed after the time for fighting them had passed, with the aim of avoiding any confrontation with them when they set out, thereby drawing them along until the day of the conquest of Mecca.
