Scholars' statements:

Ibn Taymiyya said, after mentioning the disagreement among later scholars over whether declaring the people of innovation unbelievers constitutes unbelief that takes one out of the religion or not, and whether they remain eternally in the Fire or not … He said: "The reality of the matter is that, with regard to the general wording of the imams' statements, they suffered the same problem as the early generations did with the general wording of the Lawgiver's texts. Whenever they saw the imams say, 'Whoever says such-and-such is an unbeliever,' the listener assumed that this wording applied to everyone who said it, without considering that declaring someone an unbeliever has conditions and impediments that may be absent in the case of a particular individual, and that declaring someone an unbeliever in general does not entail declaring a particular individual an unbeliever unless the conditions are present and the impediments absent. This is made clear by the fact that Imam Ahmad and most of the imams who made such general statements did not declare most of those who uttered those very words to be unbelievers.
