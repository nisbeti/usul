"My Lord, this is greater." Then, when it set, he said, "O my people, I am free of what you associate with Allah. I have turned my face, as one inclining to the truth, toward Him who created the heavens and the earth, and I am not one of the polytheists." His people argued with him. He said, "Do you argue with me about Allah, when He has guided me? I do not fear what you associate with Him, unless my Lord wills something. My Lord encompasses all things in knowledge. Will you not then take heed? How could I fear what you associate with Him, when you do not fear associating with Allah that for which He has sent down no authority to you? Which of the two parties is more entitled to security, if you know? Those who believe and do not taint their faith with wrongdoing—for them is security, and they are rightly guided. That was Our argument, which We gave to Abraham against his people. We raise in degrees whom We will. Indeed, your Lord is Wise, Knowing." (78–83)
Abraham did not mention a miracle here. Rather, he argued against them that these things they worship were created and set, so how could they be worshipped? This showed that they had associated partners with Allah without any "authority" or proof, and he made this a sufficient reason for not being secure from Allah's punishment. This shows that it is a sufficient proof, and that there is no excuse for anyone who rejects it.
