Ibn Taymiyyah said (28/212): "If it is impossible to establish the obligatory duties of knowledge, jihad, and the like except through someone who has an innovation, whose harm is less than the harm of abandoning that obligation, then securing the benefit of the obligation alongside an outweighed harm is better than the reverse." (Conclusion) After researching this issue, namely being excused on account of ignorance, the researcher has concluded the following: A person who commits disbelief out of ignorance, after having uttered the two testimonies and embraced faith in general terms, is one of two kinds. Either he did so on the basis of an interpretation that has some basis in the Sharia or in language; such a person is excused. Or he was negligent in seeking knowledge and spoke about Allah without knowledge, either denying or affirming; such a person is not excused. Whoever does not excuse people on account of ignorance and declares those who interpret matters to be disbelievers is himself excused on account of his ignorance; he cannot be declared a disbeliever for that, nor accused of Kharijism. Likewise, whoever excuses someone on account of ignorance based on an interpretation cannot be declared a disbeliever or accused of Murji'ism. And Allah knows best.
