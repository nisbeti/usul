He made it clear and called all people to it, so there must be agreement. Whoever denies this is an unbeliever, without disagreement. Once he acknowledges that, he is asked: Did it ever come from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) that he refused to accept the faith of the people of a village or a locality, or of any person who came to him, free or enslaved, man or woman, unless they first affirmed that ability precedes the act or accompanies it, that the Quran is uncreated, that Allah, Exalted is He, will be seen or will not be seen, or that He has hearing, sight, life, or any of the other superfluous matters of the theologians, which Satan introduced among them to sow enmity and hatred between them? If he claims that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) never invited anyone to Islam without first explaining these matters to them, he has lied, by the consensus of the Muslims on earth, and said what he knows to be false. He has also claimed that all the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) colluded to conceal this aspect of the Prophet's conduct. This is naturally impossible; moreover, it imputes unbelief to them, since they concealed something without which no one's Islam could be complete. If they say that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) never invited anyone to any of these matters, but that they are contained in the Quran and in his words, it is said to them: You have spoken truly. This confirms that if ignorance of any of all these matters were unbelief, the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) would not have neglected to explain them to free and enslaved men and women. Whoever considers this possible has said that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) did not convey the message as he was commanded to do; this is outright unbelief on the part of whoever permits it. It is therefore necessarily established that ignorance of all this does no harm at all. Discussion of these matters becomes obligatory only when people engage in them; then it is obligatory to clarify the truth from the Quran and Sunnah, in accordance with the words of Allah, Mighty and Majestic is He: {Be steadfast for Allah, bearing witness with justice}, and His words, Mighty and Majestic is He: {You shall make it clear to the people and not conceal it}. Whoever, after the truth has been clarified, stubbornly persists is an unbeliever, because he has not accepted the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) as judge, nor submitted to his ruling. It is authentically reported from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) that a man who had never done any good said, when death came to him, to his family: "When I die, burn me, then scatter my ashes on a windy day, half in the sea and half on land. By Allah, if Allah, Exalted is He, has power over me, He will punish me with a punishment unlike that of anyone among His creation." Allah, Mighty and Majestic is He, gathered his ashes and brought him back to life, then asked him, "What made you do that?" He replied, "Fear of You, my Lord." And Allah, Exalted is He, forgave him for this statement.

Abu Muhammad said: "This was a man who remained ignorant until he died that Allah, Mighty and Majestic is He, is able to gather his ashes and bring him back to life. Yet He forgave him because of his acknowledgment, his fear, and his ignorance."

This passage makes clear the difference between someone who believes in a general way, then remains silent and submits, without fabricating lies about Allah, and someone who does not remain silent, but instead fabricates lies about Allah by asserting some form of polytheism, unbelief, or sin without proof, and falls short in seeking knowledge of the matter. The discussion of this latter type will come later, in a passage from Ibn Hazm, God willing.
