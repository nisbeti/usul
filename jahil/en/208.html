Someone might say this, and at first glance this reconciliation may seem acceptable and reasonable. But that is not the case, because there is a legal truth of the Sharia that we must know: first, because it is a law that must be understood as it is and in its true sense.

Second: Because this understanding helps us reconcile many texts that appear to contradict and conflict with one another. What is this legal truth? It is that every act of disbelief is polytheism. It is known to everyone, or at least to jurists and students of knowledge, that every act of polytheism is disbelief. But they do not know the converse: they know that every act of polytheism is disbelief, but the fact that every act of disbelief is polytheism is not known to the vast majority of people, even though it is as true as the words you speak: every act of disbelief is polytheism, just as every act of polytheism is disbelief. There is absolutely no difference between them.

Among the proofs for this is the dialogue that Allah, Exalted is He, mentions in Surat al-Kahf between a believer and a polytheist. Allah, Exalted is He, says: {Set forth for them the example of two men: We gave one of them two gardens of grapevines, surrounded them with date palms, and placed crops between them. Both gardens yielded their produce and withheld nothing from it, and We caused a river to flow through them. He had other produce, and he said to his companion} [al-Kahf: 32–34]. The owner of the two gardens, who was an unbelieving polytheist, as you will hear, said to his believing companion: {He said to his companion, as he was conversing with him, "I have more wealth than you and greater numbers." He entered his garden, wronging himself, and said, "I do not think this will ever perish. Nor do I think the Hour will come to pass"} [al-Kahf: 34–36]. In general usage, or according to common convention, is this polytheism or unbelief? This is unbelief, because he denied resurrection and the rising of the dead: {He said, "I do not think this will ever perish. Nor do I think the Hour will come to pass. And if ...}
