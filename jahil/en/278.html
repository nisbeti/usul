[44] It appears in al-Umm, "Al-Umm by al-Shafi'i" (4/256):

"If a group of Muslims were captured and the polytheists sought their help against other polytheists like themselves, so that they might fight them, it has been said that they should fight them. It has also been said that al-Zubayr and some of his companions fought in Abyssinia as polytheists against polytheists. Those who hold this view say: What is forbidden about fighting alongside them, when the blood and property of those they fight are lawful on account of their polytheism? If someone were to say that fighting alongside them is forbidden for several reasons, one of them would be that when Muslims prevail over polytheists and take spoils, one fifth is due to those entitled to the fifth, who are dispersed across different lands. In this case, there is no way for the fifth from the spoils to reach those entitled to it, so that it can be delivered to the imam for distribution. Also, if they fight the People of the Book and they pay the jizya, they are obliged to spare their lives. If they pay the jizya, he would be unable to prevent them from sparing their lives. This would be a valid position. If they are not compelled to fight them, it would be preferable to me that they not fight. We do not know that the report about al-Zubayr is established; and even if it were established, the Negus was a Muslim: he had believed in the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), and the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) prayed [over him]." Here al-Shafi'i is observed to have transmitted the disagreement in cases where there is no necessity to fight alongside a disbeliever against another disbeliever. Those who held that it is permissible said that the basic rule is that fighting these polytheists is permissible, and that their property is lawful; therefore, whoever fights alongside a polytheist against another polytheist has done something permissible. Those who held that it is impermissible argued that the Muslims would be unable to administer the war, distribute the spoils according to the rules of the Sharia, or refrain from fighting those who paid the jizya, among other things, because they would be under the domination of the disbelievers. Then he concluded.
