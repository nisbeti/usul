For this reason, Abraham (peace be upon him), who defied his people and did not fear their blazing fire at one time, despite being alone and without equal, was in another situation considered to be, along with his wife, vulnerable and compelled to comply with the request of the unbeliever when he summoned Sarah. Indeed, al-Bukhari included this in the Book of Coercion. I mean by this the hadith of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): "Abraham migrated with Sarah. He entered a village in which there was a king—or one of the tyrants—and he sent for him, saying, 'Send her to me.' So he sent her. She stood before him, then began to perform ablution and pray, saying, 'O Allah, if I have believed in You and in Your Messenger, then do not give the unbeliever power over me.' Then he was seized and kicked with his leg." Al-Bukhari gave it the chapter heading in the Book of Coercion: "Chapter: If a woman is coerced into fornication, there is no prescribed punishment upon her."

He thus regarded this as coercion, and there was no blame on Abraham or his wife for being alone with that unbeliever, since she had been coerced, as the hadith scholar Ibn Hajar related among the statements of the scholars. They were not blamed or reproached for not fleeing, nor did anyone require them to do so. They knew best their circumstances and situation in that place.

Shaykh Muhammad ibn Ibrahim Al al-Shaykh said: "As for matters settled by tribal judges, if this was done by way of reconciliation and the reconciliation did not entail permitting something forbidden or forbidding something lawful, then the reconciliation is valid. But if it was done by way of judgment, then it is invalid, because what is known of tribal shaykhs is their ignorance and lack of knowledge of the rulings of the Sharia.
