That Allah is above His creation, that the Quran is His speech, and that He will resurrect those in the graves. If most of them were asked about this, they would be unable to answer.

As for the sixth point: namely, that the conjecture derived from the reports of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) is, in their view, stronger than the certainty based on those illusory propositions—this is known to anyone familiar with both. Someone with no experience of either hears them call their false propositions "conclusive rational proofs" and "demonstrations that yield certainty," while calling the explicit texts of the Quran and Sunnah "apparent transmitted reports that do not yield certainty." He may then come to believe they are right, out of deference to them, thinking well of them, and relying on some of the doubts they raise. But one who has insight into what the Messenger brought (peace and blessings be upon him) and into what these people hold knows with certainty that the matter is otherwise, and that the propositions by which they oppose the texts yield neither knowledge nor even conjecture. Indeed, they say: "Sound reason and innate human nature testify that these propositions are false and invalid." Even if a large group agrees on them, among all the groups of people of falsehood you will find each group agreeing on something whose corruption is necessarily evident to reason and to the innate disposition with which Allah created people. Each group of theologians bears witness against its opponents that they have contradicted sound reason and innate human nature. We have already mentioned some examples of this earlier in this book, concerning matters in which the theologians and philosophers contradicted sound reason. What is astonishing is that you see many of them assert their position categorically and declare anyone who disagrees with them an unbeliever, then themselves assert the opposite or suspend judgment on the matter. This is extremely common among them.
