It is related in the Musnad of Ahmad: Muhammad ibn Mus'ab—namely, al-Qarqasani—narrated to us; he said: al-Awza'i narrated to us, from Hassan ibn Atiyya, from Khalid ibn Ma'dan, from Jubayr ibn Nufayr, from Dhu Mikhmar, from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), who said: "You will make a secure peace with the Romans, and you and they will campaign against an enemy behind them. You will return safely and take spoils. Then you will encamp at a meadow with hillocks, and a man (1) from among the Romans will stand up, raise the cross, and say, 'Behold, the cross has prevailed!' A man from among the Muslims will stand up and kill him. At that point the Romans will break their treaty, and the great battles will begin. They will gather (2) against you, and come to you with eighty banners, each banner accompanied by ten (3)

There is no doubt that making peace with the Romans strengthens their unbelieving rule, and fighting their enemy likewise empowers them. But all these harms are merely hypothetical and are not intended by the Muslims; rather, what they intend is to ward off the harm of this other, greater enemy. This is very close to the issue before us.

Al-Sindi said: His words, "secure," mean "possessing security"; the form is one of attribution, or "secure" is used in a figurative attribution.

His statement, "Then you will fight them": that is, you and they, as in the following narration.

His statement, "an enemy," in the accusative; that is, you will unite to fight the enemy, on account of the peace treaty.

His statement, "and you will be safe," means safety.

His statement, "in a pasture": the place where animals graze.

His statement, "mounds," with two dammas and a light l: the plural of "mound," with a fatha, meaning anything gathered on the ground, whether soil or sand.

His statement, "the cross prevailed," means the religion of the Christians, with the aim of nullifying the truce, or merely out of pride and to provoke the Muslims to anger. And Allah, Exalted is He, knows best.

(Another proof) is that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said regarding the captives of Badr: "If al-Mut'im ibn Adi were alive and spoke to me about these foul men, I would release them to him." Narrated by al-Bukhari. There is no doubt that the Prophet's releasing seventy polytheist captives strengthened them, but this was certainly not what the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) intended. What seems apparent—and Allah knows best—is that he (peace and blessings be upon him) wanted to repay al-Mut'im ibn Adi for his kindness.
