Fifth: The statement of Abu Umama: "These people had been Muslims, but became disbelievers" and "They had been among the people of Islam." Sixth: There are grounds that do not constitute disbelief, but are sins: they kill the people of Islam. Killing the people of Islam is not disbelief, and they agree with us on this. But it makes killing them permissible and constitutes armed banditry if it is not based on a valid interpretation. Thus, it too is a valid ground, in light of his statement (peace and blessings be upon him), "Wherever you encounter them, kill them," on account of their armed banditry.

Seventh: "And they invoke the people of idols." This is a basis for a ruling, but it does not constitute disbelief; rather, it is a sin and does not permit killing. The apparent conclusion is that this is not the basis here for the obligation to kill these people, unless it is said that they invoke them as an act of religious devotion and hold that fighting them is forbidden, as some have maintained, as will be discussed.

If these people, in all the cases mentioned above, are a group who have committed disbelief, combined with killing the people of Islam and leaving the idolaters alone.

And now let us examine: what is the disbelief they committed? And what are the characteristics of these people that the scholars mentioned in their books?

First: It is stated in al-Muhalla (al-Muhalla bi al-Athar, 11/334):

It is thus established that interpretation varies. Any group whose interpretation in pursuit of its aims seeks to erase some part of the Sunnah—such as one who adopts the view of the Kharijites in order to take authority away from Quraysh; or to lead people back to the view that stoning is invalid, that people who commit sins are unbelievers, that Muslims may be borrowed from, or that children and women may be killed; or to proclaim the view that divine decree is invalid, or that the vision of Allah is impossible, or that Allah, Exalted is He, knows nothing until it comes to be; or to disavow some of the Companions; or to deny intercession; or to reject acting upon the established Sunnahs of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), and calls people to refer instead to someone below the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) ...
