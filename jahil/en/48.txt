He said: "Among those who transmitted the other view—that they should not be declared unbelievers—were Ali ibn Abi Talib, Ibn Umar, and al-Hasan al-Basri. This was also the view of a group of jurists, speculative theologians, and scholars of kalam. They cited as evidence the fact that the Companions and Successors granted inheritance to the heirs of the people of Harura and to those known for holding the doctrine of predestination who had died, buried them in Muslim cemeteries, and applied the rulings of Islam to them."

Ibn Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) used to pray behind Najda the Kharijite.

It is stated in Usul al-Sunna by Ibn Abi Zamanin (p. 283):

"Asad said: al-Rabi ibn Zayd told me, from Sawwar ibn Shabib, who said: Najda al-Haruri made the pilgrimage with his companions and made a truce with Ibn al-Zubayr. One of them led the people in prayer for a day and a night, and the other led them for a day and a night. Ibn Umar prayed behind them both. A man confronted him and said, 'Abu Abd al-Rahman, do you pray behind Najda al-Haruri?'

Ibn Umar replied, 'When they call, "Come to the best of deeds," we answer; and when they call, "Come to kill a person," we say, "No."' He raised his voice as he said this."

Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyya (may Allah have mercy on him) said: "Abd Allah ibn Umar and other Companions used to pray behind al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf. The Companions and Successors also used to pray behind Ibn Abi Ubayd, who was accused of heresy and called people to misguidance." (7) Majmu al-Fatawa, 3/281
