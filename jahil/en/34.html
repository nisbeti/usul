5 - Abu Hamid al-Ghazali, may Allah have mercy on him, said:

[What is meant is that one who intends good by committing an act of disobedience out of ignorance is not excused, unless he has only recently embraced Islam and has not yet had sufficient time to learn. Allah, Exalted is He, has said: {So ask the people of knowledge if you do not know} (al-Nahl: 43] (5).

(Punishment for the act, not merely for negligence) Ibn Qudama said: "If a fornicator claims ignorance of the prohibition, and it is plausible that he could have been ignorant of it, as in the case of someone who has only recently embraced Islam or was raised in a remote desert, his claim is accepted, since he may be telling the truth. But if he is someone from whom this would not be unknown, such as a Muslim raised among Muslims and people of knowledge, his claim is not accepted, because the prohibition of fornication is not unknown to someone like him, and so his lie is known. If he claims ignorance that an invalid marriage is unlawful, his claim is accepted, because Umar accepted the claim of someone who said he was ignorant of the prohibition on marrying during the waiting period, and because this is something many people may be ignorant of and which is unknown to those who are not people of knowledge." (1).

7 (Punishment for the act) - Ibn Rajab al-Hanbali, may Allah have mercy on him, said:

[If someone raised in the abode of Islam among Muslims commits fornication and claims ignorance of its prohibition, his claim is not accepted, because what is apparent belies him, even though the presumption is that he did not know of it] (2). By "what is apparent," he means that he was raised among Muslims, which makes it possible for him to know of the prohibition. His statement, "the presumption is that he did not know," is because the presumption regarding a person is ignorance, as I mentioned in the first chapter of this book.
