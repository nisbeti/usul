"And those who believe and do righteous deeds incur no blame for what they have consumed" (al-Ma'idah: 93). He cited this verse as evidence to Umar when Umar intended to carry out the prescribed punishment on him. Umar explained his error to him and carried out the punishment. Concerning this, Ibn Taymiyyah said: "Or he was mistaken and thought that those who believe and do righteous deeds were exempted from the prohibition of wine, just as those whom Umar required to repent were mistaken in this regard, and the like. They are to be asked to repent and the proof is to be established against them; if they persist, they then become unbelievers. They are not judged to be unbelievers before that, just as the Companions did not judge Qudamah ibn Maz'un and his companions to be unbelievers when they were mistaken in their interpretation."(1)
(The one who interprets) This incident indicates that error in interpretation prevents a judgment of unbelief, by the consensus of the Companions. It also falls under the general meaning of the words of Allah, Exalted is He: "There is no blame upon you for what you do by mistake" (al-Ahzab: 5).
Nevertheless, not every error in interpretation is considered an acceptable excuse and a bar to declaring someone an unbeliever:
An error in interpretation that is excusable is one that arises from examining a legal proof, but misunderstanding it.
An error in interpretation that is not excusable is one that arises from mere opinion and caprice, without relying on a legal proof. An example is Iblis's refusal to prostrate to Adam, on the grounds that "I am better than him: You created me from fire and created him from clay" (al-A'raf: 12). This is mere opinion. The esoteric interpretations by which the Batinis set aside religious obligations are likewise mere caprice.
