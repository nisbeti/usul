It is known to everyone of sound senses and sound reason that the hadiths concerning the vision, the call, the descent, the speaking, and other attributes have been transmitted through numerous channels, and that the community received them with acceptance to a degree far greater than the widespread transmission of the hadith about the two parties to a sale having the option to rescind, the hadith "There is no bequest for an heir," and the hadith concerning the grandmother's prescribed share. Indeed, there is no comparison between the widespread transmission of the hadiths concerning the attributes and that of these hadiths. Can any rational person reasonably say that the latter establish knowledge while the former do not, unless he is being obstinate?

Al-Shafi'i explicitly stated in his books that a solitary report yields knowledge. He stated this plainly in his book The Disagreement of Malik, and in the Egyptian Epistle he argued that it does not establish the kind of knowledge established by a text of the Book or a mass-transmitted report. We shall cite his wording in both books.

In the Epistle he said: "As for a practice established by a report from a particular source, where reports may differ and the report may admit of interpretation, and the report has come by way of a single transmitter, the proof concerning it, in my view, is that it must bind the scholars, so that they may not reject what has been explicitly stated in it, just as they are bound to accept the testimony of upright witnesses. This is not, however, certainty of knowledge, as is the case with a text of the Book and a report transmitted by the public from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him).
