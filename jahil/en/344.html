The question of being excused on account of ignorance is one on which the scholars differ. It should be noted that this type of declaring someone an unbeliever is less severe than the previous type, since the previous type involves declaring someone an unbeliever who has not committed any act of unbelief at all, but whose act some people mistakenly thought was unbelief. Here, however, the person has committed an act of unbelief on which there is consensus, but is not excused on account of ignorance. Thus, all the evidence cited above is even more applicable here, and Allah knows best. The researcher's view is that a person who interprets matters incorrectly should be excused on account of ignorance if he has not been negligent in seeking knowledge and has not fabricated lies against Allah. But the question under discussion here is whether someone who is not excused, despite being an interpreter, is a Kharijite. Let us first review

the views of the scholars who did not excuse people on account of ignorance, either because they did not consider ignorance an excuse, or because they considered it an excuse but held that knowledge had become widespread and that the legally responsible person had been negligent in seeking knowledge, and so had no excuse:

Ibn Hazm reported the disagreement on the question of being excused on account of ignorance. He said in al-Fasl fi al-Milal wa al-Ahwa wa al-Nihal (3/137):

Discussion of those who are declared unbelievers and those who are not

Abu Muhammad said: People have differed on this subject. One group held that anyone who disagrees with them on any matter of belief or any matter of legal opinion is an unbeliever. Another group held that such a person is an unbeliever in some matters and a sinner, but not an unbeliever, in others, according to what their intellects and conjectures led them to conclude. Another group held that anyone who disagrees with them on matters of belief is an unbeliever, but that anyone who disagrees with them on matters of rulings and acts of worship is neither an unbeliever nor a sinner, but rather a qualified interpreter who is excused if he errs and rewarded for his intention.
