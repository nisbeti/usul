So consider, may Allah have mercy on you, how no statement resembling theirs has been established from anyone among the early generations except the statement of Sufyan ibn Uyaynah. As for what is transmitted from Ahmad ibn Hanbal, if it is authentic, it has already been noted that Ahmad is not counted among the early generations, since he did not belong to the generation of the Successors of the Successors. It is also possible that these words are a general statement about the category, not about particular individuals, since it is well known of him, may Allah have mercy on him, that he did not declare the rulers of his time who held that the Quran was created to be unbelievers as individuals, as has already been transmitted from him. His words should therefore be understood in the general sense.

(Ahmad's position on excusing people on the basis of interpretation) Ibn Taymiyyah said in al-Iman al-Awsat (1/56):

Ahmad and other imams explicitly stated that these Murji'ah are not to be declared unbelievers. Whoever transmits from Ahmad or another of the imams that they declared these people unbelievers, or classifies them among the innovators whose status as unbelievers is disputed, has made a grave error. What is reliably transmitted from Ahmad and imams like him is only the declaration that the Jahmiyyah, the anthropomorphists, and people like them are unbelievers. Ahmad did not declare the Kharijites or the Qadariyyah unbelievers when they affirmed divine knowledge but denied that actions are created and that the divine will is all-encompassing, though two reports are related from him concerning declaring them unbelievers. As for the Murji'ah, his position that they are not to be declared unbelievers is undisputed. Moreover, Ahmad did not declare particular Jahmiyyah to be unbelievers, nor did he declare everyone who was called a Jahmi to be an unbeliever, nor everyone who agreed with the Jahmiyyah in some of their innovations. Rather, he prayed behind Jahmiyyah who called people to their doctrine, subjected them to an inquisition, and inflicted severe punishments on those who did not agree with them. Ahmad and imams like him did not declare them unbelievers. Rather, he believed them to be believers and imams, prayed for them, and considered it permissible to follow them in prayer, to perform the pilgrimage and fight alongside them, and to refrain from rebelling against them, just as he held regarding rulers like them.
