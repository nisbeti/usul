He said: The person who abandons the obligatory migration despite being able to undertake it, while unable to manifest his religion in the land of unbelief, may have abandoned migration out of allegiance that constitutes unbelief. If he abandoned it for some worldly benefit in the land of unbelief, then he is sinful, unless his inability to manifest his religion reaches the point of unbelief, such as when he is unable to perform the prayer. But if he abandoned migration out of love for his unbelieving people and preferring to live among them, this is allegiance that constitutes unbelief. The legal characterization of abandoning migration, in the case of this category, is therefore that it is an act that may indicate allegiance; it should not be said that it is manifest allegiance. It is well established that the call is directed only to the leaders of those who refuse, not to each of their individuals by name. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) sent invitations to Islam to Khosrow, Caesar, al-Muqawqis, and the Negus, and his letter to them was one and the same. (How Unbelief Is Established and the Question of Blind Following in Declaring Others Unbelievers)

It is noticeable that some movements go to extremes in declaring other movements working for Islam to be unbelievers, on the grounds of what they call allegiance to another group. That other group's unbelief may not be apparent to the group that did not declare it unbelieving, because of an interpretive doubt. One should not go to extremes in such matters, for the conditions for establishing unbelief are conditions that we have explained in this study.
