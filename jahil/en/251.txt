That is sufficient, in our view, to declare those who declare them unbelievers to be unbelievers. This is supported by the hadith, "If a man says to his brother, 'Unbeliever,' then it applies to one of them." In Muslim's wording: "Whoever accuses a Muslim of unbelief, or says, 'Enemy of Allah,' it will rebound upon him." He said: "It has been established that these people accuse a group of unbelief, people whose faith we know with certainty. They must therefore be judged unbelievers, in accordance with the implication of the Lawgiver's report."
Ibn Hazm likewise inclined to declare them unbelievers, and counted among their reprehensible acts, as stated in al-Muhalla bi al-Athar (11/334):
"It is thus established that interpretation differs. Any group that, in its rebellion, interprets the texts so as to erase some part of the Sunnah—such as those who adopt the view of the Kharijites in order to remove authority from Quraysh, or to return people to the view that stoning is invalid, or that people who commit sins are unbelievers, or that Muslims may be forced to lend money, or that children and women may be killed; or who openly profess that divine decree is invalid, or that the vision of Allah is impossible, or that Allah, Exalted is He, knows nothing until it comes to be; or who disavow some of the Companions, invalidate intercession, reject acting upon established Sunnahs from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), and call people to refer to someone other than the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him); or who call for withholding zakat, or withholding any right due to a Muslim, or any right due to Allah, Exalted is He—such people are not excused on the basis of corrupt interpretation, for this is utter ignorance."
These are some of the reprehensible acts of the Kharijites, of which we find not even a little among the Asharis, despite the sound hadiths that indicate that these people are unbelievers.
