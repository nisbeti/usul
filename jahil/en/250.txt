The question now is: if, by his own words, the early generations differed over declaring the Kharijites disbelievers, is this not clear evidence that they differed over the issue of declaring someone who interprets texts figuratively to be a disbeliever? Why, then, did you not declare those who did not declare the Kharijites disbelievers to be disbelievers, while you declared those who did not declare the Ash'aris disbelievers? The underlying cause in both cases is the same: declaring someone a disbeliever on the basis of figurative interpretation. If you excused those who excused the Kharijites, despite the existence of explicit hadiths declaring them disbelievers—hadiths of which there are none like them concerning the Ash'aris—and if we consider these hadiths and descriptions, we find that they contain rulings: that they are "the worst of creation," and "wherever you encounter them, kill them." Abu Umama, one of the Companions who narrated the hadith, said that they "disbelieved after their faith." These are three descriptions pertaining to this world; in the Hereafter, they are "the dogs of Hell." And he (peace and blessings be upon him) said, "They leave the religion and then never return to it." All these descriptions strongly indicate that these people are disbelievers who have left the religion. It is astonishing, then, to excuse those who did not declare these people disbelievers, despite the clarity of the texts concerning them, and then hasten to declare the Ash'aris disbelievers, those who do not declare the Ash'aris disbelievers, or those who do not declare an ignorant person who lacks knowledge of the attribute to be a disbeliever.... Moreover, among the condemnations of these Kharijites is what al-Subki says, as quoted in Fath al-Bari: "Those who declared the Kharijites and the extremist Rafidis disbelievers argued that they declared leading Companions to be disbelievers, which entails rejecting the Prophet's testimony that they would enter Paradise. He said: 'In my view, this is a sound argument.' He said: 'Those who did not declare them disbelievers argued that ruling them to be disbelievers would require that they had prior, certain knowledge of the aforementioned testimony.' This argument is open to question, for we know with certainty that those whom they declared disbelievers were upright until the time of their death.
