And he was certain of what it meant, while it did not attain, in his view, the strength of the other text; thus, it was not even speculative in his view, let alone definitive...

The point is that the imams' positions distinguish between a category and an individual. Whoever reports a disagreement has not grasped the full import of their words...

One group reports two narrations from Ahmad concerning the unrestricted excommunication of the people of innovation. But this was not the position of Ahmad or any of the other imams. Likewise, al-Shafi'i's excommunication of Hafs al-Fard when he said, "The Quran is created." Al-Shafi'i said to him, "You have disbelieved"—that is, your statement is disbelief. For this reason, he did not seek to have him killed; had he considered him a disbeliever, he would have sought to have him killed."

It is stated in Mukhtasar al-Sawa'iq al-Mursala ala al-Jahmiyya wa al-Mu'attila by Ibn al-Qayyim (p. 594):
"The aim is not to examine this issue exhaustively. Rather, the point is that error occurs in what they have called subsidiary matters, just as it occurs in what they have made foundational matters. So we ask them to provide a sound distinction between what may be established in religion by a solitary report and what may not. They will find no way to make such a distinction except through false claims. Then we ask them to distinguish between matters of fundamentals and matters of branches, and to state the criterion for doing so. Then we ask them to distinguish between what its denier incurs sin for—whether the sin of disbelief or of disobedience—and what its denier does not incur sin for. And we ask them to distinguish between what requires definitive certainty and what may be based on probability. They have absolutely no way to establish any of this.
