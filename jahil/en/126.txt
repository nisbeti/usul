(Why is an excuse granted on the basis of interpretation?) An excuse is granted on the basis of interpretation because the person interpreting has exerted his utmost effort to learn Allah's ruling. He is not like one who turns away. Thus, it is valid to say that the person interpreting is mistaken, and the mistaken person is excused. He may not, however, be excused from punishment; rather, he may be excused from being declared an unbeliever alone.
Ibn Taymiyya said: "What I have mentioned concerning a Muslim's neglecting an obligation or committing a prohibition on the basis of an interpretive judgment or imitation is clear to me, and his situation in this regard is better than that of an unbeliever acting on an interpretation. This does not prevent me from fighting a rebellious interpreter, flogging an interpreter who drinks wine, and the like. For interpretation does not remove worldly punishment absolutely, since the purpose of punishment is to prevent the harm caused by transgression." (Majmu al-Fatawa, 22/14)
He also said: "Among these people there is no one who is from the early generations of the community or its leading scholars; rather, they are Muslims who have been criticized for innovation, even if they hold it on the basis of interpretation, and whose error is forgiven." (Bayan Talbis al-Jahmiyya, 2/358) He also said: "It may be that both parties to a dispute are innovators, and that both are ignorant and acting on an interpretation." (Majmu al-Fatawa, 23/356)
Error in interpretation: Interpretation is placing a legal proof in a context other than its proper one, on the basis of independent reasoning or a doubt arising from failure to understand the implication of the text. Thus, a legally responsible person proceeds to commit an act of unbelief while not considering it unbelief, citing as proof a text whose meaning he has misunderstood. This error means that the condition of deliberate intent is absent, and error in interpretation prevents him from being declared an unbeliever. But if the proof is established against him and his error is made clear to him, and he persists in his act, he then becomes an unbeliever.
