Al-Hafiz Ibn Abd al-Barr said in al-Tamhid (18/46): In the story of one who poisoned himself
This man's ignorance of one of Allah's attributes concerning His knowledge and decree does not take him out of faith. Do you not see that Umar ibn al-Khattab, Imran ibn Husayn, and a group of the Companions asked the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) about divine decree? It is well known that they asked him about it while ignorant of it, and no Muslim could hold that by asking about it they became unbelievers, or that at the time they asked they were not believers.... He went on to say: These Companions of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him)—the learned and virtuous among them—asked about divine decree as learners who were ignorant of it, not as obstinate opponents. The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) taught them what they did not know about it, and their ignorance of it before they learned did them no harm. Had it been impermissible for them to be ignorant of it at any time, he would have taught it to them along with their profession of faith, required it of them when they entered Islam, and made it a sixth pillar of Islam. So reflect, and seek Allah's help.
What is apparent from Ibn Abd al-Barr's words here is what we established earlier: people are only required to have general faith, without affirming or denying anything in religion except on the basis of proof. The example Ibn Abd al-Barr mentioned of some of the Companions being unaware of the subject of predestination is like it.
A hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari (4/105):
Then some people from Yemen came in to see him, and he said, "Accept the good news, people of Yemen, since Banu Tamim did not accept it." They said, "We have accepted it, Messenger of Allah." They said, "We have come to ask you about this matter." He said, "Allah was, and there was nothing besides Him. His Throne was upon the water, and He recorded everything in the Reminder, and He created the heavens and the earth."
He did not declare them unbelievers for their ignorance of what was mentioned.

Likewise, Aisha's belief that Allah will call the righteous believers to account on the Day of Resurrection is an affirmation of an act of Allah based on ignorance and interpretation, and it is a matter of belief.
