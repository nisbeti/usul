It is a matter of the Sharia. Whoever claims on God's authority that He loves a particular deed without Sharia evidence has prescribed as part of the religion what God has not authorized, just as if he were to establish an obligation or a prohibition... What they mean by this is that the deed must be one that has been established, by a text or consensus, as something God loves or dislikes, such as reciting the Quran, glorifying God, supplication, charity, freeing slaves, treating people well, and disliking lies, treachery, and the like.

If a hadith is narrated concerning the merit and reward of some recommended deeds, or the disapproval and punishment of some deeds, then, when a hadith is narrated about the amounts and kinds of reward and punishment and we do not know it to be fabricated, it is permissible to narrate it and act upon it. This means that a person may hope for that reward or fear that punishment. It is like someone who knows that trade is profitable but has heard that it yields a great profit: if that is true, it benefits him, and if it is false, it does him no harm. An example of this is exhortation and warning through Israelite reports, dreams, sayings of the early generations and scholars, accounts of scholars, and the like. Such things, on their own, cannot establish a Sharia ruling, whether recommendation or anything else, but they may be mentioned for exhortation and warning, hope and fear.

If weak hadiths about merits specify and delimit something—for example, a prayer at a particular time, with a particular recitation, or in a particular manner—then this is not permissible, because the recommendation of that specific form has not been established by Sharia evidence. This differs from a report that whoever enters the marketplace and says, "There is no god but Allah..." will receive such-and-such, since remembering God in the marketplace is recommended because it involves remembering God among the heedless...

As for the specified reward reported in it, it does no harm whether or not it is established.

The upshot is that this material may be narrated and acted upon for exhortation and warning, not to establish recommendation. Belief in what it entails—that is, the amounts of reward and punishment—depends on Sharia evidence. ))End quote
