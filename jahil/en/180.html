Third: They say that solitary reports (akhbar al-ahad) establish only practice, but there are hadiths from which practice can be derived only by establishing knowledge. An example is: "Allah has cursed the woman who tattoos and the woman who has herself tattooed, the woman who joins hair extensions and the woman who has her teeth filed for beauty." The cursing of one who alters the landmarks of the land

"Whoever plays with dice has disobeyed Allah." "He cursed men who imitate women, and women who imitate men."

And many other hadiths containing the words "will not enter Paradise," such as "A talebearer will not enter Paradise." Likewise, some recommended acts, as in: "I guarantee a house in the middle of Paradise."

And likewise, "There is no marriage without a guardian,"

"There is no prayer without the Mother of the Quran,"

and many other examples. The obligation, prohibition, or recommendation is not derived from the words "Do" or "Do not," or "You should do," but rather from reports conveying knowledge that one will enter Paradise or Hell, or not enter them, that the person who does it is cursed, or that the act is invalid. All of these are matters of knowledge that must first be established; only then can a practical ruling be derived from them, namely, that the act is prohibited, obligatory, or recommended. This is so only after believing in the reward and punishment stated in the hadith. If it does not establish a belief, then consequently it does not establish a practical ruling.
