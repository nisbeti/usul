Third: It has not been transmitted from any of the early generations or the transmitters of hadith who reported this hadith that they declared Ibn Abbas, who excused them, to be an unbeliever. This clearly indicates that, in the view of the majority of them, one who excuses someone acting on a mistaken interpretation is not an unbeliever.

Fourth: This shows that Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him) did not consider a statement's necessary implication to entail unbelief unless the speaker affirmed that implication. He said to them: "As for your claim that he fought but neither took captives nor seized spoils: would you take your mother Aisha captive, deeming lawful with her what you deem lawful with other women, when she is your mother? If you say, 'We deem lawful with her what we deem lawful with other women,' then you have disbelieved. And if you say, 'She is not our mother,' then you have disbelieved, for Allah, Exalted is He, says: {The Prophet has a greater claim on the believers than they have on themselves, and his wives are their mothers.} (3) So you are caught between two errors; find a way out of them."

Qadi Iyad said:

Section: Establishing the correct position on declaring those who act on mistaken interpretations to be unbelievers. We have already mentioned the positions of the early generations on declaring innovators and followers of whims to be unbelievers when they act on mistaken interpretations and make a statement whose implications lead to unbelief, even though, when confronted with those implications, they do not affirm what their statement entails. The jurists and theologians differed on this matter just as they differed on the question itself. Some endorsed the declaration of unbelief made by the majority of the early generations, while others rejected it and did not consider them outside the community of believers. This was the position of most jurists and theologians. They said that such people are disobedient, sinful, and astray, but that we inherit from them as Muslims and apply to them the rulings applicable to Muslims. For this reason, Sahnun said that one who prays behind them need not repeat the prayer. He said this was the position of all of Malik's companions: al-Mughira.
