It is stated in

((Al-Urf al-Shadhi, Commentary on Sunan al-Tirmidhi (1/81)

They differed over declaring the Rafidah unbelievers. The Hanafis have two opinions: it was said that they are unbelievers, and it was said that they are not. The preferred view is that they are unbelievers, for whoever declares the majority of the Companions unbelievers is an unbeliever, and the Rafidah restrict Islam to nine or seven Companions.))

It is stated in

((Sharh al-Tahawiyya - Yusuf al-Ghufays (19/18, numbered automatically by al-Maktaba al-Shamela)

On the disagreement over declaring one who abandons fasting and pilgrimage an unbeliever

As for fasting and pilgrimage, a group of the early generations held that abandoning fasting is unbelief, and a group of the early generations held that abandoning pilgrimage is unbelief. The vast majority of the early and later generations held that abandoning fasting alone or abandoning pilgrimage alone is not unbelief.))

((Ibn Hubayra said: "They differed concerning one who learns magic and practices it. Abu Hanifa, Malik, and Ahmad said that he thereby becomes an unbeliever. However, some of Abu Hanifa's followers made a distinction: if he learns it in order to guard against it or avoid it, he does not thereby become an unbeliever; but if he learns it believing it to be permissible, or believing that it will benefit him, then he becomes an unbeliever. He did not consider the ruling to be absolute. If he believes that the devils do whatever they wish, then he is an unbeliever. Al-Shafi'i said: 'If someone learns magic, we say to him: Describe your magic.
