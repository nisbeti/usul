"...enemies, then He brought your hearts together, and by His blessing you became brothers" [Al Imran: 103].

But when you look at the people of innovation, you see them divided and at odds, factions and parties. You can hardly find two of them who share the same creed. They declare one another innovators; indeed, they go so far as to declare one another unbelievers: a son declares his father an unbeliever, a brother his brother, and a neighbor his neighbor. You see them perpetually in dispute, mutual hatred, and disagreement; their lives come to an end without their views ever being united. "You think them united, but their hearts are divided. That is because they are a people who do not reason" [Al-Hashr: 14]. Have you not heard that, despite the Mu'tazilites' agreement in this regard, the Baghdadis among them declare the Basrans unbelievers, and the Basrans declare the Baghdadis unbelievers? The followers of Abu Ali al-Jubba'i and his son declare Abu Hashim and his followers unbelievers, while the followers of Abu Hashim declare Abu Ali and his followers unbelievers. The same is true of the rest of their leading figures and the authors of their doctrinal treatises: when you consider their views, you see them divided, each declaring the others unbelievers. The same is true of the Kharijites and the Rafidites among themselves, and likewise all the other innovators. Is anything a clearer sign of falsehood than this? Allah, Exalted is He, said: "Those who divided their religion and became factions—you have nothing to do with them. Their affair rests with Allah" [Al-An'am: 159]. Thus Allah absolved His Messenger of those who are characterized by such division and disagreement.

(He said): The reason the people of hadith were united was that they took their religion from the Book and the Sunnah, and through transmission; this brought them unity and harmony. The people of innovation took their religion from their own reason, and this brought them division and disagreement.
