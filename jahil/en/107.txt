Consider how al-Tabari transmits al-Shafi'i's creed in full and attributes it to himself. However, may Allah have mercy on him, he does not excuse ignorance regarding the attributes that, in his view, are known through reason or innate disposition; these are the ones he mentioned at the beginning of his discussion. One observation about this group, namely those who declare the one who excuses a person acting on an erroneous interpretation to be an unbeliever, is that they often cite the words of al-Tabari, one of their leading authorities on this subject, because he does not excuse ignorance of certain attributes which, in his view, are established by innate disposition. Yet here, when al-Tabari disagrees with them by not declaring one who denies the attributes of hearing and sight an unbeliever—since, in his view, these are not established by reason or innate disposition, but by transmitted revelation—they do not declare him an unbeliever. Here, he excuses the person acting on an erroneous interpretation and the one ignorant of these attributes, even though, according to them, ignorance of them is unbelief, and one who excuses the ignorant person is an unbeliever. Yet they do not declare al-Tabari an unbeliever in this instance. This is a clear contradiction."
