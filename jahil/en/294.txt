He said regarding defending oneself in courts that rule by other than what Allah has revealed: "Among the things that may properly be cited as evidence in this context is the account of Ja'far and his fellow emigrants to Abyssinia appearing before the Negus before he embraced Islam, and defending themselves as best they could when the two envoys of Quraysh came seeking them. None of them refused to appear or felt uneasy about doing so, and the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) did not condemn it. Al-Hafiz Ibn Kathir related the account in al-Bidaya wa al-Nihaya, on the authority of Ibn Ishaq; Abu Nu'aym in al-Dala'il; al-Bayhaqi in al-Dala'il as well; and others, with chains of transmission, some of which he deemed good, and others he authenticated and strengthened. See it from (3/69) onward. It includes the account of the Negus granting them protection and supporting them, and refusing to hand them over to Quraysh...
Also suitable in this regard is citing the words of Joseph (peace be upon him), when the wife of al-Aziz accused him, saying: 'What is the recompense for one who intends evil against your family but that he be imprisoned or suffer a painful punishment?'
'He said, "It was she who sought to seduce me." And a witness from her family testified ...' [the verses]. This includes his defending himself among an unbelieving people, and some of them testifying, defending him, and declaring him innocent.
Even clearer than this is what he did in prison, when 'he said to the one of the two whom he thought would be saved, "Mention me to your master ..."' The disbelief of the king of Egypt at that time did not prevent him from doing so.
