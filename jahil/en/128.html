(The one who interprets) Qadi Iyad said: "As for one who denies an essential attribute of Allah, Exalted is He,

Or denies them knowingly, saying, for example, "He is neither knowing, powerful, willing, nor speaking," or the like, concerning attributes of perfection that are necessarily His, Exalted is He. Our imams have explicitly stated that there is consensus that whoever denies these attributes of Him, Exalted is He, and strips Him of them is an unbeliever. It is in this sense that the statement of Sahnun is understood: "Whoever says that Allah has no speech is an unbeliever." He does not declare those who interpret the texts to be unbelievers, as we have explained above. As for someone who is ignorant of one of these attributes, the scholars differed over this: some declared him an unbeliever, and this was reported from Abu Ja'far al-Tabari and others. Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari held this view at one point. Another group held that this does not remove him from the designation of faith, and al-Ash'ari later returned to this position. He said: "That is because he did not hold this belief with the conviction that it was correct, nor regard it as a religion and a law. Rather, only someone who believes his statement to be true is an unbeliever." They cited as evidence the hadith of the black woman, in which the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) only…

He asked them for nothing but affirmation of the oneness of God, and by the hadith about the man who said, "If Allah has power over me"—and in one narration, "If Allah should cause me to go astray"—then he said, "So Allah forgave him." They said: If most people were examined about the divine attributes and these were disclosed to them, only a few would be found who knew them. The other scholar answered this hadith in several ways, including that "has power" means "decrees," and that his doubt was not about Allah's power to bring him back to life, but about the resurrection itself, which can only be known through revelation; perhaps he...

There had been no revealed ruling concerning it that would decisively settle the matter, such that doubt about it would then constitute disbelief. As for what no revealed ruling addresses, it is among the things deemed possible by reason. Or "decreed" may mean "constrained," and what he did to himself was an act of self-reproach and anger at his own disobedience. It was also said: "He said only what"
