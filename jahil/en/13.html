"...and forgetfulness and what they were compelled to do." They said that earlier nations were held accountable for their mistakes and called to account for all their actions, with neither ignorance nor forgetfulness serving as an excuse for them or a reason to pardon them. But these burdens have been lifted from this nation in response to their supplication and as a mercy from Allah to them, as Allah, Exalted is He, made clear in His words: {Our Lord, do not take us to task if we forget or make a mistake. Our Lord, do not place upon us a burden as You placed upon those before us. Our Lord, do not burden us with what we have no strength to bear} (al-Baqara: 286), and His words: {There is no blame upon you for what you do by mistake, but only for what your hearts intend. And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful} (al-Ahzab: 5). It is thus clear that there is no problem in distinguishing between an original disbeliever and a Muslim who has committed a form of shirk. However, the first group, who do not excuse shirk or any of its forms, responded to this group on this particular point by saying that as soon as a Muslim engages in a form of shirk, he is a polytheist at the moment he engages in it, and is called a polytheist for the reasons already mentioned. Consequently, none of the preceding verses or hadith applies to him. Rather, the preceding verses and hadith apply to someone who has committed something short of shirk, or something obscure among matters of disbelief; yet they did not set a limit for this obscure disbelief. Therefore, those who excuse people on grounds of ignorance have held that someone who commits a form of shirk or disbelief on the basis of an interpretation is not thereby deemed a disbeliever, because the forms of shirk are not established by reason: sometimes prostrating to someone other than Allah is shirk, and sometimes it is merely a greeting.
