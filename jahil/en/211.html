Every interpreter of a verse who attributes to Allah an active attribute—that He intends such and such by this verse—and anyone who rejects this interpretation and adopts another is denying this attribute of Allah while affirming a different one for Him. One of the two must be mistaken, if the two interpretations cannot be reconciled. According to this view, those who declare interpretation to be disbelief would have to declare most interpreters disbelievers. And Allah knows best. If it is said, "Why did you not cite a practical ruling concerning fasting in the matter of being excused on account of interpretation?" we say what we said before: affirming a practical ruling, according to your classification, entails several matters of belief. These are that Allah intended this ruling and spoke it with His saying, "Be," which affirms a specific active attribute; that Allah then recorded it in the Preserved Tablet; that Allah then sent Gabriel with it to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him); and that Allah will punish or reward whoever does this thing. All of these are matters of belief from which they cannot extricate themselves, and they apply to every issue relating to actions that we will cite as evidence.

– Among the proofs is the report of Ammar: "The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) sent me on an errand, and I became junub but could not find water. So I rolled in the dust as an animal rolls, then I came to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) and told him about it. He said, 'It would have sufficed for you to do this with your hands.' Then he struck the ground once with his hands, wiped his left hand over his right, and wiped the backs of his hands and his face." And in it is the Messenger's (peace and blessings be upon him) excusing Ammar in his interpretation of the verse on tayammum. There is no doubt that this interpretation attributes this ruling to Allah in his interpretation of the verse on tayammum.
