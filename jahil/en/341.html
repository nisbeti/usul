1 Musnad al-Bazzar = al-Bahr al-Zakhkhar (1/309)

Umar said: "So I drew my sword and said, 'Messenger of Allah, let me deal with Hatib, for he has disbelieved; let me strike off his head.' The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: 'Ibn al-Khattab, how do you know? Perhaps Allah looked upon this company of the people of Badr and said, "Do as you wish, for I have forgiven you."' This hadith concerning the story of Hatib has been narrated from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) through more than one chain of transmission" [p. 310]. The hadith has been authenticated by scholars.

In this hadith, Umar declared Hatib a disbeliever, and the Messenger of Allah excused him; he did not say that he was one of the Kharijites.

2 It is stated in Sahih al-Bukhari (3/175):

The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) stood up that day and sought redress concerning Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul. The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Who will excuse me concerning a man whose harm to my family has been reported to me? By Allah, I know nothing but good of my family, and they have mentioned a man of whom I know nothing but good; he never entered my home except in my company." Sa'd ibn Mu'adh stood up and said, "Messenger of Allah, by Allah, I will deal with him for you. If he is from the Aws, we will strike off his head; and if he is from our brothers, the Khazraj, command us, and we will carry out your order concerning him." Sa'd ibn Ubadah then stood up—he was the chief of the Khazraj, and had been a righteous man before that, but zeal overcame him—and said, "By the life of Allah, you lie! You will not kill him, nor are you able to do so." Usayd ibn Hudayr stood up and said, "By the life of Allah, you lie! By Allah, we will surely kill him, for you are a hypocrite arguing on behalf of the hypocrites." The two clans, the Aws and the Khazraj, erupted into dispute until they were on the verge of fighting, while the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) was on the pulpit. He came down and calmed them until they fell silent, and he too fell silent.
