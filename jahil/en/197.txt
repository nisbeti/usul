(Very, very important note)
Some have cited the preceding proofs to argue that proof is not established against anyone until knowledge reaches him, even if he says what he says, believes what he believes, or is negligent in seeking knowledge, so long as the proof has not reached him. I consider this view mistaken for several reasons. First, a person who is negligent in seeking knowledge is not called one who has erred in the sense required for the preceding verses and hadiths to apply to him. If someone were to blindfold himself and drive his car at top speed, killing whoever he killed, would he be called one who had made a mistake? Likewise, such a person is turning away from seeking the truth; he is not even called a mujtahid, so as to fall under the hadith, "When the judge exercises independent judgment..." Moreover, after his general belief in Allah and His Messenger, an ignorant person can only be one of two types. The first is a man who is asked about a religious matter and says, "I do not know. I believe in everything the Messenger brought, may Allah bless him and grant him peace." Such a person is excused, because he has neither fabricated a lie against Allah, nor committed shirk, nor attributed to Allah something He has not affirmed of Himself, nor denied of Him something He has affirmed of Himself. Accordingly, you will find that most of the hadiths cited in this matter are understood in this way.
