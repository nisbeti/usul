If they say, "Rather, the virtue of what the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) brought as religion remains protected from error until the Day of Resurrection," then they have arrived at the truth, which is our position, and praise belongs to Allah, Exalted is He. If they say, "The attribute of every reporter and his nature are such that his report may be true or false, or mistaken; and your claim that the report of a single upright narrator in the Sharia necessitates knowledge is contrary to the nature of reports and reporters, and violates the attributes and customary patterns of all of them," we say to them: It is not to be denied that Allah, Exalted is He, may alter whatever natural properties He wills, once proof establishes that He has done so. What is astonishing is that you deny this while holding precisely the same position when you affirm that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) was protected from lying and error in conveying the Sharia. This is exactly what you have denied. Indeed, you were not content with contradicting yourselves by affirming it where you were right and denying it in the case of the report of a single upright narrator; you went further and asserted sheer falsehood when you allowed all communities to agree upon error in their consensus of opinion. That is the nature and attribute of all of them. Yet you denied the possibility of error and delusion in what you claim to be the consensus of the Muslim community alone in its reasoning by analogy. Far be it from Allah that the community should agree upon falsehood, while analogy is itself falsehood. Thus you violated customary patterns and declared natural properties impossible without proof—especially if the one opposing us is among the Murji'a, who categorically assert that it is impossible for a Jew or Christian to know in his heart that Allah, Exalted is He, is true.
