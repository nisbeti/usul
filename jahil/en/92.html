It is stated in I'tiqad Ahl al-Sunna, by al-Lalaka'i (1/176):

Muhammad ibn al-Muzaffar al-Muqri(28) informed us; he said: al-Husayn ibn Muhammad ibn Habash al-Muqri(29) narrated to us; he said: Abu Muhammad Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Hatim(30) narrated to us; he said:

I asked my father(31) and Abu Zur'a(32) about the doctrines of Ahl al-Sunna concerning the fundamentals of religion, what they had found the scholars in all regions adhering to, and what they themselves believed regarding these matters. They said: We found the scholars in all regions—Hijaz, Iraq, Syria, and Yemen—adhering to these doctrines: ... ... Whoever claims that the Quran is created is an unbeliever in Allah the Almighty, an unbelief that takes him out of the religion; and whoever doubts the unbelief of such a person, if he understands, is an unbeliever.(33) (There are two truthful narrators in its chain, and the rest of its chain consists of trustworthy narrators. Note here that they qualified this by saying, "if he understands.")

(28) Muhammad ibn al-Muzaffar ibn Ali ibn Harb, Abu Bakr al-Muqri al-Dinawari. Al-Khatib said: "We wrote down narrations from him. He was a righteous, virtuous, and truthful shaykh." Tarikh Baghdad (4/30)

(29) Al-Husayn ibn Muhammad ibn Habash, Abu Ali al-Dinawari al-Muqri. He was advanced in the science of Quranic recitation, renowned for precision, trustworthy, and reliable. Tarikh al-Islam (26/536)

(30) Abd al-Rahman ibn Muhammad ibn Idris ibn al-Mundhir ibn Dawud ibn Mihran. Al-Khalili said: "A group praised him for his asceticism, complete scrupulousness, and knowledge and practice." Al-Wafayat (18/135-136)

(31) Ubayd Allah ibn Abd al-Karim al-Qurashi, the freedman of al-Razi, the hadith master and one of the leading imams. Abu Hatim said: "He left no one like him after him in knowledge, jurisprudence, integrity, and truthfulness; this is beyond doubt." Ishaq ibn Rahawayh said: "Any hadith that Abu Zur'a does not know by heart has no basis." Tahdhib al-Kamal (19/89)

(32) Muhammad ibn Idris ibn al-Mundhir, Imam Muhammad ibn Idris al-Hanzali. He excelled in memorization and traveled widely in pursuit of the repositories of knowledge. He died in the year 277, may Allah have mercy on him. Tahdhib al-Kamal (24/381)

(33) Its chain of transmission is sound back to the two of them, and this creed is established as that of Ibn Abi Hatim.
