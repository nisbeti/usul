The Messenger is the Lawgiver himself, and forms of shirk are not a matter of reason that can be identified by reason in many cases. Prostration was not a form of shirk in the law of those before us, but it became so. The upshot is that they sought legislation of a ruling; they did not fabricate a lie against Allah by adopting this tree, nor did they attribute that legislation to Allah, so no shirk occurred on their part at all.

Likewise, the hadith about the man who reduced himself to ashes: he did not say that Allah was unable to resurrect him. Rather, he said, "If He should decree," and in another narration, "Perhaps I will elude Allah." In that narration is Hammad ibn Salama, who became confused in his later years. Both narrations indicate that the man was ignorant of this divine attribute; neither contains any indication that he affirmed its opposite or fabricated a lie about it. Likewise, the words of Aisha, "Whatever people conceal, Allah knows it"; the words of Imran ibn Husayn, "Will the people of Paradise know the people of Hell?"; and Aisha's words, "Does Allah not say, 'Then he will be reckoned with an easy reckoning'?"—and other examples that will come, if Allah wills—show only that they had general faith but were ignorant of particular matters, so they asked about them. They did not fabricate lies against Allah or attribute to Him what He had not attributed to Himself, except insofar as they inferred its affirmation from other evidence or presumed the original permissibility. For example, in the story of Muadh, prostrating in greeting kings and leaders was well known, just as it had been prescribed in the law of those before us. Muadh presumed this original rule applied to customary matters of social dealings, and acted accordingly with the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him). Thus, he had grounds for interpretation here, which excused him.
