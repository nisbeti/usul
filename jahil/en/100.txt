Likewise, Muadh was unaware of one of Allah's acts: that Allah holds us accountable for what we say. This is a matter of belief.

I consider this hadith one of the strongest proofs that a person who suspends judgment on whether someone who interprets matters incorrectly is to be declared an unbeliever should not himself be declared an unbeliever. Muadh (may Allah be pleased with him) did not know whether Allah would hold people accountable for what they said or not. There is no doubt that what they say includes words of unbelief, which are spoken by the tongue. Yet, despite Muadh's ignorance of this matter, the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) excused him and did not declare him an unbeliever. He did no more than explain it to him. The hadith relates Muadh's words:

"He said, 'Are we held accountable for what our tongues say?' The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) struck Muadh on the thigh and then said, 'O Muadh, may your mother be bereaved of you'—or whatever else Allah willed him to say to him—'and what is it that casts people on their faces into Hell except what their tongues have uttered?'" I do not know what difference there is between being ignorant of one of Allah's attributes and being ignorant of one of Allah's acts. Whoever knows the difference, let him assist us by providing clear evidence from the Book and the Sunnah; otherwise, there is no difference between one who is ignorant of this and one who is ignorant of that.
