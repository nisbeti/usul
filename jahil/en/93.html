And Ibn al-Qayyim said: "There can be no establishment of religion without the messengers. If reason has discerned the goodness of some actions and the badness of others, how can it know Allah, Exalted is He, through His names and attributes, or the signs by which He made Himself known to His servants through the tongues of His messengers? How can it know the details of His law and religion, which He prescribed for His servants? How can it know the particular things that He loves, is pleased with, is angry with, and dislikes? How can it know the details of His reward and punishment, what He has prepared for His allies and what He has prepared for His enemies, or the measures, nature, and degrees of reward and punishment? How can it know the unseen, which Allah has not disclosed to any of His creation except those of His messengers whom He has chosen? And how can it know all the other matters brought by the messengers and conveyed from Allah, which reason has no means of knowing? How, then, can reason's knowledge of the goodness and badness of some actions suffice in place of what the messengers brought? It is thus clear that what you have said is mere intimidation, filled with falsehoods. Praise be to Allah." ( )

(According to the creed of Ahl al-Sunna, al-Lalaka'i, 2/193) The chapter on the oneness of Allah, Almighty, and His attributes and names, and that He is powerful, hearing, all-knowing, seeing, wise, everlasting, and speaking. The context of what is indicated by the Book of Allah, Almighty, and what has been narrated from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), namely, that knowledge of Allah, Almighty, and His attributes is obligatory, and that this knowledge is acquired through transmitted evidence (the proofs from the Quran and Sunna), not through reason ... indicating that knowledge of Allah and the messengers is acquired through transmitted evidence (the proofs from the Quran and Sunna), as Allah, Almighty, has stated. This is the creed of Ahl al-Sunna wa al-Jama'a.
