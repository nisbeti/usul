"...among them an innovation. If he is apprehended, is he to be killed after being asked to repent, or not killed, but rather efforts made to refute his innovation? There is disagreement on this, corresponding to the disagreement over whether they are to be declared unbelievers." End quote.
From the foregoing, the following becomes clear: Some scholars held that they were unbelievers, for the reasons mentioned above, while most held that they were not to be declared unbelievers, for several reasons. First, they said that they were acting on an interpretation. Yet the hadiths do not mention that they were acting on an interpretation; rather, what is apparent is that these particular people, unlike others, were not acting on an interpretation. But how do we know that they were not? We say: We know this from the Messenger of Allah's ruling that they had left the religion, that they were the worst of creation, and that they must be killed—and nothing else—while also ruling that there is no blame on one who errs unintentionally. If it is said, "If others fall into some of the same things as these people, do you declare them unbelievers without qualification?" The answer is no, because we do not know from the report of the infallible that these people were unbelievers, as we know from the report of the infallible concerning the particular people mentioned, who were described precisely—unless they disbelieve in something necessarily known to be part of the religion and their interpretation is untenable. Ibn Hazm (may Allah have mercy on him) held this view, saying that these hadiths were revealed concerning a particular group, namely the Azariqa. It says in Lawami al-Anwar al-Bahiyya (1/87):
The Azariqa were the followers of Nafi ibn Abd Allah al-Azraq, the accursed Kharijite. A group from Basra, al-Ahwaz, and other towns in Persia and elsewhere went out with him. Their power grew, and they took control of the provinces. He had views and doctrines that they adopted along with him. Among these was his declaring Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) an unbeliever because of the arbitration. He claimed that the words of the Exalted, "And among the people is one whose speech pleases you" [al-Baqara: 204], and the rest of the verse, were revealed concerning him. He also claimed that the words "And among the people is one who sells himself, seeking Allah's pleasure" were revealed concerning Abd al-Rahman ibn Muljam (may Allah curse him).
