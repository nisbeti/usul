This statement is evidence against them, since they declare anyone who excuses another to be a disbeliever without qualification, whereas here he has stipulated a condition: "that the one excusing him understand." If that is so, their argument turns against them, for if this was the consensus of the early generations, then there is consensus here that the one who excuses another is not declared a disbeliever until he understands the proof, as is apparent from al-Lalaka'i's words.
Second: Al-Lalaka'i's statement: "Whoever suspends judgment concerning the Quran out of ignorance is to be taught and declared an innovator, but not declared an unbeliever." This demolishes their claim that ignorance regarding the attribute is no excuse. And if this is a consensus, then the early generations also reached consensus on it, so Al-Lalaka'i's statement applies to them here as well.
Third: They say that one who is ignorant of an attribute established by innate disposition or reason is an unbeliever and is not excused, and must affirm those attributes that reason or innate disposition affirms, even if no revealed text has reached him. But what is al-Lalaka'i's position, which he attributes here to Ahl al-Sunna wa al-Jama'a? Al-Lalaka'i's position, which he attributes to Ahl al-Sunna wa al-Jama'a, is that belief in Allah's attributes is established only through revealed texts, and is obligatory through revealed texts, not through reason. It says in I'tiqad Ahl al-Sunna, by al-Lalaka'i (2/193):
Chapter on the Unity of Allah, Mighty and Majestic, and His Attributes and Names,
and that He is Living, Powerful, Knowing, Hearing, Seeing, Speaking, Willing, Everlasting
An account of what is indicated by the Book of Allah, Mighty and Majestic, and what has been narrated from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), showing that
The obligation to know Allah, Exalted is He, and His attributes through revelation, not reason. Allah, Exalted is He, says, addressing His Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) in wording that is specific while the intended meaning is general: "So know that there is no god but Allah."
