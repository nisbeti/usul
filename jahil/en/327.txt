And if we wish to return to refining the effective cause (tanqih al-manat), we find that there are incidental attributes whose possessor is not judged sinful or declared an unbeliever. Examples include shaving the head, reciting the Quran, being fierce, and other such attributes that are not sins. Mentioning such attributes and dwelling on them extensively is evidence that the punishments mentioned apply to specific people. This is like the descriptions of the Messiah as one-eyed, with curly hair, and so on: these are identifying signs by which a particular person is specified, not grounds for imposing the ruling on everyone who happens to possess these attributes.
Then we find attributes that are sins, whether or not they reach the level of unbelief. The first is Dhu al-Khuwaysira's statement about the distribution, "It was not just." And his statement, "He will have followers," after which the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) described their attributes. What is apparent here is that these people will be followers and supporters of this man, and that they will share his position of diminishing the Prophet's standing (peace and blessings be upon him), since that is the only thing the man had manifested. This is the first attribute, and it is an attribute that constitutes unbelief, without doubt. The second is: "They delve deeply into religion until they leave it," and, "They leave the religion and do not return to it until the arrow returns to its bow." These two descriptions indicate that these people committed unbelief and departed from the religion. This is an effective cause that permits the Prophet's statement, "Wherever you encounter them, kill them," for leaving the religion entails being killed. The third is: "They call to the Book of Allah, yet have nothing to do with it." This also indicates that these people are unbelievers, since having nothing to do with it necessarily includes having no faith in it. They are therefore unbelievers. In Iqtida al-Sirat al-Mustaqim li-Mukhalafat Ashab al-Jahim (1/176), it says:
Allah, Exalted is He, said to His Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): {You have nothing to do with them} [al-An'am: 159]. This entails that he disavows them in every respect.
