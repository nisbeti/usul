The proof. But how is the proof established? By induction from the verses and the hadiths of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), we can derive some indications of how to establish the proof and how to know whether this man who is interpreting the texts is following his desires, or whether he seeks the truth and prefers Allah and His Messenger over all others, but has fallen into a kind of misinterpretation that led him to err on the issue at hand. The researcher will, Allah willing, present some of this evidence and comment on it.
{Say, "Then bring a Book from Allah that is a better guide than these two, and I will follow it, if you are truthful." (49) But if they do not respond to you, then know that they are only following their own desires. And who is more astray than one who follows his own desire without guidance from Allah? Indeed, Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people. (50)}
Here Allah Most High specified for the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) how to distinguish one who follows his desires from one who gives precedence to the truth: by calling that person to present the opposing evidence. If you bring this person sound evidence from the Book or the Sunnah, then one of two things is incumbent upon him: either to follow it or to produce counterevidence. If he does neither, we then know that he follows his desires, and we can say with a clear conscience that proof has been established against this person.
