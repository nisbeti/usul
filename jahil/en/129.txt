He said this while neither understanding his own words nor controlling his speech, overcome by such anguish and fear that they had taken away his reason; therefore, he was not held accountable for it. It was also said that this occurred during the interval between prophets, when mere affirmation of divine oneness was sufficient. And it was said, rather, that this is a figure of speech in Arabic whose outward form suggests doubt, while its meaning is certainty; this is called feigning ignorance, and examples of it occur in their speech, such as the words of the Exalted: {Perhaps he will take heed or fear}, and His words: {And indeed, either we or you are surely upon guidance or in clear error}. As for those who affirm the description but deny the attribute, saying, "I say that He is Knowing, but He has no knowledge; and Speaking, but He has no speech," and so on with the other attributes, following the doctrine of the Mu'tazilites, whoever judges them by the implication of what their statement leads to, and where their doctrine takes them, declares them unbelievers. For if they deny knowledge, the description "Knowing" is negated, since only one who possesses knowledge can be described as knowing. It is as though, in his view, they had explicitly stated what their words entail. The same applies, in his view, to the other groups among the proponents of figurative interpretation, such as the anthropomorphists, the Qadariyya, and others. But whoever does not consider them bound by the implications of their words, or does not hold them to what their doctrine entails, does not consider them unbelievers. He says: "For when they are confronted with this, they say, 'We do not say that He is not Knowing. We disavow the implication you have attributed to us, which we and you believe to be unbelief. Rather, we say that our statement does not lead to that, according to the principles we have laid down.'" It is on the basis of these two approaches that people have differed over declaring the proponents of figurative interpretation to be unbelievers. Once you understand this, the reason for people's disagreement on this matter will become clear to you, and the correct position is to refrain from declaring them unbelievers.
And refraining from definitively declaring them doomed to perdition, while applying the rulings of Islam to them in matters of retaliation, inheritance, marriage, blood money, funeral prayers, burial in Muslim cemeteries, and all their other dealings; but they are subjected to severe disciplinary punishment.
