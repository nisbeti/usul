This means that individuals within the abode of Islam and belonging to it must pursue the truth to the extent of their ability, even if that truth differs from the caliph's legal judgment. Likewise, the caliph must not compel people to follow his school of thought. Rather, both are commanded to follow what they each find sound in the Book and the Sunnah; otherwise, they fall into the sin of obedience, taking rabbis and monks as lords besides Allah. May Allah protect us from that. I have discussed this in detail elsewhere. For further benefit, consult the chapter on invalidating blind following in Ibn Hazm's book al-Ihkam, and al-Shawkani's book The Beneficial Discourse on the Invalidity of Blind Following, since this is not the purpose of this discussion. And Allah knows best. There is no objection to a ruler considering a particular group to be unbelievers on the basis of an interpretation he holds, while one of those engaged in religious learning holds otherwise because evidence from the Book or the Sunnah has become clear to him that rules this out. Both should counsel one another by presenting the evidence they have, while not withdrawing their hand from obedience. I have previously mentioned the Companions' disagreement with the greatest of the Rightly Guided Caliphs when evidence from the Book or the Sunnah appeared to indicate otherwise, so there is no need to repeat it here. There is no doubt that those below them have even greater reason to do so.
