Ibn Hazm said: "A group held that no Muslim is declared an unbeliever or a sinner on account of something he says concerning belief or a legal opinion, and that anyone who exercises independent reasoning in any of these matters and adheres to what he considers to be the truth is rewarded in all cases: if he is right, he receives two rewards, and if he is mistaken, one reward. This was the view of Ibn Abi Layla, Abu Hanifa, al-Shafi'i, Sufyan al-Thawri, and Dawud ibn Ali, may Allah be pleased with them all. It is also the view of everyone among the Companions, may Allah be pleased with them, whom we know to have expressed an opinion on this issue. We know of no disagreement among them at all, except for the disagreement we have mentioned concerning declaring an unbeliever anyone who deliberately leaves the prayer until its time has passed, or fails to pay zakat, perform Hajj, or fast Ramadan, or drinks wine. Those who declared people unbelievers on account of disagreements in matters of belief cited various arguments, which we shall present, if Allah, Exalted is He, wills."

But they are not satisfied with Ibn Hazm or al-Qadi Iyad, because, in their view, they are Ash'aris or Jahmis. Yet they often contradict themselves: their books are full of quotations from those they accuse of heresy, such as Ibn Taymiyya, Ibn al-Qayyim, Ibn Kathir, and others, whenever they find in them something that supports their position. I do not know how to make sense of this contradiction: at one moment these men are unbelievers whose statements are unacceptable, and at another their views and arguments are cited. Therefore, I will discuss the issue in detail later, with regard to the statements of the Companions on this matter, if Allah wills.

The second issue: their reliance on the statements of the Companions and Successors. As noted above, they have not relied on a single statement of the Companions on this issue at all. Yet they have laid down the principle that even reports with weak chains of transmission may be acted upon. Although rejecting weak reports in matters of belief and rulings—even when attributed to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him)—is now almost universally accepted, some groups have added another condition alongside soundness: if the matter concerns belief, the report must also have been transmitted by mass transmission. How, then, can these people deem it permissible to accept weak reports in matters of belief, especially reports from someone other than the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), as a result of this dubious doctrine of theirs?
