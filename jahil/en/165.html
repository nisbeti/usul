The entirety of Islam, complete, has reached us and will continue to reach us until the Day of Resurrection. If they say, "Rather, it was for him, peace be upon him, only with those who witnessed him, not in the transmission of the religion to those who came after them," we say to them: If you allow the protection from error in transmitting the religion to have lapsed after his death, peace be upon him, and allow corruption, decay, invalidity, addition, omission, and distortion to enter the religion, then on what basis do you distinguish between what you allow to happen after him, peace be upon him, and what you forbid from happening during his lifetime? If they say, "Because then he, peace be upon him, would not have conveyed what he was commanded to convey, nor would he have been protected from error, and Allah, Exalted is He, says: {O Messenger, convey what has been sent down to you from your Lord. If you do not, then you have not conveyed His message. And Allah will protect you from the people. Indeed, Allah does not guide the disbelieving people}," it is said to them: Yes, and this very conveyance that you dispute, in which, by your unanimous agreement with us, he, peace be upon him, was protected from lying and error, has reached us just as it reached the Companions, may Allah be pleased with them. There is no difference. The religion is binding upon us just as it was binding upon them, equally in every respect.

Thus, protection from error in conveying the religion is necessary, remains assured, and must continue until the Day of Resurrection, just as it was in force for the Companions, may Allah be pleased with them, equally in every respect. Whoever denies this has concluded that the proof in matters of religion is not established against us; yet proof cannot be established by something whose truth or falsehood is unknown. Then we say to them: Allah, Exalted is He, likewise says: {Indeed, it is We who sent down the Reminder, and indeed, We will preserve it} … If they claim consensus, we say to them: Among the Karramiyya are those who say that he, peace be upon him, was not protected from error in conveying the Sharia.
