The Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) brought that, yet someone who doubted it did not know that he was a Messenger. Likewise, whoever did not know that he brought monotheism, affirmation of the Messengers, affirmation of the bodily resurrection, and affirmation of the divine attributes, exaltedness, and speech, did not know that he was a Messenger. Thus, many matters of transmitted reports and religious obligations may be followed on authority by one unable to reason from evidence, just as many practical matters may not be followed on authority.
Dividing religion into what is established by a solitary report and what is not established by it is a division that is neither consistently applicable nor comprehensive, and has no sound proof to support it.
Moreover, following on authority is accepting another person's statement without proof. Whoever accepts another person's statement about what he relates from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) that he brought, whether as a report or an obligation, accepts it only because he attributes it to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him). This is proof; however, not everyone may be able to formulate its premises and rebut the doubts that oppose it. Not everyone who knows something through its proof can set it out with all its premises, express it, or rebut what opposes it. If inability to do so counts as following on authority, then the majority of the community would be following on authority in monotheism, affirmation of the Messenger's mission, and the resurrection. If inability to do so does not count as following on authority, then they would not be following on authority in monotheism, affirmation of the Messenger's mission, and the resurrection; nor would they be following on authority in most of the practical rulings they need. This is the truth: the majority of the community base their acts of worship, prohibitions, and permissions on what they have necessarily learned from their Prophet, namely, that he brought it.
