These hadiths are directed against the Mu'tazilites, for they declare the people of the Sunnah unbelievers on the basis of an interpretation they hold. Ibn Taymiyyah said: "In Minhaj al-Sunnah (5/158): 'The Kharijites declare the people of the community unbelievers, and so do most of the Mu'tazilites when someone disagrees with them (10). The same is true of most of the Rafidites. Those who do not declare them unbelievers call them sinners. Likewise, most of the people of heretical views introduce an opinion and declare those who disagree with them unbelievers. The people of the Sunnah follow the truth from their Lord, which the Messenger brought; they do not declare those who disagree with them unbelievers. Rather, they know the truth better and are more merciful toward creation, as Allah described the Muslims when He said: {You are the best community brought forth for mankind}.'"

Likewise, some Ash'aris declare the people of the Sunnah who affirm Allah's exaltedness and attributes unbelievers, and call them Hashawiyya, anthropomorphists, and assimilators. I do not know of anyone among the people of the Sunnah who called these people Kharijites.

In a study written by Professor Ridwan Shakdani, it says: "Shaykh Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi, the leading Ash'ari of his time and author of al-Muhadhdhab, upon which Imam al-Nawawi (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote his commentary, said: 'Whoever believes anything other than what we have indicated of the belief of the people of truth affiliated with Imam Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari (may Allah be pleased with him) is an unbeliever. Whoever attributes anything else to them has declared them unbelievers, and is therefore an unbeliever for declaring them so, because it is reported from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) that he said: "No man declares another man an unbeliever without one of them incurring it..."' See his Sharh al-Luma' (1/111).

Al-Subki said in his "wooden" Sayf al-Saqil, commenting on the eminent Imam Ibn al-Qayyim: 'He is the heretic, may Allah curse him! How shameless he is, and how audacious! May Allah disgrace him!' Sayf al-Saqil, p. 37. Al-Kawthari edited it and disfigured it!

Al-Kawthari went further, hurling at Ibn al-Qayyim (may Allah Most High have mercy on him) words of unbelief, abuse, and vilification that decent people would recoil from hearing. He gathered them in his treatise entitled Tabdid al-Zalam al-Mukhayyim min Nuniyyat Ibn al-Qayyim.

Al-Kawthari also said: "Ibn Taymiyyah declared the judges of the four schools in Egypt unbelievers when he prohibited visiting the Prophet's grave." Takmilat al-Sayf al-Saqil, p. 155.

Ibn Nujaym al-Hanafi al-Maturidi (d. 970 AH) said in al-Bahr al-Ra'iq, "Chapter on the Rulings Concerning Apostates" (5/129): "A person becomes an unbeliever by affirming that Allah Most High has a place. If he says, 'Allah is in heaven,' then if he intends to relate what has come in the apparent wording of the reports, he does not become an unbeliever; but if he means a place, he becomes an unbeliever."

Abu al-Mu'in al-Nasafi al-Hanafi al-Maturidi (d. 508 AH) said in Tabsirat al-Adilla (1/169): "Allah Most High has denied any likeness between His essence and anything else. Thus, the claim that He has a place contradicts this definitive text—that is, His saying, {There is nothing like unto Him}—which admits no interpretation other than its apparent meaning. Whoever rejects the text is an unbeliever. May Allah protect us from that." End quote.
