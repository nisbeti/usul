Chapter: As for one who attributes to Allah, Exalted is He, something unbecoming of Him, not by way of insult or apostasy, nor with the intention of disbelief, but by way of interpretation, independent reasoning, and error leading to caprice and innovation—such as likening Him to creation, describing Him as having a limb, or denying an attribute of perfection—this is a matter concerning which the early and later scholars differed over whether to declare its speaker and adherent an unbeliever. Malik and his companions also differed in their opinions on this, though they did not differ that such people are to be fought if they join a faction, and that they are to be asked to repent; if they repent, then well and good, but otherwise they are to be killed. Their disagreement concerned those acting alone. Most of Malik and his companions held that they should not be declared unbelievers or killed, but that their punishment should be severe and their imprisonment prolonged until they manifestly desist and their repentance becomes clear, as Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) did with Sabigh. This was the view of Muhammad ibn al-Mawwaz regarding the Kharijites, and of Abd al-Malik ibn al-Majishun; it was also the view of Sahnun regarding all people of heretical views. He interpreted Malik's statement in the Muwatta in this way, as well as what he related from Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, his grandfather, and his uncle, concerning the Qadariyya: "They are to be asked to repent; if they repent, then well and good, but otherwise they are to be killed." Isa ibn al-Qasim said of people of heretical views among the Ibadiyya, the Qadariyya, and their like, who oppose the community through innovation and distortion in interpreting the Book of Allah: "They are to be asked to repent, whether they have made this manifest or kept it concealed. If they repent, then well and good, but otherwise they are to be killed, and their inheritance goes to their heirs." Ibn al-Qasim said the same in Muhammad's book concerning the Qadariyya and others. He said: "To ask them to repent means to tell them: 'Abandon what you are following.'" The same is stated in al-Mabsut concerning the Ibadiyya, the Qadariyya, and all other people of innovation: "They are Muslims; it is only their opinion that is evil." Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz acted accordingly. Ibn al-Qasim said: "Whoever says that Allah did not speak to Moses with speech is to be asked to repent; if he repents, then well and good, but otherwise he is to be killed." Ibn Habib and others among our companions held that they are unbelievers.
