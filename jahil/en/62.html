This is what I mean here by the person interpreting the texts: the researcher considers him excused, because he did not turn away from the truth but exerted his utmost effort, yet was not guided to it. He misunderstood; he did not act with bad intent or neglect the pursuit of knowledge. Rather, he exerted his utmost effort to know the truth, but failed to reach it. I will begin by citing the scholars' statements, then discussing their evidence, Allah Most High willing.

(Interpretation and the punishment of one who interprets the texts) Ibn Taymiyya said: "What I have mentioned concerning a Muslim's abandoning an obligation or committing a prohibition on the basis of an interpretation arising from independent reasoning or following another's opinion is clear to me, and his situation in this regard is better than that of an unbeliever who interprets the texts. This does not preclude my fighting a rebellious person who interprets the texts, flogging a drinker who interprets the texts, and the like; for interpretation does not altogether remove worldly punishment, since the purpose of punishment is to prevent the harm of transgression." End quote. (Majmu al-Fatawa 22/14)

He also said: "There is no one among these who belongs to the early generations of the community or its leading scholars; rather, each of them is a Muslim who is criticized for an innovation, even if he interprets the texts in support of it, and his error is forgiven." End quote. (Bayan Talbis al-Jahmiyya 2/358)

He said: "This is the position of the jurists among the hadith scholars, such as Ahmad and others: whoever calls people to an innovation deserves punishment to protect people from his harm, even if inwardly he is exercising independent reasoning." End quote. (Majmu al-Fatawa 7/385)
