Along with his ignorance, he deliberately did something else: he spoke about Allah without knowledge or proof, and thereby fell into shirk. One who speaks about Allah without knowledge is certainly not mistaken; rather, he is in error, so he does not fall under the preceding verses. But whoever is ignorant of something, exerts his utmost effort, and says something incorrect about Allah, erring through interpretation, is excused so long as he believes in Allah and His Messenger in a general sense. This is the distinction between these three categories, and Allah knows best. God willing, in the discussion of how the proof is established, there will be an explanation of why the heedless, ignorant person who speaks about Allah without knowledge is not excused.

(Among the proofs for excusing someone on the basis of interpretation) is the Companions' statement that Amir's deeds had been nullified. This is in Sahih al-Bukhari (5/131):

"When the two groups drew up in battle, Amir's sword was short. He struck at the leg of a Jew with it, but the sword's tip turned back and struck Amir in the hollow of his knee, and he died from it. He said: When they returned, Salama said: The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) saw me holding his hand and said, 'What is the matter?' I said to him, 'May my father and mother be sacrificed for you! They claim that Amir's deeds have been nullified.' The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said, 'Whoever said that is lying. He will have two rewards'—and he joined his fingers together—'for he is a striving warrior. Few Arabs have walked with a man like him.' Qutayba narrated to us; Hatim narrated to us, saying: 'He grew up with it.'"
