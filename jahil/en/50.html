They relied on reports transmitted from the early generations that had been judged weak by hadith scholars, and which contain disparagement of the prophets and angels, such as the story of Harut and Marut, the story of Joseph's passion, the story of David's sin, the story of Solomon's ring, and the story attributing shirk to Adam and naming his son Abd al-Harith so that he might live, as they claim. I do not know what their leading scholar intended by this. Moreover, he criticized Ibn Kathir for rejecting these hadiths, and I do not know what he intended by defending this disparagement of the prophets found in these weak hadiths. This is very suspicious. Likewise, they rely on statements attributed to some early scholars that the earth is flat, while disregarding other reports and the scholarly consensus transmitted from scholars that it is spherical. They then made this one of the matters of belief, declaring anyone who disagrees an unbeliever, and consequently declaring most Muslims, past and present, unbelievers on that basis. Also, for example, it is astonishing and suspicious that they deliberately weaken reports attributed to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) that are authentic according to some hadith scholars, but which contradict their school of thought, and refuse to rely on them because, in their view, they are weak. For example, their leading scholar did this with the report about the man who scattered his own ashes, in which he says, "Perhaps I will escape Allah." This report explicitly shows that the man was ignorant of the attribute of power, and it was transmitted through a chain of narrators that many scholars deem sound. I think he himself deems it sound, but he ignored this chain, produced another chain that is weak, and rejected the report on that basis, saying it was weak. As you can see, sometimes reports from people below the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) are treated as proof, even when weak, while at other times reports from the Prophet himself (peace and blessings be upon him) are not treated as proof because they contradict their school of thought. There is no might or power except through Allah.

Although it is clear that acting upon weak reports in matters of creed and rulings is impermissible, especially when the view in question is that of a Companion, there is no harm in briefly addressing this issue so that the discussion may be complete, God willing.
