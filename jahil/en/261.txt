His position that individuals are not to be declared unbelievers is well known, as is his refusal to declare the Ash'aris unbelievers. For this reason, they declared him an unbeliever personally.
It is stated in Majmu al-Fatawa (20/31):
"In general, the reward is for following the truth according to one's own effort in reaching a judgment. Even if there were, in reality, a truth that contradicted his judgment, that truth would be more deserving of being followed, were he able to know it; but he was unable to do so. This is like those who strive to determine the direction of the Kaaba. The same applies to anyone who performs an act of worship that has been prohibited, without knowing of the prohibition, provided that it is of the same kind as what has been commanded. For example, someone who prays at times when prayer is prohibited, having received the general command to pray but not having learned of the prohibition; or someone who relies on a specific but weaker proof, such as the prayer of two rak'ahs after Asr performed by a group of the early generations, because the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) prayed them; or a prayer for which weak or fabricated hadiths have been narrated, such as the thousand-rak'ah prayer in the middle of Sha'ban, the prayer at the beginning of Rajab, and the tasbih prayer, as Ibn al-Mubarak permitted it, and other such prayers. If these fall under the general recommendation to pray, and he has not learned of anything that entails prohibition, he is rewarded for that, even if there is a prohibition concerning them in some respect, because he did not know that they were an innovation adopted as a regular practice and performed collectively every year. This is like someone instituting a sixth prayer. For this reason, if he wished to perform such a prayer without a hadith, he would have no right to do so; but when the hadith was narrated, he believed that"
