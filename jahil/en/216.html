This is a sound hadith according to the conditions of Muslim. A woman experienced a menstrual period that prevented her from fasting and praying. This is so, even though Aisha asked the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), "Does Allah know everything that people conceal?" He did not declare her an unbeliever; the hadith is in Sahih Muslim. A man also interpreted the law to mean that the prescribed punishment for fornication would be waived for his unmarried son if he paid a ransom of one hundred sheep and a slave girl. The man was not declared an unbeliever; the hadith is in al-Bukhari. A man was unaware that wine was forbidden and brought it as a gift to the Messenger of Allah. This involved deeming something forbidden to be permissible, yet he was not declared an unbeliever; the hadith is in Muslim. Abu Hurayra did not know who would be the happiest of people to receive the Prophet's intercession (peace and blessings be upon him), so the Prophet explained to him that it would be the one who said, "There is no god but Allah," sincerely from his heart; Abu Hurayra was not declared an unbeliever. The Companions did not know that they would be rewarded for sexual intercourse, and they asked, "Does one of us satisfy his desire and receive a reward for it?" The hadith is established in the Sahih collections. Aisha did not know that all the deeds of an unbeliever are rendered void. She thought that he might benefit in the Hereafter from some of his good deeds, and asked about Ibn Jud'an, saying that he used to feed guests and maintain ties of kinship: "Will that benefit him?" The Prophet explained to her that it would not, but did not declare her an unbeliever. The hadith is established in the Sahih collections. This is a matter of creed. Al-Abbas did not know that Abu Talib's punishment would be lightened because he had protected the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him). This too is a matter of creed, yet he was not declared an unbeliever. It is also reported that Imam Ahmad, may Allah have mercy on him, said (vol. 3, p. 478): Ibn Abi Adi narrated to us, from Dawud ibn Abi Hind.
