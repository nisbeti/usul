The author of al-Jami said, concerning working in the governments of unbelievers: "If it is said that Joseph (peace be upon him), by working for the unbelieving king, was undoubtedly assisting him, the answer is that this is not prohibited unless it is assistance to an unbeliever in something forbidden, in which case it is forbidden, or assistance to an unbeliever in unbelief or against the Muslims, in which case the assistance constitutes allegiance that renders one an unbeliever. The author of al-Risala al-Limaniyya confused assistance with allegiance and made every kind of assistance allegiance. Our Sharia, as well as the laws of all the prophets, forbade allegiance to unbelievers, while permitting dealings with them through buying and selling, partnership, and leasing. I mentioned this earlier among things that might be thought to constitute allegiance but do not. All of this involves a kind of assistance to them, but it is assistance in worldly matters, not assistance in unbelief or against the Muslims. An example is that the unbelievers of Mecca used to bring wheat from al-Yamama. When Thumama ibn

Athal, who was one of the leading men of Yamama, withheld wheat from them until the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) gave permission for it (1). In his commentary, Ibn Hajar said that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) did give permission for it (2). This was assistance indeed—but in a worldly matter.

If we consider the question of a Muslim working for a non-Muslim, has our Sharia given guidance on whether this is permissible or impermissible? The answer is: Yes, it has given guidance permitting it, as indicated by what al-Bukhari, may Allah have mercy on him, narrated in the Book of Hiring in his Sahih, in the chapter, "May a man hire himself out to a polytheist in enemy territory?"
