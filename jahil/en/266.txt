(Ibn Taymiyya's discussion of declaring a specific person an unbeliever)

((After mentioning the disagreement among later scholars over whether the people of innovation are declared unbelievers in a way that takes them out of the religion or not, and whether they remain eternally in the Fire or not, Ibn Taymiyya said: "The reality of the matter is that, with regard to the general wording used by the imams, they fell into the same error as those before them fell into with regard to the general wording in the Lawgiver's texts. Whenever they saw the imams say, 'Whoever says such-and-such is an unbeliever,' the listener assumed that this wording applied to everyone who said it. They did not consider that declaring someone an unbeliever has conditions and impediments that may be absent or present in the case of a specific person, and that declaring someone an unbeliever in general does not entail declaring a specific person an unbeliever unless the conditions are met and the impediments are absent. This is made clear by the fact that Imam Ahmad and most of the imams who made such general statements did not declare most of those who said those very words to be unbelievers. For example, Imam Ahmad personally dealt with the Jahmiyya, who called on him to affirm that the Quran was created and to deny the divine attributes. They put him and the other scholars of his time to the test, and persecuted the believing men and women who did not agree with their Jahmism, beating and imprisoning them … He mentioned that most of those in authority declared everyone who was not a Jahmi and did not agree with them to be an unbeliever, and treated them as unbelievers … and so on," up to where he said: "It is well known that this is among the most extreme forms of Jahmism, for calling people to a doctrine is graver than merely holding it, and rewarding those who profess it and punishing those who reject it is graver than merely calling people to it …..

"Then Imam Ahmad prayed for the caliph and others who had beaten and imprisoned him, sought forgiveness for them, and absolved them of what they had done … Had they been apostates from Islam, it would not have been permissible to seek forgiveness for them, for seeking forgiveness for unbelievers is forbidden by the Book.
