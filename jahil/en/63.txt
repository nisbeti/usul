He said: "Whoever adopts singing and clapping as worship and a means of drawing near to Allah has thereby imitated the polytheists and resembled them in something that is not the practice of the believers—the Emigrants and the Helpers. If he does this in the houses of Allah, his resemblance to them is all the greater, and he is occupied by it instead of prayer, remembrance of Allah, and supplication to Him. His resemblance to them has thus become grave, and he has a great share of the blame indicated by His saying, Exalted is He: {Their prayer at the House was nothing but whistling and clapping.} Yet this may be forgiven him because of his sincere effort, or because of good deeds that erase sins, or for some other reason. This is among the matters in which a distinction is made between a Muslim and a disbeliever. But his being distinct from the polytheists in other respects does not prevent him from being blameworthy, outside the bounds of the Sharia, and engaged in innovation." End quote (Majmu al-Fatawa 11/596).
He said: "The ignorant person must turn back and not persist in his ignorance or oppose what the Muslim scholars hold; for by doing so he becomes an ignorant, misguided innovator." End quote (Majmu al-Fatawa 7/682).

Error in interpretation: Interpretation (ta'wil) is placing a legal proof in a context other than its proper one, through independent reasoning or a doubt arising from failure to understand the indication of the text. A legally responsible person may therefore proceed to commit an act of disbelief without considering it disbelief, citing as proof a text whose meaning he has misunderstood. This error means that the condition of deliberate intent is absent, and error in interpretation prevents him from being declared a disbeliever. But if proof is established against him and his error is explained to him, and he persists in his act, he then becomes a disbeliever. The evidence for this is the incident of Qudama ibn Maz'un. Qudama deemed drinking wine permissible—and deeming it permissible is disbelief—citing the saying of Allah, Exalted is He: {There is no blame on those who believe and do righteous deeds for what they have consumed} (al-Ma'ida: 93). He cited this verse to Umar when Umar intended to impose the prescribed punishment on him. Umar explained his error to him and imposed the punishment.
