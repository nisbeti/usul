(Excusing Ignorance and Interpretive Error)

(Introduction) One of the reasons for this study is to bring about reconciliation among groups of Muslims: between the group that does not excuse someone who, despite having testified to the two testimonies of faith and embraced faith in general terms, interprets the matter in a way that has some basis in the Sharia or the Arabic language, and therefore declares such people disbelievers and declares those who excuse them disbelievers; and the other group, which excuses those who interpret matters in this way among Ahl al-Sunna, but nevertheless does not excuse the first group for declaring such people disbelievers, accusing them of being Kharijites and applying to them the hadiths of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) concerning the Kharijites. Is someone from among the people of the qibla who makes every effort in interpretation truly a disbeliever? And is someone who declares him a disbeliever and does not excuse him a Kharijite? This is what the researcher seeks to answer. As for any error in reasoning in this study, I retract it, in my lifetime and after my death. I seek only to set things right as far as I am able, and my success is only through Allah.

Among the great deeds that Islam encourages and urges people to undertake is reconciliation between people. Islam is a religion of love, unity, and harmony among Muslims. Allah, Exalted is He, says: ﴾There is no good in much of their private conversation, except for those who enjoin charity, kindness, or reconciliation between people. Whoever does this, seeking Allah's pleasure, We shall grant him a great reward.﴿ [al-Nisa: 114].

Imam al-Tabari, may Allah have mercy on him, said regarding "or reconciliation between people": "It is reconciliation between those at odds or in dispute, in a manner that Allah has permitted, so that they may return to what brings affection and unity of purpose, in accordance with what Allah has permitted and commanded."
