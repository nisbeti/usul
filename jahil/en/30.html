Prayer was obligatory upon him, yet he remained for a period without praying. He was not required to make up the prayers, according to the more apparent of the two opinions of the scholars. This is the position of Abu Hanifa and the literalists, and one of the two positions in the school of Ahmad—until he said: "The correct view in all of this is that a ruling is established only when one is able to know it, and that he is not required to make up what he did not know was obligatory." (8)

(The following statement is explicit that he is subject to the threat for committing what is forbidden, and that the matter is not merely the sin of his negligence in seeking knowledge.) He said: "This is because the threat applies to one who commits what is forbidden only if he knows that it is forbidden, or is able to know that it is forbidden. For someone raised in the desert, or someone newly entered into Islam, who does something forbidden without knowing that it is forbidden, does not sin and is not subject to the prescribed punishment, even if he has no legal evidence on which to base his belief that it is permissible. One who has not received the hadith prohibiting something, but relies on legal evidence in holding it permissible, is all the more deserving of being excused." Ibn Taymiyya also said: "As for one who deliberately distorts the Book, in its wording or meaning, or who knows what the Messenger brought and opposes it, such a person deserves punishment. The same applies to one who is negligent in seeking and following the truth, following his own desires and occupying himself with worldly affairs instead." (5)
