The fourth type of declaring someone an unbeliever is when the unbelief is unanimously agreed upon, and the one who declares him an unbeliever excuses him on account of ignorance, but believes that the person who committed the act of unbelief had knowledge, and therefore has no excuse of ignorance. This is like Umar's treatment of Hatib, according to some interpretations.
And likewise the action of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh toward Usayd ibn Hudayr. This is the mildest form of declaring someone an unbeliever, and exercising independent judgment on this matter is quite plausible and has considerable scope. If the first three issues admit of interpretation, is it not possible that this one does too?
