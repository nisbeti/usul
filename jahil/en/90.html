(23) Talha ibn al-Husayn ibn Muhammad ibn Ibrahim ibn Ali al-Salhani was from the people of Isfahan. Al-Sam'ani said: "He was a righteous shaykh who heard many reports. He lost his sight toward the end of his life. He heard from his great-grandfather, Abu Dharr Muhammad ibn Ibrahim al-Salhani." He died in the year 515.

Al-Tahbir fi al-Mu'jam al-Kabir (1/350–352)

(24) Abu Dharr Muhammad ibn Ibrahim ibn Ali al-Salhani narrated from Abu Muhammad Abd Allah ibn Muhammad ibn Ja'far ibn Hayyan, the hadith scholar known as Abu al-Shaykh. He narrated some of his books from him, such as The Character and Qualities of the Prophet, al-Sunna al-Saghir, and al-Birr wa al-Sila. Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Umar ibn Azizah narrated from him. Yahya ibn Manda said: "He died in the year 440, one month before Ibn Ridhah."

Ikmal al-Ikmal (2/644); al-Tahbir fi al-Mu'jam al-Kabir (1/350–352)

(25) The words of Abu al-Shaykh are authentically established from him, so the chain of transmission of the author of al-Mahajja to him is sound.

(26) Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Amr ibn al-Dahhak ibn Makhlad al-Shaybani was an ascetic and a great hadith scholar, an accomplished imam who followed the transmitted reports and authored many works. Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Madini al-Bazzaz said: "I came to Basra while Ahmad ibn Hanbal was still alive and asked who among them was the most learned in jurisprudence. They said: 'There is no one in Basra more learned in jurisprudence than Ahmad ibn Amr ibn Abi Asim.'"
