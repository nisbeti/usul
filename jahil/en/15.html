…another god alongside Allah, for which he has no proof. His reckoning is with his Lord. Indeed, the disbelievers do not prosper." Thus, He declared anyone who invokes anyone besides Allah without proof to be a disbeliever. In this respect, they are not excused. Those mentioned in the hadith as being punished, such as the Prophet's father (peace and blessings be upon him) and Amr ibn Luhayy, belong to this group; these conditions were met in their case, so punishment was due to them.

However, this group that excuses them split into two groups. One group said that it is sufficient for the proof to be established that a person has the ability to learn. If a person is able to acquire knowledge but falls short in doing so and commits an act of shirk, then he is a disbelieving polytheist, because he is no longer considered ignorant or mistaken, but rather one who turns away from the truth and commits disbelief through turning away. As you can see, this criterion is not well-defined, since the ability to acquire knowledge is relative. Thus, they said that a recent convert to Islam and someone raised in a remote desert are not deemed disbelievers, because they are unable to acquire knowledge. Since this criterion is not well-defined, rulings on declaring some people who engage in major shirk to be disbelievers have differed in certain countries, depending on whether or not they are considered to have had the ability to acquire knowledge. As for the other group that excuses them, they said that the ability to seek knowledge is not sufficient for the proof to be established; rather, the proof must reach the person legally accountable before he can be declared a disbeliever. If one of them is able to acquire knowledge but turns away from it, then falls into shirk while being a Muslim, he is not judged to be a polytheist, because the proof did not reach him. He is only sinful for falling short in learning what is obligatory for him. They said, "Failing to seek knowledge of the details despite being able to do so does not, by itself, entail disbelief, unless its cause is aversion to what one has previously learned, contempt for it, or arrogance toward it."
