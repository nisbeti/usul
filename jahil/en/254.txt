The apparent meaning intended is that they left Islam and retained no connection to it, just as an arrow passes through its target, because of its speed and the strength of the archer, so that it retains nothing of the target. He alluded to this by saying, "It passes beyond the dung and the blood." The author of al-Shifa said concerning this: "Likewise, we decisively declare an unbeliever anyone who makes a statement by which he seeks to lead the community astray or to declare the Companions unbelievers." The author of al-Rawda cited this from him in the chapter on apostasy and endorsed it. Most Sunni scholars of legal theory held that the Kharijites are sinners, and that the rulings of Islam apply to them, since they profess the two testimonies of faith and regularly observe the pillars of Islam. They were deemed sinners only because they declared Muslims unbelievers, relying on a corrupt interpretation; this led them to deem the blood and property of those who opposed them lawful, and to testify that they were unbelievers and polytheists. Al-Khattabi said: "Muslim scholars agree that the Kharijites, despite their misguidance, are a sect among the Muslim sects. They permitted marrying them and eating their slaughtered animals, and held that they are not declared unbelievers so long as they adhere to the foundation of Islam." Iyad said: "This question nearly became more perplexing to theologians than any other, to the point that the jurist Abd al-Haqq asked Imam Abu al-Maali about it, and he excused himself, saying that admitting an unbeliever into the faith community and expelling a Muslim from it is a grave matter in religion." He said: "Before him, Qadi Abu Bakr al-Baqillani had refrained from giving an answer, saying: 'The people did not explicitly declare unbelief; rather, they made statements that lead to unbelief.'" Al-Ghazali said in The Distinction between Faith and Heresy: "What is proper is to avoid declaring someone an unbeliever whenever there is any way to do so. For deeming it lawful to shed the blood of those who pray and affirm the oneness of God is an error, and the error of leaving a thousand unbelievers alive is less grave than the error of shedding the blood of even one Muslim." Among the evidence cited by those who did not declare them unbelievers is the Prophet's statement in the third hadith of this chapter, after describing them as passing out of the religion as an arrow passes through its target: "The archer looks at his arrow."
