If it is sound, but he errs in it, his error is forgiven and he is rewarded for performing an act of a kind that is prescribed. The same applies to someone who fasts on the day of Eid without knowing that this is prohibited. This is unlike an act whose kind is not prescribed, such as associating partners with Allah: there is no reward for this, even though Allah does not punish its perpetrator until the message has been conveyed, as He, Exalted is He, says: {We would never punish until We had sent a messenger}. Yet although he is not punished, he is not rewarded either. Rather, this is as Allah, Exalted is He, says: {We shall turn to whatever deeds they did and make them scattered dust}. Ibn al-Mubarak said: "These are deeds done for other than Allah." Mujahid said: "These are deeds that were not accepted." And He, Exalted is He, says: {The deeds of those who disbelieve in their Lord are like ashes blown about by the wind} to the end of the verse. Their deeds are thus invalid, and there is no reward for them. If the Messenger forbids them from these deeds and they do not desist, they are punished; punishment for them is conditional upon the Messenger's message having been conveyed. As for their being invalid in themselves, that is because they are not commanded: every act of worship that is not commanded must be forbidden. Then, if someone knows that it is forbidden and does it, he deserves punishment. If he does not know, he does not deserve punishment. If he believes that it is commanded and it belongs to a kind of act that is prescribed, he is rewarded for it. But if it belongs to the kind of associating partners with Allah, nothing of that kind is commanded, although some people may think that certain forms of it are commanded. Such a person is not exercising independent legal reasoning (ijtihad), because one who exercises independent legal reasoning must follow a legal proof, and there can be no legal proof for these acts.
