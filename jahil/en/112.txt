(Hazimi's own words on the one who interprets and the one who excuses) Hazimi said:
"What is meant is that he makes an outrageous statement, as some have put it, one that is far removed from the truth. At that point we say: What is this considered? It is considered a slip. An example of this in our present age is the one who says that ruling by other than the law of Allah, Exalted is He, is lesser disbelief. This is a grave slip, and some of the great scholars have said it. But they are not to be followed in that; their standing is to be respected, but they are not to be followed. It should be made clear that this is a slip, and that the statement is, in principle, disbelief. But we do not declare everyone who says something that constitutes disbelief in matters such as these to be a disbeliever. Rather, we say: This is a slip, and this is an ugly, outrageous statement. But if it becomes clear that he is a scholar and that his intention is good, he is not declared a disbeliever. If we know that he is a person of caprice—and this is the more common case—then they are disbelievers, and are included among those who rule by false deities and the like, because this is considered something necessarily known to be part of the religion.
Likewise, regarding the issue of excusing ignorance: if these polytheists are excused, then if he is a scholar, the case is like the one just mentioned. Otherwise, the basic ruling is that he is a disbeliever, for he has not declared disbelievers those whom Allah, Exalted is He, and His Messenger declared disbelievers.
Second: You must not follow this scholar in his slip. If you follow him out of caprice, then he may not be a disbeliever, but you are. Is that correct? It does not necessarily follow. Some people, when they want to corner you, say: 'Then, if you say that ruling by other than the Sharia is disbelief, you must declare so-and-so and so-and-so disbelievers.' We say: No, that does not follow. I declare you a disbeliever, but I do not declare him one. It does not necessarily follow. Why? Because we know that the scholar may have some doubt, and his intention may be good; in that case, this is an excuse. The scholars have proceeded on this basis. But that has not become apparent in your case, and you are not even one of the scholars. Who has permitted you to follow him? One must return to the Book and the Sunnah. So, if that is the case, it does not necessarily follow that if you declare someone a disbeliever, you must declare the person who followed him a disbeliever. Say: It absolutely does not follow, for this is knowingly following an error. Whoever does not know that it is a slip is more excusable than him, though both are negligent. That is, even if he does not know it is a slip and follows him, he is considered negligent, because he has not returned to the source. And what is the source? The Book and the Sunnah. He must investigate the Book and the Sunnah, not the statements of so-and-so and so-and-so among men."
