Qadi Iyad said: "As for anyone who knowingly denies or rejects one of Allah's essential attributes, Exalted is He, such as saying, 'He is not knowing, powerful, willing, or speaking,' or the like of the attributes of perfection that are necessarily His, Exalted is He, our imams have explicitly stated that there is consensus that whoever denies these attributes of Him, Exalted is He, and strips Him of them is an unbeliever. This is how the statement of Sahnun is to be understood: 'Whoever says that Allah has no speech is an unbeliever.' He does not declare those who interpret the texts to be unbelievers, as we have already explained. As for someone who is ignorant of one of these attributes, the scholars differed on this point. Some declared him an unbeliever, and this was reported from Abu Ja'far al-Tabari (the statement of al-Tabari was quoted earlier: he declares ignorant of the attributes that are known only through innate disposition to be unbelievers, but not of other attributes, such as hearing and sight, in his view), and others. Abu al-Hasan al-Ashari held this view at one point. Another group held that this does not remove a person from the designation of faith; al-Ashari later returned to this position. He said: 'For he did not believe this as something whose correctness he was certain of and which he regarded as religion and law. Rather, only someone who believes his statement to be true is an unbeliever.' They cited as evidence the hadith of the black woman, and that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) only…
He asked her for nothing but affirmation of God's oneness, and cited the hadith about the man who said, "If Allah should have power over me"—in one version, "He will surely punish me"—and then said, "So Allah forgave him." They said: Even if most people were examined about the divine attributes and they were disclosed to them, only a few would be found who knew them.
