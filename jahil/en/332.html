In this way, the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) testified that they would depart from the religion as an arrow passes through its prey, when he said (peace be upon him) that they would kill the people of Islam and leave the idolaters alone. This is one of the signs of his prophethood (peace and blessings be upon him), for he foretold this, and it is one of the particulars of the unseen; it came to pass exactly as he said." End quote.

Here the meaning of his words (peace and blessings be upon him), "and they will leave the idolaters alone," becomes clear. They used to deem it forbidden to kill the People of the Book and the polytheists, as Ibn Hazm said. Thus, this rationale is also one that entails disbelief: their apparent leaving them alone indicates that they do so as an act of religious devotion, and this too is a rationale that entails disbelief.

It is stated in al-Milal wa al-Nihal (1/120):

The Azariqa had eight innovations:

The first was that he declared Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) an unbeliever, and said that Allah revealed concerning him: {And among the people is one whose speech about the life of this world pleases you, and who calls Allah to witness what is in his heart, yet he is the fiercest of opponents.} 1 He declared Abd al-Rahman ibn Muljam—may Allah curse him—correct in his action, and said that Allah, Exalted is He, revealed concerning him: {And among the people is one who sells himself, seeking Allah's pleasure.} 2

Imran ibn Hittan, the Kharijite jurist, ascetic, and greatest poet, said of Ibn Muljam's blow3—may Allah curse him—to Ali (may Allah be pleased with him):

"O blow from one who sought nearness, intending by it ... only to attain the pleasure of the Lord of the Throne.

Whenever I remember him, I deem him ... the weightiest of all creation in Allah's sight."
