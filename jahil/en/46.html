Sufyan, from Jabir, said: The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said, "My Companions are like stars; whichever of them you follow, you will be guided." Abu Muhammad said: Abu Sufyan is weak, and al-Harith ibn Ghussayn is Abu Wahb al-Thaqafi. Salam ibn Sulayman narrates fabricated hadiths, and this is certainly one of them. Thus, this is a rejected report transmitted through a weak chain of narration. Abu Umar Yusuf ibn Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Barr al-Namari wrote to me that this hadith was also narrated through Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd al-Ammi, from his father, from Said ibn al-Musayyib, from Ibn Umar; and through Hamzah al-Jazari, from Nafi, from Ibn Umar. He said: Abd al-Rahim ibn Zayd and his father are both abandoned narrators, and Hamzah al-Jazari is unknown. Al-Namari wrote to me: Muhammad ibn Ibrahim ibn Said told us that Abu Abd al-Rahman ibn Mufarrij related to them, saying: Muhammad ibn Ayyub al-Samut told us that al-Bazzar said, "As for what is narrated from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), 'My Companions are like stars; whichever of them you follow, you will be guided,' this is a statement that is not soundly established as coming from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him)." Abu Muhammad said: It has thus become clear that this report is not established at all; there is no doubt that it is fabricated, because Allah, Exalted is He, says of His Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), {Nor does he speak from his own desire. It is only a revelation sent down to him.} Since everything he (peace and blessings be upon him) says concerning the Sharia is true, it is all from Allah, Exalted is He, without doubt; and what is from Allah, Exalted is He, contains no contradiction, as He says: {Do they not reflect upon the Quran? Had it been from anyone other than Allah, they would have found much contradiction in it.} Allah, Exalted is He, has forbidden division and disagreement, saying: {Obey Allah and His Messenger, and do not dispute, lest you lose heart and your strength depart.}

"And be patient; indeed, Allah is with the patient." It is impossible that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) would command people to follow every speaker among the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them), when some of them deem something lawful and others deem it unlawful. If that were so, selling wine would be lawful, in imitation of Samura ibn

Jundub. Eating hail would be lawful for a fasting person, following the example of Abu Talha, and unlawful, following that of others among them. Omitting the ritual bath after intercourse without ejaculation would be obligatory, following the example of Ali, Uthman, Talha, Abu Ayyub, and Ubayy ibn Ka'b, and unlawful, following that of Aisha and Ibn Umar. Selling fruit before signs of ripeness appear on it would be lawful, following the example of Umar, and unlawful, following that of others among them. All of this is transmitted in our sources with chains of transmission. End quote.

The upshot of what we have mentioned is that there is no explicit evidence establishing the authority of the opinion of any of the Companions, let alone those of lesser rank. If they say that their opinion is authoritative in matters of belief, then whoever disagrees with them is deemed an unbeliever.

The answer is: Ibn Taymiyya said: "He also said in Mukhtasar al-Fatawa al-Misriyya: 'The division of issues into fundamental issues, denial of which constitutes disbelief, and subsidiary issues, denial of which does not constitute disbelief, has no basis among the Companions, the Successors, or the imams of Islam. Rather, it was adopted from the Mu'tazila and similar imams of innovation, who are inconsistent. For when they are asked, "What defines the fundamentals of religion?" and they answer, "Issues of belief," they may be told: People have disagreed over whether Muhammad saw his Lord, whether Uthman or Ali was superior, many meanings of the Quran, and the authentication of some hadiths. These are matters of belief, yet there is no disbelief in them, by the agreement of the Muslims. The obligation of prayer, zakat, and pilgrimage, and the prohibition of indecencies and wine, are practical matters, yet one who denies them is unanimously judged to be an unbeliever ...'"

If it is said that the foundational principles are those that are definitive, it may be replied that many matters of inquiry are not definitive. Whether a matter is definitive or speculative varies from person to person: what is definitive for one may not be definitive for another, as in the case of someone who hears the wording of a text and is certain of its intended meaning, while another text does not carry the same force for him; for him, it is not speculative, let alone definitive...

The point is that the positions of the leading scholars distinguish between the category and the individual case, and whoever has reported a disagreement has not grasped the depth of their statement ...

One group relates two reports from Ahmad concerning the unrestricted excommunication of the people of innovation. But this is not the position of Ahmad or of any of the other imams. Likewise, al-Shafi'i declared Hafs al-Fard an unbeliever when he said, "The Quran is created." Al-Shafi'i said to him, "You have disbelieved"—that is, what you said is disbelief. For this reason, he did not seek to have him killed; had he considered him an unbeliever, he would have sought to have him killed.

It is clear from what has preceded that the Companions differed over some matters of belief on the basis of interpretation, yet they did not declare one another unbelievers. It will be shown explicitly later that Ibn Abbas did not declare the Kharijites unbelievers, but regarded them as people who interpreted the texts and excused them on that basis. If their statements are authoritative, then they must follow them in this matter of excusing people on the basis of interpretation: they must not declare an interpreter an unbeliever, even if his interpretation falls under what they have included among matters of belief. Otherwise, they will have contradicted themselves. God willing, we will devote a separate discussion to the matter of excusing people on the basis of interpretation.
