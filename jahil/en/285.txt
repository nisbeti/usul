The third issue: When the upper hand belongs to the unbelievers and the Muslim is subject to them, is it permissible for a Muslim prisoner—who does not fear that he will be led astray while in captivity—to fight alongside them against other unbelievers in order to secure his release?
The scholars differed on this issue, holding two views:
The first view: It is forbidden to fight alongside them. This is the position of Abu Hanifa and his companions, the position of Malik, and the apparent position of al-Shafi'i's school.
Muhammad ibn al-Hasan said: "If the authority of the people of polytheism is dominant, Muslims should not fight alongside the people of polytheism, unless they fear for their own lives, in which case they may defend themselves."(86) Al-Siyar al-Saghir (249). In Al-Siyar al-Saghir, ed. Khadduri (p. 249), it says:
"I asked him about Muslims seeking the help of the people of polytheism against the people of war: Do you see any harm in that, and do they have a share of the spoils? He said: 'There is no harm in seeking their help if authority belongs to the people of Islam and they are the dominant and prevailing power. In that case, there is no harm in seeking the help of the people of polytheism. But if the authority of the people of polytheism is dominant, Muslims should not fight alongside the people of polytheism, unless they fear for their own lives, in which case they may defend themselves.' He said: 'The people of polytheism have no share, but they may be given a discretionary portion.'"
It says in Sharh al-Siyar al-Kabir: "If the people of war say to prisoners in their custody, 'Fight alongside us against our enemy, the polytheists,' and the prisoners do not fear for their lives if they refuse, they should not fight alongside them.
"For this fighting involves manifesting polytheism, and the fighter puts his life at risk. There is no dispensation for this except with the intention of strengthening the religion or defending himself. If they fear those others for their lives, there is no harm in their fighting them, because they are now warding off the danger of being killed.
"For they are safe from those who hold them in their custody, but they would not be safe from the others if they fell into their hands. It is therefore permissible for them to fight in self-defense.
"2973. If they say, 'Fight alongside us against our enemy, the polytheists, or we will kill you,' there is no harm in their fighting to ward them off."
