If they say, "Then you must say that those who transmitted the religious reports uttered by the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) are infallible in transmitting them, and that each one of them is protected in his transmission from deliberately lying and from falling into error," we say to them: Yes, this is what we say, and on this we are certain and firm. Every upright person who transmitted a report from

The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), in matters of religion, or his action, peace be upon him: that narrator is protected from deliberately lying, and this is certain in the sight of Allah, Exalted is He. He is also protected from the possibility of erring in it, unless a clarification is conveyed; and Allah, Exalted is He, must clarify what he has erred in, just as He did with His Prophet, peace be upon him, when he mistakenly performed two rak'ahs instead of three, on account of the proofs we have already presented concerning the preservation of the entire Sharia and the clarification of what is not part of it. We know necessarily that anyone who speaks truthfully in any report is, without doubt, protected from lying and error in that report. So what is objectionable about this? If they say, "Allah, Exalted is He, has charged us to think well of Him, and the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said, 'Allah, Exalted is He, says: I am as My servant thinks of Me,'" we say: This has nothing to do with making religious rulings on the basis of conjecture. Rather, it is all one matter, because He, Exalted is He, has forbidden us to say of Him what we do not know. We do not know whether He will forgive us or punish us, so we are obliged to refrain from asserting anything about that, while maintaining hope and fear. He has forbidden us to make claims about religion, prohibition, permissibility, and obligation that we do not know, and He has clarified everything of this that He has imposed upon us. Thus, we must be certain of all of it, just as we must be certain that the unbelievers will remain forever in the Fire and the believers forever in Paradise; there is no difference.
