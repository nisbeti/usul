Whoever excuses the Kharijites' disbelief. It will be shown that among those who did not declare them disbelievers were Ibn Abbas and Ali. (Another contradiction in their rejection of an infinite regress)
Abu Ubayda al-Jazairi said, "Whoever does not declare a person whom Allah has explicitly declared a disbeliever to be a disbeliever—such as Iblis—is himself a disbeliever, as is whoever excuses him, and whoever excuses that person, and so on ..." Then he said, "But an infinite regress is not addressed in matters other than these, where there may be some doubts concerning the excuse given by the one who excuses, such as the Jahmiyya, the issue of the createdness of the Quran, and the like."
I do not know what he means by saying that it is "not addressed." Does he mean that he does not affirm an infinite regress in this case, that an infinite regress is invalid, and that the one who excuses the excuser is therefore excused? That is what his words appear to mean. Or does he mean that he suspends judgment on the matter? If he means that he does not affirm an infinite regress and that the one who excuses the excuser is not declared a disbeliever, then the pressing question here is: Why is the one who excuses the excuser not declared a disbeliever?
Let us give an example. They declare Ibn Taymiyya a disbeliever because he does not declare the Asharis disbelievers and excuses them. If you ask them, they answer with the reasons I mentioned earlier: denying Allah and His Messenger, ignorance of the true nature of Islam, and violating consensus.
So Ibn Taymiyya is a disbeliever who interprets the matter differently and is not excused for his interpretation, just as the Asharis are disbelievers who interpret the matter differently and are not excused for their interpretation. If someone then says, "Ibn Taymiyya is not a disbeliever, and he is excused," it seems, as I understand them, that they do not declare him a disbeliever.
