Ibn Taymiyya, Shaykh al-Islam, transmitted this from the followers of the four imams and others, saying:
"As for the second category of reports, these are reports narrated only by a single upright narrator or the like, whose wording and meaning have not been transmitted by mass transmission, but which the community has received with acceptance, either by acting upon them or affirming them … These yield certain knowledge according to the vast majority of the community of Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him), among both the early and later generations. There was no disagreement about this among the early generations. As for the later generations, this was the position of the leading jurists among the followers of the four imams. The issue is recorded in the books of the Hanafis, Malikis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis, such as al-Sarakhsi and Abu Bakr al-Razi among the Hanafis; Shaykh Abu Hamid, Abu al-Tayyib, and Shaykh Abu Ishaq among the Shafi'is; Ibn Khuwaz Mindad and others among the Malikis; and Abu Ya'la, Ibn Abi Musa, Abu al-Khattab, and others among the Hanbalis. It was also the position of Abu Ishaq al-Isfarayini, Ibn Furak, and Abu Ishaq al-Nazzam among the theologians. Only a group, such as Ibn al-Baqillani and those who followed him, including Abu al-Ma'ali, al-Ghazali, and Ibn Aqil, disputed this. See: Mukhtasar al-Sawa'iq al-Mursala, 2/372–373."
In Mukhtasar al-Sawa'iq al-Mursala ala al-Jahmiyya wa al-Mu'attila (p. 553), it says:
"This is what those who deny that reports from the Messenger of Allah yield knowledge have relied upon. By doing so, they violated the consensus of the Companions, which is known with certainty through the consensus of the Successors and the consensus of the imams of Islam. They thereby agreed with the Mu'tazila, Jahmiyya, Rafida, and Kharijites, who violated this sanctity. Some of the scholars of legal theory and jurists followed them; otherwise, no early imam is known to have held this position. Rather, the imams explicitly stated the opposite of what they say.
