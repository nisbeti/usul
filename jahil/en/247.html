The early scholars differed over declaring many groups to be unbelievers. Yet the one who declared them unbelievers did not say to the one who did not, "You have denied Allah and His Messenger, since they judged this act to be unbelief, and you have denied them by saying that this act is not unbelief."

Here I will mention some individuals over whose status the Sunnis differed. It is stated in al-I'tisam by al-Shatibi, edited by al-Shaqir, al-Humayd, and al-Sini (1/223):

"For the early scholars and others differed over declaring many of their sects to be unbelievers, such as the Kharijites, the Qadariyya, and others."

It is stated in al-Sawa'iq al-Muhriqa 'ala Ahl al-Rafd wa al-Dalal wa al-Zandaqa (2/630):

"Know that the Sunnis differed over declaring Yazid ibn Mu'awiya and his successor after him to be unbelievers. One group said that he was an unbeliever, citing the well-known account related by Sibt ibn al-Jawzi and others: when the head of al-Husayn (may Allah be pleased with him) was brought, he gathered the people of Syria, began striking the head with a cane, and recited verses by Ibn al-Ziba'ra."

It is stated in Mirqat al-Mafatih Sharh Mishkat al-Masabih (4/1641):

"They differed over declaring someone who is ignorant of one of the attributes of Allah, Exalted is He, to be an unbeliever. Qadi Iyad said: Among those who declared him an unbeliever was Ibn Jarir al-Tabari, and Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari initially held this view. Others said that he is not declared an unbeliever on this account, unlike one who denies the attribute. Abu al-Hasan later returned to this view, and it became his settled position. He said: This is because the person holds this belief with certainty that he is right and regards it as a religious ruling; only someone who believes that his own doctrine is true is declared an unbeliever."
