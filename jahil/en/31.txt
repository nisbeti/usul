(Punishment is for the act, not for falling short) He also said: [If it is asked: Is this person excused for his misguidance if he thinks he is rightly guided, as Allah, Exalted is He, says: {And they think that they are rightly guided} (al-A'raf: 30)? The answer is: No. This person and others like him who are misguided have no excuse, for their misguidance stems from turning away from the revelation brought by the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him). Even if he thinks he is rightly guided, he has fallen short by turning away from following the caller to guidance. If he goes astray, it is only because of his own negligence and turning away. This is unlike one whose misguidance is due to the message not reaching him and his inability to access it; such a person has a different ruling. The warnings in the Qur'an apply only to the former. As for the latter, Allah does not punish anyone until after the proof has been established against him.] (4).
(Punishment is for the act, not for falling short) Ibn al-Qayyim also said: [It is authentically reported from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) that he said, "Whoever calls to misguidance will bear a sin like the sins of those who follow him, without that diminishing their sins in the least." This indicates that the disbelief of those who follow them is due simply to following and imitating them. Yes, this matter must be explained in detail to resolve the difficulty: the distinction is between an imitator who is able to learn and know the truth but turns away from it, and an imitator who is in no way able to do so.
