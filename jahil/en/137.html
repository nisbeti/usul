If someone says that these people are among the innovators whose excommunication is disputed, he has made a grave error. What is reliably reported from Ahmad and other imams is only that they declared the Jahmiyya, the anthropomorphists, and the like to be unbelievers. Ahmad did not declare the Kharijites or the Qadariyya to be unbelievers when they affirmed divine knowledge but denied that human acts are created and that the divine will is all-encompassing. However, two reports are related from him concerning whether they are unbelievers. As for the Murji'a, there is no disagreement in his position that they are not to be declared unbelievers. Yet Ahmad did not declare specific individuals among the Jahmiyya to be unbelievers, nor did he declare everyone who was called a Jahmi to be an unbeliever, nor everyone who agreed with the Jahmiyya in some of their innovations. Rather, Ahmad and those like him prayed behind Jahmiyya who promoted their doctrine, put people to the test, and severely punished those who did not agree with them. Ahmad did not declare them unbelievers. Instead, he considered them believers and imams, prayed for them, and held that one could follow them in prayer, perform the pilgrimage and fight alongside them, and refrain from rebelling against them, just as he held regarding other imams like them. He condemned the false doctrine they had introduced, which was grave unbelief, even though they themselves did not know that it was unbelief. He would denounce it and strive, as far as possible, to make them abandon it. Thus he combined obedience to Allah and His Messenger in upholding the Sunnah and religion and condemning the innovations of the heretical Jahmiyya, with observing the rights of the believers among the rulers and the community, even when they were ignorant innovators and unjust sinners. Those well-known figures, such as Hammad ibn Abi Sulayman, Abu Hanifa, and other jurists of Kufa, held that verbal profession and belief in the heart were part of faith. This was also the position of Abu Muhammad ibn Kullab and those like him. Their position on this did not differ, nor is it reported that they said faith is merely affirmation in the heart. Rather, they attributed this position to "Jahm ibn Safwan," saying that he held faith to be merely knowledge in the heart, even without professing it verbally.
