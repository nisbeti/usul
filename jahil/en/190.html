Like the light of the sun, it appears to those whose insight is illuminated; and he clothed falsehood in the darkness of night. It is not surprising that falsehood should be confused with daylight by one blind in sight, just as truth and falsehood are confused by one blind in insight.

Muadh ibn Jabal said in his judgment: "Accept the truth from whoever says it, for the truth has a light." But when hearts grew dark and insights were blinded by turning away from what the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) brought, and the darkness increased as they contented themselves with the opinions of men, truth became confused with falsehood for them. They deemed it possible that his sound hadiths, transmitted by the most upright and truthful members of the community, could be lies, while they deemed it possible that the false, fabricated, and contradictory hadiths that accorded with their desires could be true, and so they took shelter behind them.

The crux of the matter is this: can the report of upright, trustworthy transmitters, which Allah Most High has made it obligatory for Muslims to act upon, be false or mistaken in reality, with Allah providing no indication of that? Whoever says that it yields certain knowledge says that this is not possible; rather, whenever the conditions that make acting upon it obligatory are met, what it reports must be established in reality. On this basis, they disputed whether one who rejects it is an unbeliever, since it is among the proofs that yield certain knowledge, just as they discussed whether one who denies consensus is an unbeliever. If someone rejects a sound report because he believes the transmitter erred or lied, or because he believes that the infallible would not say such a thing, or because he believes it was abrogated, or for a similar reason, and rejects it through independent reasoning and out of zeal to uphold the truth, he does not thereby become an unbeliever or a sinner.
