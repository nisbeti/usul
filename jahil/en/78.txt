Others were uncertain about this, refraining from declaring him an unbeliever or from denying that he was one. Malik's two differing opinions on the matter, and his hesitation about repeating the prayer behind them, stem from this. The judge Abu Bakr, the leading authority among the scholars of verification and truth, adopted a similar view, saying that this is one of the difficult questions, since the people did not explicitly name the...
Disbelief; rather, they uttered a statement that leads to it. His position on the issue was as inconsistent as that of his imam, Malik ibn Anas. In some of his statements, he said that those who held the view that they were unbelievers on account of their interpretation could not be married, their slaughtered animals could not be eaten, and prayers could not be offered over their dead; whether they could inherit from them was disputed, in accordance with the disagreement over the inheritance of an apostate. He also said: "We allow their Muslim heirs to inherit from their deceased, but we do not allow them to inherit from Muslims." For the most part, he leaned toward refraining from declaring them unbelievers on account of the eventual implications of their position. The position of his teacher, Abu al-Hasan al-Ashari, was likewise inconsistent on this matter, though his predominant view was to refrain from declaring them unbelievers.
Ibn al-Qayyim, may Allah have mercy on him, said in Zad al-Ma'ad, in the chapter "An indication of the legal lessons to be drawn from the Conquest of Mecca": "This shows that if a man, acting on his interpretation and out of anger for Allah, His Messenger, and His religion—not for his own desires or personal interests—accuses a Muslim of hypocrisy or disbelief (as Umar accused Hatib of disbelief), he does not thereby become an unbeliever; indeed, he does not incur sin for it. Rather, he is rewarded for his intention and purpose. This is unlike the people of whims and innovations, who declare others unbelievers and innovators because they oppose their whims, innovations, and sects. They are more deserving of those judgments than the people whom they declare unbelievers and innovators." End quote (3/423).
