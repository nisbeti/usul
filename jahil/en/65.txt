Just as error in interpretation prevented Qudamah from being declared an unbeliever, it also prevented Hatib from being declared an unbeliever; both had fought at Badr. Allah, Exalted is He, said: {There is no blame upon you for what you do by mistake} [al-Ahzab: 5].
In explaining Hatib's excuse, Ibn Hajar said: "Hatib's excuse is what he himself mentioned: he did this on the basis of an interpretation that it would cause no harm." [(2)
Ibn Taymiyyah also said: "As for the four obligations, if someone denies the obligation of any one of them after the proof has reached him, he is an unbeliever. The same applies to anyone who denies the prohibition of an outwardly evident matter whose prohibition is widely transmitted, such as indecencies, injustice, lying, wine, and the like. But as for someone to whom the proof has not been established—for example, someone who has only recently entered Islam, or grew up in a remote desert where the laws of Islam did not reach him, or someone who mistakenly thought that those who believe and do righteous deeds are exempt from the prohibition of wine, as did those whom Umar ordered to repent, and others like them—they are to be asked to repent and the proof is to be established against them. If they persist, they then become unbelievers; they are not judged to be unbelievers before that, just as the Companions did not judge Qudamah ibn Maz'un and his companions to be unbelievers when they erred in their interpretation." [(3)
