They stop at the number mentioned there, so their claim is absurd and false, and an assertion without proof; or they refer to something nonexistent in a matter without which, according to their position, accepting the Quran and religion would not be valid. This would invalidate the religion and the Quran altogether and prohibit relying on them. We seek refuge in Allah from that. The same applies to the obligation to obey anyone who received a Quran or Sunnah from those messengers and conveyed it to someone else. For those lands are vast, and it is impossible for each of those messengers to meet every man and woman among them. Rather, he conveys it, and the one to whom he conveys it conveys it in turn, and so on indefinitely, lest some ignorant person say, "This was specific to those messengers."

No two Muslims disagree that if a trustworthy Muslim entered the land of unbelief, called a people to Islam, recited the Quran to them, and taught them the religious laws, they would be obliged to accept it, and proof would thereby have been established against them. The same would apply if the caliph or ruler sent a messenger to one of the kings of unbelief or to one of the nations of unbelief,

calling them to Islam and teaching them the Quran and the laws of religion. There is no difference. No Muslim has ever said that the people of Yemen were entitled to say to Muadh, or to anyone whom the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) sent to any region as a teacher, jurist, and Quran reciter: "Yes, you are the Messenger of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), and the bond of faith is binding

upon us; but we will not accept from you or take from you what you have given us as legal rulings and taught us of the rules of prayer, the cases concerning zakat, and the rest of the religion, from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), nor what you have recited to us of the Quran on his authority, because lying is possible on your part."
