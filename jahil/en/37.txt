A group has now emerged, a new group whose theory, as the researcher has distilled it by examining the books of one of their shaykhs, called Abu Ubayda al-Jazairi, may be summarized as follows. First: They say, "We take our creed from the early generations." According to the statements of theirs that I have traced, they mean by this: "What is meant by knowledge is the Book, the Sunnah, and the reports of the Companions. The statements of the Successors follow the reports of the Companions, as a second rank. As for the words of those who came after them, they are not truly called knowledge among the hadith scholars and the early generations in general." (The Book of Knowledge.) They said, "The statements of the Companions are authoritative in everything, in creed and jurisprudence; likewise, the statement of a Successor is authoritative in matters of creed, reports, divine attributes, and jurisprudence," and they attributed this to the early scholars!!! Second: Even weakly transmitted statements of the Companions are authoritative. He said, "The early generations relied on weak statements of the Companions"??!!! Third: They also considered weak statements of the Successors authoritative if they appear in one of these books. Their leading theorist said, "If you find a report in the books of Ibn al-Mubarak and Ahmad, namely their books on asceticism, or in Ibn Abi Hatim's Tafsir, or in Abdullah's al-Sunna, or in Ibn Abi Shayba's Musannaf, these authors, even when they transmit through a weak chain, do not transmit texts containing objectionable material. The fact that a hadith is mentioned by the well-known authorities I have named is of benefit, even if its chain is weak: it indicates that it has a basis, and its being well known among them means it is sound"??!!! Third: This led them to accept statements attributed to the early generations declaring anyone who denies certain attributes or beliefs to be an unbeliever, and declaring him an unbeliever in absolute terms, as well as declaring anyone who does not declare him an unbeliever to be an unbeliever in absolute terms. These statements are either weakly transmitted from them, God willing, or are general and do not identify anyone in particular, such as, "Whoever says that is an unbeliever." They interpreted these texts to mean that the early scholars who declared those who denied certain attributes to be unbelievers intended every person who said such a thing, with no excuse for anyone who said it, even though most of their statements, as will be shown, are either weak or general. On the basis of the foregoing principles, they said that this was the doctrine of the early generations; so they declared those who interpret the attributes to be unbelievers, and declared those who excuse them to be unbelievers. On this basis, they declared anyone who excuses someone on grounds of ignorance to be an unbeliever, categorically, despite the existence of explicit statements in the very same books on which they relied, transmitted from other early scholars, which stipulate that a person must possess knowledge for the proof to be established against him. There are also other statements from them excusing those who interpret the texts, and consequently excusing those who excuse such interpreters. But they have ignored these, as we shall explain, God willing. On the basis of this chain of principles, they declared all Ash'aris to be unbelievers, including their scholars, even well-known figures such as al-Nawawi, Ibn Hajar, and others. Then they declared as unbelievers those who did not declare these individuals to be unbelievers and excused them, such as Ibn Taymiyya, Ibn al-Qayyim, and Ibn Kathir. After that, they moved on to another issue: they made certain questions of interpretation, such as whether the earth is spherical or flat, matters of definitive doctrine, relying on some earlier statements that it is flat, while also ignoring statements attributed to the early generations that it is spherical, in a highly arbitrary manner. They ruled that the earth is flat, not spherical, and made this one of the definitive doctrines that cannot be interpreted otherwise. Consequently, anyone who disagrees is an unbeliever.
