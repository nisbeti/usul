The text is His saying, Exalted is He: {So take a lesson, O people of insight} [Surat al-Hashr, part of verse 2]. This is incompatible with the permissibility of following another's opinion without evidence. As for consensus, the Companions agreed that it was permissible to disagree with any individual Companion. Abu Bakr and Umar did not object to those who disagreed with them, nor did either of them object to the other in matters over which they differed. As for analogy, a person is able to ascertain the ruling by a method of his own, and therefore following another's opinion without evidence must be forbidden to him, just as it is in matters of the fundamentals of religion."(1)
Al-Shawkani added the following evidence from those who deny that a Companion's opinion is authoritative: "The truth is that it is not authoritative. For Allah, Glorified is He, sent to this community no one but our Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him). We have only one Messenger and one Book, and all of this community are commanded to follow His Book and the Sunnah of His Prophet. In this respect, there is no difference between the Companions and those who came after them. Whoever says that proof in the religion of Allah, Mighty and Majestic, is established by anything other than the Book of Allah and the Sunnah of His Messenger, and what refers back to them, has spoken about Allah's religion without proof and established as part of this Sharia a law that Allah did not command. This is a grave matter." He attributed this position to the majority.(2)
In general, those who hold the opposing view have cited several proofs that might suggest that a Companion's opinion or action is authoritative. Those who hold this view maintain that, in this case, one acts on the Companion's opinion in the specific matter where it differs from the apparent meaning of the text, because the Companion has reported the Prophet's intent (peace and blessings be upon him) concerning that apparent meaning.(3)
Evidence cited by those who regard the opinions and actions of the Companions as authoritative:
First: What was narrated from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), that he said: "My Companions are like stars; whichever of them you follow, you will be guided." Ibn Hazm said: "As for the hadith mentioned, it is false and fabricated."(4)
Al-Shawkani said: "This has never been established."(5) Al-Albani said: "Fabricated."(6)
Second: What is reported from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), that he said: "You must follow my Sunnah and the Sunnah of the rightly guided caliphs after me."(7) Abu Dawud narrated it. Al-Albani said: Al-Tirmidhi called it "a hasan sahih hadith"; al-Harawi said, "This is among the finest hadiths of the people of Syria"; al-Bazzar said, "It is an established, sahih hadith"; and al-Hakim said, "It is sahih and has no defect."(8)
(Response): First, the Companions themselves did not understand this hadith to mean that it was obligatory to follow the practices of the four caliphs even when they contradicted the text. Al-Razi said: "They agreed that it was permissible to disagree with any individual Companion. Abu Bakr and Umar did not object to those who disagreed with them, nor did either of them object to the other in matters over which they differed."(9) Moreover, at most, the hadith calls for following the Sunnah of the caliphs, and no one else's, if we grant that this is what the evidence establishes.
Accordingly, we say: Whoever wishes to follow the Sunnah of the caliphs, which they agreed upon, should not object to someone who departs from their Sunnah and method because the text has become clear to him to the contrary. More than that, the four caliphs agreed that the text takes precedence over the opinion of any one of them. Following their Sunnah collectively means giving precedence to the text over the opinion of each of them. This is made clear by the following examples.
