Everything stated above concerning the lack of probative authority in a Companion's opinion applies all the more to what has been transmitted from the Successors and those after them. However, what I have observed, after examining the statements of their theorists, is that they have not produced a single text from the Companions declaring an interpreter among the people of heretical sects to be an unbeliever, or declaring one who excuses him to be an unbeliever. I cite here two reports, one from Qadi Iyad and the other from Ibn Hazm, showing that the Companions' position was to excuse people on the basis of interpretation.
(What has been transmitted from the Companions concerning declaring those who interpret texts to be unbelievers)(1)((Qadi Iyad said: "People have differed over declaring those who interpret texts to be unbelievers. Once you understand this, the reason for their disagreement will become clear to you, and the correct position is to refrain from declaring them unbelievers.
And refraining from definitively condemning them to perdition, while applying the rulings of Islam to them in matters of retaliation, inheritance, marriage, blood money, funeral prayers, burial in Muslim cemeteries, and all other dealings with them. However, they are dealt with sternly through painful discipline, severe rebuke, and social boycott until they abandon their innovation. This was the practice of the earliest generation toward them. In the time of the Companions and afterward, among the Successors, there arose people who held these views concerning predestination, as well as the Khawarij and the Mu'tazila. Yet they did not disinter their graves or deprive any of them of inheritance; rather, they boycotted them and disciplined them with beatings, exile, or execution, according to their circumstances, because they were disobedient, misguided sinners who committed major sins. (I say: See here the account of Ali and Ibn Abbas with the Khawarij, which I will mention later: Ibn Abbas said to the Khawarij, regarding Aisha, "Are you insulting your mother?" This clearly shows that Ibn Abbas did not declare them unbelievers, since he called Aisha the Mother of the Believers and their mother. Al-Ash'ari has quoted the statement, "All the Khawarij say that the Quran was created" (Maqalat al-Islamiyyin, 108). This is evidence that Ibn Abbas excused those among them who held their view through interpretation; nevertheless, the Companions did not declare them unbelievers on that account.)
