Likewise, the hadith about the man who scattered his own ashes. Abu Muhammad said: "We say to anyone who declares a person an unbeliever merely for holding the same opinion, without the proof having been established against him and his having defied the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), and without finding any unease in himself over what he brought: Tell us, did the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) leave anything of Islam—belief in which is required, and denial of which makes one an unbeliever—without making it clear and calling all people to it? The answer must be, 'No.' Whoever denies this is an unbeliever, without disagreement. Once he acknowledges that, he is asked: Has it ever been reported of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) that he refused to accept the faith of the people of a village or a locality, or of any person who came to him, whether free or enslaved, man or woman, unless that person affirmed that ability precedes the act or accompanies it; or that the Quran is uncreated; or that Allah, Exalted is He, is seen or is not seen; or that He has hearing, sight, life, or any of the other superfluous matters of the theologians, which Satan introduced among them in order to sow enmity and hatred between them? If he claims that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) never invited anyone to embrace Islam without first explaining these matters to them, then he has lied, in contradiction to the consensus of the Muslims throughout the world, and has said what he knows to be false. He has also claimed that all the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) colluded to conceal that this was something the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) did. This is inherently impossible; moreover, it implies that they were unbelievers, since they concealed something without which no one's Islam is complete. If he says that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) never called anyone to any of these things, but that they are contained in the Quran and in his words, then he is told: You are right. It is thereby established that ignorance of any of all these matters would have been unbelief.
