If a person utters or commits an act of disbelief, and some people become aware of it, but only one man, a child, or a woman testifies against him, then this act of disbelief is not established against him, because the required number of witnesses to prove apostasy has not been met in his case. In outward legal judgment he is a Muslim, though in reality he is a disbeliever. ......This third scenario was the usual state of the hypocrites in the time of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him). They would speak words of disbelief among themselves, but none of them would testify against another, as Ibn Taymiyya said: "He would be a hypocrite inwardly, but would be unable to manifest apostasy; rather, he would speak hypocritically with those close to him" (3). Sometimes a Muslim would hear them and testify to what he had heard, but this was not sufficient to establish it. Thus Zayd ibn Arqam testified against Abdullah ibn Ubayy that he had said, "If we return to Medina, the mightier will surely expel the humbler," as is established in the Sahih. Although revelation confirmed that Zayd was truthful, the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) did not hold them accountable on the basis of revelation, but rather according to the legally prescribed means of proof. Moreover, much of what the hypocrites said was ambiguous in meaning, not explicit, as Allah, Exalted is He, says: {And you will surely recognize them by the tone of their speech} Muhammad: 30. The tone of speech is what is understood from its meaning without being stated explicitly; this was mentioned by al-Qurtubi.

This is how the scholars answered the question: Why did the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) not kill the hypocrites? Ibn Taymiyya, may Allah have mercy on him, said: "Most of them did not say things amounting to disbelief that could be established against them by evidence; rather, they would outwardly profess Islam, and their hypocrisy would sometimes be recognized by a word that someone heard"
