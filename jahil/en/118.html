(50) Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Muhammad ibn Ubayd Allah ibn Abd al-Samad ibn al-Muhtadi Billah, Abu al-Husayn, son of al-Muhtadi Billah. Al-Khatib said: "He was trustworthy and distinguished." History of Baghdad (11/108–109).

(51) Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Husayn ibn Abd Allah ibn Harun, Abu al-Husayn al-Daqqaq, known as the nephew of Maymi. Al-Khatib said: "He was trustworthy, reliable, religious, and virtuous."

History of Baghdad (3/88–89).

(52) Ali ibn Muhammad ibn Said, Abu al-Hasan al-Mawsili, who settled in Baghdad. Abu Nuaym said: "He is a liar." Ibn al-Furat said: "He was confused and not commendable."

History of Baghdad (12/82); History of Islam (26/194).

(53) I have found no biographical entry for him. Al-Khatib mentioned in the biography of Ali ibn Muhammad al-Mawsili that he narrated from a group of unknown people, so perhaps he was one of them. History of Baghdad (12/82).

(54) Shaheen ibn al-Sumayda, Abu Salama al-Abdi. Ibn Abi Yala said: "He transmitted some things from our Imam." He did not mention any criticism or commendation of him, so he is virtually unknown.

Classes of the Hanbalis (1/172).

(55) Al-Husayn ibn Ali ibn Yazid, Abu Ali al-Karabisi. Al-Khatib said: "He was perceptive, learned, and a jurist, and he had many works on jurisprudence and legal theory that attest to his sound understanding and extensive knowledge. Reports from al-Karabisi are very rare. This is because Ahmad ibn Hanbal used to criticize him over the question of the wording, and al-Karabisi also used to criticize Ahmad. For this reason, people refrained from taking reports from him." History of Baghdad (8/63).

(56) This chain of transmission contains two unknown narrators and a liar, so discard it.

(57) This is a continuation of Imam Ahmad's statement through the preceding chain of transmission. Its chain contains two unknown narrators and a liar, so discard it.
