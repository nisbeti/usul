Issues of beliefs: If the disagreement concerns the attributes of Allah, Mighty and Exalted, then he is an unbeliever; if it concerns anything less than that, then he is a sinner. A group held that no Muslim becomes an unbeliever or a sinner because of a statement he makes concerning belief or a legal opinion, and that anyone who exercises independent reasoning on any such matter and adopts as his religion what he considers to be the truth is rewarded in every case: if he is right, he receives two rewards, and if he is wrong, one reward. This is the view of Ibn Abi Layla, Abu Hanifa, al-Shafi'i, Sufyan al-Thawri, and Dawud ibn Ali (may Allah be pleased with them all). It is also the view of everyone among the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) whose position on this issue we know; we know of no disagreement among them on this at all, except for the disagreement we have mentioned concerning declaring an unbeliever one who deliberately abandons prayer until its time has passed, or fails to pay zakat, or abandons the pilgrimage, or fails to fast Ramadan, or drinks wine. Those who declared people unbelievers on account of disagreements in matters of belief argued from various points, which we shall present, if Allah, Mighty and Exalted, wills.

Abu Muhammad said: They have cited a hadith from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) that the Qadariyya and the Murji'a are the Magians of this community, and another hadith stating that this community will split into seventy-odd sects, all of them in the Fire except one, which will be in Paradise.

Abu Muhammad said: These are two hadiths that are not sound at all in terms of their chains of transmission. Anything of this kind is not proof even according to those who accept solitary reports, so how much less according to those who do not accept them? They cited as evidence the established report from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him): "Whoever says to his brother, 'O disbeliever,' one of them has incurred disbelief."
