And severe rebuke and ostracism, until they turn back from their innovation. This was the practice of the earliest generation toward them. The Companions' position on declaring those who interpret texts erroneously to be unbelievers was that, during the time of the Companions and afterward among the Successors, people arose who held these views of the Qadariyya, and the Kharijites and Mu'tazilites appeared. Yet they neither exhumed their graves nor deprived any of them of inheritance. Rather, they ostracized them and disciplined them with beating, exile, and execution, according to their circumstances, because they were dissolute, misguided, disobedient sinners who committed major sins. (I say: See here the hadith about Ali and the Kharijites.)

(Faulty interpretation is not accepted)

Al-Qadi Iyad quoted in al-Shifa the scholars' statement: "Claiming an interpretation of an explicit expression is not accepted." End quote (2/217).

Shaykh al-Islam stated this explicitly in al-Sarim al-Maslul, p. 527.

Thus, if someone is known and notorious for his heresy and manipulation of the proofs of the Sharia, or engages in acts of unbelief that are explicit and clear and admit of no interpretation, his claim of interpretation is not accepted. There is no ijtihad or interpretation that makes engaging in explicit unbelief permissible, for no unbeliever is without faulty interpretations with which he tries to patch up his unbelief.
