The Kharijites are among the worst of creation, the dogs of Hellfire. They must be fought without the need to establish proof against them on account of a form of interpretation. This is like the Dajjal: the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) described him by physical characteristics and actions, so when we find them, we know that he is the Dajjal—for example, that he is one-eyed, has tightly curled hair, and has a wiped-out left eye. The apparent meaning is the first possibility, and Allah knows best, for several reasons.
First: He (peace and blessings be upon him) informed us that they must be killed. It is known that if killing is on account of disbelief, it can only take place after proof has been established. Likewise, if it is on account of armed rebellion, it can only take place after it is known that the one who rebels against the imam has no valid interpretation. Since the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) ordered that they be killed—and this includes any one of them who can be apprehended—we know that proof was certainly established against them. The apparent meaning of the hadiths is that they are disbelievers, for several reasons.
First: He informed us that they "leave the religion and then do not return to it." They deliberately opposed revelation, and the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) knew this through revelation. If you say that they may have been ignorant people acting on an interpretation, you fall into the very position you sought to avoid: if they were acting on an interpretation, then they were ignorant in a compounded way. The fact that the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) judged that they would leave the religion and that they were the worst of creation, while, according to your view, they were ignorant, indicates that ignorance is no excuse, and that whoever falls into disbelief through interpretation or ignorance is a disbeliever. Al-Tabari used this as evidence for declaring those who act on interpretations to be disbelievers, and the contemporary group that declares such people disbelievers and does not accept ignorance as an excuse followed him in this. You certainly do not hold that view. It is therefore clear that they were deliberate opponents, because one who errs is excused, in light of the words of the Exalted: "There is no blame upon you for what you do by mistake, but only for what your hearts intend." And he (peace and blessings be upon him) knew this through revelation.
