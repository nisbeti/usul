And it was sound in his view. If someone disagrees with him, exercising independent reasoning about something whose true basis has not been made clear to him, then he is mistaken, excused, and rewarded once, as he (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "When a judge exercises independent reasoning and is correct, he receives two rewards; if he is mistaken, he receives one reward." Anyone who holds a belief, makes a statement, or performs an act is a judge with respect to that matter. If he disagrees with him through his actions, while knowingly opposing the truth and believing otherwise than what he acts upon, then he is a sinful believer. If he knowingly opposes him in his words or in his heart, then he is a disbeliever and an idolater. This applies equally to matters of belief and to legal opinions, in light of the texts we have cited. This is the view of Ishaq ibn Rahawayh and others, and it is what we hold. Success is granted by Allah, Exalted is He." End quote. However, Ibn Hazm stipulated that an interpretation must have some basis in language or the Sharia, must not contradict what is necessarily known of the religion—which is what consensus means in his view—and must not be a stubborn rejection of the proof after it has been established against the person.
He said in al-Muhalla bi al-Athar (11/343):
"Whoever adopts an interpretation in which he is mistaken, but does not thereby contradict consensus, does not intend to oppose the Quran and the ruling of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), deliberately opposing them, nor stubbornly reject them after proof has been established against him; or whoever sets out seeking worldly dominance, but does not waylay travelers, shed blood wantonly, or take property unjustly—such a person is the rebel, between whom and those against whom he has rebelled reconciliation should be made, in accordance with the verse concerning the rebels and with what he (peace and blessings be upon him) said about the Khawarij emerging between two groups of his community: one of them is rebellious, and it is the group that kills Ammar; the other is closer to the truth. He (peace and blessings be upon him) praised those who brought reconciliation between them.
As we have narrated through al-Bukhari: Sadqah related to us; Ibn Uyaynah related to us; Abu Musa related to us, from al-Hasan, who heard Abu Bakrah say: 'I heard the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him)...'
