He said: The following are the reasons for declaring the Ash'aris unbelievers, without excusing them on the basis of interpretation:

"First: Denying Allah Most High's exaltedness, which is the most heinous of them.

Ibn Taymiyya said regarding Allah's exaltedness: 'For this reason, the Salaf were unanimous in declaring whoever denied this an unbeliever, since in their view it is known by necessity as part of the religion.' Al-Dar' 7/26." End quote.

Comment: It is astonishing that they cite Ibn Taymiyya here, since, as noted above, Ibn Taymiyya did not declare the Ash'aris individually to be unbelievers, nor did he transmit such a view from the Salaf, as they do when declaring individuals unbelievers. Rather, he transmitted a general, unrestricted ruling from the Salaf here. Ibn Taymiyya also said: "An unrestricted declaration of unbelief, like an unrestricted threat, does not entail declaring a particular person an unbeliever until the proof has been established against him, the rejection of which constitutes unbelief." And: "Ibn Taymiyya says: 'As for declaring someone an unbeliever, the correct view is that whoever among the community of Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him) exercises independent judgment and seeks the truth, but errs, is not an unbeliever; rather, his error is forgiven.'"
