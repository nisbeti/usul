The Ansar had forgotten his statement, "The leaders are from Quraysh." Anas related it, and Ubadah ibn al-Samit related something that indicates the same. They would only abandon their own reasoning when something from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) had reached them. Abu Hurayra relates that they, may Allah be pleased with them, were occupied with their wealth and trade, whereas he himself kept close to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) and witnessed what they did not. We have mentioned this hadith in the chapter on consensus in this book of ours, in a section entitled "Refuting the claim that when the majority agree on an opinion and one person disagrees with them, his opinion is disregarded." Our having mentioned it there makes it unnecessary to repeat it here.

If we find that a Companion was unaware of the Sunnah, or that it reached him and he interpreted it in various ways—as they did regarding the prohibition of domestic donkeys—al-Bukhari related that they differed: one said they were forbidden because they ate dung; another, because they had not been subjected to the fifth; another, because he feared the riding animals would perish. Some said, rather, that they were categorically forbidden at that time. Ali said: All of this is false except the view that they were categorically forbidden. A text has come prohibiting them in themselves, because they are impure; Anas related this. Since all that we have mentioned is sound, and blind imitation is entirely invalid, what reason is there to follow a Companion's opinion when it does not conflict with a report—let alone when it is accompanied by a conflict with a report? In the chapter on invalidating blind imitation in this book, we have recorded what they issued as legal opinions, though the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) informed them that it was not so. Ali said: Whenever those who evade the truth cling to things of this sort and the like, when confusion overwhelms them, they are the furthest of all people from it.

Ibn Hazm said:

"Al-Ihkam fi Usul al-Ahkam by Ibn Hazm" (6/76):

"As for what they rely on, namely the report that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said to Abu Bakr and Umar, 'Were it not for your disagreement with me, I would not have disagreed with you,' the first point is that this report is unsound. Even if it were sound, it would be proof against following their opinions, because what it says is that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) prevented them from acting on their opinions in worldly matters. It is therefore incumbent upon us to follow him (peace and blessings be upon him) and not to adopt their opinions in matters of the Sharia. This is clear. As for his saying (peace and blessings be upon him), 'You must adhere to my Sunnah and the Sunnah of the rightly guided caliphs,' we know that he (peace and blessings be upon him) would not command what is beyond one's ability. We find that the rightly guided caliphs after him (peace and blessings be upon him) differed greatly. Thus, there must be one of three possibilities, with no fourth: either we adopt everything over which they differed—and this is impossible and beyond our ability, since it entails accepting a thing and its opposite. It is not possible, for example, to give the grandfather a share to the exclusion of the siblings, following the view of Abu Bakr and Aisha, while giving him only one-third and the remainder to the siblings, following Umar's view, or giving him one-sixth and the remainder to the siblings, following Ali's school of thought, and likewise in every matter over which they differed. This possibility is therefore invalid.
