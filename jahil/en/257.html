The pressing question here, then, is: why? If the very same reason for which you declared Ibn Taymiyya an unbeliever—namely, that "he excused people who had no valid interpretation, while himself interpreting"—is also present in this man who excused Ibn Taymiyya, since "he excused a man who had no valid interpretation, while himself interpreting," then this is a contradiction. You would therefore have to declare everyone an unbeliever and accept an infinite regress, or excuse everyone because the existence of a doubt removes the ruling of unbelief. The latter seems more likely to me, and Allah knows best.

(Are the Ash'aris and Maturidis Jahmis?)

It is noticeable that most of the texts they cite from people below the level of the Companions—for they have not cited anything from the Companions at all—that declare certain deeds to be unbelief occur in discussions of the createdness of the Quran or of the Jahmiyya. The question now is: are the Ash'aris, upon whom they apply these texts, Jahmis, or do they believe that the Quran is created, such that their use of the statements of those they cite is valid? As we have already shown, most of these statements are weak, and most come from people outside the first three generations, who fall outside the meaning of the Salaf.

Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyya, may Allah have mercy on him, said in al-Tis'iniyya (1/265–271): "The Jahmiyya are of three degrees:

1. The worst of them are the extremists, who deny Allah's names and attributes. If they call Him by any of His most beautiful names, they say that it is metaphorical; in reality, according to them, He is neither living, knowing, powerful, hearing, seeing, nor speaking, and He does not speak. The scholars have likewise described the true meaning of their position, as Imam Ahmad did in what he included in al-Radd ala al-Zanadiqa wa al-Jahmiyya, and as Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari did in al-Ibana and Maqalat al-Islamiyyin.

This position, which is the position of the extremists who truly deny the names, is that of the esoteric Qarmatians and those who preceded them among their brethren, the Sabean philosophers.

2. The second degree of Jahmism is the Jahmism of the Mu'tazila and those like them. They generally affirm Allah's most beautiful names, but deny His attributes. They also do not affirm all of Allah's most beautiful names on the basis of ..."
