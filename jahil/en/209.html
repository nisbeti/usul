His companion said to him, as he was conversing with him, "Have you disbelieved in the One who created you from dust, then from a drop of fluid, then fashioned you into a man?"} [al-Kahf: 35–37] His believing companion said: {But as for me, He is Allah, my Lord, and I associate none with my Lord.} [al-Kahf: 38] He concluded his admonition and conversation with his companion by saying that he did not associate partners with Allah as his companion did: {and I associate none with my Lord.} [al-Kahf: 38] This shows that the man who owned the two gardens—that is, the two orchards—when he doubted the resurrection and the raising of the dead, associated partners with Allah, Mighty and Majestic. That is why his believing companion said to him: "You have disbelieved and associated partners with Allah, but I associate none with my Lord." The conclusion of the story also confirms this, for at the end of the verses it says: {And his fruits were encompassed, so he began wringing his hands over what he had spent on it, while it lay fallen upon its trellises, and he said, "Would that I had not associated anyone with my Lord!"} [al-Kahf: 42] What was it that he associated with Allah? Apparently, he denied the resurrection and the raising of the dead. So where was the association of partners? Here lies the point: association with Allah means that every disbeliever, whatever the reason for his disbelief, has taken his own desire as his god. Thus, association with Allah applies to every kind of disbelief into which a person falls. This is why we said that there is a religiously established meaning: every disbelief is association with Allah. The context we have mentioned to you is the clearest proof of this.

Accordingly, it is not valid to reconcile the previously mentioned verse, {Indeed, Allah does not forgive associating partners with Him} [al-Nisa: 48], with this hadith in the manner we related earlier. For, after this explanation, the meaning of the verse is: Allah does not forgive disbelief in Him—that is, whether the disbelief is linguistically termed association with Allah or not.
