He said in his fatwas: "Those who declared the Kharijites and the extremist Rafidites unbelievers argued that their declaring the leading Companions unbelievers entailed rejecting the Prophet's testimony that they were destined for Paradise (peace and blessings be upon him)." He said: "In my view, this is a sound argument." He said: "Those who did not declare them unbelievers argued that judging them to be unbelievers would require that they had prior, definitive knowledge of the testimony mentioned." This is open to question, for we know with certainty that those whom they declared unbelievers were deemed upright until the time of their deaths, and that is sufficient for us to hold that those who declared them unbelievers are themselves unbelievers. This is supported by the hadith, "If a man says to his brother, 'You are an unbeliever,' then one of the two has incurred it." In Muslim's wording: "Whoever accuses a Muslim of unbelief, or says, 'Enemy of Allah,' it will rebound upon him." He said: "It has been established that these people accuse a group of unbelief, people whose faith we know with certainty. Therefore, in accordance with the implication of the Lawgiver's report, they must be judged unbelievers. This is similar to what they have said about one who prostrates to an idol and the like, even though there is no explicit denial in such an act, after they had defined unbelief as denial. If they argue that there is consensus that whoever does that is an unbeliever, we say: These reports concerning these people likewise entail that they are unbelievers, even if they did not hold with certainty that those whom they declared unbelievers were deemed upright. Nor will their general belief in Islam and their performance of the obligatory acts save them from being judged unbelievers, just as that would not save one who prostrates to an idol." I say: Among those who inclined toward some aspects of this discussion was al-Tabari in his Tahdhib. After citing the hadiths in this chapter, he said: "It refutes the view of those who say that no one among the people of the qibla leaves Islam, after becoming subject to its rulings, except by knowingly intending to leave it. This view is invalidated by the hadith's words: 'They speak the truth and recite the Quran, yet they pass out of Islam and do not cling to any part of it.' It is well known that they only deemed the blood and property of Muslims lawful because they erred in their interpretation of Quranic verses, understanding them in a sense other than what was intended."
