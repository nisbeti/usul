This hadith is clear evidence for what we are establishing: this was a judge who issued a ruling, but issued it out of ignorance. His ignorance here was no excuse; rather, Allah, Exalted is He, will punish him, because his duty was to remain silent about what he did not know, not to invent a new ruling. The hadith thus shows that ignorance is not an excuse in every circumstance.

Al-Nawawi said in his commentary on Sahih Muslim, in his discussion of the hadith, "When a judge rules and exercises independent reasoning, then gets it wrong, he has one reward": The scholars said: The Muslims are unanimously agreed that this hadith concerns a judge who is learned and qualified to judge. If he gets it right, he has two rewards: one for his independent reasoning and one for getting it right. If he gets it wrong, he has one reward for his independent reasoning. They said: As for someone who is not qualified to judge, it is not permissible for him to judge. If he does so, he earns no reward; rather, he is sinful, and his ruling is not enforceable, whether or not it accords with the truth. This is because his getting it right is merely coincidental, not based on a legal foundation. He is therefore disobedient in all his rulings, whether or not they are correct; all of them are rejected, and he is excused in none of them. End quote.
