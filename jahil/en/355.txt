He said in Minhaj al-Sunna (5/158): "The Kharijites declare the people of the community to be unbelievers. Likewise, most of the Mu'tazilites declare those who oppose them to be unbelievers,(10) as do most of the Rafidites. Those who do not declare them unbelievers deem them sinful. Likewise, most of the people of heretical doctrines introduce an opinion and declare those who oppose them in it to be unbelievers. The people of the Sunna follow the truth from their Lord, which the Messenger brought, and do not declare those who oppose them in it to be unbelievers. Rather, they know the truth better and are more merciful toward creation, just as Allah described the Muslims when He said: {You are the best community brought forth for mankind}."

He also said: "Among the people of innovation are those who have faith inwardly and outwardly, but also have ignorance and injustice, so that they err in some of the Sunna. Such a person is neither an unbeliever nor a hypocrite. He may then commit aggression and injustice that make him sinful or disobedient; he may be mistaken in his interpretation, with his error forgiven; and, alongside that, he may possess such faith and piety as grant him a share of Allah's friendship, in proportion to his faith and piety." End quote. Majmu al-Fatawa (Dar Ibn Hazm edition), 3/220.

Ibn al-Qayyim (may Allah have mercy on him) said in Zad al-Ma'ad: "Among the principles is that if polytheists, people of innovation and immorality, rebels, or oppressors seek something that honors one of the inviolable matters of Allah, they should be granted it, given it, and assisted in it, even if they are denied other things. They are to be helped in what honors the inviolable matters of Allah, not in their unbelief or rebellion, and they are to be prevented from anything beyond that. Whoever seeks assistance in something beloved to Allah and pleasing to Him should be granted it, whoever he may be, so long as assisting him in that beloved thing does not result in something even more hateful to Allah."
