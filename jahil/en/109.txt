Ibn Abi Shayba related in his Musannaf (15/327), from Kathir ibn Numayr, who said: "While I was at Friday prayer and Ali ibn Abi Talib was on the pulpit, a man came and said, 'There is no judgment but Allah's.' Then another stood up and said, 'There is no judgment but Allah's.' Then they rose from different parts of the mosque, declaring Allah's judgment. He gestured to them with his hand: 'Sit down. Yes, there is no judgment but Allah's—a word of truth by which falsehood is sought. Allah's judgment awaits you. For now, you have three guarantees from me, so long as you remain with us: we will not prevent you from using Allah's mosques to remember His name; we will not withhold from you any share of the spoils so long as your hands are with ours; and we will not fight you unless you fight us.' Then he resumed his sermon." Its editor said: "Its chain of transmission is sound." Al-Tabari also related the hadith (5/73) through the route of Abu Mikhnaf.

Al-Tabari (5/73) and Ibn Abi Shayba (15/312) related from Abu Razin, who said: "When arbitration took place at Siffin and the Kharijites separated from Ali, they returned in opposition to him, while Ali remained with his army until he entered Kufa with the people and his army. They went on to Harura with their army. Ali sent Ibn Abbas to them, and he spoke with them, but his words did not persuade them. Then Ali went out to them and spoke with them until they and Ali agreed to accept a settlement. They returned and entered Kufa, both he and they having agreed to it. They stayed for two days or thereabouts. He said: Al-Ash'ath ibn Qays came in—he used to visit Ali—and said, 'People are saying that you have recanted your disbelief for their sake.' The next day, Friday, Ali ascended the pulpit, praised and extolled Allah, and delivered a sermon in which he spoke of them and their separation.
