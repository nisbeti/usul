But this would be a departure from Islam, because it would mean that the religion of Allah, Exalted is He, was left to our choice, so that each of us could forbid whatever he wished and permit whatever he wished, and one of us could forbid what another permits. Allah, Exalted is He, says: {Forbidden to you are carrion, blood, the flesh of swine, and that over which any name other than Allah's has been invoked, and the strangled, the beaten to death, the fallen to death, the gored to death, and that which wild beasts have eaten—unless you have slaughtered it—and what has been slaughtered
...over the stone altars, and that you seek to determine your fate by means of divining arrows. That is wickedness. Today those who disbelieve have despaired of your religion, so do not fear them; fear Me. Today I have perfected your religion for you, completed My favor upon you, and chosen Islam as your religion. But whoever is compelled by severe hunger, without inclining toward sin—then Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful} And His saying, Exalted is He: {Divorce is twice; then either retain her honorably or release her kindly. It is not lawful for you to take anything of what you have given them, unless both fear that they cannot maintain the limits set by Allah. If you fear that they cannot maintain the limits set by Allah, then there is no blame upon either of them for what she gives up to secure her release. These are the limits set by Allah, so do not transgress them. Whoever transgresses the limits set by Allah—it is they who are the wrongdoers} And His saying, Exalted is He: {Obey Allah and His Messenger, and do not dispute, lest you lose heart and your strength depart. Be patient; indeed, Allah is with the patient} refutes this corrupt view and establishes that whatever was forbidden then remains forbidden until the Day of Resurrection; whatever was obligatory then remains obligatory until the Day of Resurrection; and whatever was lawful then remains lawful until the Day of Resurrection.
Also, if this were so, then when we followed the opinion of one of them, we would necessarily have abandoned the opinion of the other. In that case, we would not be following their practice; rather, we would have fallen into opposition to the hadith mentioned, as they themselves did, whether they wished it or not. I recall that this happened with a mufti we had in al-Andalus, who was ignorant. His custom was to be preceded by two men upon whom the issuing of fatwas in that period depended. Beneath their fatwas, he would write, "I hold the same opinion as the two shaykhs." It so happened that those two shaykhs disagreed. When he wrote beneath their fatwas what we have mentioned, someone present said to him, "The two shaykhs have disagreed." He replied, "And I disagree whenever they disagree." Abu Muhammad said: Since these two possibilities have been disproved, only the third remains: to follow what they unanimously agreed upon. That can only be what all the Companions, may Allah be pleased with them, agreed upon together, and their following the practices of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) and adhering to them. Furthermore, when the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) commanded that the practices of the rightly guided caliphs be followed, there must necessarily be one of two possibilities: either he (peace and blessings be upon him) permitted them to establish practices other than his own—and no Muslim would say this; whoever permits it has disbelieved and apostatized, and his life and property are lawful—or else the matter is otherwise. For the entire religion consists either of what is obligatory or what is not obligatory, and either what is forbidden or what is lawful; there is absolutely no other category in religion. Whoever permits the rightly guided caliphs to have a practice that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) did not establish has permitted them to forbid something that was lawful during his lifetime until he died, or to make lawful something the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) had forbidden, or to make obligatory a duty the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) had not made obligatory, or to waive a duty the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) had imposed.
And he did not abandon it until he died. Anyone who permits any of these possibilities is an unbeliever and an idolater, by the consensus of the entire Muslim community, without any disagreement. And Allah Most High is the source of success. This possibility has thus been disproved, praise be to Allah. As for the possibility that he commanded us to follow them in their adherence to his Sunnah (peace and blessings be upon him), this is precisely what we say: this hadith admits no other interpretation at all. Nothing remains except this, and their contention has fallen away. There is nothing in the world without a textually established Sunnah concerning it. We have explained this in the chapter on invalidating analogy in this book. And success is from Allah, Exalted is He.
They argued on the basis of what was reported to us by Abd Allah ibn Rabi, who said: Muhammad ibn Muawiya related to us, saying: Ahmad ibn Shuaib related to us, saying: Muhammad ibn Bashshar related to us, saying: Abu Umar related to us, from Sufyan—that is, al-Thawri—from al-Shaybani—that is, Abu Ishaq—from al-Shabi, from Shurayh, that he wrote to Umar asking him a question. Umar wrote back to him: "Judge according to what is in the Book of Allah. If it is not in the Book of Allah, then according to the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him). If it is in neither the Book of Allah nor the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), then judge according to what the righteous have judged. If it is in neither the Book of Allah nor the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), and the righteous have not judged concerning it, then, if you wish, go ahead, and if you wish, hold back. I consider holding back to be better for you. Peace be upon you." Abu Muhammad said: This report is against them, not in their favor, because Umar did not say, "according to what some of the righteous have judged"; rather, he said, "according to what the righteous have judged." That means the consensus of all the righteous. This report also shows that Umar permitted refraining from judgment by analogy and preferred doing so. They may also be told, regarding their argument based on the report commanding adherence to the Sunnah of the rightly guided caliphs, that this is evidence against them: the Sunnah of all the rightly guided caliphs, without any disagreement among them, was not to imitate anyone, nor to imitate one another, but to seek out the Sunnahs of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) wherever they found them, then turn to them and act upon them. Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) strongly rebuked a man who asked him a question about the pilgrimage. When Umar gave him a ruling, the man said, "The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) gave me this very ruling." Umar struck him with his whip and said, "You asked me about something on which the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) had already given a ruling, and I might have contradicted him." We have transmitted this through Abd al-Razzaq. Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) also said: "Opinion from us is a burden we take upon ourselves, whereas opinion from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) was truth."
