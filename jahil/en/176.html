Ibn Hazm said: "Another proof is that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) sent a messenger to every king among the kings of the earth who were neighbors to the lands of the Arabs. Some of those who disagree with us have objected to this, saying that delegations and merchants came at the Prophet's command, so he did not rely on the messenger alone. Abu Muhammad said: This is sophistry and deception, which can only persuade the weak. We have no doubt that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) did not limit the aforementioned messengers to informing people of his appearance and miracles, which were reported by delegations and travelers. Rather, he instructed them to teach those who embraced Islam the laws of Islam and the rulings on worship and other matters, none of which had been conveyed by delegations and travelers. The sending of these messengers is well known and reliably transmitted by numerous narrators. Thus, the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) required every king and his subjects to accept what the messenger sent to them informed them of regarding the laws of their religion. Ali said: The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) likewise sent Muadh to al-Jund and parts of Yemen, and Abu Musa to another region, namely Zabid and elsewhere;

and Abu Bakr to lead the people in the pilgrimage, and Abu Ubayda to Najran, and Ali to Yemen as a judge. Each of these went to a region to teach its people the laws of Islam. He likewise sent a commander to every region that embraced Islam, whether far from him or near, such as the farthest parts of Yemen, Bahrain, and all the other regions, districts, and tribes that embraced Islam. To each group he sent a man to teach them their religion and the Quran, give them legal rulings on matters of their religion, judge disputes among them, and convey to them what Allah required of them.
