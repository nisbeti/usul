Others distinguished between the fundamentals and the subsidiary matters by saying that the fundamentals are matters whose denial constitutes disbelief, such as divine oneness, prophethood, the Resurrection, and affirming the divine attributes; while subsidiary matters are those whose denial does not constitute disbelief, such as the obligation to recite al-Fatiha in prayer, the requirement of composure, the obligation to wipe the entire head in ablution, and the like. This distinction is neither universally applicable nor reversible, for many subsidiary matters are such that denying them constitutes disbelief, while many fundamental matters are such that denying them does not, as has been explained above.
Moreover, declaring someone a disbeliever is a ruling of the Sharia: a disbeliever is one whom Allah and His Messenger have declared a disbeliever. Disbelief is the denial of something known to have been brought by the Messenger, whether it belongs to the matters you call doctrinal or practical. Whoever denies something brought by the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him), after knowing that he brought it, is a disbeliever, whether the matter is subtle or weighty in religion.
Others distinguished between the fundamentals and subsidiary matters by saying that the fundamentals concern reports, while subsidiary matters concern commands. This distinction is no different from the distinctions mentioned above, and it too is unsound, for the servant is charged with affirming both, through knowledge, faith, action, love, acceptance, allegiance, and enmity, as stated above.
Others distinguished between them by saying that the fundamentals are matters in which following another's opinion is impermissible, while it is permissible in subsidiary matters. Besides being an impermissible circular argument, they may be asked: "In what matters is following another's opinion permissible?" They would answer: "In subsidiary matters." And in what matters is it impermissible? "In the fundamentals." This distinction is also unsound both in its application and its converse, for following another's opinion is impermissible in many subsidiary matters, such as the obligation of purification, prayer, and zakat, and the prohibition of wine, usury, indecencies, and injustice. For whoever does not know that the final word is cut off here.
