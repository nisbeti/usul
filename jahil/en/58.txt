Al-Zarkashi said in al-Bahr al-Muhit: "If a Successor states an opinion on a matter in which there is no room for analogical reasoning, it is not accorded the status of a Companion in our view, contrary to al-Sam'ani, as mentioned earlier. The author of al-Ghaya, among the Hanbalis, said: 'If someone gets up from a night's sleep and dips his hand into a vessel before washing it, al-Hasan al-Basri held that its purifying quality is lost. This contradicts analogical reasoning. When a Successor says something like this, it is authoritative, since it is most likely that he reported it on the authority of the Companions, or on the basis of a text established for him.' The author of al-Musawwadah said: 'The apparent meaning of Ahmad's words and those of our companions is that this is not taken into consideration; rather, it is treated like his own independent judgments.' End quote." At the very most, and by way of conceding as much as possible, if the Successors' opinions are authoritative, then they must stop at the Successors. Why, then, do they not cite those who came after the Successors in support of their position? I know of no one who holds that the opinions of the Successors of the Successors are authoritative. And if their argument is that matters of creed leave no room for personal opinion, then this must lead to an infinite regress here; they would have to regard the opinion of every scholar who has addressed a matter of creed, right up to our own time, as authoritative, since such matters are not based on personal opinion. Yet they would never accept this.
The era of the Successors of the Successors is the era following that of the Successors (may Allah be pleased with them). It began around the year 170 AH.
Al-Suyuti said: "The sounder view is that a generation—that is, a century—is not defined by a fixed period. The generation of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) comprised the Companions, and their period extended from the beginning of his mission until the death of the last of the Companions, a span of one hundred and twenty years. The generation of the Successors lasted from one hundred years to around seventy, and the generation of the Successors of the Successors extended from then until around the year 220." End quote.
On the basis of this division, they would have to refrain from citing the statements of scholars who came after the year 170. I have said all this only by way of concession.
