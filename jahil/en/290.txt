This is what Allah, Exalted is He, says: {Alif Lam Mim. The Romans have been defeated} up to His words {And on that day the believers will rejoice in Allah's victory}. Sufyan said: I heard that they prevailed over them on the day of Badr.
This is a good, rare hadith. We know it only through the hadith of Sufyan al-Thawri, from Habib ibn Abi Amra.)) The apparent indication of this hadith is that rejoicing at the victory of a disbeliever is not disbelief. There is no doubt that his victory will lead to the establishment of his unbelieving rule on earth. But since the Romans were less unbelieving than the Persians, the Muslims rejoiced at the reduction of disbelief in the world, and wished for the Romans to prevail, not because of their disbelief, but because their disbelief was less than that of the Persians. Here arises the question of weighing good deeds against bad deeds. Allah knows best.
(Some matters in which the person involved has an interpretation excusing him from being declared a disbeliever)
Drawn from al-Jami and al-Thalathiniyya. My purpose in presenting these matters is only to show that the person involved has an interpretation that prevents him from being declared a disbeliever, even if, in my view, some of them involve sin. I quote here the statements of those who hold these views.
(Declaring someone a disbeliever on account of what his words necessarily entail and lead to)
The author of al-Thalathiniyya says: "Ibn Hazm, may Allah have mercy on him, says: 'As for declaring people disbelievers on account of what their statements lead to, that is an error, because it attributes a lie to one's opponent and makes him say what he did not say. Even if it necessarily follows from his position, nothing has been established beyond contradiction, and contradiction is not disbelief. Indeed, he has done well, since he has avoided disbelief ...'" He continued: "Thus it is established that he is not declared a disbeliever.
