Third: Their claim that the reason for declaring the one who excuses someone acting on an interpretation to be an unbeliever is that he has opposed consensus. I do not know what they mean by this. If they mean that consensus arose among the early generations on declaring someone acting on an interpretation to be an unbeliever, then we have already cited the well-known disagreement on this matter, so there is no need to repeat it here. Moreover, they have recently contradicted themselves, since they acknowledge that there was disagreement among the early generations about excusing an interpretive Kharijite. So where, then, is the consensus?
If they say that the early generations agreed against the view of the Ash'aris and others who interpret the attributes out of ignorance, the answer is: yes. Likewise, we know of no disagreement among them about rejecting and repudiating what the Kharijites held concerning the matters mentioned, nor about rejecting what the Qadaris held. Yet despite these sects' opposition, disagreement arose among the early generations about declaring them unbelievers, and disagreement arose over whether or not to excuse them. This was because some members of these sects might not regard this consensus as sound, might consider it speculative, or might point to matters concerning the divine attributes for which almost nothing at all was transmitted from the Companions, either about the attributes themselves or their interpretation. They might also have a doubt based on a verse or hadith, as happened with the Ash'aris regarding the words of the Exalted: {There is nothing like Him}; this led them to interpret the attributes.
