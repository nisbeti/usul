Because they are now warding off the worst kind of killing from themselves, and killing those polytheists is lawful for them. There is nothing wrong with undertaking something lawful when necessity is established because of coercion; indeed, it may even be obligatory, as in eating carrion and drinking wine ... If they were to say to the prisoners, "Fight with us against our enemy from among another people at war, on condition that we release you when our war is over," and they believed that they were sincere, there would be nothing wrong with their fighting alongside them.
Because by doing so they are warding off captivity from themselves. This is no less permissible than when they fear for their lives at the hands of those polytheists: just as they may undertake it in that case, so too may they do so here.
If it is asked: How can this be permissible for them when it strengthens them against the Muslims? For if they defeat their enemy and feel secure from them, they will turn to fighting the Muslims. They may also take their horses and weapons and use them to strengthen themselves against the Muslims.
We say: That is speculative, whereas the deliverance they gain now from captivity at the hands of the polytheists by fighting alongside them is certain, so this consideration takes precedence.
Do you not see that if they asked the Muslim leader to ransom them in exchange for an equal number of polytheists, or for horses and weapons, it would be permissible for him to do so in order to free them from captivity, even if those they received in exchange would use what they took to strengthen themselves against the Muslims? If they were to say, "Fight with us against our enemy from among the polytheists, on condition that we let you remain in our land and do not allow you to return to your families," then they should not fight alongside them ... For if they are safe from harm to their lives or limbs at their hands, there is no difference between their being detained in their land and their being held in their prisons. In either case, they suffer the distress of being cut off from their families and from their fellow believers. So they should not fight to uphold the rule of polytheism when they gain no evident benefit from doing so.
- If they are in distress and affliction, fearing for their lives, there is nothing wrong with their fighting alongside the polytheists if they say, "We will get you out of this."
For they have a valid purpose in this fighting: to ward off the affliction and distress that have befallen them." So consider, may Allah have mercy on you: despite the Hanafi school's strictness in prohibiting this, they nevertheless permitted supporting a disbeliever against another disbeliever, even at the cost of blood and limbs, in order to ward off affliction and harm from themselves. How many afflictions and hardships have befallen Muslims at the hands of unjust rulers, such that they needed only to vote for the lesser harm, without fighting. And Allah knows best.
