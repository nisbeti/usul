Therefore, the researcher holds that the two sects referred to in the hadiths are the Muhakkima and then the Azariqa. In al-Farq bayn al-Firaq (p. 62), it says:

"The Azariqa were a people who held the view of the first Muhakkima." Thus, it is clear here that the two sects held the same view. We mention here some of the characteristics of these two sects:

In al-Farq bayn al-Firaq (p. 174), it says:

Among the views of the Azariqa was that Allah, Exalted is He, would punish the child of a polytheist for the deed of his father.

In al-Fasl fi al-Milal wa al-Ahwa wa al-Nihal (4/144), it says:

The rest of the Azariqa, who were the followers of Nafi ibn al-Azraq, held that stoning an adulterer who was married was invalid. They cut off a thief's hand at the shoulder, and required a menstruating woman to pray and fast during her menstruation. Some of them said, "No; rather, she must make up the prayers when she becomes pure, just as she makes up the fasts." They deemed the blood of children lawful if they were not in their army, and also killed women who were not in their army. The Azariqa disavowed anyone who stayed behind from going out to fight, whether due to weakness or for any other reason. They declared anyone who opposed this view an unbeliever after the death of the first among them to hold it, but did not declare as an unbeliever anyone who opposed it during his lifetime.
