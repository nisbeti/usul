Al-Thawri said, "They may fight alongside them" (note here that al-Thawri permitted this without qualification).

Al-Awzai said, "They may fight only if they stipulate that, should they prevail, they will return them to the Abode of Islam."

Al-Shafi'i has two opinions.

Abu Ja'far said: "The fighting to which their allegiance has led them is fighting under the banner of unbelief. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said, 'I disavow every Muslim who is with a polytheist, such that their two fires are not visible to one another.'

It is also narrated that a polytheist man came to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) to pledge allegiance to him and fight alongside him while still a polytheist. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said to him, 'Go back, for we will not seek help from a polytheist.'" 2

"Furthermore, the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) used to prescribe conditions for fighting. One of them was calling them to Islam. Then he said, 'If they refuse, call on them to pay the jizya. If they agree, accept it from them; but if they refuse, fight them.'

If it is said that it is narrated that al-Zubayr fought in Abyssinia alongside the Negus against another enemy,

the response is that the Negus was a Muslim. Moreover, no fighting is mentioned in that report; rather, he went to learn their news, then informed the Emigrants."

The evidence of al-Tahawi's statement that aiding an unbeliever against another unbeliever falls under the hadith, "I disavow every Muslim who resides among polytheists," has also been discussed as follows:

First point: This hadith is weak, and the leading scholars deem it defective on account of its being mursal, including Ahmad, al-Bukhari [51], Abu Hatim [52], al-Daraqutni [53], al-Bayhaqi [54], and others. However, the researcher considers this hadith authentic,

and the leading scholars have continued to cite it and similar reports as evidence for the prohibition on residing among unbelievers.

Second point: Fighting alongside them does not entail residing among them. Armies are for the most part on the move, and someone who travels to them and fights alongside them against other unbelievers is not thereby a resident. Scholars have disagreed over the length of time that, if spent there or intended to be spent there, makes a person a resident: is it four days, fifteen days, twenty days, or is it determined by custom?
