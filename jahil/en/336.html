Whoever says, "I sell myself, seeking Allah's pleasure," is subject to the rulings, even though he is called a seller, and its plural is "sellers." Likewise, not everyone who rises up against a ruler is subject to the hadiths, because the criteria mentioned by the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), and stated in the hadiths, for classifying someone as a Kharijite do not apply to him.

Otherwise, Muawiya and those with him rose up against Ali, yet no one said that these people were Kharijites in the technical sense. Al-Husayn and those with him, among the most virtuous of the Companions, rose up against Yazid; and Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr and those with him, among the best of the Companions, also rose up against him. Zayd ibn Ali, who was one of the virtuous, rose up against Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik with the support and funding of Imam Abu Hanifa. Yet none of the scholars said that these men are called Kharijites in the technical sense. This issue has already been discussed above, but in al-Shahrastani's words there are two qualifications that the compromisers deliberately overlook. The first is his phrase, "the rightful imam." Are these tyrants rightful imams? Is their appointment as imams valid?

The second is his phrase, "whom the people have agreed upon." There is no doubt that by "the people" he means the believers, not the polytheists who call for secularism, socialism, or democracy, and associate other tyrants with Allah in His rule. So have the monotheistic believers, who affirm Allah's sole authority to rule, agreed to choose these tyrants to govern them by the laws of disbelief? These are two questions that need to be answered before judging everyone who rises up against a tyrant to be a Kharijite. If you cannot answer them, then know that you are following your own desires. And who is more astray than one who follows his own desire without guidance from Allah? Distinguish between a specific label and the criterion for a ruling; otherwise, you would have to call everyone who supported the Messenger an Ansari and rule that Allah is pleased with him.
