First, some held that ignorance is never an excuse in matters of belief. They based this on the fact that Allah created people upon the religion of Islam, namely, monotheism. By this, they meant singling out Allah in worship, which is obedience, as well as the forms this obedience takes. For example, in their view, the ugliness of prostrating to anyone other than Allah is established by reason and by innate disposition. Likewise, some of Allah's attributes, such as knowledge, power, and life, are, in their view, known by innate disposition. Some included hearing and sight among what is known by innate disposition, while others, such as al-Tabari, did not. Although this group, which has a hadith-based strand, includes attributes of Allah such as hearing and sight among what is known by innate disposition, they did not declare al-Tabari an unbeliever for this, since he excused ignorance of the attributes of hearing and sight. In their view, one who is ignorant of an attribute known by innate disposition is an unbeliever; yet they did not declare him an unbeliever. Rather, they often cited his statements in support of the view that ignorance is no excuse.
