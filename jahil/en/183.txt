Ibn Abi Yunus said at the beginning of al-Irshad: "A solitary report (khabar al-wahid) entails both knowledge and action." Qadi Abu Ya'la stated that this position is held by the majority.
Shaykh Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi said in his works on legal theory, such as al-Tabsira, Sharh al-Luma, and others. These are his words in the commentary: "A solitary report, when the community receives it with acceptance, entails knowledge and action, whether everyone acts upon it or only some do." He did not report any disagreement on this among the companions of al-Shafi'i. Qadi Abd al-Wahhab, among the Malikis, attributed this position to a group of jurists. The Hanafis explicitly state in their books that a widely transmitted report (khabar al-mustafid) entails knowledge. They cite as an example the Prophet's statement (peace and blessings be upon him): "There is no bequest for an heir." They say that although it was transmitted only through solitary chains, it is accepted. They also cite the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud concerning two parties to a sale who disagree: the seller's word is accepted, or they rescind the sale. They further cite the hadith of Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf concerning the collection of jizya from the Magians, and likewise the hadith of al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba and Muhammad ibn Maslama concerning giving a grandmother one-sixth. The early and later generations agreed to apply the rulings of these reports when they heard them. This demonstrates that their transmission is sound and that they are free of defect. Even if some people opposed them, in our view these are anomalous opinions, and their proponents are not counted in determining consensus."
He said: "We have said that reports following this course entail knowledge of the truthfulness of the one who reported them because, when we find that the early generations agreed to accept a report of this description without verifying it or opposing it on the basis of the foundational principles, or when a similar report is related, while we know their methods of accepting reports, examining them, and comparing them with the foundational principles, this indicates to us"
