In all these possibilities, the point of blame is the error in ijtihad, owing to the weakness of the evidence relied upon by those who were mistaken. For the indications of the disbelief of those under discussion outweighed the evidence for their Islam, which consisted merely of uttering the word of Islam, while refraining from showing allegiance to the Muslims. This verse is evidence that when a mujtahid relies on weak evidence of a kind that a scholar would not ordinarily use as proof, he is not far from the possibility of being...

the blame—in this world—for making an error in something in which people of knowledge do not err.

The clause "and Allah has cast them back because of what they have earned" is circumstantial, meaning: if you have differed concerning them, Allah has already returned them to their evil state, because arkas means "to return to riks," and riks is close in meaning to rijs.

In the hadith in Sahih concerning dung, it says: "This is filth."

It is also said that the meaning of "he caused him to relapse" is "he turned him back," that is, he returned him in a vile manner; this is close in meaning to the first interpretation. Allah made their return to disbelief a recompense for their corrupt belief and their lack of sincerity toward His Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him). For deeds beget others of their own kind: a righteous deed brings an increase in righteous deeds, while an evil deed leads to the utmost sins. For this reason, the Qur'an repeatedly reports that deeds are a cause of attaining the utmost ends of their own kind.

And His saying, "Do you wish to guide those whom Allah has led astray?" is an explanatory resumption arising from the reproach and astonishment in His saying, "What is the matter with you that you have become two parties regarding the hypocrites?"
