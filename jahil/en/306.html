The other is that he should not be punished with the penalties that are Allah Most High's right, such as permitting his blood to be shed or his property to be seized, lest he be held accountable for this without complete legal proof. Were this permissible, it would lead to chaos, with blood and property being deemed lawful on the basis of mere accusation. Rather, he may be punished with lesser measures, such as shunning him, refraining from marrying him or arranging a marriage for him, and refraining from performing the funeral prayer over him when he dies, and the like (1). Ibn Taymiyya said concerning the hypocrites: "At first, the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) would perform the funeral prayer over them and seek forgiveness for them, until Allah forbade him to do so, saying: {And never pray over any of them who dies, nor stand at his grave} (al-Tawba: 84), and saying: {Whether you ask forgiveness for them or do not ask forgiveness for them, even if you ask forgiveness for them seventy times, Allah will never forgive them} (al-Tawba: 80). So he would neither perform the funeral prayer over them nor seek forgiveness for them. Yet their blood and property remained inviolable; he did not deem lawful against them what he deemed lawful against the unbelievers who do not claim to be believers, but instead openly profess unbelief and not faith" [(2).

Evidence that an individual may judge another to be an unbeliever when he knows that the person has committed unbelief is Allah Most High's saying: {Set forth for them the parable of two men: We gave one of them two gardens of grapevines, surrounded them with date palms, and placed crops between them. Both gardens yielded their produce and fell short of nothing; and We caused a river to flow through them. He had wealth, and he said to his companion, as he conversed with him, "I have more wealth than you and a stronger following." He entered his garden, wronging himself, and said, "I do not think this will ever perish. Nor do I think the Hour will come; and even if I am returned to my Lord, I will surely find a better place of return than this." His companion said to him, as he conversed with him, "Do you disbelieve in the One who created you?"} (al-Kahf: 32–37). The first man disbelieved by doubting the Resurrection: {Nor do I think the Hour will come}.
