There is no doubt that weak hadiths do not yield knowledge or preponderant probability; rather, they yield probability in the sense of doubt. This kind of probability is forbidden to follow in the Book and the Sunnah, and its adherent is blameworthy: he speaks about Allah and His Messenger without knowledge, and this is among the gravest sins.

Ibn Hazm said: "Ibn Hazm (may Allah have mercy on him) also said in al-Ihkam (1/133):

'Among the errors made by some hadith scholars is their saying: "So-and-so may be relied upon in matters of exhortation, but not in legal rulings."

Abu Muhammad (that is, Ibn Hazm) said: This is false, because it is an invalid division for which there is no proof; indeed, the proof refutes it. That is because every person on earth must be either sinful or not sinful. If he is not sinful, then he is upright, and there is no third category.

The upright person falls into two categories: a jurist and a non-jurist. The upright jurist is accepted in every matter, while the sinful person is not relied upon in anything.

The upright person who is not a memorizer is not accepted in any matter whatsoever, because the condition for acceptance specified by Allah Most High is not present in him (that is, His saying, "so that they may gain understanding of the religion and warn their people"). Whoever is upright in some of what he transmits is upright in the rest of it. It is impossible to deem some of his reports acceptable and the rest unacceptable unless there is a text from Allah Most High or a consensus distinguishing between them. Otherwise, this is arbitrary judgment without proof and speaking without knowledge, which is impermissible.' End quote."
