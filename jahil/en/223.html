"A word of truth is snatched by a jinni and cast into the ear of his human ally, and they mix with it a hundred lies." Agreed upon. Here, some of the Companions were unaware that Allah alone possesses knowledge of the unseen, and that He may disclose some of it to certain people, such as soothsayers. The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) informed them that this was not correct, but did not declare them disbelievers. This is a matter of creed. Praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds, and Allah knows best.

(How the proof is established)

The question now is: how is the proof established against a person, such that establishing it confirms the legal ruling of disbelief against him, and consequently the rulings applicable to disbelievers are applied to him? We said earlier that there are categories of people who are not excused, despite affiliating themselves with Islam and bearing witness to the two testimonies. They include everyone who falls short in seeking religious knowledge, thereby becoming ignorant, and then begins to fabricate lies about Allah without knowledge, associating others with Him in supplication, vows, or otherwise, without knowledge—whether he invented this himself or followed someone he takes as an authority without proof, since following another is no excuse. Rather, if he did not know, he was obliged to believe in Allah in a general way and entrust to Allah, Glorified and Exalted, what he did not know. The second category likewise includes everyone who comes to hold a belief, but whose belief results from a false interpretation unsupported by either the Sharia or the language. I have already explained false interpretation and that it is not accepted. The third category is one that has fallen into disbelief as a result of an interpretation that has some basis in language or the Sharia. In this case, the proof is established against him.
