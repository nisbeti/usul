Abu Muhammad said: What makes this clearer than anything else in this matter is the statement of Allah, Exalted is He: {When the disciples said, "O Jesus, son of Mary, can your Lord send down to us a table spread with food from heaven?"} up to His statement: {and know that you have told us the truth}. These disciples, whom Allah, Mighty and Majestic is He, praised, spoke out of ignorance when they said to Jesus (peace be upon him), "Can your Lord send down to us a table spread with food from heaven?" Yet this did not invalidate their faith. There is no way around this. They would only have disbelieved had they said this after the proof had been established and made clear to them.
Abu Muhammad said: There is a necessary proof about which there is no disagreement: the entire community is in agreement, with no dissent from anyone, that whoever deliberately alters a verse of the Quran, knowing that it appears differently in the written copies, or deliberately omits a word, or deliberately adds a word to it, is an unbeliever by the consensus of the entire community. Yet a person may make a mistake in recitation, adding one word and omitting another, and altering his speech in ignorance, thinking that he is correct; he may insist on this and argue about it before the truth has become clear to him. In the view of no one in the community is he thereby an unbeliever, a transgressor, or a sinner. But once he has consulted the written copies, or someone whose report constitutes proof has informed him of this, if he persists in his error, then in the view of the entire community he is undoubtedly an unbeliever on that account. This is the ruling that applies throughout the whole religion."
