Among those from whom the other view was transmitted—that they should not be declared unbelievers—were Ali ibn Abi Talib, Ibn Umar, and al-Hasan al-Basri. This was also the view of a group of jurists, legal theorists, and theologians. They cited the fact that the Companions and Successors granted inheritance to the heirs of the people of Harura and to those known for holding the doctrine of predestination who had died, buried them in Muslim cemeteries, and applied the rulings of Islam to them. Malik said of the Qadariyya and other people of innovation that they should be asked to repent; if they repent, then well and good, but otherwise they should be killed, because they spread corruption in the land. He said the same of the brigand: if the ruler sees fit to kill him, he may do so, even if he has not killed anyone. The brigand's corruption concerns only property and worldly interests, though it may also extend to religious matters, such as obstructing the pilgrimage and jihad. The corruption caused by people of innovation, however, is mostly against religion, though it may also affect worldly affairs through the enmity they sow among Muslims.

(Ali and Ibn Abbas's position on the interpretation of the Kharijites)
I will present this hadith in full here to clarify Ali and Ibn Abbas's position regarding the Kharijites who interpreted the texts, and to show that they excused them rather than declaring them unbelievers. This is so even though the researcher considers the Kharijites (those meant by the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him) to be unbelievers—not because they interpreted the texts and were mistaken, but because there is an established text stating that they would leave the religion, that they are the worst of creation, and that they are the dogs of Hell, describing and characterizing them as such. Nevertheless, I present this hadith to clarify two points: first, that some of the early generations excused those who interpreted the texts; and second, that no report from any of the Companions declares someone who excuses such a person to be an unbeliever. Even if some considered that interpreter an unbeliever, there is no report from any of the early generations declaring Ibn Abbas an unbeliever for excusing the Kharijites.
