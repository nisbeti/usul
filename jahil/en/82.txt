Umar ibn al-Khattab (may Allah be pleased with him) acknowledged to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) that he had not understood the verse concerning kalala, yet he did not declare him an unbeliever on that account, nor deem him sinful, nor tell him that he had thereby incurred sin. He only rebuked him sternly for repeatedly asking about it. Likewise, a number of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) gave incorrect legal opinions during the lifetime of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him). When that reached him, he did not declare any of them an unbeliever on that account, nor deem them sinful, nor say that they had thereby incurred sin, for none of them opposed him deliberately. An example is the legal opinion of Abu al-Sanabil ibn Ba'kak concerning the two waiting periods, and that of those who ruled that an unmarried adulterer was to be stoned. We have examined this at length in our book entitled The Book of al-Ihkam on the Principles of Legal Rulings. Furthermore, the aforementioned verse cannot be reconciled with the position of any of those who disagree with us except by means of an omission. They say that the words in His saying, Exalted is He, {Those whose striving is lost in the life of this world} are the predicate of an omitted subject, and this can only be so by omitting the subject, as though He had said, "They are those ..." No one may claim that anything has been omitted from the Quran unless another clear text necessitates it, or there is consensus on it, or it is required by sensory necessity. Thus their position is invalid and becomes a claim without evidence. As for us, the word "those" retains its ordinary meaning, without any omission: it is an adjective describing "the greatest losers," and it is the predicate of the subject in His saying, Exalted is He, {Those are the ones who disbelieved}. The same applies to His saying, Exalted is He, {And they think that they are upon something, but they are indeed the liars}. Yes, this describes the people whom Allah Most High characterized in the beginning of the verse, and the pronoun refers back to them; they are the disbelievers, as stated explicitly at the beginning.
