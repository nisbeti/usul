Al-Nawawi said, concerning the man who killed himself: "A group said: This man was ignorant of one of the attributes of Allah, Exalted is He. The scholars differed over whether someone ignorant of an attribute is to be declared an unbeliever."
The judge said: Among those who declared him an unbeliever on that account was Ibn Jarir al-Tabari; Abu al-Hasan al-Ashari also said this initially.
Others said: One does not become an unbeliever through ignorance of the attribute, nor does one thereby cease to be called a believer, unlike one who denies it. This was the position to which Abu al-Hasan al-Ashari returned, and upon which his view settled, because he did not hold that belief with certainty, deeming it correct and regarding it as religion and law. Rather, only one who believes his own doctrine to be true becomes an unbeliever. They said: If people were asked about the attributes, those who knew about them would be found to be few.
A group said: This man lived during an interval when mere affirmation of divine oneness was sufficient, and there was no obligation before the revealed law had been conveyed, according to the soundest position, because of the words of the Exalted: {We would never punish until We had sent a messenger.}
A group said: It is possible that, in the time of their law, pardoning an unbeliever was permissible, unlike in our law. This is among the things deemed possible by reason according to the Sunnis; we have only prohibited it in our law on the basis of revelation, namely His saying, Exalted is He: (Indeed, Allah does not forgive associating anything with Him). And other proofs. Allah knows best (1). End of quotation.
