The issues of the branches are definitively established, even if there is disagreement about them, and even if one who errs in them is not sinful because the evidence is obscure to him. Even if they are definitive, it does not follow that there must be agreement on definitive matters. The judge conceded this in cases where the text was obscure to someone.
Some have mentioned another distinction, saying: "Issues of the foundations are matters of knowledge, while issues of the branches are matters of knowledge for which two things are required: knowledge and action." What is required in practical matters is also knowledge and action: the heart's love and aversion—its love for the truth indicated and contained in them, and its aversion to the falsehood that contradicts them. Thus, action is not confined to the deeds of the limbs. Rather, the deeds of the heart are the basis of the deeds of the limbs, and the deeds of the limbs follow from them. Every matter of knowledge is therefore accompanied by the heart's faith, affirmation, and love; that too is action—in fact, it is the basis of action. This is something many theologians discussing matters of faith have overlooked, supposing that faith is merely affirmation, without deeds. This is among the ugliest and gravest errors, for many unbelievers were certain of the truthfulness of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), with no doubt about it; yet that affirmation was not accompanied by the heart's love for what he brought, acceptance of it, desire for it, or allegiance and enmity on its account.
Do not neglect this point, for it is extremely important; through it, the true nature of faith is known.
Matters of knowledge are matters of action, and practical matters are matters of knowledge. For the Lawgiver did not deem mere action, without knowledge, sufficient from those legally responsible in practical matters, nor mere knowledge, without action, in matters of knowledge.
