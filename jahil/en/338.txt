Their presence in the generations of the early Muslims, among the Successors, shows that, in their view, the absence of an excuse on grounds of ignorance or interpretation when declaring someone a disbeliever was not what made that person an extremist.
Second: Declaring someone an unbeliever on account of a sin concerning which there is disagreement as to whether it constitutes unbelief. There is no doubt among any of the imams that if someone declares another person an unbeliever over a matter concerning which the scholars disagree as to whether it constitutes unbelief, the one who declares him an unbeliever is not called a Kharijite, nor do the hadiths about the Kharijites apply to him.
And mention here some of the individuals whom the Sunnis differed over whether to declare unbelievers. It appears in al-I'tisam by al-Shatibi, edited by al-Shuqayr, al-Humayd, and al-Sini (1/223)
This is because the scholars among the earliest generations and others differed over declaring many of their sects unbelievers, such as the Kharijites, the Qadariyya, and others (7)(8)(9).
It is stated in
((Al-Sawa'iq al-Muhriqa 'ala Ahl al-Rafd wa al-Dalal wa al-Zandaqa (2/630):
Know that the Sunnis differed over whether to declare Yazid ibn Mu'awiya and his successor after him unbelievers. One group said that he was an unbeliever, citing the well-known report from Sibt ibn al-Jawzi and others: when the head of al-Husayn (may Allah be pleased with him) was brought, he gathered the people of Syria, began prodding the head with a cane, and recited verses by Ibn al-Ziba'ra.))
(Mirqat al-Mafatih Sharh Mishkat al-Masabih, 4/1641)
