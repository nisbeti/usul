Perhaps no revelation had come to them that established it decisively, in which case doubting it would be disbelief. As for what no revelation has addressed, it is among the things reason deems possible. Or perhaps "decreed" means "made narrow," and what he did to himself was an act of self-reproach and anger at his disobedience.

It was also said that he said what he said while not understanding his own words or controlling his speech, because of the anguish and fear that had overwhelmed him and taken away his reason; therefore, he was not held accountable for it. It was also said that this occurred during a period without revelation, when mere affirmation of divine oneness sufficed. Another view is that this is a figure of speech in Arabic whose form suggests doubt but whose meaning is certainty. This is called "feigning ignorance," and examples of it occur in their speech, such as the words of the Exalted: {Perhaps he will take heed or fear}, and His words: {And indeed, either we or you are upon guidance or in clear error}.

As for those who affirm the description but deny the attribute, saying, "I say He is Knowing, but He has no knowledge; and Speaking, but He has no speech," and likewise with the other attributes, following the doctrine of the Mu'tazila: whoever judges them by the implication of their statement and the conclusion to which their doctrine leads, declares them disbelievers. For if they deny knowledge, then the description "Knowing" is negated, since only one who possesses knowledge can be described as knowing. It is as though, in his view, they had explicitly stated what their words entail. The same applies, in his view, to the other groups of those who interpret the attributes figuratively, including the anthropomorphists, the Qadariyya, and others.

Whoever does not consider them bound by the implications of their words, nor hold them to what their doctrine entails, does not consider them disbelievers. He says: "For when this is pointed out to them, they say, 'We do not say that He is not Knowing. We disavow the implication you have attributed to us, which we believe, as do you, to be disbelief. Rather, we say that our statement does not lead to that conclusion, according to the principles we have laid down.'"
