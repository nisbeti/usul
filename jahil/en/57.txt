It conveys only conjecture, in the sense of doubt. So how can they rely on it after that, even if it comes from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him)? What, then, of something coming from someone below him, whose statements are not authoritative in the first place? It is noticeable that this group often say and repeat, "We transmit from the early generations, we follow the early generations, and our school is the school of the early generations," yet you will find no precise definition of whom they mean by this. So I would like here to touch on the question of who the early generations are.

(Definition of the early generations)
In al-Nihaya fi Gharib al-Athar (4/81), it says:
In the report, "[The best of you are my generation, then those who come after them]," this means the Companions, then the Successors. A generation (qarn) is the people of each era, and it is the approximate span of the lifespans of the people of each era. The word derives from iqtiran, as though it were the span during which the people of that era are close to one another in their lifespans and circumstances.
It is also said that a generation is forty years; it is also said, eighty; and it is also said, one hundred. It is also said that the term is unrestricted as to time. It is the verbal noun of qarana, yaqrinu. End quote. On the basis of this definition, if by "the early generations" they mean those whose views are authoritative on the issue, according to their position—even though I have explained that the statement of a Companion himself is not authoritative—then, since this is the position of some, I have met them on their own terms. Ibn Muflih said in Usul al-Fiqh: "The position of a Successor is not authoritative according to Ahmad and the scholars, because that would lead to an infinite regress. Some Hanafis have reported two narrations from him." End quote.
