Among what was mentioned earlier were the criteria found in the hadiths, as well as the descriptions of this group. It became clear from these that many of the criteria are grounds for declaring them unbelievers, making them highly deserving of the command to kill them and of being called the dogs of the people of Hell. Those actions mentioned are sufficient to rule that they have left the religion.

After that came two very strange and vile opinions, based on two false premises. The first opinion is that everyone who rebels against the imam is a Kharijite, and therefore all the hadiths about the Kharijites apply to him.

The second opinion is that everyone who declares a Muslim an unbeliever is a Kharijite, and therefore the hadiths about the Kharijites apply to him.

First: Refuting those who hold the first opinion:

None of the criteria mentioned in the hadiths refers to rebelling against the imam. The criteria mentioned by the scholars likewise contain no reference to rebelling against the ruler. Indeed, in the hadiths, every expression meaning "leaving" refers to leaving the religion, as in the words, "They delve deeply into religion until they leave it," and "They pass out of the religion." Al-Shawkani said: (Nayl al-Awtar, 7/187)

"His words: 'Chapter on fighting the Kharijites': They are the plural of kharija, meaning a group. They were so called because they left the religion and introduced innovations, or because they left the best of the Muslims." End quote.

Their leaving "the best of the Muslims" means that they separated from them; they were not part of their company. It does not mean that they rebelled against them, since "from" is not "against." The apparent meaning, then, is that they separated from the community through the unbelieving practices they introduced.
