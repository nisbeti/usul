It is noticeable that many who belong to the Islamic movement accuse anyone who declares the excuser to be an unbeliever of being a Kharijite. Here we find them falling into what they tried to avoid: they condemn the person who declares the excuser to be an unbeliever because the excuser is acting on a mistaken interpretation.

So how can someone who interprets the matter and errs be declared an unbeliever, when the evidence from the Book and the Sunnah rules that out? Yet they also find that the person who declares the excuser to be an unbeliever is acting on a mistaken interpretation in this matter, but they do not excuse him on account of his interpretation. Instead, they hasten to call him a Kharijite. You know what this term entails in the way of condemnations and attributes described by the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him): that they are "the worst of creation," and his command to kill them, "Wherever you find them, kill them," and his calling them the dogs of Hell. If they accuse someone of being a Kharijite, they must therefore apply this severe threat to him. Hence the need to examine who the Kharijites are.

(Who are the Kharijites?)

Some scholars, such as Ibn Hazm, defined the Kharijites. In al-Fasl fi al-Milal wa al-Ahwa wa al-Nihal (2/90), he wrote:

"Whoever agrees with the Kharijites in rejecting arbitration, declaring those who commit major sins to be unbelievers, advocating revolt against unjust rulers, holding that those who commit major sins will remain in the Fire forever, and holding that the imamate may belong to someone other than Quraysh, is a Kharijite. But if he disagrees with them on these matters, while agreeing with them on other matters over which the Muslims differ, then he is not a Kharijite."

There is no doubt that if we wish to apply a ruling to someone, we must first determine the effective cause of that ruling—that is, what is the cause whose presence in a person leads us to judge him to be a Kharijite?
