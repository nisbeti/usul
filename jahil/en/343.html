Here, Khalid assumed that these people remained in disbelief, yet the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) did not punish him or dismiss him, nor did he say that he was one of the Kharijites.

Ibn Taymiyyah says (Majmu al-Fatawa, vol. 3): "If a Muslim acts on the basis of an interpretation in fighting or declaring someone an unbeliever, he does not become an unbeliever on that account. This is like when Umar ibn al-Khattab said to Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a, 'Messenger of Allah, let me strike the neck of this hypocrite.' The Prophet said, 'He was present at Badr. How do you know? Perhaps Allah looked upon the people of Badr and said, "Do as you wish, for I have forgiven you."' This is in the two Sahihs. Also in them, in the hadith of the Slander, Usayd ibn Hudayr said to Sa'd ibn Ubadah, 'You are a hypocrite, arguing on behalf of the hypocrites.' The two groups disputed, and the Prophet reconciled them. Among these people of Badr, one said to another, 'You are a hypocrite,' yet the Prophet declared neither of them an unbeliever; rather, he testified that they would all enter Paradise."

What preceded were proofs cited by those who say that one is not held accountable for an interpretation in declaring someone an unbeliever.

Its perpetrator is not called a Kharijite; otherwise, we would be accusing half the scholars of the Muslim community of Kharijism. This invalidates the claim of those who say that anyone who interprets matters in declaring someone an unbeliever is one of the Kharijites.

The fourth type of declaring someone an unbeliever is when an act is unanimously agreed to constitute disbelief, but the person who pronounces the ruling of disbelief applies it to specific individuals without considering the impediment of ignorance; that is, he does not excuse the person who committed the act, because in his view ignorance is no excuse at all.

So, is someone who does not excuse ignorance on the basis of an interpretation considered one of the Kharijites?
