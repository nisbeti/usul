Among those who explicitly stated that a solitary report (khabar al-wahid) yields knowledge are Malik, al-Shafi'i, the companions of Abu Hanifa, Dawud ibn Ali and his companions, such as Abu Muhammad ibn Hazm; this was also explicitly stated by al-Husayn ibn Ali al-Karabisi and al-Harith ibn Asad al-Muhasibi.
Ibn Khuwaz Mindad said in his book on the principles of jurisprudence, after mentioning the solitary report narrated by only one or two people: "This kind of report, too, can yield necessary knowledge." Malik explicitly stated this. Ahmad said regarding the hadith about the vision: "We know that it is true, and we are certain of our knowledge of it." Al-Marwadhi likewise narrated: "I said to Abu Abd Allah, 'There are two people here who say that a report entails action but does not entail knowledge.' He disapproved of this and said, 'I do not know what this is.'" The Qadi said: "The apparent meaning of this is that he equates knowledge with action." The Qadi also said at the beginning of the section on reports: "A solitary report entails knowledge if its chain of transmission is sound, there is no disagreement in its transmission, and the community has received it with acceptance. Our companions state this categorically, saying that it entails knowledge even if the community has not received it with acceptance." He said: "The school's position is as I have described, and no other."
He thus explicitly stated that this is the school's position. In the narration of Ahmad ibn al-Husayn al-Tirmidhi, he stated that one must affirm a report from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him). Another narration from him indicates that a solitary report does not yield knowledge, for in the narration of al-Marwadhi he said: "When a hadith comes from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) with a sound chain of transmission and contains a ruling or an obligation, I act upon it and worship Allah in accordance with it, but I do not testify that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said it." The discussion of the meaning of this narration will come later, if Allah Most High wills.
