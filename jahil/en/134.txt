Ibn al-Wazir said: "There is no disagreement that whoever denies what is necessarily known to everyone is an unbeliever, and cloaks himself in the name of interpretation where interpretation is impossible, as do the heretics in interpreting all the Most Beautiful Names, indeed the entire Quran, the revealed laws, and the Hereafter, including resurrection, the Day of Judgment, Paradise, and Hell." End quote from Ithar al-Haqq ala al-Khalq, p. 415.

Among such matters, certainly, is the foundation of monotheism (tawhid), which entails devoting every kind of worship exclusively to Allah. To undermine this foundation by claiming an interpretation that permits associating partners with Allah, Exalted is He, and taking rivals alongside Him is among the clearest forms of falsehood—the very falsehood that all the messengers were sent to refute and condemn.

(Disagreement over declaring the interpreter an unbeliever, and scholars' views on declaring the interpreter an unbeliever)

Ibn Taymiyya said: "The scholars have disagreed over declaring the people of innovation and caprice unbelievers and consigning them to remain eternally in the Fire. There is no imam from whom only one view has been reported on this matter; rather, two views have been reported from Malik, al-Shafi'i, Ahmad, and others. Some of their followers came to report this disagreement as applying to all people of innovation and to their remaining eternally in the Fire, to the point that they committed themselves to consigning everyone whom they personally considered an innovator to eternal punishment. This involves an incalculable degree of error. Others reacted in the opposite way, coming to think that no one among the people of caprice should be categorically declared an unbeliever, even if they have adopted heretical views and the doctrines of those who deny Allah's attributes and those who espouse union with Him.
