And as for the sign (alamah), it is a characteristic agreed upon by two individuals. When one of them sees it, he understands the matter they have agreed upon, such as the saying of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) to Ibn Mas'ud: "Your permission is to lift the veil and to listen to my voice until I prohibit you." Thus, lifting the veil and listening to the movement of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) was a sign of permission for Ibn Mas'ud. And as he (peace and blessings be upon him) said, "I can recognize the voices of the group of the Ash'aris reciting the Qur'an when they enter at night, and I know their places from their voices reciting the Qur'an at night, even though I have not seen their places when they descended during the day." Therefore, the voices of the Ash'aris reciting the Qur'an were a sign of their place of descent. From this, the flags placed in the wilderness for guiding the way and the flags in armies for identifying the position of the leader were derived.
Abu Muhammad said, "And this is a fourth meaning.
It has also been called the meanings of the causes, and this is from their great confusion and corrupt attachment. The meaning is merely the interpretation of the term, such as when someone says, 'What is the meaning of haram?' You would say to him, 'It is everything that is not permissible to do.' Or if he says, 'What is the meaning of fard?' We would say, 'It is everything that is not permissible to leave.' Or he might ask, 'What is the scale?'
We say that it is a tool by which the variation in the quantities of bodies is known; this and similar things are the meanings, and this is also a fifth thing.
All of this does not establish a reason for the laws nor does it necessitate analogy (qiyas), because if the sign (alamah) is designated to recognize something specific, there is no way to recognize something else through it in any manner. If that were the case, it would not be a sign, nor would it have been designated as such, and confusion would arise.
Abu Muhammad said: When these five meanings that we mentioned are different and distinct, each one of them being unlike the other, and all having different definitions and ranks, it is necessary that no one of them be called by a name that belongs to another, so that understanding remains clear and they do not become confused, with some being called by another's name, which would lead to placing a meaning in the wrong context and invalidate the truths. The root of every calamity, confusion, and corruption is the mixing of names, and the occurrence of one name on many meanings, such that the informant uses that name while intending one of the meanings it encompasses, leading the listener to understand a different meaning than what the informant intended, resulting in calamity and confusion. This is the most harmful and destructive thing in the Sharia for those who believe in falsehood, except for whom Allah, Exalted is He, grants success.
These four names, namely the cause (illa), the purpose (ghرض), the reason (sabab), and the sign (alamat), we have clarified that their meanings are different and that their designations are various. We have decisively addressed the ailment of those who wish to impose the name of cause in the Sharia on the meaning of reason, thereby excluding what is not permissible to believe, which is that the laws were legislated by Allah, Exalted is He, for causes that necessitated their legislation, or to attribute falsehood to Allah in claiming that He legislated causes that He, Exalted is He, nor His Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) specified, nor permitted. The people of causes must follow one of these two paths, and both are destructive.
And we do not deny that Allah, Exalted is He, has made some things a cause for some of the laws He has legislated; rather, we affirm this and establish it where it has been mentioned in the texts, such as his saying (peace and blessings be upon him), "The most grievous of people in Islam are those who ask about something that is not prohibited, then it is prohibited because of their question." Just as Allah has made the disbelief of the disbeliever and his dying as a disbeliever a cause for his eternal abode in the Fire of Hell, and dying in faith a cause for entering Paradise, and just as He has made theft under certain conditions a cause for amputation, and slander under certain conditions a cause for flogging, and unlawful sexual intercourse under certain conditions a cause for flogging and stoning, and just as we affirm these specified causes, we likewise deny that anyone can claim a cause where it has not been specified.
And we do not say that all laws are for reasons; rather, we say that none of them is for a reason except what has been explicitly stated to be for a reason. As for the rest, it is merely something that Allah, Exalted is He, willed, who does as He wishes. We do not prohibit or permit, nor do we add or subtract, and we do not say anything except what our Lord, Mighty and Majestic, and our Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) have said. We do not exceed what they have said, nor do we leave anything of it. This is the pure religion that no one is permitted to contradict or have a different belief about. And with Allah, Exalted is He, is success.
And Allah, Exalted is He, said, describing Himself, { He is not questioned about what He does, but they will be questioned. } So He informed us of the distinction between us and Him, and that His actions are not subject to accountability.
