The explanation of Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) indicates that it is the truth) (132).

His statement (by Allah, the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) did not die, and Allah will surely resurrect him, and He will cut off the hands and feet of men) (133). Then his retraction from that.

It is clear from the above that he (may Allah be pleased with him) makes mistakes just as humans do, so we do not abandon the words of the infallible (peace and blessings be upon him) for the words of someone who is subject to error. Even if Allah placed the truth on his tongue in all matters, as they say, Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) strongly denied a man who asked him about a matter in Hajj, and when he gave him a fatwa, the man said, "This is how the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) gave me a fatwa." Umar struck him with a stick and said to him: "You asked me about something that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) has already given a fatwa on, so should I contradict him?" We narrated it through the chain of Abdul Razzaq. Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) said, "Our opinion is mere conjecture, while the opinion of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) was the truth." Abu Muhammad said, "Whoever follows them should follow them in this agreement of abandoning taqlid (imitation) and in what they unanimously agreed upon regarding following the Sunnah of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) and in what they prohibited regarding conjecture" (134).

It is clear from all of the above that it is not permissible to contradict the text for the words of anyone, regardless of who they are.
