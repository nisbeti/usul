Thus, the law has witnessed the type of cause, making the two rulings homogeneous: (the prohibition of selling to the nomad by the settled) and (the prohibition of placing the burden of loss on the owner of the commodity), because the two causes are homogeneous in that they fall under the principle of (prioritizing the public interest over the private interest), even though they differ in essence. The reason for prohibition in the first example is the same as (selling to the nomad by the settled), as it is a clear, defined description. When the sale from the settled to the nomad occurs, the prohibition is established. In the second example, the cause is (the liability of the owner of the commodity with the common laborer), so the Malikis resort in this case to abandoning the authentic hadiths due to their belief that they are of uncertain authenticity, in favor of what they call the objective of the legislator, which is prioritizing the public interest over the private interest, a matter they consider certain.

Among the examples where Imam Malik rejected authentic hadiths based on the interest-oriented objective related to financial and economic jurisprudence is:

- His rejection of the hadith "The two sellers have the option until they part" (28), because the period of option is not time-bound and may be prolonged, which constitutes gharar (uncertainty), thus contradicting the prohibition of selling gharar. They said: Even if one of them conditions an unknown duration for the option, it would be invalid by consensus. How then can a ruling established by law be valid when it is not permissible to condition it by law?(29).

They also rejected the hadith of Rafi' ibn Khadij: "We were with the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) at Dhul-Hulaifah from Tihamah, and we obtained sheep and camels. The people hurried and boiled them in pots, so the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) came and ordered them to be overturned."(30).
