It has been confirmed that Abdullah ibn Ubayy, after he and those who assisted him in that act disbelieved, manifested repentance and Islam, and the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) accepted that from them, and he did not know their inner state regarding what they were upon in disbelief? Or regarding what they manifested of repentance? However, Allah, Exalted is He, is All-Knowing of that, and He is undoubtedly the One who will hold them accountable on the Day of Resurrection(108).

Seventh: They have argued based on the actions of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them), and there is the issue of whether the statement of a Companion is a proof or not, which al-Razi summarized by saying, "The fourth issue, the truth is that the statement of a Companion is not a proof. Some have said it is a proof absolutely, and some have differentiated and mentioned various aspects: (one) it is a proof if it contradicts analogy. (Two) the statements of Abu Bakr and Umar (may Allah be pleased with them) are only a proof. (Three) the statements of the four caliphs are a proof. We have the text, consensus, and analogy. As for the text, it is His saying, Exalted is He, {So take heed, O people of insight} (Al-Hashr: 2). This contradicts the permissibility of imitation. As for the consensus, it is that the Companions unanimously agreed on the permissibility of contradicting any one of the individual Companions; Abu Bakr and Umar did not deny anyone who contradicted them, nor did either of them deny his companion in matters they disagreed upon. As for analogy, it is that he is capable of grasping the ruling in a way, so it is obligatory for him to refrain from imitation as in the principles"(109).
