He (peace and blessings be upon him) is a legislator, so opposing legislation with legislation – if it is correct to consider it opposition – is not the same as opposing legislation with public interest (maslaha). When the Sharia comes with a ruling to ease things for the people, we do not abandon the rulings and introduce new rulings to ease things for the people from ourselves, for we are not legislators. Indeed, all of Allah's legislation is ease and benefit, as He, Exalted is He, said {Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship}(103).
Sixth: They cited the approval of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) of the Companions' (may Allah be pleased with them) disobedience to his command to pray in Banu Qurayza because some of them reached the time of 'Asr on the way, and they said: "Rather, we will pray, and that was not what was intended from us." He mentioned this to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), and he did not reprimand any of them(104). They said that here the Companions left the text for the sake of public interest, and this indicates the permissibility of deriving a partial objective and the permissibility of managing practical rulings in this derived objective even if it contradicts the apparent indication(105).
(Response): I do not know what interest they thought the Companions left the text for, as the hadith only indicates that they understood that he (peace and blessings be upon him) intended only to hasten and feared missing the time, so they prayed on the way. Al-Nawawi (may Allah have mercy on him) said: "As for the disagreement among the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) regarding hastening the prayer at the time of Thaqif and delaying it, the reason is that the evidences of the Sharia conflicted for them, as prayer is commanded at its time, while the implication of the Prophet's (peace and blessings be upon him) statement that no one should pray the Dhuhr or 'Asr except in Banu Qurayza is that worship is to go to them and not to be preoccupied with anything else, and that delaying the prayer is not intended in itself as a delay. Some of the Companions took this implication considering the meaning rather than the wording, so they prayed when the time was missed.
