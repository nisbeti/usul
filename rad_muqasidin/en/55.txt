The public interest (maslaha) contradicts his view(88).
(Reply) First: Whoever attributed to him the denial of consensus may mean: the denial of the possibility of achieving consensus due to difficulty, and even if it were to be achieved, it would be a proof. This is what Ibn Hazm (may Allah have mercy on him) held, as he restricted consensus to what is known of the religion of Islam by necessity only, transmitted by continuous narration (tawatur)(89). Also, the established consensus of the Companions(90), and Ibn Hazm's view in denying the validity of istihsan (juridical preference) in religion has been previously mentioned, which is that the text should not be rejected for an opinion, preference, or the statement of anyone. This is one of the narrations from Imam Ahmad, who said: "Whoever claims consensus is a liar; perhaps people have disagreed"(91). No one has reported from Ahmad the statement attributed to al-Tufi.
Secondly: Whoever attributed to him the denial of the consensus, meaning its validity, even if it were established, may argue that the evidence could converge on misguidance according to the one who holds this opinion, which is al-Nizam(92).
As for al-Nizam, we do not know where al-Tufi got the idea that he speaks of upholding interests, as he had little faith in analogy (qiyas) and opinion (ra'y), and it is clear that upholding the public interest depends—first and foremost—on analogy and opinion, so how could he then advocate for it?
This statement from al-Tufi is contradictory, as he claims that consensus is a proof for establishing the public interest while knowing that there is no consensus, as previously mentioned, and then he made the public interest a proof for invalidating consensus on a specific issue if it contradicts.
