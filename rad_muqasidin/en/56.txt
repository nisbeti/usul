Fourth: He said: The texts are different and contradictory, and they are the cause of the blameworthy disagreement in rulings, while the consideration of public interests is a real matter in itself that does not differ.
(Response): If he means the contradiction in the origin of the texts, then it is refuted by the saying of Allah, Exalted is He, {And if it had been from other than Allah, they would have found within it much contradiction} (93). And if he means the apparent contradiction, this is a matter that occurs, and we have been commanded, in the presence of contradiction and disagreement, to refer back to the texts of the Book and the Sunnah, not to what each one sees as a public interest. Allah, Exalted is He, said {And whatever you disagree over, its ruling is to Allah} (94), and He said {And if you disagree in anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger} (95). Thus, referring to the one thing is what resolves the disagreement, not referring to whatever one of them thinks is a public interest; this, as we have previously quoted from Ibn Hazm, is the cause of disagreement(96).
Fifth: Al-Tufi used the following hadith as evidence for his point, from Aisha that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Did you not see that your people, when they built the Kaaba, limited themselves to the foundations of Ibrahim (peace be upon him)? I said, O Messenger of Allah, should you not return it to the foundations of Ibrahim (peace be upon him)? He said: If it were not for the recent disbelief of your people, I would have done it."(97) Agreed upon. He said: This indicates that building it on the foundations of Ibrahim is obligatory in its ruling, and leaving it is for the public interest(98).
