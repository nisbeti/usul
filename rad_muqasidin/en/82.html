(Al-Radd): First: The researcher does not know what benefit (maslaha) there is for the people in what they understood from the action of Umar (may Allah be pleased with him). Many often use the term benefit to refer to what is advantageous and facilitates matters for people from their perspective. Here, Umar's action is nothing but a tightening of the rules upon the people according to their understanding of the text. There is no doubt that pronouncing divorce—if the three are uttered together in one phrase—three times is more difficult than pronouncing it once. If these individuals oppose this with the hadith and say: it can be inferred from Umar's abandonment of the text that people should be cautious, even if it is more severe, it would be more appropriate than their claim. This would necessitate abandoning the texts of legal facilitation and imposing the stricter interpretation due to Umar's action, which, according to their view, is that of a rightly guided caliph who prioritizes his Sunnah over the text. There is no power and no strength except with Allah. It is astonishing that those who advocate for facilitation and benefit here have abandoned his action of pronouncing three divorces together in one phrase for the sake of benefit, and instead adopted the practice of pronouncing three as one(172). Thus, Umar's action is a proof when it aligns with their desires, and at other times it is not a proof, but rather his supposed and imagined intention of deliberately opposing the text for what he sees as a benefit is the proof. This is how they play with the religion of Allah, Exalted is He, and there is no power and no strength except with Allah.

(172) The Egyptian Personal Status Law adopts the opinion of Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) and Ibn al-Qayyim that the three divorces count as one, even though this contradicts the action of Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) in their view. They agreed with him in deriving the ruling (meaning that he did this for the sake of public interest (maslaha)) but disagreed with him in the original apparent opinion, as they claim.
