First: Indeed, Allah, Exalted is He, only sent the messengers (peace and blessings be upon him) to achieve the interests of the servants, based on inductive reasoning, and whenever we find an interest that is likely to be required by the Sharia(212).

(Response): It has been previously stated that inductive reasoning has also indicated that there are some rulings legislated for the sole purpose of testing, and there is no apparent benefit for the servant in this world. Therefore, associating the matter at hand with worldly interests is no more valid than associating it with the other purpose intended for testing and examination. Associating the matter at hand with one of them without the other is a judgment without evidence. This has been explained in detail before. It has been objected to this evidence that the interest in the general analogy is unaddressed, and that acting upon it is no more valid than disregarding it. This means that associating it with recognized interests is no more valid than associating it with nullified interests, as the legislator has considered some interests while nullifying others. Thus, preferring its consideration over its nullification is a preference without justification, which is invalid(213).

Secondly: The general principle derived from the inductive study of the texts of the Sharia is equivalent in strength to the general principle derived from the wording, as al-Shatibi said: "Generalization, when established, does not necessarily have to be established solely from the wording of generality, but from the inductive study of the contexts of meaning until a general, comprehensive concept is formed in the mind, which operates in judgment like the generalization derived from the wording"(214). Thus, the general interest (maslaha) established according to al-Shatibi is based on inductive reasoning, and its generality is inductive, so it must be applied to all the branches presented.
