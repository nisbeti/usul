Weak(23) and authenticated by al-Albani through his chains(24). If there is a worker whom people trust with their belongings, such as a weaver or a cobbler, they are not liable for what is lost in their hands except in cases of transgression, because the original principle is non-transgression. His oath suffices for the hadith ("But the oath is upon the one who claims")(25).
However, the Malikis viewed this differently, stating that the public interest (maslaha) necessitates holding these craftsmen liable, asserting that the original principle is their transgression. The public interest is a general interest, while the interest of these craftsmen is a private interest. It has been established through the examination of the texts of the Sharia that the general interest takes precedence over the private interest. This examination leads to certainty and is derived from texts such as "Prohibiting the resident from selling to the nomad"(26). Here, the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) prioritized the interest of the people of the market, which is a general interest, over the interest of the nomad, which is a private interest. Similarly, the prohibition of intercepting caravans and the prohibition of hoarding—all these evidences, along with others not mentioned—indicate that prioritizing the general interest over the private interest is intended by the legislator. None of these evidences indicate this matter in isolation, but this intention is derived from the totality of these texts and those rulings(27).
