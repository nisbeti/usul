For society and individuals (251). There is no doubt that there are many matters that are considered beneficial by people but have been disregarded by the Sharia.

Thus, the purpose or objective, or wisdom, in their view, behind legislating a ruling is the benefit of the servants (ibad), and this is what is meant by their saying that the appropriate description is one that results in a benefit or harm depending on whether the ruling is a command for an action or a prohibition against it.

However, the determination of the appropriate beneficial description in the ruling is influenced by the jurist's perspective on what constitutes benefit (maslaha) and what constitutes harm (mafsada), as well as their view on the issue of the goodness or badness of actions. A jurist may conceive of a benefit that later, upon examination and research, proves to be a harm, and vice versa. Alternatively, it may be that the Sharia has abrogated or disregarded it, or that there is something more beneficial and more suitable for reasoning. Al-Shawkani says: "The appropriateness: it is also referred to as the connection, the benefit, the inference, and the consideration of the objectives (maqasid)." (252)

Thus, the reason ('illa) according to these usul scholars is a sign established by the legislator as a cause for the legal ruling. This sign must be apparent and not hidden, such as travel being a reason for shortening the prayer; travel...

It is an evident matter that must be consistent and not vary with different individuals; thus, it is not valid to make hardship a reason for shortening (the prayer) because it is not consistent. The reason must be appropriate, meaning it must achieve a benefit for the servants. In this example, that benefit is the alleviation of hardship, and the realization of this benefit and the removal of this harm is what they call wisdom (hikmah) or objective (maqsad) or the intention of the Legislator.

This concept of the cause (illa) differs from what Ibn Hazm calls a cause, because for him "it is the name for any attribute that necessitates a certain matter with absolute necessity, and the cause never separates from the effect (ma'lul) at all."
