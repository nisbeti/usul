In what they spoke of knowledge of the unseen, which no one knows except Him, and of which they have no knowledge, they said: "Glory be to You," as a way of exalting Allah from having anyone know the unseen other than Him - "We turn to You in repentance; we have no knowledge except what You have taught us," distancing themselves from knowledge of the unseen, except what You have taught us as You taught Adam."

And al-Tabari commented on Ibn Abbas's statement: "Abu Ja'far said: This narration from Ibn Abbas indicates that Allah, Exalted is He, saying: 'And when your Lord said to the angels, I am creating a vicegerent on earth,' is a speech from Allah, Exalted is He, directed to a specific group of angels rather than to all of them, and that those who were addressed were a tribe of Iblis specifically - those who fought with him among the jinn of the earth before the creation of Adam - and that Allah only singled them out with this statement as a test and trial for them, to make them aware of their limited knowledge and the superiority of many who are weaker than them in creation over them, and that His honor is not attained through the strength of bodies and the might of forms, as Iblis, the enemy of Allah, thought. And it is clear that their statement to their Lord: 'Will You place therein one who causes corruption and sheds blood,' was a mistake on their part and a conjecture about the unseen; and that Allah, Exalted is He, informed them of the evil of what they spoke of, and made them aware of it until they repented and turned to Him for what they said and uttered from conjecturing about the unseen, and distanced themselves from Him knowing the unseen other than Him, and He revealed to them from Iblis what he had concealed of the arrogance that he had been hiding from them."

So look, may Allah have mercy on you, at the context of all these narrations, for they indicate that the angels did that out of error, and this is supported by the context of the verses themselves. For Allah, Exalted is He, did not respond to them by saying, "I do this for the benefit of such and such," but rather said to them: "Indeed, I know."
