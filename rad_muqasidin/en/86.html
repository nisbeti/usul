He strived to understand the text and apply it(181).

There is no doubt that the definite singular noun with the article "the" is one of the forms of generality. Ibn Qudamah said about the forms of generality, "The third type: the singular term, such as 'the thief' and 'the female thief,' and 'the fornicator' and 'the female fornicator,' as it is established. It is referred to as the singular noun defined by the non-specific 'the,' and its being one of the forms of generality is the view of al-Shafi'i, Ahmad, Ibn Burhan, Abu al-Tayyib, and al-Buwayti, and al-Amidi transmitted it from most scholars(182). Therefore, based on this, Umar's understanding that lives are taken for lives is indeed an application of the form of generality in the verse.
