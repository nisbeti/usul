This is the definition adopted by the majority of contemporary scholars in their reasoning, and thus it will be the one relied upon in this discussion, God willing.

First - The concept of objectives (maqasid) according to those who affirm them:

The researcher will limit himself to one comprehensive definition, which is "what the Legislator intended to achieve through the rulings or a group of them"(11).

The one who proposed this definition pointed out its benefit in being both comprehensive and restrictive, as he defined it by "what the Legislator intended to achieve" so that everything intended by the Legislator in legislation is included in the definition, whether it pertains to the interests of the obligated individuals or not, such as trials. He did not say "its achievement for the people," but rather "its achievement" alone, so that the definition encompasses what was intended by the legislation to be achieved for them, such as interests and trials, or what is to be achieved from them, such as compliance and understanding. The term "rulings" refers to the very texts that contain the obligations(12).
