The place where a thing must be placed is defined by Allah, Exalted is He; if it is placed in a different location, this is called oppression (zulm).

And we ask them about usury (riba): is it from justice or from oppression? If they say it is from oppression in reason, I say: usury was permissible in the early days of Islam, and the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) dealt with it for a period of time, and it was lawful for them. Was this ruling before its prohibition just or oppressive? If they say it was just, they are correct, and its being just is only due to Allah, Exalted is He, permitting it. If they say it was oppressive, then how could Allah, Exalted is He, and His Messenger approve of oppression? They ask the same question about drinking alcohol, temporary marriage (mut'a), and marrying more than four wives, but the researcher will try to limit himself to the aspect of financial transactions related only to the economic side.

The polytheists objected to their Lord with such objections; they wanted to permit an increase in money in exchange for a delay, as this is something that reason does not deny. So when they sold something and the debt of the buyer became due, the creditor would say to the debtor: "Extend the term for me, and I will increase your money." It was said to them when they did that: "This is usury and is not permissible." When they were told that, they said: "It is the same for us whether we increase at the beginning of the sale or at the time of payment."(35) They then made their famous statement: "Indeed, trade is just like usury." The two are indistinguishable in reason, but Allah responded to them by saying: "And Allah has permitted trade and prohibited usury."(36)
