Indeed, every ruling has a cause for its legislation, and this cause necessitates that Allah legislate certain good rulings. It is essential in our minds to realize a purpose, which is the welfare of the servant. These rulings are binding upon us even before the arrival of the Sharia. The second group believes that there are causes and motivations for legislating the rulings, but these motivations do not necessitate that Allah must legislate these rulings; rather, Allah legislated them purely out of His grace. These rulings have goals or purposes, and all these purposes are for the benefit of the servant. However, the intellect comprehends this in general, while understanding the benefit, wisdom, or purpose in each specific ruling is a matter that is difficult for the intellect. Therefore, the matter is constrained by the existence of the Sharia, and there is no obligation except after its arrival, as Allah, Exalted is He, says: "And We would not punish until We had sent a messenger." Among those who hold this view are Ibn Taymiyyah and Ibn al-Qayyim. The third group believes that it is not permissible to say that there is a cause or motivation that led Allah to legislate this ruling unless He Himself stated that.
