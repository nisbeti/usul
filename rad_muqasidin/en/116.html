Khadijah said to him: "No, rejoice! By Allah, Allah will never disgrace you. By Allah, you maintain family ties, speak the truth, bear the burdens of others, help the needy, honor your guests, and assist in times of need for the truth."

Reflecting on this situation and this statement should direct attention to three aspects of it, all of which testify to the inherent goodness and evil of actions according to reason:

1- The Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) was already embodying these virtues before the revelation and before the Prophethood, appreciating them with his sound mind and pure nature.

2- Lady Khadijah (may Allah be pleased with her) also appreciates these noble actions, accepts them from her husband, and considers them among his noble qualities and virtues, without relying on any legal ruling in this regard.

3- Lady Khadijah is certain that these actions can only receive approval and love from Allah, and that their doer can only receive care, honor, and a good reward from Allah.

Response: We have previously stated that justice and benevolence, and their opposites from the concepts of evil, immorality, and oppression, are all meanings that are not determined by our minds but are according to what Allah intended. Even Ibn al-Qayyim, who cited the words of the Sheikh to argue for the reasoning based on public interest (maslaha), look at what he says.
