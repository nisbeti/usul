From this text, it is clear that the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) according to al-Shatibi are divided into primary objectives, which he means, and Allah knows best, is the objective of creating people for worship or the objective of the servant's compliance with the divine command, as will be discussed, God willing. These primary objectives have no share for the servant, nor are they subject to his whims; rather, they are solely for Allah, Exalted is He. However, after the legislation from Him, Exalted is He, there are what are called secondary objectives, which are related to the one obligated (mukallaf). These objectives may either align with the intent of the legislator or contradict it. If they align, they are permissible and beneficial; if they contradict, they are prohibited and harmful.

The concept of objectives according to al-Shatibi

He says: "The objectives that are considered are of two types: one pertains to the intent of the legislator, and the other pertains to the intent of the obligated. The first is considered from the perspective of the legislator's intent in establishing the Sharia from the outset, from the perspective of his intent in making it understandable, from the perspective of his intent in establishing obligations based on it, and from the perspective of his intent in bringing the obligated under its ruling; these are four types"(274).

Subhi Taha says: "Al-Shatibi clarified that the objectives of the legislator from the Sharia are four types: his intent in establishing the Sharia from the outset, his intent in making it understandable, his intent in establishing obligations based on it, and his intent in bringing the obligated under its ruling. This is not merely al-Shatibi's opinion, but it is established among all; no one can deny it. For everyone agrees that the Sharia aims to bring benefits to people and also aims to make clear to the addressed what it contains of rulings and to obligate them to those rulings and their compliance with them. These objectives have different realities; some are for bringing benefits to people, some are a test for them to distinguish the pure from the impure, some are for their understanding so that the proof may be established against them, and some are their actions, which are obedience and compliance with what is stated in the Sharia. It is known that not all of this is beneficial for people, as some people do not successfully pass this test, some do not comply, and some go astray in what they understand. Allah, the Exalted, says: {And We did not send any messenger except with the language of his people to make clear to them. Then Allah leads astray whom He wills and guides whom He wills. And He is the Exalted in Might, the Wise} (4) [Ibrahim] (275). Thus, the benefit is not in the intent of obligation, nor in the intent of compliance, nor in the intent of understanding, but rather the benefit is in the very act of compliance, in benefiting from what is understood, and in successfully passing the test. Therefore, it is established that the realities of the Sharia are different, and thus it is difficult to provide a definition that encompasses them. This may be another reason added to what I mentioned earlier for the lack of definitions by the predecessors regarding the objectives of the Sharia, and it may be the reason why contemporary definitions have focused on defining only the first type, which is what the phrase 'objectives of the Sharia' generally refers to among the majority of scholars of Sharia who are not specialized in the science of usul.
