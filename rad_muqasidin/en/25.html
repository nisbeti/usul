Zanjani (573 - 656 AH = 1177 - 1258 CE)

Mahmoud ibn Ahmad ibn Mahmoud ibn Bakhtiyar, Abu al-Manaqib Shihab al-Din Zanjani: a linguist and one of the jurists of the Shafi'i school. He was from Zanjan (near Azerbaijan). He discusses deriving branches from the roots (p. 38)

“Al-Shafi'i (may Allah be pleased with him) and the majority of the people of the Sunnah held that purity and impurity, as well as all other legal meanings such as servitude, ownership, emancipation, and freedom, and all other legal rulings like whether a place is pure or impure, and whether a person is free or a slave, are not attributes of the entities to which they are ascribed. Rather, Allah established them as a matter of control and worship without any reasoning behind them. There is no one to overturn His decree or to question His judgment; He is not questioned about what He does, but they will be questioned. Our limited opinions, weak minds, and deficient thoughts cannot reach an understanding of their realities and what pertains to them in terms of the interests of the servants. Thus, that is achieved implicitly and as a consequence, not as a primary aim, since the interest (maslaha) is not obligatory to be realized in His judgment.

He argued in this regard that if Allah, Exalted is He, can punish the disbeliever for his disbelief and the immoral for his immorality, and there is no interest for anyone in that, then it is permissible for Him to legislate laws even if they involve harm and there is no interest for anyone in them. For this reason, Allah, Exalted is He, has tasked humans with what is beyond their capacity, as He said: {So bring ten chapters like it, fabricated} {So bring a chapter like it}. And He said to the angels: {Inform Me of the names of these if you are truthful}. All of this is a tasking for humans.”
