He said, "Indeed, the ultimate goal of reason is to grasp in general the goodness of what the Sharia has detailed, or its evil. Thus, reason comprehends it as a whole. The Sharia then comes to detail it. This is similar to how reason understands the goodness of justice. As for whether this specific action is just or unjust, this is something that reason is unable to comprehend in every action and contract. Likewise, it is incapable of grasping the goodness of every action and its evil.
The Sharia comes to detail and clarify that. What clear reason has understood from this, the Sharia comes to affirm it. What was good at one time and evil at another, and reason has not guided to the time of its goodness from the time of its evil, the Sharia commands it at the time of its goodness and prohibits it at the time of its evil. Similarly, an action may contain both a benefit and a harm, and reason cannot determine whether the harm outweighs the benefit. Thus, reason hesitates in that. The Sharia comes to clarify this, commanding the greater benefit and prohibiting the greater harm. Likewise, an action may be beneficial for one person and harmful for another. Reason does not grasp that. The Sharia comes to clarify it, commanding it for whom it is beneficial and prohibiting it for whom it is harmful. Similarly, an action may appear to be harmful, while within it is a great benefit that reason cannot perceive, and it can only be known through the Sharia, such as jihad and killing in the cause of Allah. It may appear to be beneficial, while within it is a great harm that reason cannot perceive, so the Sharia comes to clarify what is within it of the greater benefit and harm. This, while what reason is unable to grasp regarding the goodness and evil of actions is not less than what it can perceive of that. Thus, the need for messengers is essential. In fact, it is above all needs. The world is in greater need of them than of the messengers, may Allah's blessings be upon them all."
