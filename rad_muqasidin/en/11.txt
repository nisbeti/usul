They have adopted this view because, upon examining the texts of the Sharia, it becomes clear that the Sharia takes into account some interests of the servants. This induction leads to certainty, and al-Shatibi held this view. Accordingly, if a definitive interest (maslaha) contradicts a hadith of singular transmission (ahad), which is considered speculative by them, whether this hadith is general in wording or specific, they prioritize the interest on the grounds that this hadith is of uncertain authenticity, while the interest is definitive; thus, the definitive takes precedence over the speculative. If the text is of certain authenticity, as it is established by the Qur'an or by a mutawatir (consecutively transmitted) hadith, but it is of uncertain implication due to being general, for example, and the general is considered speculative in implication by the Malikis, then it is permissible to specify it by the interest at that point. (4)
However, some contemporary scholars who adopted the concept of public interest (maslaha) did not consider what al-Shatibi looked at regarding the condition that this interest must have evidence supporting its validity. Rather, they acted upon the rejected interests, as will become clear, God willing.
