If the evidence is not established, and the expressions of the jurists differ in defining the claimant and the defendant, al-Qarafi said, "The claimant is the one whose statement contradicts the origin or the custom, and the defendant is the one whose statement aligns with the origin or the custom"(193).
It is noted here from al-Qarafi's words that the apparent customs and habits take precedence over the origin if they testify for the person; thus, if his statement contradicts the prevailing custom, he is the claimant. Ibn Taymiyyah said: "If they disagree regarding the receipt of the dowry, the correct view is that if the prevailing custom is that receipt occurs in debts or tangible assets, then the statement of the one who aligns with the custom is accepted, and this is consistent with our principles and those of Malik regarding the conflict between the origin and the custom, and it appears that the custom is preferred"(194). This clarifies the reasoning behind the statement of Ali (may Allah be pleased with him), and Allah, the Exalted, knows best what is correct.
Twelfth: They argued that Umar suspended the punishment for theft during the year of famine, and they said this is a prioritization of public interest (maslaha) over the text, and the text is His saying, the Exalted, {And as for the thief, the male and the female, amputate their hands in recompense for what they earned as a deterrent from Allah. And Allah is Exalted in Might and Wise}(195). The hadith has been reported in the Musannaf of Abdul Razzaq from Yahya ibn Abi Kathir, who said that Umar said, "Do not cut off in a year of famine or drought"(196). The term "a year of famine" refers to a year of drought and scarcity.
