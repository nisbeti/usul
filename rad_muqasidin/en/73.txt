In their trade, they have the land of war and they have the tithe (ushr) from it. So Umar consulted the companions of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) regarding this, and they all agreed on it. He was the first to take the tithe from them"(143). If this hadith is authentic, which is apparent as it is disconnected, then it seems that they were people of war, and the ruling regarding them has already been established. Perhaps Umar's evidence is the consensus here, and this is clear, not the public interest (maslaha) as they claim. There is no contradiction in the narration with any text, nor is there anything in it that suggests Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) prioritized public interest over the text. Ibn Hazm interpreted Umar's taking of the tithe as being based on a peace treaty they had made, saying, "As for the disbelievers, Allah has only made the jizyah obligatory upon them. If this was a peace treaty along with the jizyah, then it is a rightful and valid covenant." They cited the hadith of Anas ibn Sirin from Anas ibn Malik from Umar ibn al-Khattab: "Take from the Muslims one dirham for every forty dirhams, and from the people of dhimmah (dhimmis) one dirham for every twenty dirhams, and from those without dhimmah one dirham for every ten dirhams"(144).
Abu Muhammad said: "All of this is not a proof because it is not from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him). How could it be, when we have narrated from Umar clarifying all of this? From Abu Mujliz, who said that Umar sent Ammar, Ibn Mas'ud, and Uthman ibn Hunaif to Kufa, and he mentioned the hadith in which Uthman ibn Hunaif measured the land and placed on it such and such, and he took from the wealth of the people of dhimmah who trade with it one dirham for every twenty dirhams, and he imposed on their heads—excluding women and children—twenty-four. Then he wrote this to Umar, and he approved it. It is established that this was in the original covenant and contract with them. And from Abdullah ibn Khalid al-Absi, who said: I asked Ziyad ibn Hudayr, 'Who were you taking the tithe from?' He said: 'We did not take the tithe from a Muslim or a protected person; we took the tithe from the merchants of the people of war, just as they take the tithe from us when we go to them.'" So it is established.
