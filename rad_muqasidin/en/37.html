Likewise, the good things were known before their permissibility, and similarly the bad things. Ibn Taymiyyah says, "It has been established by the address and the wisdom derived from the laws three types: the first is that the action may involve a benefit or harm, even if the Sharia did not intend it. For example, it is known that justice involves the benefit of the world, while oppression involves its corruption. This type is good and bad, and it can be known by reason and the Sharia that such actions are bad; however, this does not attribute a quality to the action that was not there before. But it does not follow from the existence of this badness that its doer will be punished in the Hereafter if the Sharia did not intend that. This is something that the extremists among those who claim the existence of good and evil have erred in, for they said that people are punished for their bad actions even if no messenger was sent to them. This contradicts the text, as Allah, Exalted is He, says, "And We would not punish until We sent a messenger" { وَمَا كُنَّا مُعَذِّبِينَ حَتَّى نَبْعَثَ رَسُولًا } (45), and He, Exalted is He, says, "Messengers as bringers of good tidings and warners, so that there will be no excuse for the people against Allah after the messengers" { رُسُلاً مُّبَشِّرِينَ وَمُنذِرِينَ لِئَلاَّ يَكُونَ لِلنَّاسِ عَلَى اللَّهِ حُجَّةٌ بَعْدَ الرُّسُلِ } (46).

The second type is that when the legislator commands something, it becomes good, and when he forbids something, it becomes bad, and the action acquires the quality of goodness and badness through the address of the Sharia.

The third type is that the legislator commands something to test the servant whether he obeys or disobeys, and the intended purpose is not the action commanded, as when Ibrahim was commanded to sacrifice his son. When they both submitted and he laid him down on his forehead, the intended purpose was achieved, and he was ransomed with a sacrifice. Thus, the wisdom arises from the command itself, not from the commanded action. This type and the previous one are not understood by the Mu'tazila, who claimed that goodness and badness can only exist in what is characterized by them without the command of the legislator.
