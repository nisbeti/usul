The combination of the two leads to the elevation of the hadith, and it ascends to the degree of authenticity, if Allah wills.
From this hadith, these individuals wanted to say that everything that involves hardship is harmful, and that every harm has been commanded by the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) to be removed, and thus every harm is contrary to public interest (maslaha). Therefore, every text that results in hardship and, consequently, harm must be removed based on this hadith.
(Reply to this): Not every harm and hardship that befalls a servant leads to the lifting of the obligation and the abandonment of the text. Al-Shatibi (may Allah be pleased with him) states: "The eleventh issue: where the hardship experienced by the obligated person is outside the usual hardships in ordinary actions, such that it leads to religious or worldly corruption, the aim of the legislator in this case is to lift it in general, and this is supported by the previous evidence. Therefore, concessions (rukhas) have been legislated in this regard without restriction. However, if it is not outside the usual, but rather occurs in a manner similar to how hardship occurs in such ordinary actions, then although the legislator did not intend for it to occur, he also does not intend to lift it. The evidence for this is that if he intended to lift it, it would not be possible for the obligation to remain alongside it, because every ordinary or extraordinary action entails effort and obligation. If the Sharia necessitated the lifting of that effort, it would necessitate the lifting of the obligation itself, which is not correct; thus, what it entails is also incorrect"(87).
From this, it becomes clear that abandoning the text due to harm or hardship in this absolute manner is a rejection of the Sharia in general. And may Allah grant success. More explanation of this principle will come, if Allah wills, when discussing the issue of necessity.
