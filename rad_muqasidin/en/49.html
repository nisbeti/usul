“(I have married you or I have given you in marriage)” and the prohibition of consummating the marriage by referring to it with the phrase “then consummate the marriage”(75).

From the above, it is clear that He, Exalted is He, decrees what He wills, and that He has not left the matter to the whims of humans or what they find pleasing. Thus, the statement of those who claim that the purpose of Allah, Exalted is He, is what His servants see as beneficial is invalid, and the claim that what the servants see as beneficial takes precedence over the established texts from the Book and the Sunnah is also invalid. It is evident that they have not provided any evidence for their claim, and that all evidence returns the judgment to Allah alone, who said, “And Allah knows, while you do not know”(76). Therefore, it is established that anyone who says, “I know what is beneficial” is a liar, and may Allah grant success.

The evidence of al-Tufi regarding his view of prioritizing benefit over text and consensus

It has been established that al-Tufi believes that benefit (maslaha) is an independent legal proof separate from the legal texts. In his view, it does not require a foundational testimony for its consideration but rather relies on rational judgment derived from customs and experiences.

Al-Tufi states, “It should not be said that the Sharia is more knowledgeable of their interests, so take from its evidence, for this is only said in acts of worship that conceal their benefits from the workings of minds and customs. However, the benefit of governing the obligated individuals in their rights is known to them by virtue of custom and reason. Therefore, if we see that the evidence of the Sharia is deficient in providing it, we know that we have referred to its attainment for its guardianship”(77).

From here, the difference between al-Tufi and the third path, which is that of the Malikis, who consider benefit, becomes evident.
