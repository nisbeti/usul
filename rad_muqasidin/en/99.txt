It is forbidden for a person obligated to act to bring forth a prohibition from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him). If he does so, we hear and obey; otherwise, his statement is void. Whoever claims there is an obligation must bring forth a command from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him). If he brings it, we hear and obey; if he does not, his statement is void. From Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, from his father, that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "The most grievous of Muslims in sin is the one who asks about something that is not forbidden, and it becomes forbidden because of his question"(220)(*). The text of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) indicates, as you hear, that everything for which there is no prohibition from Allah, Exalted is He, is not forbidden, and likewise, the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) informed us regarding obligations as well.
And Allah, Exalted is He, said: {And do not say about what your tongues describe, 'This is lawful and this is unlawful,' to invent falsehood against Allah. Indeed, those who invent falsehood against Allah will not succeed}(221). Thus, it is forbidden to rule on something in religion by declaring it forbidden or lawful, and whoever does so is called a liar, and his action is a lie unless Allah prohibits it or permits it in the text or consensus. And Allah, Exalted is He, said: {Say, 'Have you considered what Allah has sent down to you of provision and made of it lawful and unlawful? Say, 'Has Allah permitted you, or do you invent [something] about Allah?'}(222). Thus, Allah, Exalted is He, names the one who prohibits without permission from Allah regarding the prohibition of that thing or permits without permission from Allah regarding its permissibility as an inventor of falsehood(223). And the hadith states: "What Allah has made lawful in His Book is lawful, and what He has made unlawful is unlawful, and what He has remained silent about is excused, so accept from Allah His excusal." Al-Hakim said: The chain of narration is authentic, and Al-Dhahabi agreed.
