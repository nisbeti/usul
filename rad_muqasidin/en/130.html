The sayings of al-Shatibi and commentary on them

He said: "The prohibited causes are causes of corruption, not of benefits, just as the permissible causes are causes of benefits, not of corruption" (254).

From this text, it becomes clear that al-Shatibi considered every prohibited cause to be a cause of corruption. Thus, every cause for acquiring wealth through what is unlawful is prohibited, such as usury (riba), uncertainty (gharar), gambling (maysir), or other types of sales that are forbidden; they are purely corrupt and have no benefit in them, even if people perceive them as beneficial, and the opposite is true.

He said: "If that is the case, it is not correct for anyone to claim that the Sharia was established according to the desires and interests of the servants" (255).

This is a clear statement from him that refutes anyone who says that the Sharia is merely a reflection of what the servants see as beneficial.

He said: "The fact that a benefit is a benefit intended by the ruling and a corruption is a corruption likewise is something that is specific to the Legislator; there is no room for reason in this based on the principle of negation of rational good and evil. If the Legislator has legislated a ruling for a particular benefit, then He is the one who establishes that benefit; otherwise, it could logically be that it is not so, as all things are equal in relation to their original status, and there is no judgment by reason regarding their goodness or badness" (256).

From this text, it becomes clear that rational good and evil have no place in determining what is beneficial and what is corrupt, and there is no room for reason or desire in that; rather, the Legislator is the one who establishes the benefits, and no one else.
