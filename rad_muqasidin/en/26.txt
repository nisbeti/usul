What is beyond His capacity is a harm in which there is no benefit, and the secret of this principle is that Allah, Exalted is He, is the Owner of the kingdom and the Creator of creation. They argued in this regard by using the analogy of the witness to the unseen based on the principle of good and evil. They claimed that the legislation of the ruling is not for the sake of benefit; it is mere frivolity and foolishness, and frivolity is considered ugly in reason. It is like a wise man undertaking to measure water from one sea to another; it is indeed ugly for him to do so, and he deserves blame for it.
And when this principle is established, we say that al-Shafi'i (may Allah be pleased with him) saw that worship (ta'abud) in rulings is the foundation, and the possibility of worship (ta'abud) predominates, and he built its issues in the branches upon it.
Abu Hanifa (may Allah be pleased with him) believed that reasoning (ta'lil) is the foundation, and he based his issues in the branches on it.
To clarify the doctrine of Ibn Taymiyyah and Ibn al-Qayyim and those who agree with them, I will quote his statement.
Ibn al-Qayyim said, and he is among those who assert that reason perceives good and evil before the law, and among those who advocate for reasoning.
In the Key to the Abode of Happiness, there is a statement that undermines the position of the proponents of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia): "Indeed, the ultimate capacity of reason is to grasp in general the goodness of what the Sharia has detailed, or its evil. Thus, reason comprehends it as a whole, while the Sharia provides the details. This is similar to how reason understands the goodness of justice. As for whether this specific action is just or unjust, this is something that reason is incapable of comprehending in every action and contract. Likewise, it is unable to comprehend the goodness or evil of every action."
