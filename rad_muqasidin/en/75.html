The response: A careful examination reveals otherwise. Every ruling has a basis for its application, and the basis for applying this text is the reconciliation of hearts. Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) observed that Islam had gained strength, and the greatest empires in the world had submitted to it. Islam no longer needed the reconciliation of hearts or the appeasement of those whose hearts were to be reconciled. If the text revolves around its reason for existence or non-existence, then the application of the text itself necessitates refraining from granting this group of people anything after Islam had gained strength and its state had become powerful. Is this not an ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) within the text? Is it an infringement upon Umar to say that he prioritized public interest (maslaha) over the text?(148).

Fifth: They argued against Umar's not dividing the land of al-Sawad among the conquerors, contrary to the text (And know that whatever you obtain of war booty, then indeed, for Allah is one fifth of it)(149). He imposed kharaj (tax) on it and designated it as a waqf (endowment) for the Muslims. Instead of dividing the four-fifths of the land that the Muslims had gained among the conquerors, as the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) did with the land of Khaybar, and as the verse states, he made it a waqf for all Muslims.

(Response): First:

1- Ibn al-Zubayr, Bilal, and others called for the division of the land, and when they disputed, the reference should be what Allah the Exalted has commanded to be referred to, as He says (And if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger, if you believe in Allah and the Last Day)(150).
