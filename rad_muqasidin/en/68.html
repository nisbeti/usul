It is either obligatory or non-obligatory, or it is either forbidden or permissible; there is no division in the religion other than these categories at all. Therefore, whoever permits that the rightly guided caliphs have a Sunnah (tradition) that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) did not establish, or that they may introduce an obligation that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) did not impose, or that they may abolish an obligation that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) imposed and did not abolish until he died, then whoever allows any of these matters is a disbeliever (kafir) and a polytheist (mushrik) by the consensus of the ummah without disagreement. And with Allah's help, this matter has been invalidated, and all praise is due to Him. Alternatively, it may be that he intended by following them to emulate his Sunnah (tradition) (peace and blessings be upon him). Thus, we say that this hadith does not admit of any interpretation other than this at all. And it is said to them in their argument by what has been narrated regarding adhering to the Sunnah of the rightly guided caliphs that this is an evidence against you because the Sunnah of all the rightly guided caliphs, without disagreement, is that they should not imitate anyone and that they should not imitate one another, and that they should seek the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) wherever they find it and turn to it and act upon it(125).

Fourth: They argued with the saying of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), "Follow those after me, Abu Bakr and Umar"(126). The response to this is the same as the response to the previous argument.
