He ordered him to wear a headscarf, so he wore it, and the women were captivated by his eyes. Umar said, "By Allah, you will not stay with me in a land where I am." He said, "O Commander of the Faithful, why?" He replied, "It is what I say to you," and he sent him to Basra(184).

(Reply): First: This narration is weak regarding Umar, as it contains Muhammad ibn Sa'id ibn Ziyad al-Qurashi al-Basri, about whom Abu Hatim said: He is a fabricator of hadith, inconsistent in his narrations, and weak. Abu Zur'ah said about him, "Weak in hadith, he is nothing, and his narrations are to be abandoned"(185). In the hadith, there is Jahm ibn Uthman; Abu Hatim said he is unknown(186). Al-Albani(187) said he is not known, so the hadith is not authentic from Umar (may Allah be pleased with him), and it cannot be used as evidence for the issue at hand. And Allah, the Exalted, grants success. The text of the hadith itself indicates its weakness; if Umar exiled him out of fear of temptation for the women of Medina, who are known for their piety and righteousness due to their close time with the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) and their companionship with his companions (may Allah be pleased with them), then how could he send him to the women of Basra, who are further removed in time from the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) and the companions?
