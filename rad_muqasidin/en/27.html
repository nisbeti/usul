The laws come to detail and clarify that. What the clear intellect perceives from that, the laws come to affirm it. What is good at one time may be bad at another time, and if the intellect does not discern the time of its goodness from the time of its badness, the laws come to command it at the time of its goodness and to prohibit it at the time of its badness. Likewise, an action may contain both benefit and harm, and the intellect cannot determine whether its harm outweighs its benefit. The intellect then pauses on that. The laws come to clarify this, commanding the greater benefit and prohibiting the greater harm. Similarly, an action may be beneficial for one person and harmful for another. The intellect does not perceive that. The laws come to clarify it, commanding it for the one for whom it is beneficial and prohibiting it for the one for whom it is harmful. Likewise, an action may appear harmful on the surface, but within it is a great benefit that the intellect cannot perceive, and it can only be known through the law: like jihad and killing in the cause of Allah.
