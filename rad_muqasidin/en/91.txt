(Al-Radd): Sheikh Ibn Uthaymeen (may Allah have mercy on him) says, "It is not permissible to suspend these punishments under any circumstances, and what has been narrated about Amr (may Allah be pleased with him) that he lifted the punishment during the year of famine requires two things.
The first thing: the authenticity of the narration, as we demand from anyone who claims this to provide a reliable transmission to the Commander of the Faithful, Umar ibn al-Khattab (may Allah be pleased with him).
The second matter: that Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) only lifted the punishment due to an existing doubt, as the people were in a state of famine, and a person might take something out of necessity, not for the sake of satiation. It is known that it is obligatory for Muslims to feed the one who is in dire need of food. Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) feared that this thief was in dire need of food and was prevented from it, so he seized the opportunity and stole. This is what is befitting of Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) if the narration attributed to him is authentic."(197)
It is noted from the hadith being cited two things:
First: That the narration of Yahya ibn Abi Kathir did not mention it in a form of hearing, as he said: "Umar said," and Ibn Hajar said, "Yahya ibn Abi Kathir al-Yamani is from the minor successors, a well-known memorizer with many transmissions, and it is said that he did not have authentic hearing from a companion, and al-Nasa'i described him as a mudallis."(198) The hadith of a mudallis is not accepted if he does not explicitly state that he heard it, and this is well-known among the hadith scholars.(199) Yahya al-Qattan said: "The mursal narrations of Yahya ibn Abi Kathir...
