And he said, "The difference is that the question directed to Allah, or to any of His actions, or any of His sayings, or any of His rulings, if its purpose is: objection, denial, mockery, or accountability, then it is misguidance and disbelief. From this perspective, the question mentioned in the verse that Ibn Hazm used as evidence for the prohibition of seeking the reasons behind rulings is His saying, Exalted is He: {And let those in whose hearts is disease and the disbelievers say, 'What did Allah intend by this as an example?' Thus Allah leads astray whom He wills.}

So the question in the verse is posed by the disbelievers and those in whose hearts is disease, and it carries the meaning of mockery and belittlement, as is clarified in the reason for its revelation.

(The response) This is a specification without a specific reason; the question includes the question of inquiry, which is the reality in language, and then it encompasses more than one metaphorical meaning according to those who accept metaphor. Then, those among the polytheists of Quraysh or others who say it is permissible to question Allah with a question of accountability are all polytheists and people of the Book who believe in His lordship, and it is far-fetched that the verse denies those who ask this question.
