As for his argument using the words of Khadijah, it is very strange, as it suggests that the Quraysh did not know any religion that would guide them towards good, whereas the opposite is true. In fact, they had remnants of the religion of Ibrahim (Abraham), and some of them were following the religion of Ibrahim, such as Zayd ibn Amr ibn Nufail. The laws of the religion of Ibrahim were widespread, such as pilgrimage (hajj), as well as other laws from other religions like Judaism and Christianity. Therefore, the author's claim that Khadijah derived the rulings of these matters solely through her intellect is a significant overreach and a claim that requires evidence.

Commentary:

What has been mentioned above are the arguments of those who say that the Sharia (Islamic law) has objectives (maqasid) that Allah, Exalted is He, intended behind legislating the rulings. The primary objective that Allah, Exalted is He, legislated is what His servants perceive as beneficial. It is noted from the above that if this claim were accepted, the Sharia would become chaotic among rational individuals, who would pull at its edges based on their conjectures without regard to its foundations from the Book and the Sunnah. This would necessitate changing the rulings with the change of individuals, the passage of time, and the differences in locations. This leads to an overall alteration of the Sharia and the establishment of a new law, which is unanimously considered invalid. They are required to provide evidence for what they claim, yet they have not presented any explicit proof to support their position, and all they possess is mere conjecture, which is prohibited. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said, "Beware of conjecture, for conjecture is the most false of speech."
