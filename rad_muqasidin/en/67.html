He (peace and blessings be upon him) said that Abu Bakr and Umar are the best of their men after the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), and they erred as... Abdullah ibn al-Zubair informed them that a delegation from Banu Tamim came to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), and Abu Bakr said: "Appoint al-Qaqa' ibn Ma'bad ibn Zararah," while Umar said: "Rather, appoint al-Aqra' ibn Habis." Then this was revealed: {O you who have believed, do not raise your voices above the voice of the Prophet, and do not speak loudly to him as you speak loudly to one another, lest your deeds become worthless while you perceive not}(124). Ibn al-Zubair said that thereafter, whenever Umar narrated a hadith from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), he would do so quietly. Abu Muhammad said: "Whoever errs, it is not permissible to accept his word without evidence that validates it. As for his saying (peace and blessings be upon him), 'Adhere to my Sunnah and the Sunnah of the rightly guided caliphs,' we know that he (peace and blessings be upon him) does not command what cannot be done, and we find that the rightly guided caliphs after him (peace and blessings be upon him) differed greatly. Therefore, there must be one of three possibilities, with no fourth: either we take everything they disagreed upon, which is impossible and cannot be done, as it contains contradictions; thus, this option is invalid. Or it may be permissible for us to choose any of those matters, which is a departure from Islam because it would mean that the religion of Allah, Exalted is He, is left to our choice. Furthermore, if this were the case, then if we followed the opinion of one of them, we would have abandoned the opinion of the other, and that is inevitable. Thus, we would not be following their Sunnah, and we would have fallen into contradiction with the mentioned hadith. The only remaining option is to take what they unanimously agreed upon, and that is only what the other companions (may Allah be pleased with them) agreed upon in following the Sunnah of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) and adhering to it. Moreover, when the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) commanded adherence to the Sunnah of the rightly guided caliphs, it necessarily implies one of two things: either he (peace and blessings be upon him) permitted them to establish practices other than his practices, which no Muslim would say. Whoever allows this has disbelieved and apostatized, and his blood and wealth become lawful, for the entire religion...
