He said: "I have not known a horse before this that has reached this," so Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) said, "Then we take one sheep from every forty sheep and do not take anything from the horses; take one dinar from every horse."(153) Here, he (may Allah be pleased with him) contradicted the text and imposed on the people what was not obligatory upon them.
(Response): The action of Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) is understood as "Umar did this when it was pleasing to its owners"(154), as al-Bayhaqi (may Allah have mercy on him) said, and there is nothing wrong with this in terms of pleasing the soul. This is supported by what al-Bayhaqi narrated and indicated, and he applied the previous hadith to it, which is the narration of Harithah ibn Mudrib who said: A group of people from the Levant came to Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) and said: "We have acquired wealth in horses and slaves, and we would like to have zakat and purification on it." He said, "What my two companions did before me, I will do." Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) consulted Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) and a group of the companions of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), and Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) said: "It is good if it is not a tax imposed on them regularly."(155)
Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) clarified that the element of obligation should not be present, nor should the element of regularity, thus confirming that it was not obligatory, nor did the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) or Abu Bakr do it, and its owners are the ones who requested it willingly. There is nothing wrong with this, as what is prohibited is the element of obligation according to the hadith "The wealth of a person is not lawful except with his good pleasure."(156) "It has been confirmed that Umar only took it as a voluntary charity from them, not as an obligation."
