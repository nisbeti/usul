The issue presented is one of the specific matters from that alleged generality, especially - and I say especially - when a correct evidence from the authentic Sunnah comes to exclude this matter and there is a specific text regarding it; it is not permissible to reject this specification, as they did in rejecting the hadith "the two sellers have the choice until they part"(218) which is related to the general prohibition of selling uncertainty. And Allah, Exalted is He, said: {And do not say about Allah that which you do not know}(219). So where do we have the knowledge that Allah, Exalted is He, legislated that ruling for the sake of such and such benefit, and then from where do we have the knowledge that Allah, Exalted is He, must never specify that ruling and must apply it to all similar branches? There is no power and no strength except with Allah.

The third evidence: They said, "If the public interest (maslaha) in the appropriate analogy (qiyas) is not considered, it would lead to some occurrences being devoid of the ruling of Allah, Exalted is He, because the texts and the analogies derived from them are limited, while incidents are not limited, and the limited cannot suffice for the unlimited. Thus, it is necessary that some new incidents be devoid of rulings, and the absence of rulings on incidents is invalid."

(Response): Ibn Hazm (may Allah have mercy on him) said, "We bring you one text that encompasses every incident that has occurred or will occur until the Day of Judgment, and it is the authentic report we mentioned earlier with its chain of narration, which is his saying (peace and blessings be upon him), 'Leave me as long as I leave you, for those before you were destroyed due to their excessive questioning and their disagreement with their prophets. If I command you with something, then do of it what you can, and if I prohibit you from something, then avoid it'(4). It is thus established that what the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) did not mention is not obligatory because he did not command it, and it is not forbidden because he did not prohibit it, so it remains necessarily permissible. Whoever claims otherwise...
