But perhaps the guidance of Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) is the guidance of the Malikis in this issue, as they refer in this matter to prioritizing the apparent over the original. If the apparent indicates the wrongdoing of the craftsmen and the joint workers, and if they claim at that point that they did not fall short or neglect, they are in this case contradicting the apparent, as the apparent indicates wrongdoing by the workers. Since they prioritize the apparent over the original, then the apparent is what they rely on, and the one who claims contrary to the apparent is the claimant mentioned in the hadith, “The burden of proof is on the claimant, and the oath is on the one who denies”(190). Therefore, he must provide proof and establish that he did not exceed or fall short, and if he does not bring proof, he is liable. This is their understanding of the hadith, as their principle is “the one who contradicts the apparent is the claimant”(191), differing from others among the Shafi'is, as their principle is “the original is not abandoned even if the apparent contradicts the original”(192). This is a difference in interpreting the meaning of the claimant, and perhaps Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) understood this as well. The disagreement is a disagreement in interpreting the meaning of the Prophet’s (peace and blessings be upon him) statement regarding “the claimant,” and not a contradiction to the text based on public interest, and may Allah the Exalted grant success. This disagreement is a well-known foundational disagreement, as Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) held that the craftsman is the claimant, and he must be held liable unless he brings proof that he did not exceed, because the apparent indicates wrongdoing due to its prevalence and notoriety. Ibn Farhun said, “Know that the science of adjudication revolves around knowing the one being claimed against, as it is a fundamental issue, and they did not differ in the ruling for each of them, and that the claimant must provide proof if the demand is denied, and that the one being claimed against must take an oath.”
