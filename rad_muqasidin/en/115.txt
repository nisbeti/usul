Al-Raisuni said in response to that, "The theory of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) according to Imam al-Shatibi" (p. 246).
Indeed, denying the goodness and corruption in actions, and the benefit and harm they may bring, and that reason guides you to much of this, is not only a departure from rationality, but also a neglect and suspension of the clear indications found in a great many legal texts.
The Noble Qur'an has commanded the doing of good, righteousness, and what is known, and has prohibited evil, corruption, and what is reprehensible. It has commanded justice and benevolence, and has forbidden indecency and oppression. It has informed that it has made lawful the good things and prohibited the vile.
If it were not for the fact that these commandments and prohibitions have meanings understood by those addressed, there would be no benefit in addressing them with them. Who can deny that when people were addressed with them, they had a clear understanding of their content and meaning? And that they were addressed based on that understanding? Indeed, they valued them appropriately. For this reason, they knew that one who commands this good and this righteousness, and forbids this evil and this corruption, without exceeding them, cannot be a liar. From this, a number of distinguished Arabs hastened to embrace Islam.
