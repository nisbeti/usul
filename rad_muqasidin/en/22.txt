So we pause at this point, and these rulings are judged as good only by the Sharia, and it is not correct to say that there is an objective from Allah unless He, Exalted is He, states that. We must not speak about Allah what we do not know. Ibn Hazm and those who agreed with him held this view; however, a fourth, strange school emerged that is different from these scholars, which is the view of al-Tufi, the Hanbali. Many contemporary scholars of objectives (maqasid) relied on this view, whether intentionally or unintentionally, which is that Allah has a general purpose and objective, which is to eliminate harm and achieve benefit, and this is known through reason in transactions, not in acts of worship. Thus, texts are abrogated in transactions if they contradict what people see as beneficial. However, the Malikis do not abrogate texts for this reason unless the Sharia supports their type from more than one perspective, and thus they elevate it to the level of certainty, so that the speculative texts are abrogated or interpreted accordingly.
