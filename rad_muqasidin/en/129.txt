They refer to it in the definition as the legal cause (illa shar'iyya). Al-Zarkashi said: "It is divided into rational (aqliyyah): which does not become a cause by the making of a maker but by itself, and it is necessary and does not change with time, like the movement of the mover, and legal (shar'iyya): which has become a cause by the making of a maker. Then he said: 'The legal causes are only called causes metaphorically or broadly; otherwise, in reality, the cause is what necessitates the ruling by itself, which is the rational cause, while that which necessitates it through another is not a cause but rather an indicator of the ruling." (253)

The concept of objectives (maqasid) according to al-Shatibi
It is noted from al-Shatibi's words about interests (masalih) that what he intended by the interests that are to be preserved and attained is everything that the Legislator has commanded, and not everything that people desire and think is an interest. The harm (mafsada) that is to be avoided and minimized is everything that Allah has prohibited, and not everything that is difficult for the servants to do. The Sharia did not come in accordance with the desires of the servants in everything they wish for; rather, it came only to achieve servitude to Allah alone by adhering to His obedience and avoiding His prohibitions, Exalted is He. The greatest interest for creation is their attainment of Allah's pleasure and their entry into His Paradise, and the greatest harm is the attainment of Allah's wrath and their entry into His Hellfire. Any hardship that results from obligation is insignificant compared to the hardship resulting from the Fire, may Allah protect us from it. Thus, the intended meaning here is the purpose in the definition adopted by the researcher: the purpose of creating the servants is indeed to worship Him, Exalted and Most High. Allah made obedience to Him a means to enter Paradise and gain His pleasure, and made disobedience a means to His Hellfire and wrath, as He made clear.
