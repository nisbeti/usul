This evidence, as conveyed by al-Ghazali, indicates that he said they may have relied on meanings that they know are supported by the principles of the Sharia (Islamic law), even if they do not specify them(227).

(Risalat al-Raisuni on Ibn Hazm and the response to him)

Raisuni said, "The meaning of the verse { لا يُسْأَلُ عَمَّا يَفْعَلُ وَهُمْ يُسْأَلُونَ } is that Allah, Exalted is He, is not questioned about His actions, nor is anyone allowed to object to His actions and rulings. Allah judges, and there is no one to reverse His judgment. In contrast, the servants are questioned, held accountable, blamed, and they make mistakes. This is because Allah, on one hand, is the Creator of all things and the Owner of all things; to Him belongs what is in the heavens and what is in the earth; to Him belongs the first and the last. And on the other hand, He is the Most Just of judges, the Most Merciful of the merciful, and the Most Truthful of speakers, and He is the All-Knowing, the All-Aware. Based on this foundation—or these foundations—come His actions and rulings. There is no room for correction or objection in them.

For this reason, the Lord, Exalted is He, { لا يُسْأَلُ عَمَّا يَفْعَلُ } means He is not questioned in a manner of accountability or objection.

This is the meaning of the question in the verse. There is no doubt that directing such a question to Allah, Exalted is He, is disbelief.

Here lies the error—or fallacy—of Ibn Hazm in prohibiting reasoning based on this verse and similar verses. The question regarding the reasons for legal rulings, and likewise the question about the secrets and wisdoms behind Allah's actions, is a question of understanding and learning; it is based on the principle of "inquiry," which is the request for understanding. This type of questioning and inquiries has been expressed by the prophets and the righteous.
