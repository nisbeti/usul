Based on what has preceded, the researcher believes that anyone who relies on what is called public interest (maslaha) to justify a ruling that contradicts the Sharia is relying on falsehood, and the researcher will mention, if Allah wills, examples of that later on.

Similarly, anyone who relies on the theory of objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) as a general concept to justify any ruling that contradicts the ruling of Allah and His Messenger is relying on nothing. Unfortunately, the researcher has found many proponents of secular and liberal thought in the current times who rely on this theory, I mean the theory of maqasid, sometimes calling it the general principles of the Sharia, and at other times the spirit of the Sharia, or the spirit of the text. They depend on it to justify their deviation from the clear and explicit Sharia rulings. I do not know of any spirit of the Sharia or what is called general principles; rather, these are names for which Allah has revealed no authority. Therefore, they should not be heeded or taken into account. For this reason, the researcher has elaborated on this topic, even though it is fundamentally an usul (principles) topic, but it is closely related to contemporary financial and economic jurisprudence issues. In fact, most contemporary economic fatwas that contradict the explicit texts of the Qur'an or the Sunnah are based solely on what they call the objectives of the Sharia and what has branched from it.
