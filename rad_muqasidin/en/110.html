And the question is not about the motive for legislating the ruling, meaning what made Allah legislate this ruling, which is the question of "why" or "for what reason." If we were to descend further, then Mary and Zechariah only asked Allah, "How can we have this now?" in order to obtain the answer, and we have nothing but the illusions of the proponents of objectives (maqasid) and their false assumptions. Thus, it is correct that the question of Mary and Zechariah (peace be upon them) was about the aspect (of the matter) and not about the motive for the legislation, so there is no basis for using this as evidence here.

Thirdly, his citing the hadith of Umm Salamah: "And al-Nasa'i and Ahmad narrated regarding the reason for the revelation of the verse that Umm Salamah said to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): 'Why are we not mentioned in the Qur'an as the men are mentioned?' So Allah revealed this verse: 'Indeed, the Muslim men and the Muslim women...' (the verse)."

The response to that is that the question was in the form of "Why are we not mentioned?" and not "Why does Allah not mention us?" Umm Salamah was asking about the reason from their side that made Allah not mention them, similar to the companions' question of "How is this?" This is like the narration in the Sunan of al-Darimi (1/500):

Sulaiman said, "O Abu Hazim, why do we dislike death?" He said, "Because you have ruined the Hereafter and built up the world, so you dislike to be transferred from construction to ruin." He was only asking about the reason within them that made them dislike death, not what was the reason that made Allah cause them to dislike death.
