Evidence of Those Who Advocate for Public Interest (Maslaha Mursala)

Below are their evidences as they are scattered in the books of usul (principles of jurisprudence), whether from the imams or from contemporary scholars. The mention of the statements of some contemporary scholars is merely to complete the discussion, so that it cannot be said that there are other evidences. Therefore, I have attempted to summarize all that has been cited in support of public interest (maslaha mursala).
The foundation of their evidence is that the legislator has placed the genus of public interests within the genus of rulings. Based on this evidence, one contemporary scholar said(130): "As for when there is no text or consensus regarding the ruling on an incident, nor analogy to it - and there is a public interest in it - it is likely that it is required by the Sharia, for wherever there is public interest, there is the law of Allah, Exalted is He - and this incident takes on a legal ruling based on the public interest sought therein."
"The approval of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) of Mu'adh's ijtihad (independent reasoning) when he sent him as a judge, and his question about what he should rule if a matter arose for which he found no ruling in the Book or the Sunnah, and his saying: 'I will exercise my judgment and not hold back.' The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) struck his chest and said: 'Praise be to Allah who has guided the Messenger of the Messenger of Allah to what pleases Allah.'"(131)
The scope of working with public interest is in transactions and similar matters that fall under the category of customs. The original principle in this type of obligations is to pay attention to the meanings and motivations upon which the rulings are based; thus, they are reasonable interests through which the mind can perceive the goodness of what the Sharia demands and the evil of what it prohibits. And Allah, Exalted is He, has obligated upon us what our minds can perceive as beneficial and has prohibited what our minds can perceive as harmful..."(132)
__________
(130) This is Dr. Mustafa al-Baghā in his book The Impact of Disputed Evidences, p. 54.
(131) The Authority of Public Interest (Maslaha Mursala), p. 88. There is much discussion about this hadith, and it is rejected by Ibn Hazm, and al-Albani also rejected it, yet it is cited in the books of jurisprudence.
(132) See al-Muwafaqat, vol. 2, p. 225, and The Impact of Disputed Evidences, p. 55.
