As for Umar's (may Allah be pleased with him) establishment of imprisonment, his evidence was the action of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him). Look at what Abd al-Rahman al-Maliki said(215): "-Imprisonment: the legal imprisonment is the hindrance of a person and preventing him from acting on his own, whether that is in a country, a house, a mosque, or a prison designated for punishment or otherwise. The evidence that imprisonment is a punishment from the Sharia is what has been narrated from Bahz ibn Hakim from his father from his grandfather: 'That the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) imprisoned a man on suspicion and then released him.' And from Abu Huraira: 'That the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) imprisoned someone on suspicion for a day and a night.' Imprisonment during the time of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) was in a house or in a mosque, and the same was true during the time of Abu Bakr. There was no designated prison for adversaries. When it came to the time of Umar, he bought a house for Safwan ibn Umayyah for four thousand dirhams and made it a prison. Umar imprisoned al-Hutay'ah for slander, and he imprisoned Subay' for his questioning about al-Dhariyat, al-Mursalat, al-Nazi'at, and similar verses..." Thus, imprisonment was indeed practiced by the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him). The establishment of a permanent prison is for the complete application of Islam as the number of Muslims increased and the territory of the Islamic state expanded, similar to the establishment of the diwan for the same reason. It is for the application of Islamic punishments. The same can be said about everything that is introduced for the sake of organization and the proper care of Muslim affairs, and the proper application of Islam, such as dating by the Hijra or otherwise. The same applies to modern means and inventions such as cars, airplanes, machines, factories, telephones, radios, televisions, and rockets... The original ruling regarding things is permissibility unless there is evidence of prohibition. Thus, traveling is permissible, and the means are permissible unless there is evidence of their prohibition. Therefore, it is permissible to ride a beast, a car, or an airplane, but it is not permissible to ride a pig, for example, not because it is unsuitable for that, but because the evidence has prohibited it, and its prohibition applies to all forms of benefit from it.
