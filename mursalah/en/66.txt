The Sharia (Islamic law) aims to preserve life by all means, and thus mutilation has not been legislated even though it is the most effective deterrent and punishment. Likewise, killing has not been legislated for theft or drinking alcohol. If a ruling is established for the benefit of these interests, and it is not known that the Sharia has preserved that interest by establishing that ruling, it would be a matter of placing the Sharia based on opinion and a ruling based on pure reason." This is a valuable statement. Similarly, it was rejected by Majd al-Din Ibn Taymiyyah. He said in his manuscript(159): "The unestablished interests (masalih mursala) cannot be the basis for rulings, as stated by al-Baqillani and a group of theologians."
Taqi al-Din Ibn Taymiyyah also rejected it, stating(160): "The unestablished interests are a form of legislation in religion that has not been permitted, and they resemble in some respects istihsan (juridical preference), rational improvement, and opinion, and the like." Ibn al-Hajib al-Maliki also rejected it. It is mentioned in the book (Hashiyat al-Taftazani and al-Jurjani on Mukhtasar al-Muntaha al-Usuli): "The unestablished interests are interests that do not have a foundational basis in the Sharia, even if they align with the principles of interests and are accepted by reason. Then he said: We have no evidence, so it must be rejected as in the case of istihsan"(161).
Ibn Humam al-Din al-Iskandari al-Hanafi also rejected it. He said(162): "... This category known as unestablished interests, the preferred view is to reject it. There is no evidence for its consideration, and it is a legal evidence that must be rejected. They said that many occurrences would be left without rulings, and we said we deny the necessity (i.e., we do not concede that the absence of consideration leads to occurrences being devoid of rulings) because generalities and analogies are comprehensive, and even if it were absent, the negation of every specific evidence would lead to its ruling being the original permissibility (i.e., if in an incident there is no basis from the four sources of evidence, then one acts according to a general principle established in the Sharia by consensus, which is original permissibility, as it is the default for things)."
The esteemed Imam, Sheikh Muhibb Allah ibn Abd al-Shakur, who is of the Hanafi school, also rejected the unestablished interests. He said(163): "If no one knows of any considerations that are appropriate, it is strange to consider it as unestablished; it is called unestablished interests, which is a proof according to Malik, while the preferred view among the majority is to reject it.
