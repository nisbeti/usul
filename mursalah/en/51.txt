The acts of worship are based on the interests of the Hereafter; they do not disagree with what we have mentioned. Rather, their intention is that they are not based on a clear, suitable description in the minds that can be used for analogy, unlike transactions and other rulings that are directly detailed based on the interests of worldly life. Most of them are based on clear reasons in the minds, which allows for analogy to be made upon them. Thus, he considers interests (masalih) a suitable description, merely because it becomes clear in the mind that they are interests, and thus they become a reason for the rulings.
As for Imam al-Nabhani, he discusses the topic of the causality of interests in detail under the title: "The pursuit of interests and the prevention of harms are neither a reason for the Sharia as a whole nor a reason for any specific ruling"(114). He distinguishes the objective of the Sharia or the goal from the reason, stating(115): "The objective of Islamic Sharia as a whole is the pursuit of interests and the prevention of harms, and the pursuit of interests is not a reason for Islamic Sharia as a whole, nor is it the goal of every specific ruling from the rulings of the Sharia, nor a reason for any specific ruling." He also states(116): "Accordingly, the pursuit of interests and the prevention of harms do not exist in the legal rulings, neither in terms of their derivation nor in terms of their legislation, and they are not reasons for the rulings in any way. Even the legal reasons derived from legal evidence are not the interests of the servants, but rather the meanings indicated by the legal evidence, regardless of interests and harms."
