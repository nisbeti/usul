His statement here pertains to the hired craftsman, and his assertion that it does not contradict a text is incorrect. This is because it contradicts the hadith of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): "Whoever entrusts a deposit and it perishes, he is not liable." As for his claim that not holding liable leads to abandoning the practice of manufacturing or opens the door to lying, deceit, and embezzlement, the response to this is that the Sharia ruling must be applied regardless of the outcomes perceived by minds. As for abandoning manufacturing, there is no harm in that if it results from the application of the Sharia, and it will then be the good and the interest. However, if it leads, according to his statement, to lying, deceit, and embezzlement, then the legislator did not legislate to prevent that by abandoning rulings, but rather legislated punishments that are derived from their sources. Moreover, I believe that holding craftsmen liable leads to their scarcity, which is a harm, while not holding them liable leads to their abundance, then to competition, then to price reduction, and to the flourishing of the industry. Industry is the backbone of nations today, so the interest lies in not holding them liable. Whose mind, then, would be the source for determining the interest upon which legislation is based?
It has been attributed to Ali ibn Abi Talib (may Allah be pleased with him) his statement regarding holding craftsmen liable: "People cannot be right except with that." This does not indicate his consideration of public interest (maslaha mursala). If this is established from him, it is understood in the context of sales. It has been narrated that Ali did not hold anyone among the hired workers liable. Here is what al-Bouti himself transmits from al-Shafi'i in al-Um(225): "He said in al-Um: (It may be narrated from a source that the scholars of hadith do not affirm that Ali ibn Abi Talib (may Allah be pleased with him) held the washer and dyer liable, and said: People cannot be right except with that. Ibrahim ibn Abi Yahya narrated this to us from Ja'far ibn Muhammad from his father that Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) said that, and it is narrated from Umar holding craftsmen liable from a weaker source than this, and we do not know of either of them being established.
