The opinion of Imam Izz al-Din ibn Abd al-Salam

Imam Izz al-Din ibn Abd al-Salam rejects legislation based on public interest (maslaha) that is not explicitly established. For him, public interest is defined by legal rulings. He states(288): "Most of the interests and harms of this world are known by reason," then he says(289): "There is no comparison between the interests and harms of this world and those of the Hereafter," and then he says(290): "As for the interests and causes of both worlds and their harms, they can only be known through the Sharia." Therefore, his conclusion is that interests and harms can only be known through the Sharia, because the interests and harms of this world, that is, interests based on personal desire or rational determination, are insignificant compared to the interests and harms of the Hereafter. The interests and harms of the Hereafter can only be known through transmission. Thus, the interest is what the Sharia commands, and the harm is what the Sharia prohibits. This is the ultimate statement regarding the non-recognition of public interest as a source or foundation for legislation. This clarifies that his use of the term public interest does not indicate interests based on opinion, desire, or reason; rather, for him, public interest is the legal ruling and what the legal ruling necessitates. In fact, if matters are left to reason, it may determine interests or harms that are contrary to what is established in the Sharia, and consideration is only for the Sharia. Therefore, you will not find any importance in his view in labeling something as harm or interest; rather, some things that the Sharia commands he may label as harm, and some things that the Sharia prohibits he may label as interest. For example, he states(291): "Sometimes the causes of interests are harms, and they may be commanded or permitted not because they are harms but because they lead to interests, such as cutting off the hands of those who steal to preserve lives, and risking lives in jihad. Likewise, all legal punishments are not sought because they are harms but because they are intended by their legislation, such as cutting off the hand of the thief, executing the highway robber, killing the criminals, stoning the adulterers, and exiling them, as well as the discretionary punishments.
