The texts indicate that this Sharia has objectives that serve as the purpose of its application to creation. Allah, Exalted is He, said: "And We have not sent you except as a mercy to the worlds"(67). This text negates—by His saying "and We have not"—the idea that the sending of the Messenger has any purpose other than being a mercy to the worlds. The sending of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) was indeed through the message, so the message itself is the mercy, meaning that the existence of this message applied to people is the mercy. If we wish to understand this mercy, we can imagine a society where there is no trace of the application of Islam, and where its systems and laws are of human origin; such laws lead to corruption, injustice, misery, and a life of hardship. Meanwhile, the Sharia of Islam brings people out of corruption into righteousness, from darkness into light, and from misery into prosperity.

And Allah, Exalted is He, said: "And We send down from the Qur'an that which is healing and mercy for the believers"(68). The two verses indicate what the Legislator intends from the message and from the Qur'an, which is healing and mercy, and these are objectives that result from application. They are objectives in the sense of goals, meaning results that may arise from the Sharia or may not arise. The texts do not indicate that the attainment of these results is inevitable, because there are people who reject this message, and there are communities that reject it and do not apply it, but rather fight against it; thus, the goal, or the mercy for which the message was sent, will not be realized among them. The sending of the Messenger as a mercy, and the sending down of the Qur'an as healing, means the intended state that is to exist from the sending of the message and the sending down of the Qur'an, or in other words, that Allah, Exalted is He, has shown mercy to the worlds by sending them the message, or by sending them the medicine or healing remedy they need for their ailments of misery, corruption, and hardship. The Sharia was sent to be a mercy, and there is nothing in the two verses that indicates it was legislated because it is a mercy; rather, the meaning is that its legislation and sending is the mercy.
