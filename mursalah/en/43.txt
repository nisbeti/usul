If something is prohibited and, from the perspective of reason, there is a benefit alongside the harm, then the benefit is not the intended purpose of the prohibition; rather, the harm is the intended purpose. It is seen that this applies to all details of the Sharia, meaning that the benefit (maslaha) for which there is no text operates in the same manner, and it is given a ruling based on what it brings of benefit or what it averts of harm. He says(96): "Every legal principle that is not supported by a specific text, and is consistent with the actions of the Sharia and whose meaning is derived from its evidences, is valid and can be relied upon, and referred back to if that principle has become established by its total evidences, because the evidences do not necessarily indicate certainty in the ruling by themselves without the addition of others to them... and this includes the type of reasoning with the mursal (unattributed hadith) which was relied upon by Malik and al-Shafi'i, for although it does not have a specific text supporting the branch, it is supported by a general principle, and the general principle, if it is definitive, may equal the specific principle." However, al-Shatibi sets a criterion for considering the benefit that has no text, saying(97): "It is necessary to consider the alignment with the intention of the legislator, because benefits are only considered benefits in the way that the legislator has established them." He also conditions that the benefit should not be negated in the view of the Sharia.
