The last point is that the participation of people in public roads and utilities and the allowance of the right to benefit from them by traveling on them, as well as the prohibition of the exclusivity of one person over another in this regard, are considered legislative rulings. As for organizing the implementation of these rulings through various means and methods such as sidewalks, traffic signs and signals, and the procedural steps necessary to verify the eligibility to drive vehicles on them, all of this is considered procedural rulings.

Thus, it is understood from the memorandum that public interest (maslaha) is not a legislative source according to the scholars who accept it, but rather it is the means and methods necessary for implementing the legal rulings. The memorandum also stated(286): "Legislative rulings must be derived from the evidence of the Sharia, and it is prohibited to take them from other sources."

It also stated(287): "The Book indicates that the material means in existence are permissible to benefit from, as they have been made subservient by Allah, Exalted is He, to His creation and as a favor upon them. Allah, Exalted is He, said: {He is the One who created all that is in the earth}. The Sunnah and the actions of the rightly guided caliphs also indicate that procedural rulings and purely worldly rulings are generally permissible unless there is specific evidence prohibiting any of them, such as what was established in the hadith regarding pollinating the date palms, in which he (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "You know best about your worldly affairs," and the digging of the trench, which was inspired by the Persians as a defensive policy, and some of the Companions learning the craft of catapults and using them, and Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) using the diwan inspired by non-Muslims to organize matters of grants procedurally, among other examples. Therefore, whenever it is established that a ruling is procedural, any means or method by which the legislative ruling derived from the evidence of the Sharia is implemented may be adopted, provided it achieves the objectives in the best manner, while ensuring the simplification of procedures and facilitating them, and being gentle with the people when enacting them if they pertain to the state. These means and procedures are what the scholars of the principles of Sharia sometimes refer to as public interest (maslaha) considering that there is no specific evidence for them, but they fall under the general legislative ruling, which is a means and method for its implementation."
