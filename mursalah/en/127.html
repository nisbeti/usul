Acting with the Intention of Achieving Public Interest

What has been previously stated regarding the prohibition of using public interest (maslaha) as evidence for ruling on an action does not mean that seeking public interest is not permissible. It is natural for a person to be inclined towards what they perceive as beneficial and to shy away from what causes them harm; however, the actions they undertake in both cases must adhere to the legal rulings.

The legal rulings are five: what is established as obligatory by evidence must be performed regardless of its outcome, what is established as recommended remains recommended even if we see that there is benefit in abandoning it, what is established as disliked remains so even if it appears to us that there is benefit in doing it or harm in leaving it, and what is established as forbidden must be avoided, even if reason suggests that there is benefit in doing it or harm in leaving it. These rulings do not change except if there is evidence from the Sharia that abrogates them, as in certain cases indicated by the Sharia, such as not cutting off the hand of a thief during a famine, consuming carrion in cases of necessity, and uttering a word of disbelief under duress, and similar situations.

As for what is permissible, which is what the Sharia indicates is allowed, it is natural for a person to act in it according to what brings them benefits—as they perceive them—or to satisfy their desires, or according to what wards off harm—as they perceive it—or what alleviates pain or reduces burdens and restrictions.

Engaging in trade is permissible, engaging in agriculture is permissible, and it is permissible for a person to work as a hired laborer or to be an employer; they may work in whatever they see as beneficial for them, such as what yields them more profit, or what provides them with more comfort, or what aligns with their inclinations and tastes, provided they fulfill the rights of Allah in any work they choose.

A person may consume foods and wear clothing that they like according to their interests, desires, tastes, or inclinations, provided they avoid the forbidden, such as pork, carrion, intoxicants in food, and anything that exposes the private parts or silk for men in clothing.
