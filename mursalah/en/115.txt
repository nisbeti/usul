He said: "And thus he rejected it and considered it akin to istihsan (preferential reasoning) in judgment, as we have clarified from his texts previously. As for the unqualified public interest (mislaha mursala) according to him, it is not so; rather, it is that which the Sharia has indicated is intended through evidence from the Book, the Sunnah, and consensus (ijma). He states: 'Every public interest that pertains to the preservation of a legal objective known to be intended by the Book, the Sunnah, and consensus is not outside these foundations, but it is not called qiyas (analogy); rather, it is called public interest (mislaha mursala) since qiyas is a specific principle, and the fact that these meanings are intended is known not by one evidence but by many evidences that are countless from the Book and the Sunnah, and the circumstances and various signs, which is why it is called public interest (mislaha mursala)'(298). Therefore, he agrees here with al-Qarafi and al-Shatibi in that the unqualified public interest is not devoid of evidence in the absolute sense, but it has evidence or countless evidences. The discussion is not about the term unqualified public interest, but about the intended meaning of it. If the intended meaning is that it is unqualified from evidence in the absolute sense, then no one has said that. And if the intended meaning is that it has evidence that is beyond count but does not have a specific evidence particular to it, then there is no disagreement in considering it. Al-Ghazali states: 'And if we interpret public interest as the preservation of the objectives of the Sharia, there is no room for disagreement in following it; rather, it must be definitively established as a proof'(299).

Thus, al-Ghazali distinguishes between istislah (public interest reasoning) and unqualified public interest (mislaha mursala), rejecting istislah unanimously because it is mere opinion and not revelation, while accepting unqualified public interest if it is intended by the Sharia, and its intention is known through evidence from the Book, the Sunnah, and consensus.

If we say that unqualified public interest is what is deemed beneficial by reason, and no evidence has come regarding it from the Book, the Sunnah, or consensus, then it is rejected by him. This is clear from following his statements, and the discussion is not about the term since there is no dispute in terminology, but rather about the meaning of the terminology.
