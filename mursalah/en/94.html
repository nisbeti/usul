One-fifth of it was allocated, and the lands were divided into one thousand eight hundred shares, divided into eighteen sections, each section comprising one hundred shares. Because the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) divided the lands of the conquered territory (anwa) in Khaybar among the Muslims, they requested Umar ibn al-Khattab (may Allah be pleased with him) to divide the land of al-Sawad among them. However, Umar refused to yield to the opinion of the Companions who were demanding the division of the land in this matter. In fact, it is reported that he said: "O Allah, suffice me with Bilal and his companions." He consulted the Ansar and the Muhajirun, and then said: "I saw that I should hold the lands with their inhabitants and impose a tax on them, and upon their necks is the jizya (poll tax) which they will pay, so it will be a spoils for the Muslims, the fighters and their offspring, and for those who come after them. Do you see that these frontiers must have men who will remain steadfast in them? Do you see that these great cities like Syria, al-Jazira, Kufa, Basra, and Egypt must be filled with armies and provisions for them? From where will these be provided if the lands and their inhabitants are divided?" Then he said: "And there is no one among the Muslims except that he has a right and share in this spoils." They agreed with him on his opinion and all said: "Your opinion is the best, and what you said and saw is good. If these frontiers and cities are not filled with men, and provisions are not provided for them, the people of disbelief will return to their cities..."(256). Some of the Companions, such as Bilal, Abdur-Rahman ibn Awf, and al-Zubair, argued with Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) and all the Companions regarding the action of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) in dividing the land of the conquered territory in Khaybar. However, Umar saw that the situation had changed, that there were soldiers enlisted, and that there were many Muslims who did not attend the battle and were not among the fighters, and they had descendants. All of this required substantial funds and large expenses, so he believed that the interest of the Muslims necessitated holding these lands and not dividing them among the warriors, but rather keeping them under the control of their inhabitants, imposing a tax on them, and spending its revenue on the Muslims. He consulted the Companions on this, and they agreed with him, saying: "Your opinion is the best."

This issue, as I have mentioned above, occurred regarding the lands of the conquered territory in Iraq, Syria, and Egypt.
