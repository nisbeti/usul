The public interest (maslaha), al-Ghazali stipulates here that the interest must be necessary, meaning it is not sufficient for it to be merely a need (hajiyya) or an enhancement (tahsiniyya), and it must be comprehensive, that is, for the preservation of the whole even at the expense of the part or some. This is evident from the example he provided regarding Muslims on a ship. It is also required that it be definitive (qat'i) and not speculative (dhanni), as in the case of using a Muslim as a shield in a fortress. Al-Ghazali stated regarding these examples and similar ones: "When we interpret the public interest as the preservation of the objectives of the Sharia, there is no room for disagreement in following it; rather, it must be established that it is a proof, and where we mentioned a disagreement, it is when two interests and objectives conflict, and at that point, the stronger must be preferred"(301). The examples we have cited from him pertain to the conflict of interests and objectives. The Sharia texts indicate the demand for the preservation of the whole and the preservation of the part or the portion. However, a conflict arises such that one must be sacrificed, either the whole or the part. What is to be sacrificed? Here too, al-Ghazali rejects that the determination of which should be sacrificed is based on reason; rather, it must be the Sharia that clarifies which of the two interests or objectives is primary and more preferable. He presented a discussion on this, stating(302): "In the matter of using a shield, opposing the objective of the Sharia is forbidden, and refraining from fighting the disbelievers is a violation of the objective of the Sharia. If it is said that we do not deny that opposing the objective of the Sharia is forbidden, but we do not concede that this is a violation, we say: overpowering the disbelievers and the dominance of Islam is an objective, and in this is the eradication of Islam and the dominance of disbelief. If it is said that refraining from harming a Muslim who has not sinned is an objective and this is a violation of the objective, we say: this is an objective, and we have been compelled to violate one of the objectives, and there must be a preference, and the particular is insignificant in relation to the comprehensive. This is particular in relation to the comprehensive, so it cannot oppose the comprehensive. If it is said that it is acknowledged that this is particular, but it is accepted that the particular is insignificant in relation to the comprehensive, then the insignificance of the Sharia towards it is known through a text or analogy based on a text. We say: we have recognized this not through a specific text but through various rulings and the conjunction of indications, leaving no doubt that preserving the plan of Islam and the lives of Muslims is more important in the objectives of the Sharia than preserving a specific individual for an hour or a day, especially when the disbelievers will return to kill him; this is something that cannot be doubted as well."
