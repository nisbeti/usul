Similarly, among the examples they have deduced is His saying, Exalted is He: “And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me”(187). This is a text that indicates a specific objective of the Legislator regarding a particular action, and it does not imply any benefit, nor does it indicate that this action is for the sake of benefit. Likewise, if we consider it rationally, is the reality of creating people for worship a benefit for them? Worship involves obligations and restrictions, and how can reason determine the benefit of one who does not exist due to the obligation of creation?

As for their argument from His saying, Exalted is He: “And We have not sent you, [O Muhammad], except as a mercy to the worlds”(188), and from His saying: “And We send down of the Qur'an that which is healing and mercy for the believers”(189), and from His saying: “This is enlightenment for mankind and guidance and mercy for a people who are certain”(190), we have discussed this previously(191).

And as for His saying, Exalted is He: “Indeed, Allah commands you to render trusts to whom they are due and when you judge between people to judge with justice. Excellent is that which Allah instructs you. Indeed, Allah is ever Hearing and Seeing. Allah commands you to render trusts to whom they are due and when you judge between people to judge with justice. Excellent is that which Allah instructs you. Indeed, Allah is ever Hearing and Seeing”(192), this text also does not indicate benefit, neither in its literal meaning nor in its inferred meaning, nor does it contain anything that indicates that the purpose of legislation is benefit or that benefit is its motivator or impetus. Rather, it is reason that sees justice as a benefit, and that immorality, wrongdoing, and oppression as corruption. These terms cannot be understood unless they are connected to a foundation that we understand by analogy. Do you not see that the positive and distorted laws have what they call justice, and they have what they call immorality and wrongdoing, and they have what they call truth and falsehood? Yet, if we look at the reality of their justice and compare it to our Sharia, we would find it to be pure oppression, and we would find their truth to be falsehood, and their goodness to be immorality... These terms can only be understood through what they indicate. The terms justice, goodness, immorality, wrongdoing, and oppression mentioned in the verse can only be understood in reality through what they indicate.
