From this, we understand what al-Shatibi means by public interest (maslaha), and this is the meaning that the imams and scholars from the early generations intended when they spoke about bringing about benefits and averting harms, and when they established the jurisprudential principles regarding that. This is not like what some contemporary scholars say to justify modern fatwas and to permit some of what Allah has prohibited, taking the benefits as they perceive them with their minds as a source of legislation.
Al-Shatibi elaborated and discussed in his book al-I'tisam ten examples of public interests (masalih mursala) that confirm for us the meaning of public interests as he mentioned. These are the examples:
The compilation and copying of the Qur'an. He said regarding it(279): "For this relates to the preservation of the Sharia, and the command to preserve it is known, and to prevent the means to disagreement in its origin, which is the Qur'an, and it is known that there is a prohibition against disagreement in that with no further elaboration."
The consensus of the Companions on the punishment for the drunkard being eighty lashes. This, in his view, is established by the consensus of the Companions, and the reason for its transmission is that there is no specific text regarding a certain number, but the Companions have agreed on what is best in a permissible matter, relying on the fact that the Sharia establishes causes in some instances in place of the effects(280).
Liability of craftsmen. Regardless of whether the liability of craftsmen is permissible or prohibited, al-Shatibi's statement regarding liability relates to the principle that what is necessary for fulfilling an obligation is itself an obligation. Through these examples, his applications of public interests become apparent, and in this meaning, there is nothing in them, nor are they considered a source of legislation. Likewise, his position is clarified through the rest of his examples, which are:
Punishing the accused.
Imposing taxes.
Punishment by taking wealth for certain offenses.
