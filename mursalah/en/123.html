Regarding the silence on prohibition specifically concerning the general evidence of permissibility, the texts indicate that the default ruling for things is permissibility unless there is evidence of prohibition. What is meant by things here are the entities, so the absence of a text indicating the ruling of a specific matter does not mean that the legislator is silent about it; rather, it means that he is silent about its prohibition, and its ruling is clarified by the legal principle that the default ruling for things is permissibility unless there is evidence of prohibition. In this regard, the saying of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) is relevant: "The most grievous of Muslims is the one who asks about something that has not been prohibited, and it becomes prohibited because of his question"(310). His saying "has not been prohibited" means that it falls under the categories of what is permissible according to the general rule. And his saying "and it becomes prohibited" means that a specific text has been revealed that specifies the general rule.

As for the silence regarding additional detail and clarification of what has already been clarified, it is as if the text comes to clarify the ruling of a specific action, such as His saying, Exalted is He: {And those who make zihar from their wives, then return to what they said, must free a neck}(311). The mention of "a neck" does not specify whether it is a believing neck or a disbelieving one, a female or a male neck, a child or an adult neck. This is not silence about clarifying the ruling; rather, it is silence about restricting the ruling, that is, silence about providing additional clarification. If the legislator does not specify the type of neck that must be freed, then any neck would suffice. The absence of specification here is considered a concession.

An example of this is also found in the Noble Qur'an in His saying, Exalted is He: {Indeed, Allah commands you to slaughter a cow}(312). If they slaughtered it without any question, their slaughtering of any cow would suffice. If they slaughtered a cow that was a mature one, or a young one, or not yellow, or had spots, meaning it did not conform to the descriptions that were later requested of them, their action would still be sufficient, and their act would be excused. Thus, the lack of specification here does not pertain to the existence of a case with no ruling; rather, it pertains to the lack of additional detail and clarification of what has already been clarified.
