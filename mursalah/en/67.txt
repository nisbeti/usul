He was following the principles of reason. They said: First: If you did not consider this, the occurrences would be empty. We say: We deny the necessity because the generalities and analogies are general, and also the absence of a basis is a basis for permissibility. Secondly: The companions (may Allah be pleased with them) were content with taking into account the interests. We say: Rather, they only considered what they were aware of regarding the consideration of its type or its kind.
And al-Shawkani also addresses this, as he rejects qiyas (analogy) as a principle, and takes from qiyas only what has a stated cause, even if he does not call it qiyas. He says(164): "And when you have understood what we have clarified and all that we have established has become clear to you, know that the accepted qiyas is that which has a stated cause in the text and in which there is a definitive negation of difference, and what falls under the category of the implied meaning of the speech or the nuance of the speech according to the terminology of those who call that qiyas, and we have previously established that it is from the concept of agreement. Then know that the deniers of qiyas did not claim to disregard everything that is called qiyas, even if it has a stated cause or is definitively negated in terms of difference, rather they considered this type of qiyas to be indicated by the evidence of the origin, included under it and subsumed within it..." His statement means that he accepts the stated cause or that in which there is a definitive negation of difference and does not accept the inferred cause. He does not accept the cause that is based on analogy, as analogy is a form of inference. His rejection of maslaha mursala (unrestricted public interest) is more appropriate, as it is based on the unrestricted analogy which is not a form of inference at all, but rather a matter of rational judgment.
The Zahiri (literalist) and Twelver (ithna 'ashari) sects have also rejected istislah (public interest), as they do not accept qiyas (analogy) at all.
Al-Shatibi, in his Muwafaqat and in his I'tisam, advocates for the acceptance of public interests (maslaha mursala) and states that the two imams, Abu Hanifa and al-Shafi'i, adhered to it(165). This is contrary to what al-Amidi mentioned(166). Imam Shihab al-Din al-Qarafi, of the Maliki school, also accepts it. He says(167): "As for public interest (maslaha mursala), it is reported that it is specific to us, and when the schools of thought are lacking, they find that when they analogize and differentiate between the two issues, they do not seek a witness by considering that meaning."
