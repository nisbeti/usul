Many of the usul scholars (theologians) adhere to this classification, asserting that the interests (masalih) that the Sharia (Islamic law) safeguards and promotes fall into these three categories: necessary, supplementary, and embellishing. Most of those who have written on this subject in our time support the notion of these objectives (maqasid) and categorize them in the same manner, namely into necessary, supplementary, and embellishing. The necessary objectives are almost unanimously agreed upon by the usul scholars to be five: the preservation of religion, life, intellect, lineage, and wealth. Some have added a sixth, which is the preservation of honor. However, there is no consensus among the laws on the five mentioned. Al-Shawkani stated in Irshad al-Fuhul(61): "It has been objected to the claim of consensus among the laws on the five mentioned that wine was permissible in earlier laws and in the early days of Islam, and it was responded that the permissible in those laws is what does not reach the level of intoxication that removes the intellect, for it is prohibited in every religion, as al-Ghazali said. Ibn al-Qushayri narrated this from al-Qaffal, then he disputed it, saying: The news has been transmitted that it was permissible absolutely, and it has not been established that the permissibility was to the extent that does not remove the intellect. Al-Nawawi also stated in his explanation of Muslim, and his wording is: As for what is said by those who have no understanding that intoxicants have never ceased to be prohibited, this is false and has no basis." Al-Shawkani adds(62): "I say: I have contemplated the Torah and the Gospel and found in them only the absolute permissibility of wine without any restriction against intoxication; rather, they explicitly mention what follows wine in terms of intoxication and its permissibility, thus the claim of consensus among the religions on prohibition is not established. Some later scholars have added a sixth, which is the preservation of honor, for the custom of rational people is to sacrifice their lives and wealth for their honor, and what is redeemed by necessity is more deserving of protection, and it has been legislated that the punishment for slander is a limit." Al-Qarafi also counted the necessary objectives as six and called them the universals(63).
Contemporary usul writers repeat that the necessities are five and that they are observed in every religion, mentioning them while omitting the preservation of honor, except for Imam Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani, who acknowledges the objectives of the Sharia but does not accept the claim that they are observed in every religion, and he argues that the necessities are eight, not five.
