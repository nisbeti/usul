The Position of Scholars on Public Interest (Maslaha)

I almost titled this section "The Scholars' Rejection of Public Interest" instead. For no one can be said to have accepted it without reservation. However, it is often attributed to Malik and the Malikis, and many contemporary scholars advocate for it. Dr. al-Bagha(137) states: "The words of the usuliyyin (jurisprudents) almost converge on the fact that the opinion of public interest is a matter of disagreement, and the prevailing view is that it is not valid for inference, as there is no evidence to support its consideration, and only Imam Malik (may Allah have mercy on him) has adopted this view."

Some contemporary proponents of public interest argue that the four imams have indeed accepted it, despite reports of their rejection of public interest—except for Malik. They also claim that the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) accepted it. Al-Bouti(138) states: "Public interests are accepted by consensus among the Companions, the Tabi'in (Successors), and the four imams." These conclusions and opinions contradict the transmitted texts; so where is the truth? Furthermore, the conclusions of contemporary scholars contradict the clear and explicit reports from the imams, as reported from Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal, and even what they themselves stated, such as al-Ghazali in al-Mustasfa. What, then, is the truth?

The truth is that if a correct narration is reported from any of them stating that they do not accept public interest, we must affirm what they said, unless it becomes clear that they have abandoned that statement. However, to delve into their jurisprudential branches and search for some rulings, and if we find some of them for which we do not know the evidence, to hastily claim: "Its evidence was public interest," is not correct at all, nor does it hold any validity in reasoning. The statement of those who say, "Public interests are accepted by consensus among the Companions, the Tabi'in, and the four imams," is a bold claim that has no justification other than a disregard for knowledge and a reckless approach to expressing opinions.
