The unregulated interests (masalih mursala) are not a legal proof.

We have previously clarified that the argument based on unregulated interests is disputed among the imams and usuliyyin (jurisprudents), and that the majority reject it. This is the correct view, and Allah knows best. I will discuss this now through:
First - Discussing the evidence presented by those who advocate for it.
Second - Presenting evidence that it is not a legal proof.
Third - Clarifying that those who spoke of unregulated interests did not mean that they are unregulated in an absolute sense, but rather that they are unregulated in relation to the specific evidence on the particular issue, while they fall under the general or universal evidence. This will be illustrated through the views of the two imams, al-Qarafi and al-Shatibi. I have presented al-Shatibi's view first due to the clarity and prominence of the intended meaning in his texts, followed by al-Qarafi's view.
Fourth - Clarifying that those who spoke of interests among the imams did not intend the deception that those who mix truth with falsehood resort to, but rather intended the interest: that which is sought by the Sharia, and this will be shown through the views of the two imams, Ibn Abd al-Salam and al-Ghazali.
Fifth - Refuting two doubts:
That there are recognized interests, canceled interests, and interests that are neither recognized nor canceled.
The argument for permissibility based on the absence of evidence for prohibition, or based on the action not contradicting the Qur'an, the Sunnah, consensus, and analogy.

Moreover, I have appended the last three points mentioned here (the third, fourth, and fifth) to the fourth chapter of this book.
