Hajj. Contemporary scholars may have transmitted what al-Shawkani mentioned, citing Ibn Daqiq al-Eid(142): "There is no doubt that Malik has a preference over other jurists in this regard, followed closely by Ahmad ibn Hanbal, and others hardly escape from considering it." Al-Qarafi(143) stated: "It is, upon investigation, present in all schools of thought."
Such statements cannot be firmly attributed to Imam Ahmad, as his principles do not include public interest (maslaha), and his stance on reasoning is far removed from the general public interest. As for what is reported from some scholars that it exists in all schools, we will elaborate on this, as those who accept it do not mean that it is universally applicable, but rather that it is derived from specific evidence, not from general or comprehensive evidence.
Regarding what Dr. al-Bouti mentions in his book (The Criteria of Public Interest) about the absence of public interest (maslaha) among the principles relied upon by Imam Ahmad not preventing it from being considered valid by him, I do not find this argument sound. Even if that does not prevent it, what is the proof that it is considered valid by him? The research into his jurisprudential opinions does not justify the claim that he considers public interest (maslaha), as the existence of a matter for which we do not find its evidence is not sufficient to claim that he derives rulings from public interest (maslaha).
What some attempt to assert, that the concept of public interest falls within the broad meaning of analogy (qiyas), is a statement without benefit, as analogy is different from public interest (istislah). If deriving a ruling based on public interest is included within analogy, then everything said about categorizing the reasoning into valid, abrogated, and general would be invalidated, and consequently, public interest (istislah) would be invalidated. This argument is not based on an understanding of the subject: analogy is valid because it is supported by conclusive evidence. Public interest (maslaha) does not fall under analogy, and this is what the scholars who accepted public interest (maslaha) have established. There is no need for lengthy discussion on this; those interested may refer, for example, to al-Shatibi's statements in al-I'tisam, which we will mention when discussing al-Shatibi's opinion.
