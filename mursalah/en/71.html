Discussion of the Evidence Presented by Advocates of Istislah

We have previously mentioned the evidence relied upon by those who advocate for istislah, and I will discuss it here in the same order that I presented it earlier.

The first evidence: The legislator has established the genus of interests within the genus of rulings

This is their first evidence and it is the cornerstone of their arguments. It is based on the premise that the legislator has objectives that are the interests of creation, and that the original principle in the Sharia is to consider these interests(179).

Indeed, the Sharia has objectives, as indicated by the Sharia itself, and these objectives pertain to the Sharia as a whole and not to each individual ruling. This is evidenced by the texts that imply that the Sharia has objectives, such as His saying: “And We have not sent you, [O Muhammad], except as a mercy to the worlds” {وما أرسلناك إلا رحمة للعالمين}(180), and His saying: “And We send down in the Qur'an that which is healing and mercy for the believers” {وننزل من القرآن ما هو شفاء ورحمة للمؤمنين}(181), and His saying: “This is a guidance for the people and a mercy for those who are certain” {هذا بصائر للناس وهدى ورحمة لقوم يوقنون}(182). Reflecting on these texts and scrutinizing their implications reveals that mercy, healing, and guidance are the results of accepting, applying, and adhering to the Sharia, and it indicates that these results or interests are a consequence of applying the Sharia as a whole. When the Sharia is implemented in society, its condition becomes upright, it flourishes, and it adheres to the sound path and the straight way that the Qur'an guides to: “Indeed, this Qur'an guides…”
