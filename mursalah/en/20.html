In the disagreement regarding consensus (ijma), Dr. Kamil Musa(29) states: "The Zahiris condition that consensus must be among the Companions; otherwise, it is not valid, due to their view that the statements of those in authority (ahl al-hall wa al-aqd) are only considered in relation to the Companions, and because they are well-known and limited in number in Medina. As for those after them, they have multiplied and spread across distant lands ... The consideration of consensus among the Shi'a is not because it is a consensus, but because it includes the statement of the infallible Imam, and his statement alone is a proof for them. The consensus among the Khawarij is the consensus of the Companions before the division occurred; as for after it, the consensus is only that of their sect, because the criterion is the statement of the believers, and no believer is recognized by them except one who adheres to their doctrine. The consensus of the Companions is a proof without dispute among those whose opinion is valued. As for the consensus after them, there is disagreement regarding its possibility of existence; the Zahiris denied it, as did Imam Ahmad ... Malik said: The consensus of the people of Medina is sufficient on its own, and the disagreement of others is not considered. The majority did not see this, while the Imami and Zaydi Shi'a stated: The consensus of the Ahl al-Bayt is a proof, and by Ahl al-Bayt is meant: Fatima, Ali, Hasan, and Husayn."

There has also been disagreement regarding the definitiveness of the proof of consensus. Al-Shatibi(30) said: "... Some usul scholars have argued that the status of consensus as a proof is speculative, not definitive, as they did not find in the individual proofs, when considered separately, anything that provides certainty."

There has also been disagreement on whether the denial of the authority of consensus constitutes disbelief or not. Sheikh Muhammad al-Khudari Bak(31) said: "Some usul scholars stated that denying the ruling of definitive consensus is disbelief, such as the consensus of the Companions based on explicit statements transmitted from them in a mutawatir manner, because denying it entails denying a definitive proof, which in turn involves denying the truthfulness of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him), and this is disbelief. A group said: It is not disbelief because the proof of the authority of consensus is not definitive, thus it is speculative and does not provide knowledge." He also quoted Imam al-Haramayn(32): "As for speculative consensus, the one who denies its ruling is not a disbeliever by consensus."
