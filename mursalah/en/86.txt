And among what has been reported from al-Shafi'i in al-Um (The Book of al-Shafi'i) is his statement(221): "The depositary (mustawda') is not liable unless he acts contrary to the agreement. If he does act contrary, he will not be released from liability except by returning the deposit to its owner, even if he returns it to the place where it was, because his initial handling of it was as a trustee, and he has moved beyond the limits of trust, and the owner of the property did not renew the trust with him." Al-Shafi'i (may Allah be pleased with him) does not find an excuse for those who consider the hired worker (sani') to be liable because it contradicts the hadith, unless he says, for example(222): "The trustworthy (amin) is one to whom you have given something willingly, trusting in his honesty, not one who is given a wage for anything of what you have entrusted to him. My giving this wage differentiates him from the trustworthy who took what he was entrusted with without compensation." The material, when given to the worker, is merely a deposit and nothing else, and the hadith applies to it. As for the wage given to the worker, it is not in exchange for the deposit of the raw material, but rather it is a wage for the work done on it. Therefore, there is no liability on the worker. However, if a person is given a wage for safeguarding the material or item, he is also not liable, because it is a deposit and he is a trustee, unless he is negligent, and the details of this matter are found in its places in the books of jurisprudence. If the deposit is entrusted to him with a wage on the condition that he is liable, then this condition of liability is invalid, because it is not in the Book of Allah, and every condition not found in the Book of Allah is invalid. This is contrary to the hadith that removes liability from the trustworthy. This condition makes the contract akin to insurance contracts, and all insurance contracts are prohibited by Sharia(223).
In summary, making artisans liable is not permissible. Advocating for it based on public interest (maslaha) or preference (istihsan) is abandoning the Sharia for personal whims. As for al-Bouti's statement(224): "The rationale for the public interest (in making artisans liable) is that people have a need for craftsmanship, and they often leave their belongings with others, while they are mostly unknown in terms of their craftsmanship and trustworthiness, and negligence and lack of care are prevalent among them. If they were not held liable, it would lead to one of two things: either a complete abandonment of craftsmanship, or they would work without being liable for anything, thus opening the door to lying, deceit, and embezzlement of funds.
