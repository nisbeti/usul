"We have permitted the consumption of another's wealth under coercion due to our knowledge that wealth is insignificant in the balance of Sharia, alongside blood, and this is known through many evidences."

Imam al-Ghazali (may Allah have mercy on him) affirms that the preference between two interests or objectives or two harms is solely a matter of Sharia and not for any human being to decide. He says(303): "If it is said, why did you not understand that preserving the greater is more important than preserving the lesser in the matter of the ship, in coercion, and in famine? We say: We did not understand this, as the ummah has unanimously agreed that if two people are coerced into killing a person, it is not permissible for them to kill him, and it is not permissible for Muslims to consume the flesh of a Muslim in famine. Thus, consensus prevents the preference of the greater. As for the preference of the whole, it is known either by certainty or by a presumption close to certainty, which must be followed in Sharia, and there is no text to the contrary. In contrast, the majority is prevented by consensus in coercion and in famine. Therefore, under these conditions that we have mentioned, it is permissible to follow interests, and it becomes clear that istislah (public interest) is not a fifth source in itself, but whoever seeks public interest has legislated just as whoever seeks what is good has legislated."

Furthermore, al-Ghazali also said in response to those who mistakenly think he advocates for public interest(304): "If it is said that you have leaned towards the opinion of public interests in most of these matters and then you have included this principle among the imagined principles, let this be included among the correct principles so that it becomes a fifth source after the Book, the Sunnah, consensus, and reason(305). We say: This is among the imagined principles, for whoever thinks it is a fifth source has erred, as we have returned public interest to the preservation of the objectives of Sharia, and the objectives of Sharia are known through the Book, the Sunnah, and consensus. Thus, any interest that does not return to the preservation of an objective understood from the Book, the Sunnah, and consensus is among the strange interests that do not align with the actions of Sharia, and it is invalid and discarded. Whoever adheres to it has legislated just as whoever seeks what is good has legislated."
