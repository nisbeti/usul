The implication of this statement is that the Sharia is incomplete and we must add sources from our own to complete it, which contradicts the text of the Qur'an: "Today I have perfected for you your religion"(203). Furthermore, this is an unsuccessful logical hypothesis - that is, the limitation of texts and the unlimited nature of events - do you not see that a single text, such as the legal maxim derived from the texts of the Qur'an, which is: the default ruling of things is permissibility unless there is evidence for prohibition, provides a ruling for all things in the world, no matter how numerous they are?

All events since the completion of the revelation of legislation until the Day of Judgment have their rulings in the Sharia based on its limited texts. This is not through induction but through the texts of the Qur'an: "Indeed, We have sent down to you a Book in which is your mention"(204), "We have not omitted in the Book from anything"(205). The proofs are not only the Qur'an and the Sunnah, but they include the Qur'an, the Sunnah, the consensus of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them), and analogy (qiyas), and these are complete in themselves without any human addition; any addition to the proofs under the pretext of the inadequacy of the proofs for new occurrences is merely an accusation of deficiency against the Sharia, which contradicts the Qur'an. The addition would be rejected rulings because they do not come from the Ruler, who is Allah alone(206). As for their claim that the additional proof must be the public interests (masalih mursala), even if we assume we concede the need for an additional proof, making it the public interests is an arbitrary determination without evidence.

The fifth proof(207): that the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) acted upon the public interests.
