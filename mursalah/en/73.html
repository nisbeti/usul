And my evidence here against their statement is the existence of some texts that contradict this general principle for them. In fact, all the texts they cited as examples of induction do not contain any indication of public interest (maslaha), and I will address here most of them. Among their evidences is His saying, Exalted is He: “Messengers bringing good tidings and warning, so that people will have no argument against Allah after the messengers”(184). The intended purpose of the Legislator here is that people have no argument against Allah, and there is no indication of public interest in it, neither in its literal meaning nor in its implied meaning. More than that, there is no public interest for the servants according to reason, nor is there any interest of a kind that is considered suitable or appropriate that minds would accept. Likewise, there is no suitable description that conveys or implies causality. Thus, the argument from this text is invalid. Furthermore, how did those who advocate for public interests find a benefit for people in this text? Which is better for people: to have an argument or not to have one? Is it not better—and thus in their interest—to have an argument so that they may be saved from punishment like the people of the interval? Similarly, among their examples of induction is His saying, Exalted is He: “And He it is who created the heavens and the earth in six days, and His Throne was upon the water, that He might test you as to which of you is best in deed”(185), and His saying: “Who created death and life to test you as to which of you is best in deed”(186). If the Creator's purpose in creating the heavens and the earth and in creating death and life is to test people as to which of them is best in deed, then where is the indication in the text that the purpose of the Legislator is public interest? There is no such indication in the text. And just as we said in the previous text, we say here: does reason see that testing people is in their interest? There is no way to support this claim. And can reason judge that the public interest for the servants is that they be created and tested, either to Paradise or to Hell? Therefore, not only do the texts not contain any indication of public interest, but the claim of this interest is not something that minds would accept. Public interest is not a clearly suitable and defined description. Likewise, there is no public interest here according to the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia).
