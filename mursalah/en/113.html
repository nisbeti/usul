The evils that the Sharia has mandated to achieve the genuine benefits associated with them, and its naming as benefits is a metaphorical expression of naming the cause by the name of the effect."

And he says(292): "Perhaps the causes of evils are benefits that the Sharia has prohibited, not because they are benefits, but because they lead to evils, such as the pursuit of forbidden pleasures, doubts, and disliked matters while neglecting the hardships of obligatory and recommended acts. These are benefits that have been prohibited not because they are benefits, but because they lead to genuine evils, and their being called evils is a metaphorical expression of naming the cause by the name of the effect."

Thus, for him, benefits are what the Sharia has requested or what is necessitated by what the Sharia has requested. Evils are what the Sharia has prohibited or what is necessitated by that prohibition. If the benefit has not been established by the Sharia, he rejects it. Therefore, he advocates for istihsan (juridical preference) and rejects masalih mursala (unrestricted benefits). He says(293): "The uniqueness of God in obedience is due to His exclusive bestowal of the blessings of creation, preservation, sustenance, and religious and worldly reform. There is no good except that He brings it, and there is no harm except that He removes it. None of the servants is more deserving of obedience than another, as none of them has any favor in relation to God regarding what I have mentioned. Likewise, there is no judgment except His; His rulings are derived from the Book, the Sunnah, consensus, sound analogies, and valid proofs. Therefore, no one has the right to exercise istihsan or to employ masalih mursala, nor to imitate anyone who has not been commanded to be imitated, like a mujtahid (juridical scholar) imitating another mujtahid or imitating the Companions. In these matters, there is disagreement among scholars, and it is a refutation against those who oppose his statement, Exalted is He: 'The judgment is only for Allah; He has commanded that you not worship except Him'(294)."

In these statements that we have cited from Imam Izz al-Din ibn Abd al-Salam, there is enough to refute any distortion of his words.
