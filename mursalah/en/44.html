The rationale for bringing about benefits and averting harms

To this, some jurists (usuliyyin)(98) go, considering benefits as a reason for the Sharia, and bringing about benefits and averting harms as reasons for legal rulings. Al-Amidi, for example, considers benefit as a description that can validly be established as a reason for a ruling, but he stipulates that the evidence must indicate that it is a benefit for it to be considered valid. He dedicates a section to this and states(99): "The eighth section on establishing the evidence that relevance and consideration are indicators of a description being a reason, because the rulings were legislated for the objectives of the servants; as for the fact that they are legislated for objectives and rulings, this is indicated by consensus and reason."

Al-Shatibi holds that customs are among those matters in which the legislator (al-shari) has considered the meanings, that is, the underlying reasons (illal), and he sees these reasons as interests (masalih). He states(100): "The principle in customs is to pay attention to the meanings... For we have found the legislator aiming for the interests of the servants, and the ordinary rulings revolve around them wherever they go. You see a single matter being prohibited in a situation where there is no interest, but if there is an interest, it is permitted, like the dirham for dirham with a delay, which is prohibited in sale but permissible in a loan... And the legislator has elaborated on the reasons and rulings in the legislation of the chapter on customs... Most of the time, he has justified them with the appropriate reasons that, when presented to the minds, are accepted. Thus, we understand from this that the legislator intended to follow the meanings in this regard, not to adhere strictly to the texts, unlike the chapter on acts of worship...".

Thus, we find among many of the usuliyyin (jurisprudential scholars) the view that the relationship between the ruling and the description is a cause (illa), and this was the entry point for the discussion of public interests (masalih mursala), the studies of relationships, and the cause and its pathways, or the indicators of it from the discussions of qiyas (analogy). Al-Qarafi (may Allah be pleased with him) said(101): "The indicators of the cause are eight: text, implication, relationship, analogy, rotation, examination, extension, and refinement of the basis." Al-Razi (may Allah be pleased with him) mentioned in Al-Mahsool that they are ten with an addition.
