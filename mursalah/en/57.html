The reality is constantly changing and developing, and the new developments require an endless and limitless number of rulings. As for the texts or partial principles, they are limited and finite, and the finite cannot suffice for anything other than the finite. Therefore, it is necessary to have a way to establish partial rulings, and this way is through public interest (maslaha), based on the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) in a comprehensive manner. Some scholars of hadith(133) say: "The changing morals, integrity, and conditions of people may lead to a situation where what was once a benefit becomes a harm. If the door of legislation through public interest were not opened for the jurists, Islamic law would become narrow in addressing the interests of the people, falling short of their needs, and would not be suitable for keeping pace with various places, times, environments, and conditions. This is contrary to what is known about it in terms of flexibility, comprehensiveness, and expansiveness."(134)

"The companions of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) undertook many actions for which there was no prior evidence to consider, but they did so purely based on the benefit they perceived in them, ... rather, they legislated for these incidents (and rulings) what they saw as achieving the public interest (maslaha) that brings benefit or prevents harm, according to what their minds comprehended."(135).

One of the matters that it is claimed the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) acted upon as an inference from public interest (maslaha mursala) is:

Their agreement (may Allah be pleased with them) to compile the scattered pages in which the Qur'an was written into a single mushaf occurred when the killing of the memorizers of the Qur'an increased in the Battle of Yamama during the time of Abu Bakr, at the suggestion of Umar (may Allah be pleased with them both), when he saw the benefit in this compilation.

The appointment of Abu Bakr to Umar and his nomination for the leadership after him has no apparent support other than the public interest (maslaha) that he considered.
