In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful
Introduction
The study of the authority of public interest (maslaha mursala) is a foundational issue that has been debated both in the past and in modern times. However, there is a clear distinction between the disagreements among the imams and usulists in the early periods, which were characterized by a multitude of scholars, the flourishing of knowledge and jurisprudence, and the dominance of Islam, and the disagreements among contemporary scholars in the later periods, which are marked by a scarcity of scholars and their reliance on holding certificates.
In the early periods, the majority of scholars held that public interest is not a valid proof. In contrast, in the later periods—such as our own—you can hardly find anyone who engages in a proper legal discussion on this matter, nor can you find anyone who rejects it.
Moreover, in these recent times—characterized by the decline of Islam and the subjugation of Muslims to their enemies, as well as the replacement of Islamic concepts with Western or Eastern concepts derived from disbelief—you find scholars who are agents of disbelief and its rulers, who have taken prominent positions in knowledge and fatwa, altering the rulings of Allah and issuing fatwas declaring the unlawful as lawful, under the pretext of public interest and the changing times, and under the pretext that the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) must be preserved, regardless of how this preservation is achieved. Thus, you see usury becoming permissible, the rulings on punishments changing, and there being no issue with this as long as the goal is deterrence and prevention; we can deter in ways other than the old methods. Mixing between genders becomes widespread, seeking assistance from disbelievers to fight Muslims becomes a lawful act, and making peace with the Jews and recognizing their sovereignty over Muslim lands becomes something encouraged by Islam, as it promotes peace.
For this reason, many of the certainties and fundamentals of Islam are obscured, and many foreign ideas are promoted as if they are legal principles, when they are not. Such as: "what has become widespread is accepted," and "the change of rulings with the change of times cannot be denied," and "wherever there is public interest, there is the law of Allah," and customary practices, among others.
