As for those who expanded on deriving the rationale and considered it a cause, even if it were devoid of legal consideration, and unleashed their minds to search, estimate, and determine for the rulings causes that the Sharia does not support with general or specific evidence, they have fallen into errors. They noticed that their rational justifications often conflict with the Sharia, and they observed that the rulings of the Sharia frequently contradict the requirements of rational suitability. An example of this is the waiting period (iddah) of a free woman and that of a slave woman, the nakedness (awrah) of a free woman and that of a slave woman, considering only water for purification, and in its absence, the use of dust, and the non-consideration of chemical purifiers like alcohol and similar substances. This is where the Sharia does not align with rational suitability.
When encountering these contradictions, it was noted that some rational suitability sometimes aligns with the Sharia, while some appears to conflict with it. Some have no text to support them nor to refute them. Thus, they divided suitability into three categories:
The first category: which is the suitability that the Sharia acknowledges and is referred to as the acknowledged suitability.
The second category: which is the suitability that the Sharia has annulled, which the mind establishes as a cause for legislation, but the Sharia indicates its unsuitability and lack of causation.
The third category: which is the rational suitability that the Sharia has neither acknowledged nor annulled. This is referred to as the unqualified suitability.
They stated that suitability is interest (maslaha). They said: unqualified interest (maslaha mursal). This is akin to considering the genus of the description in the genus of the ruling. For whatever description the ruling was established for, that description is an interest. The mind sees nothing suitable except what it perceives as an interest. Thus, all causes are encompassed by the description of interest. Moreover, interest exists in every ruling that the legislator has sought, and harm exists in every ruling that the legislator has prohibited. Preventing harm is an interest, so interest becomes the suitable description in any ruling, whether it is a request for action or a request for abstention.
