The opinion of a group of contemporary scholars

A large number of our contemporary scholars have gone to the view that the term "public interest (maslaha) that is not explicitly mentioned" is a name that some scholars of the principles of Sharia have sometimes used to refer to the means and methods by which legislative rulings are implemented, and this reinforces the understanding we have presented regarding the imams' views on public interest that is not explicitly mentioned.

This opinion has become clear in the memorandum submitted by a crowd of scholars from the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia to King Fahd under the name "Memorandum of Advice," which was published in the magazine Al-Wa'i in issues 69, 70, and 71 for the year 1993. I will mention here some excerpts from what the magazine reported and what was included in the memorandum to clarify the scholars' opinions and the content of the opinion.

The magazine stated(285): "... the materials or procedural rulings, and these are the materials that stipulate rulings related to material means and methods by which legislative rulings are implemented, which is what scholars of the principles of Sharia have sometimes referred to as public interest that is not explicitly mentioned." The scholars then provided examples of public interest that is not explicitly mentioned. The memorandum stated: "For example: the collection of zakat on specific funds, determining who is obligated to pay it, and distributing it to specific categories of people, all of this is considered legislative rulings since the ruling of the obligation to collect zakat by the state, and not giving it to those who are not entitled, and the conditions of zakat such as the amount and the reason for it, and what prevents its payment from debts and so forth, all of this is considered legislative rulings for the actions of those obligated by duty or status. As for the means used to carry out this legislative ruling, such as transportation methods, written or magnetic records, and how the collectors connect with the people, either by going to them or inviting them to pay their zakat to the zakat department by writing to them via registered mail, and the period during which this occurs, whether at the beginning, middle, or end of the year, all of these matters are considered procedural rulings related to the method and means of implementing this legislative ruling.
