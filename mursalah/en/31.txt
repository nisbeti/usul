As for if the intention is not of the type of excess needs, it is the third category, which pertains to improvement and adornment and the care for good practices in customs and transactions. This is like depriving slaves of the eligibility to bear witness, in that the slave is of a lower status and rank because he is subjugated to the owner, occupied with serving him." End quote.
Imam al-Shatibi (may Allah have mercy on him) says(57): "These objectives do not exceed three categories: the first is that they are necessary, the second is that they are needed, and the third is that they are for improvement. As for the necessary, its meaning is that they are essential for the establishment of the interests of religion and the world, such that if they are lost, the interests of the world will not proceed correctly, but rather in corruption, chaos, and loss of life, and in the other case, loss of salvation, bliss, and returning with manifest loss." He also says(58): "The total of necessities is five, which are: the preservation of religion, life, lineage, wealth, and intellect, and they have said that they are to be observed in every community." He adds(59): "As for the needs, their meaning is that they are required in terms of expansion and alleviating the hardship that generally leads to difficulty and distress resulting from the loss of what is sought. If they are not observed, hardship and distress will generally befall the obligated." An example of this according to al-Shatibi is "the concessions that ease the burden in cases of illness, travel, the permissibility of hunting, and enjoying the good things that are lawful in food, drink, clothing, and housing, and similar matters...".
"And as for improvements, their meaning is to adopt what is appropriate from the virtues of customs and to avoid the disgraceful conditions that rational minds find repugnant. This encompasses the category of noble morals. Among his examples are: 'removing impurities in acts of worship and purification, covering the private parts, adorning oneself, and drawing closer through voluntary good deeds, almsgiving, acts of devotion, and similar things.' Al-Shatibi considers the necessary objectives as the foundation for the needed and the improved, and the needed objectives as the foundation for the improved objectives."(60)
