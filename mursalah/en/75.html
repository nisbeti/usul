The Sharia has clarified in other places that justice is what is commanded, and it prohibits the wrongdoing that other texts have identified as wrongdoing. Similarly, this applies to goodness, lewdness, and oppression. What is commanded by the legislator is justice, and what is prohibited is wrongdoing, not because reason has examined and found that what the legislator commands aligns with what reason recognizes as justice, goodness, or what is known, and that what is prohibited aligns with what reason recognizes as lewdness, wrongdoing, or oppression, but rather because the command comes from the Creator, who alone has the authority to command and prohibit. To Him alone belongs the determination of what is good and what is bad. As for what al-Bouti argued when he cited this verse to support the concept of public interest (maslaha) by saying, "Justice is the straight line between the extremes of excess and deficiency"(193), this statement lacks clarity. For this statement to have a meaning that can be understood by reason, one must know what excess and deficiency are. Could it not be that what a person perceives with their reason and based on their interest as excess is actually justice or deficiency? And what reason perceives as deficiency is actually justice or excess, and what is seen as justice is actually excess or deficiency, and likewise what is seen as interest is actually corruption or the opposite? Did Allah, Exalted is He, not say: {And if the truth had followed their desires, the heavens and the earth and whoever is in them would have been corrupted}(194)? And also His saying: {And know that among you is the Messenger of Allah; if he were to obey you in much of the matter, you would surely be in trouble}(195)? Therefore, Allah, Exalted is He, commands us in this verse to uphold the justice that He has established as justice and clarified for us in the Sharia, and prohibits us from the wrongdoing that He has established as wrongdoing and clarified for us in the Sharia. The use of the term public interest for what Allah commands is because Allah commanded it, not because we understood and determined what the interest is and then found the Sharia commanding us what we understood and determined! No, it is not so. Thus, this verse does not provide evidence for public interest, nor does it indicate that the Sharia takes public interest into account.

In this way, the argument based on the induction of the Sharia that the interests of the servants are a fundamental principle in legislation falls apart, and the claim of making the legislator's category of interests a category of rulings is invalidated.
