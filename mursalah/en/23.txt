Analogy (qiyas) as a principle of legislation is rejected by the Zahiri school and by the Imami Shia. Ibn Hazm vehemently rejected analogy. And although Ibn Hazm was mistaken in his rejection of analogy, he – and Allah knows best – was not mistaken in rejecting analogy that is based on any unifying reason.
The denial of analogy (qiyas) has only been attributed to the Zahiris, the Imami Shia, and some of the Mu'tazila.
In the context of the disagreement regarding the cause (illa), al-Amidi(38) says: "They differed on the permissibility of the cause in the original sense being merely an indication; the preferred view is that the cause in the original sense must be understood as a motivator, meaning it must include a valid wisdom that is intended by the Legislator in enacting the ruling."
Some have argued that it is permissible to provide a rationale based on any abstract description, even if the context of the description does not indicate causality. Al-Amidi (may Allah have mercy on him) said(39): "Most scholars have maintained that it is impermissible to justify a ruling based on a wisdom that lacks a defining criterion, while a minority have permitted it. Among them, some have distinguished between apparent wisdom that is self-evident and hidden wisdom that is ambiguous, allowing justification based on the former but not the latter, and this is the preferred view." Therefore, Sheikh Muhammad al-Khudari (may Allah have mercy on him) has stated that the rationale is applied in relation to two concepts:
"The first is the wisdom that motivates the legislation of the ruling, which is a benefit sought to be attained or completed, and a harm sought to be averted or minimized"(40). He means here that it is understood from the text.
The second: "What exists is a presumption for the existence of wisdom"(41), and this is when the wisdom is hidden. What is meant here is that there is nothing in the texts that indicates it, neither explicitly nor implicitly.
As for Imam al-Nabhani, he holds that the legal cause (illa) is derived only from the Qur'an, the Sunnah, and the consensus of the Companions (ijma'), and he does not see any legal value in the cause unless it is indicated by the texts. He says(42): "It becomes clear from the examination of the legal texts in the Qur'an and the Sunnah that the legal text indicates the cause either explicitly, implicitly, or through deduction."
