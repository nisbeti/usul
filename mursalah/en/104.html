There is no specific or particular text that indicates this, but there is a general principle or comprehensive evidence under which the means or methods fall. It divides them into two categories: entities and actions. The entities are means to which the ruling of the original principle in things applies. The actions are methods that are subject to the rule that what is necessary for the completion of an obligation is itself obligatory.

And his statement: "And if it is not stipulated, then it is either rational or customary, so it does not have to be legal" refers to the fact that the ruling based on public interest (maslaha) may have its evidence in "what is necessary for the completion of an obligation is itself obligatory," and this is a necessary principle in both legal and non-legal matters. The public interest (maslaha mursala) according to al-Shatibi does not mean that it is unrestrictedly sent forth, but rather that it refers to means or methods that are derived from a specific evidence or particular proof that directly indicates this detail, but they are not derived from a general evidence or comprehensive proof under which they fall. He stated in al-Muwafaqat(275): "Every legal principle that does not have a specific text supporting it, and is compatible with the actions of the Sharia and whose meaning is taken from its evidences, is valid and can be relied upon if that principle has become certain through its collective evidences. Because the evidences do not have to indicate certainty in the ruling by themselves without the addition of others to them, as previously mentioned, because that is nearly impossible, and this includes a type of unrestricted reasoning..."

What reinforces this meaning of public interests (masalih mursala) according to al-Shatibi is his statement(276): "If we were to assume the preservation of the Qur'an and knowledge without books, that would be valid, and likewise, all other necessary interests can be preserved." For the writing of the mushaf and the writing of knowledge, such as hadith and jurisprudence, are among the necessary methods for preserving the Qur'an and preserving knowledge. If we were to assume that the preservation of the Qur'an and knowledge is possible through a method other than writing, that would be acceptable legally. It is as if al-Shatibi is saying: the preservation of the Qur'an and the preservation of knowledge is an obligation, and it is necessary to adopt the required method for that, even if there is no specific evidence stipulated for it. The absence of a specific text makes that method a public interest (maslaha mursala), and if there is only one method, then that method is obligatory based on "what is necessary for the completion of an obligation is itself obligatory," and if there are multiple methods, then any one of them that is adopted is valid.
