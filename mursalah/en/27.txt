The Tabi'in (successors) (53), and Medina is the gathering place of knowledge and scholars, the sun of Islam and the source of its light. Reason dictates that the consensus of the people of Medina on a matter should continue what was prevalent during the time of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) and during the time of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him). However, this statement is not definitive from one perspective, nor can it be said to continue in every subsequent era. Therefore, it is not a proof, as previously mentioned in our discussion about the consensus of the Companions (54).
As for the opinion of a Companion, Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal stated it, and the majority agree that it is not a proof. It is permissible to follow it as a matter of imitation, not as a proof in itself, since the proof lies in what has been revealed. The proof is established in the words of the Companions if they reach a consensus. As for one or two or more, their saying or action is not a proof.
The legislation of those before us has also been a subject of disagreement. Some said: The legislation of those before us is legislation for us as long as it has not been abrogated. Others said that all previous laws are generally abrogated, and this is the correct view, and Allah knows best. He, Exalted is He, said: “For each We have made a law and a method” {لِكُلٍّ جَعَلْنَا مِنكُمْ شِرْعَةً وَمِنْهَاجًا} (55).
The principle of istihsan (juridical preference) has been attributed to Abu Hanifa and Imam Ahmad (may Allah be pleased with them), based on what is found in their respective schools of thought regarding branches of law. However, al-Shafi'i rejected it in his book on the invalidation of istihsan. Al-Amidi rejected it in al-Ihkam, and al-Ghazali in al-Mustasfa. Al-Shafi'i is famously quoted as saying: "Whoever prefers has legislated," and istihsan has been regarded by those who rejected it as a statement based on whim and desire. The opinions on its definition are varied, leading to different meanings.
As for the proof of masalih mursala (public interest) or istislah, I will address it in detail, clarifying who advocated for it and who rejected it, along with a detailed discussion on the foundation upon which this proof is built, which is the concept of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia). I will exert my efforts to extract what I see as correct from their statements.
