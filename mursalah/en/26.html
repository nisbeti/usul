The consideration of differences and others is ultimately based on the fact that the Sharia (Islamic law) was revealed to bring about benefits and avert harms. The principle of blocking the means (sadd al-dhara'i) is recognized by al-Shatibi(47).

As for induction (istiqra), it is not a proof but rather a method of knowledge and exploration that involves the thorough examination of things of the same kind or that share common characteristics. It is a generally valid method of understanding, not specific to legal matters, and the judgment on its validity or invalidity is subject to reason; it falls within the realm of logical or mathematical inquiries(48).

As for the minimum requirement (al-takhfif)(49), it is also not a proof or a source of legislation, but rather an investigation into the scope of rulings. This means that the ruling is related to this amount or to something greater or smaller than it. These amounts are the subject of the ruling, that is, its scope, and if it is valid, it is a legal ruling and not a principle. It may be considered in the context of preference.

As for custom (urf)(50), some jurists have accepted it while others have rejected it. A review of the opinions regarding custom indicates that it does not stand on its own as evidence. What some have considered as evidence for custom is merely a matter of terminology(51), such as languages and specific terminologies, or a matter of estimation(52), like the estimation of wages or dowries. However, custom, which refers to the prevalent practice among a specific group or what is repeated in actions to become customary, its prevalence or habitual nature does not constitute evidence nor does it render it lawful; rather, it is subject to the indications of the Sharia to determine its permissibility or prohibition based on detailed evidence for each action.

As for the claim of the consensus of the people of Medina, it is attributed to the Malikis, and according to Imam Malik (may Allah have mercy on him), it refers to the practice of the people of Medina.
